# Chapter 8 — Arrival

Allison didn’t go over right away.

The days that followed were ordinary in all the ways that mattered—classes, homework, the steady rhythm of school settling back into place. Kara filled the space like she always did. Teachers assigned work. Evenings passed. Nothing demanded a decision.

Evan didn’t bring it up again.

He didn’t text. He didn’t check in. The invitation stayed exactly where she’d left it—unfinished, untouched, quietly present. Allison noticed the absence more than she would have noticed pressure. The space he left felt deliberate.

That, somehow, made it heavier.

She caught herself thinking about his house at strange moments. Standing in line for lunch. Half-listening in class. Lying awake later than she meant to, staring at the ceiling while the thought circled without landing.

Not yet had felt like relief at the time.

Now it felt like something she was carrying.

When she finally told her mom she was going over to Evan’s house, the words came out carefully, like they might tip something if she said them wrong.

“Okay,” her mom said easily. “I’ll be here.”

That was it. No questions. No follow-up.

Allison grabbed her jacket and stepped outside, the late afternoon air cooler than she expected. The walk over felt longer than usual, even though nothing about the distance had changed.

By the time she reached his porch, the hesitation had settled into something quieter and heavier. Not doubt. Not fear.

Momentum.

Evan’s house looked the same as it always had.

That was the first thing Allison noticed, and the thing she tried not to lean on too much.

The front light was on. The porch steps creaked in the familiar way. Through the front window she could see the lamp in the living room, the one with the shade that was always slightly crooked. It was a place she’d been before without thinking about it, which should have made stepping up onto the porch easy.

It didn’t.
