# Chapter 7 — The Invitation

Allison didn’t tell Kara about Mara.

Not because she’d decided not to—just because the moment never quite arrived.

Kara filled the walk home with the usual stream of commentary. A quiz she thought she’d bombed but probably hadn’t. Someone in chem who still didn’t know how to use the sink correctly. Weekend plans that were half-formed but already felt like they belonged to both of them.

Allison listened. She nodded. She laughed in the right places.

It took more effort than usual.

Every so often, Kara glanced sideways at her, like she was checking something against a private scale. Allison felt the looks land without knowing what they were measuring.

“You okay?” Kara asked eventually.

“I’m fine,” Allison said automatically.

Kara accepted the answer and moved on. The ease of it left Allison with a brief, hollow relief that faded almost immediately.

At home, everything held. Backpack by the door. Shoes kicked off. Her mom asking if she wanted a snack. The kitchen smelling faintly like something warm and familiar. Nothing had changed, which made the pressure under Allison’s ribs harder to place.

She went to her room and sat on the bed with her phone in her hands.

There was a message from Evan, timestamped nearly an hour earlier.

*You okay?*

Allison stared at it longer than she meant to.

She typed *Yeah* and erased it.

Typed *I think so* and paused, her thumb hovering. That felt like opening a door she wasn’t ready to stand in.

She locked her phone without sending anything.

Time passed in small pieces. Homework half-started and abandoned. The sound of her mom moving around the house. The sky outside her window shifting from pale afternoon to something darker and quieter.

Her phone buzzed again.

*No rush,* Evan had added. *Just checking in.*

The words should have made it easier.

They didn’t.

Downstairs, her mom called up that dinner would be ready soon. Allison went down and helped set the table, the normalcy almost a relief. They talked about school in broad, unimportant strokes. A teacher who assigned too much reading. A rumor about the football field lights finally getting fixed.

“You’ve been a little quiet today,” her mom said, not accusing, just noticing.

Allison considered lying. Considered saying she was tired.

“I’m fine,” she said instead. It wasn’t a lie. It just wasn’t the whole shape of it.

Her mom nodded, satisfied enough, and changed the subject. The moment passed without leaving a mark.

Later, the knock came.

“Allison?” her mom called. “Evan’s here.”

Allison’s chest tightened—not sharply, just enough to notice. She took a breath and stood, aware of how deliberate the movement felt.

“I’ll be right there.”

Evan stood on the porch when she opened the door, hands tucked into the pockets of his jacket. He didn’t step inside. He didn’t lean forward. He looked like he’d already decided where the boundary was and stopped there on purpose.

“Hey,” he said.

“Hey.”

There was a pause. Not awkward. Just open.

“I didn’t mean to drop by,” Evan said. “My mom asked me to tell you you’re welcome to come over sometime. If you want.”

The phrase landed heavier than it should have.

“She didn’t mean—” He stopped, then shook his head. “There’s no expectation. I just… didn’t want it to feel like you had to respond to a text.”

Allison appreciated that more than she expected.

“Do you want to walk?” he asked after a moment. “Just around the block. Or not.”

Allison glanced back into the house. Her mom had already gone back to what she’d been doing, the door left open behind her like a quiet option. Allison stepped outside and closed it gently.

“Okay,” she said.

They walked without a clear destination, following the familiar streets that curved away from her house. The air was cooler now, carrying the smell of damp leaves and someone grilling a few houses down.

They didn’t talk right away.

Evan matched her pace without comment. Didn’t rush her. Didn’t fill the silence. It made the quiet feel intentional, which somehow made it heavier.

“I didn’t know what to say,” Allison said finally. “About your text.”

“That’s okay,” Evan said, then paused. “I mean—I figured.”

“I kept typing things,” she said. “None of them felt right.”

“Yeah,” he said. “That sounds about right.”

They walked another block. The houses blurred together into the same shapes she’d known her whole life. Everything looked normal. That made the pressure feel harder to explain.

“I don’t feel ready,” Allison said quietly. “And I don’t know what I’m not ready for.”

Evan nodded. “That makes sense.”

She appreciated that he didn’t try to fix it.

They turned back toward her house without naming the decision. By the time they reached her porch again, the sky had darkened another shade.

“I’ll come by,” Allison said. “Just not yet.”

“That’s fine,” Evan said. “Really.”

She believed him.

Still, when she stepped back inside, her shoulders ached slightly, like she’d been holding herself differently without realizing it.

Later that night, she opened her notes app.

- I didn’t answer.  
- Time makes things heavier.  
- Saying *later* is still a choice.

She stared at the last line, then locked her phone.
