# Chapter 6 — Consent-First Contact

*Allison’s Notes (written in the bathroom, then not reread):*  
- She asked first.  
- She didn’t explain anything.  
- I don’t know if that makes me feel safer or worse.

The stall door clicked softly when Allison locked it. She stayed there longer than necessary, listening to the low, even hum of the ventilation fan until her breathing matched it. The quiet helped, but only the way a closed door helped—temporary, borrowed.

She balanced her phone against her knee and typed with one thumb.

The first line came easily. The second took longer. By the time she reached the third, her thumb hovered, uncertain, as if the act of finishing the thought would solidify something she wasn’t ready to carry yet.

She typed it anyway.

Then she stared at the screen, waiting for the feeling that usually came after writing things down—the small sense of containment, like she’d boxed something up neatly enough to set aside. It didn’t come. She locked her phone without rereading any of it.

The bell rang while she was washing her hands. The sound jolted her, sharper than usual. She looked up at her reflection, half-expecting to see something different. There wasn’t. She still looked like herself, which made the tightness in her chest feel unearned.

She took an extra second before opening the door.

The hallway rushed in around her—voices overlapping, lockers slamming, the familiar current of people moving because they were supposed to. It all looked exactly the same. That should have been reassuring. Instead, it made her feel slightly out of step, like she’d paused while everything else kept going.

Her phone buzzed almost immediately.

*Where’d you disappear to?* — Kara

Allison read the message twice. She could picture Kara’s tone perfectly: casual, half-joking, already planning the next thing. Allison typed, erased, then typed again.

*Needed air.*

It was true. It didn’t explain anything. She sent it before she could overthink it and slipped the phone into her pocket, as if not looking at it might keep the moment contained.

Outside, the courtyard felt cooler than the hallway, the late afternoon light stretching shadows across the stone edging and benches. Allison sat where she could feel the warmth of the sun through her jeans and focused on the ordinary details—the scrape of shoes on concrete, the distant sound of someone laughing too loudly, the faint smell of cut grass.

She told herself she was just taking a minute.

That was when she noticed Mara.

Evan’s mom stood a few steps away, not moving closer, not pretending to be busy. Allison had been in her kitchen more times than she could count. Had eaten her food, listened to her talk about grocery sales and weather and whether Evan was sleeping enough. Mara was familiar in the way adults became familiar when they were part of someone else’s safe place.

Which made it strange that she hadn’t just sat down.

Mara waited until Allison looked up.

“Allison,” she said gently. “May I sit?”

The question landed softly.

And didn’t go away.

Allison almost nodded out of habit. The hesitation that followed surprised her—the brief, suspended moment where the answer didn’t come automatically. She felt suddenly aware of the space between them, of the way the question had turned the pause itself into something that mattered.

“Yes,” she said.

Mara chose a place beside her that left space between them. Not the easy closeness of a kitchen table. Not distance, either. Just enough room that Allison noticed it.

“I’m not here to explain anything,” Mara said. “And I don’t have answers unless you ask for them.”

Allison folded her hands in her lap. Her fingers pressed together too tightly before she forced them to relax. She nodded once, because that was easier than finding words.

“Oh,” she said, finally.

Mara smiled, careful and warm without being reassuring. “I just wanted to check in. And to tell you that if you ever want to talk—about anything—you can choose to.”

Choose.

The word settled somewhere low and heavy, behind Allison’s sternum.

Mara didn’t soften it. She didn’t follow it with a suggestion, or a question, or the gentle parental guidance Allison was used to from adults who thought they were helping. There was no *when*, no *how*, no *what would happen if*.

The absence felt intentional.

Allison had spent years navigating expectations she could sense without being told—teachers, counselors, strangers who mistook calm for compliance. This was different. This wasn’t an expectation she could slide under or smooth over. This was space, and it demanded something just by existing.

She became aware of Evan then, at the edge of the courtyard. He leaned against a tree, hands in his pockets, watching without staring. He didn’t step in. Didn’t look away. He stayed exactly where he was, like he was holding himself still on purpose.

Allison wondered how much effort that took.

When Mara stood, she didn’t extend the moment or cut it short. She didn’t offer a card, didn’t suggest coming by the house later, didn’t reframe the conversation as something ongoing. She inclined her head slightly, as if acknowledging that the exchange had been complete on its own terms.

“Nothing is required,” she said. “Including a response.”

Then she walked away.

The space she left behind felt larger, not emptier—like something had been carefully removed rather than added.

Allison stayed where she was, watching students cross the courtyard, listening to conversations drift past her without catching. Her phone buzzed again in her pocket. She didn’t check it. The idea of answering anyone felt heavier than it should have.

For the first time since the watching had begun, she didn’t feel managed.

She felt trusted.

The realization didn’t bring relief. It brought weight. Trust meant attention. It meant that doing nothing was no longer invisible—it was something someone else would have to hold.

Evan joined her only after a minute had passed, sitting far enough away that their shoulders didn’t touch.

“You okay?” he asked.

“I think so,” Allison said. The words came out slower than usual. “I just… didn’t expect that.”

Evan nodded, like that made sense. “You don’t have to do anything.”

She believed him.

That didn’t make the pressure in her chest ease. It only made it clearer that whatever this was, it wasn’t going to resolve on its own.
