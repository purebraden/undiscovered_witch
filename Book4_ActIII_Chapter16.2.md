## Chapter 16.2 — The Direction She Was Already Facing

Allison felt it before she noticed she had stopped walking.

The corridor curved gently ahead of her, familiar stone and soft light, nothing out of place. She had taken this path dozens of times since arriving. It led nowhere important. That was why she liked it.

Except now she was standing still.

She hadn’t meant to stop. There hadn’t been a sound, or a shift in the air, or anything she could point to and say *that’s it*. Her body had simply… paused. As if it had reached a question before her thoughts had.

Allison frowned and glanced around.

Nothing had changed.

That was the unsettling part.

She took another step—and felt the hesitation return, not as resistance, but as *orientation*. Like the moment before turning your head when you already know someone is behind you.

She exhaled slowly.

*Okay,* she told herself. *Just notice.*

She closed her eyes—not to look inward, but to stop the visual noise from distracting her. The sensation sharpened immediately. Not pressure. Not urgency.

*Direction.*

There was a presence nearby.

Not close enough to touch. Not far enough to ignore.

It didn’t feel like attention. It felt like gravity—something existing steadily enough that everything else adjusted around it.

Allison opened her eyes again and turned slightly, following the feeling without quite deciding to. The corridor widened ahead, opening into a crossing space she rarely used. She had never had a reason to go there.

She felt one now.

The pull didn’t hurry her. That mattered. If it had, she would have resisted. Instead, it waited, patient and unassuming, as if it had always been there and she was only just noticing.

Her footsteps echoed softly as she walked.

With each step, the pathways she had sensed before rearranged—not tightening, not opening, but *aligning*. Like lines on a map settling into coherence. She could feel how moving toward this presence carried obligation, though she didn’t yet know what kind.

That frightened her.

She slowed, then stopped again.

*You don’t have to,* she reminded herself.

The pull did not argue.

It didn’t insist.

It simply remained.

Allison swallowed and took another step.

She became aware of something else then: the pull wasn’t asking her to *do* anything. It wasn’t offering answers. It wasn’t promising relief.

It was asking her to be *present*.

That, somehow, was harder.

She reached the edge of the crossing space and paused, hands curling slightly at her sides. The light here was different—not brighter, just steadier, less prone to shadow. She could feel the presence more clearly now, still indistinct, still unnamed.

Whoever—or whatever—it was, it wasn’t hiding.

It was waiting.

Allison felt a brief, sharp urge to turn back. Not from fear, exactly. From the knowledge that once she stepped fully into this space, pretending nothing had changed would no longer be possible.

She thought of the careful waiting she had been practicing. The way she had learned to make herself smaller without meaning to. The way silence had taught her habits she hadn’t questioned.

Her chest tightened.

*I’m allowed to walk toward something,* she thought.

The realization didn’t feel rebellious.

It felt overdue.

Allison stepped forward.

The presence did not advance.

It did not retreat.

It simply resolved, just enough for her to understand one thing with sudden, uncomfortable clarity:

This was not chance.

She had been facing this direction for a while now.

She had just finally noticed.
