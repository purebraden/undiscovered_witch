# Chapter 39 — Transfer, Then Voice

“Four.”

The word slid instead of landing. Rachel felt it catch in the hollow behind her eye, like it had been said somewhere else first and only arrived now because it remembered to.

The doorway waited ahead of her. The frame stood inside it, upright, ribs exposed, inner surface dull and stippled. It didn’t glow. It didn’t hum yet. That came later.

Hands on her arms. Not gripping. Positioned. The woman on her right kept her thumb hovering just above Rachel’s pulse, never quite touching. The man on her left smelled like disinfectant soaked into old fabric. Someone behind them shifted weight and the floor answered with a soft vibration, a patient mechanical breath from somewhere under the tiles.

“Five.”

They stepped.

Rachel’s foot lagged.

Not refusal. Not fear. The instruction just arrived late, like it had been routed the long way around.

The woman’s thumb dipped, corrected. Someone murmured, low, meant for the others.

“—watch timing—”

The frame’s inner ring twitched. A sound like something clearing its throat.

“Six.”

The hallway lights flickered out of order. Not off. Just wrong. One strip brightened while the next dimmed, a ripple that moved too fast to follow.

Rachel swallowed. Air scraped her throat.

Her shoulder brushed the frame and didn’t feel it until a heartbeat later. Cold bloomed across skin that had already moved on.

“Seven.”

The inner ring slid forward.

Metal shrieked, thin and high. The sound bent. Rachel’s teeth rang in response.

“Hold—”

The word came apart in the air.

Her weight tipped. The man tightened his grip by reflex. Pressure spiked and went somewhere else instead. Her vision jumped sideways. The hallway stretched. A face elongated, mouth opening too slowly.

The frame closed another inch and stopped.

Grinding. Not steady. Searching.

Rachel’s scream came out wrong. It folded back on itself, a loop that never found an edge.

The world slipped.

Not stopped. Misaligned.

The hands on her arms dragged behind her body. The sound of the grinding stretched into a line she could see, thin and vibrating between the rings. Someone’s voice ran fast and left its echo standing alone.

Her chest locked.

The pressure pressed a word into her head: stop.

Not the ceiling speakers. Inside.

Not loud. Not gentle. Just there, pressed exactly where the pressure peaked.

Rachel choked. Her mouth moved. Nothing followed.

The grinding wavered.

You’re crossing crooked, the voice said.

The frame shuddered. A spark snapped from the console to the wall. The tech standing there flinched and didn’t get his hand up in time. Blood opened along his temple, narrow and straight, like it had been placed.

“—medical—”

The word came from too far away.

Rachel’s lungs burned.

Breathe, it said. Not a command. A timing note.

She pulled air and it hurt. Heat flared behind her eyes. The pressure slid, not easing, just relocating.

The inner ring lurched forward again.

“No—wait—”

The woman’s glove caught between metal surfaces. She cried out, sharp and surprised. Skin tore with a small sound that didn’t belong in a hallway.

Something jumped tracks inside Rachel.

There, it noted.

Her body twisted.

Ugly. Fast. No plan.

Her shoulder screamed as she wrenched free. The glove came loose with a wet snap and the woman hit the wall hard enough to leave a mark that lagged behind her.

The frame screamed back.

Lights died.

Emergency strips flared red, uneven, pulsing like they couldn’t agree on a rhythm.

Rachel dropped to her knees just outside the threshold. Palms slapped tile. Pain arrived in pieces, late and overlapping. Her breath stuttered and didn’t want to line up.

Boots scraped. Someone fell. Someone else swore.

The frame slammed shut with a heavy mechanical finality, empty.

Rachel stayed on the floor because standing required agreement she didn’t have yet.

Inside her head, the pressure changed shape.

Not gone. Focused.

Better, it evaluated.

“Get out,” Rachel whispered. Her throat felt flayed. She wasn’t sure the sound left her.

A pause. Short. Calculated.

No, it said.

Hands reached for her again and hesitated.

“Don’t move,” someone said. Too loud. Too close.

Rachel pushed back on shaking arms. The floor tilted. Her knees argued with gravity and lost halfway.

The woman with the torn glove sat against the wall, staring at her hand like it had been swapped while she wasn’t looking.

The tech with the bleeding temple pressed gauze to his head and didn’t blink.

A voice cracked overhead through the ceiling speakers, clipped and thin.

“Transfer aborted. Reclassify subject. Initiate contin—”

The word broke. Static ate the rest.

Wrong cadence, it noted.

Rachel staggered to her feet. The frame loomed beside her, silent now, pretending it had behaved.

“I can’t,” she said, and laughed because the sound fell out of her mouth before she could stop it. “I don’t—”

Too many steps, it said.  
A beat.  
They’re late again.

More boots. Faster. Different pattern.

Someone raised a weapon and didn’t fire.

Rachel backed away from the doorway, each step a negotiation that kept changing its terms. The red lights pulsed out of sync with her heartbeat. Or maybe the other way around.

“Please,” someone said. Not procedural. Human.

Rachel’s heel caught on nothing and she windmilled, barely catching herself on the wall. The touch burned.

Inside her head, the presence shifted closer—not intimate, not invasive. Occupied.

If they clamp you again, it will spill wider, it warned.

Her stomach dropped.

“What do you want?” she said, to the wall, to the frame, to the air.

The answer came without hurry.

Motion, it said.

The word landed and stayed there, heavy and unfinished.

Rachel turned and ran.

The hallway didn’t quite keep up.

# Chapter 40 — Baseline Break

Rachel ran and the hallway refused to decide what it was.

Red emergency strips pulsed overhead, not in a steady rhythm but in a pattern that kept forgetting itself, and every footfall landed before its sound caught up, a dull echo arriving late like it was embarrassed to be behind. The air burned metallic at the back of her throat, heat riding her breath until it tasted like she’d bitten down on a penny and kept it there.

Boots chased her—not one set, not even a line, too many feet hitting together, confident, synchronized, the sound of people moving the way the building expected them to move. The corridor liked that sound and answered it, floor and walls carrying the rhythm forward for them.

Her heel slid on stone that hadn’t been slick a second ago, balance pitching forward and correcting late, knees absorbing a fall that never fully happened. She gasped and the breath came out wrong, half-formed, and the pressure pressed a word into her head—left—cutting across the motion before she knew where left was supposed to be.

She didn’t turn because she didn’t know where left *was* yet, not in a space that kept rearranging its edges. She kept running straight and the corridor narrowed around her in increments too small to measure until her shoulder scraped stone that hadn’t been there, skin tearing, the sting arriving a beat after the contact and then staying too long.

Someone shouted behind her, the sound stretching, thinning, then snapping as if it had hit a seam in the air.

“Stop—”

Her lungs burned as the hallway dipped, and a word—now—tangled with her breathing just as her body lurched left anyway, an ugly half-turn where her hips went first and her shoulders followed, foot planting on nothing and finding tile late as she slammed into a side passage that hadn’t existed when she looked. The turn crushed her ribs together and her vision paled at the edges, light washing thin.

A heavy door slammed somewhere above or below her, the sound coming twice—one version close, one far—each insisting it was the real one.

The passage sloped downward, not stairs, not a ramp, just enough of an angle to pull at her calves and make her stomach float as if the floor had decided gravity was optional. She kept running because stopping would mean standing still inside a place that had begun to anticipate her.

Her breath hitched. Her chest seized for half a heartbeat and then released too late to be helpful, something inside her tightening in response, a pressure like a hand closing, and a warning—don’t—threaded through the sensation without volume or urgency, too late to explain what she was doing wrong.

She was running. She was breathing. She was—

The floor arrived late under her next step and her ankle rolled hard.

Pain flashed white, sharp and immediate, then arrived again slower and deeper, a second copy of the same signal sliding in behind the first like it was trying to catch up. She bit back a cry that still leaked out of her throat as boots hit the turn behind her and the corridor answered with a dull crack, tile giving under weight that trusted it too much.

A shout cut off mid-syllable. Another kept going, layered over it.

She glanced back and regretted it immediately. One of them was down on a knee, one hand splayed against the wall as if he could brace the building into behaving, head jerking in small delayed motions while his other hand reached for his radio and passed over it twice, fingers closing on air that wasn’t there yet.

The others flowed around him without slowing. Procedure did not turn its head.

Rachel faced forward again and swallowed bile as the pressure pressed a single instruction—faster—into the back of her awareness.

“No,” she rasped, the word breaking apart before the breath finished forming, and not speed. Timing slid in behind it, close enough to feel like it was leaning.

Her footfalls were wrong; she could feel it now, each step arriving half a beat out of phase with the hall, her body moving before her mind finished deciding, her thoughts chasing after her own motion like they were late to themselves.

The passage opened abruptly into a wider space that smelled like dust and oil and old paper, the scent landing all at once and then again, delayed.

Archive.  
No sign, no placard. The smell told her.

Metal shelving rose on either side, crates stacked too neatly, a desk bolted to the floor with a lamp that was off. A strip of light above it flickered bright-dim-bright, counting and forgetting where it was in the sequence.

Rachel ran between shelves and clipped a corner that hadn’t been there yet and then was, pain blooming late and multiplying into a hot throb that didn’t quite match the cut. She stumbled and caught herself on the desk, palm slapping wood that flexed under her weight like it was tired.

She froze with her hand still pressed flat, fingers splayed, the room going still in the way a held breath goes still.

Boots hit the threshold behind her.

“Hands visible! Stop moving!”

The voice was too close and she didn’t move because moving felt like it would tear something loose. Her heart hammered, the lamp trembling, cord shifting an inch across the desk before stopping, the overhead light flickering and steadying and flickering again, each change landing just after it should have.

The pressure in her chest leaned toward the voice, curious, and too many eyes slid through her without sound.

Her mouth opened. No sound came.

They entered in numbers that wouldn’t stay fixed—three, then five, then more—faces smooth behind shields, movements practiced and careful, built for spaces that obeyed. They slowed when they saw her hand on the desk, not because of the desk itself but because she was touching something.

“Back away,” a man said through a speaker grille that flattened the words into something almost prerecorded.

She tried to lift her hand and her fingers stayed for a beat longer than they should have, skin dragging against the wood like the surface had teeth. When she jerked free, the desk creaked and then creaked again, the sound repeating itself a fraction late as the lamp slid half an inch.

One of them flinched and caught himself mid-step, boot hovering before settling too carefully, like the floor might punish commitment.

“Don’t approach,” another voice snapped. “Maintain baseline distance.”

Baseline.

Rachel laughed once, short and broken, the sound coming back to her late, echoing as if someone else had done it, and they want you still settled into the space the laugh left behind.

She stared at the nearest shield, at her own warped reflection in the curved plastic. She looked wrong. She looked late.

“What happens,” she asked, breath tearing in and out, “if I can’t?”

No one answered. They didn’t negotiate. They adjusted.

A flat panel unfolded from a case, its grid of dim lights pulsing in patterns too clean for this room, two of them holding it like a shield and angling it toward her without stepping closer. The grid brightened and the pressure in her chest reacted instantly, tightening like a muscle bracing, her breath catching as the air thickened and the space stiffened as if the room itself had been ordered to hold still.

Her knees trembled. She lowered herself onto the bolted stool behind the desk, her body arriving in the sit late, hips dropping after the decision, spine catching up.

The grid eased. Shoulders loosened a fraction.

“Good,” someone said. “Maintain that.”

Her hands shook on her thighs, the tremor shrinking but not stopping, twitching in tiny delayed pulses, the pressure inside her staying tight and unsatisfied.

A radio crackled. “Subject located. Initiate containment corridor. Bring frame.”

Frame.

Her stomach dropped and “Please” slipped out before she could stop it, the word tasting wrong as soon as it left her mouth.

A shield tilted—assessment, not sympathy.

“Don’t make it worse,” someone said softly.

Ask the room slid through her before she could reject it, and she stared at the desk, the lamp, the cord, the small rectangle of shadow beneath it that didn’t line up with the light.

“No,” she whispered.

You already did, it said.

Her fingers twitched. The grid tightened. A boot slid a fraction on tile that had gone slick.

The room listened.

Rachel closed her eyes and didn’t reach for anything, didn’t picture moving the space, just shifted her weight inside her own body—forward, back—enough to be a choice, and the pressure snapped toward it as the lamp slid again, not far, just enough.

The grid stuttered, one light blinking out and coming back late as someone swore behind a shield.

“Stop—”

She opened her eyes. Nothing had resolved. The team was still there, still aiming, still trying to hold a space that refused to stay held, the pressure leaning closer, tight and interested, again threading through the moment without volume.

She didn’t move. The lamp slid again, cord dragging; a shield bumped a shelf and paper rasped, the sound arriving late and multiplying into dry static.

The grid flared, trying to correct, the air thickening until her lungs stuttered and heat breathed out of the wall, dust waking.

“Abort the grid!” someone shouted. “It’s feeding the desync!”

The panel went dark. The stiffness stayed.

Heavy boots gathered outside. The transfer team.

“Clear the archive,” a confident voice called from the hall. “We’re moving her.”

Too many bodies decided at once; relief and fear rippled, and the pressure tilted toward the doorway, drawn by cadence and the promise of a clamp—if they frame you again, it spills.

Rachel stood in pieces, legs straightening before her torso finished, balance arriving late and overshooting, hands lifting with palms open—not surrender, not threat, just visible.

“Don’t,” someone said, but the word didn’t carry.

She backed between shelves that felt closer than they had been a second ago, paper smelling sharp, ink and dust and old glue, hit a shelf that wasn’t there yet, swore as a folder slid off and hit the floor, the papery slap arriving half a beat after it landed.

The doorway filled with heavier armor, a frame on wheels unfolding behind them, metal whispering as rings aligned with clean, merciless geometry.

The room was too full.

Choose, it said.

“Where?” she whispered.

Nothing answered.

The frame finished unfolding.

Her vision slid sideways. Her weight shifted. The pressure snapped toward the movement like a held breath released as the archive light flickered and paper lifted in a slow, dry flutter, as if someone had exhaled through the shelves.

Rachel ran—not toward the door but into the narrow gap between two shelving units that should have been a dead end.

The space opened.

Not a door. Not a seam.

A mistake.

She fell through it shoulder-first, scraping skin and bone, breath knocked out of her as the world peeled away and the next one took too long to arrive. A shout followed her late and then cut off clean.

Rachel hit cold tile and rolled, lungs dragging for air.

The pressure inside her held tight.

Not guiding.  
Not apologizing.  
Waiting.

# Chapter 41 — The Wrong Floor

Rachel hit tile that wasn’t meant to be a floor.

Cold punched up through her shoulder and ribs as she rolled, skin scraping hard enough to burn, breath leaving her in a single useless rush that refused to come back right away. The world lagged behind the impact—sound arriving late, then all at once—metal clattering somewhere above her, paper tearing, a distant shout stretched thin like it had been pulled through water.

She lay there for half a second too long, lungs fluttering uselessly, chest spasming as if breathing had been rerouted and hadn’t found the new path yet.

Up, the pressure urged.

The word cut in without volume, not urgent, not kind, and her body obeyed before her head finished catching up, hands scrambling against tile that felt slicker than it looked. She pushed herself to a knee and nearly pitched forward again as gravity shifted sideways, the floor tilting just enough to be wrong.

Air rushed back into her chest in jagged pulls that burned. Her ankle screamed when she put weight on it, pain arriving late and then stacking, one signal tripping over the next.

The space around her did not resemble the archive.

No shelves. No paper smell. No desk.

The ceiling was lower here, unfinished concrete with seams that didn’t line up, fluorescent strips buzzing unevenly overhead, one flickering with a rhythm that didn’t match the others. The walls were bare except for conduit and a single painted line that ran waist-high and then vanished into nothing where the wall broke off into a corridor that bent sharply out of sight.

Rachel staggered upright and leaned against the wall, palm sliding over cold paint.

Above her—*above*—came noise.

Boots. Shouts. Orders snapping back and forth too quickly to separate. Metal whining as something heavy was moved into place. The sound of authority colliding with a space that had stopped agreeing.

Her heart hammered so hard it blurred her vision.

She took one step and the step landed twice.

The corridor ahead of her looked longer than it should have been, lights spaced unevenly, the far end dimming and brightening like it couldn’t decide whether to exist yet. A sign hung crooked from the ceiling by one bolt, swaying slightly, the letters stuttering as she tried to read them.

—MAINT—

The rest of the word arrived too late to matter.

Rachel limped forward, each step a negotiation with her ankle, with gravity, with the sense that the floor was listening for mistakes. Her hand brushed the wall for balance and left behind a faint smear of blood she didn’t remember earning.

Her breathing started to even out. Not because she calmed down—because the pressure inside her shifted, redistributing, pressing less on her chest and more along her spine, a presence that felt like it was settling in to watch.

Wrong level, it noted.

“What does that mean,” she whispered, voice hoarse, and the words came back to her a fraction late, echoing like someone else had spoken them.

No answer. Just the pressure holding steady.

She reached the corner and stopped short.

The corridor opened into a long maintenance bay filled with equipment that didn’t match—carts pushed half into walls, ladders bolted to nothing, a row of lockers dented and tagged with numbers that repeated out of sequence. The floor was scuffed with overlapping tracks that didn’t line up, some ending abruptly in the middle of the room like whatever made them had stopped existing.

A door at the far end stood ajar, light spilling out from behind it in a clean, confident rectangle that didn’t flicker.

Rachel stared at that light.

Her stomach twisted.

“That’s not—” she started.

Not for you, it said.

The pressure tightened slightly, enough to be felt, not enough to hurt.

She backed away from the door instead, turning down a narrower side passage that smelled faintly of oil and ozone. The lights here were dimmer, the hum uneven, one strip buzzing loud enough to set her teeth on edge.

Her foot caught on a seam in the floor that hadn’t been there a second earlier and she stumbled, catching herself on a railing that vibrated under her grip like it was carrying a current.

Pain flared up her arm, delayed and sharp.

She gasped and the sound snagged halfway out, throat closing reflexively as the pressure inside her surged in response.

Careful, it warned.

“Don’t,” she said, louder this time, and kept moving.

The passage sloped again, shallow but persistent, pulling her downward until her calves ached and her ankle screamed every time she tried to compensate. Sweat slicked her palms. Her heartbeat thudded out of sync with the lights, each pulse arriving a beat late.

The noises above her grew more distant.

Not gone. Redirected.

She reached another corner and slowed, breath coming in harsh pulls, vision swimming. The wall here was marked with faded symbols—arrows, hazard triangles, numbers crossed out and rewritten until they were illegible. Someone had scratched a word into the paint with something sharp and then tried to scrub it away.

The word came into focus and then slipped.

—DO—  
—NOT—  
—USE—

Rachel swallowed.

She took one more step and the floor dipped abruptly, not enough to be a fall but enough to steal her balance. She windmilled, grabbed at empty air, and slammed shoulder-first into a closed door that flexed under the impact.

The door didn’t open.

The wall behind it *did*.

Not breaking. Not collapsing.

Just… sliding.

A seam appeared where there hadn’t been one and widened with a soft, wet sound, concrete shifting aside like it had been waiting for permission. Cold air rushed out, smelling different—cleaner, sharper, like rain trapped underground.

Rachel froze, hand pressed against the door, heart hammering.

“No,” she whispered.

The opening widened another inch.

This is what you wanted, it said.

“I didn’t,” she said, voice shaking.

You wanted out of the frame.

Her stomach dropped.

Boots thundered somewhere above her again, closer now, the sound of pursuit reorganizing itself. A voice barked an order that made the corridor vibrate in response.

The opening in the wall was wide enough now to see into.

Not another hallway.

Not a room.

A space that didn’t finish resolving—edges blurring, light bending strangely, the floor inside slanting at an angle that didn’t correspond to gravity. Something inside it moved, not fast, not slow, just *out of phase* with the rest of the world.

Rachel backed away from it, shaking her head.

“No. No. No.”

The pressure inside her leaned forward, interested.

If they reach you here, it tears wider, it warned.

Her ankle screamed as she shifted her weight. She glanced back down the corridor she’d come from—empty now, lights flickering, the hum uneven—but the sound of boots was too close, echoing wrong, doubled.

She looked at the opening again.

Her breath hitched.

“Where does it go?” she asked, not expecting an answer.

The pressure tightened, almost approving.

Somewhere that won’t hold them, it answered.

Her heart slammed against her ribs.

The corridor lights flared bright and then dimmed, shadows jumping. A shape appeared at the far end of the passage—armor, reflective, too clean for this place.

Rachel made a sound that might have been a laugh or a sob and stepped backward.

The floor behind her shifted.

Not yet.
Waiting.

# Chapter 42 — What Slips Through

Rachel didn’t jump.

She stepped because her ankle failed first, because the floor behind her loosened its opinion about being solid, because the space in front of her felt crowded by intent even though nothing had touched her yet. Her heel crossed the seam and the moment stretched thin, tugged sideways, then let go all at once.

The floor didn’t drop so much as forget her.

She slid—half standing, half falling—cold air tearing up around her legs as the concrete edge skimmed her calf and vanished, the sound she made not matching fear so much as shock at being misplaced. Gravity hesitated. Her stomach did something unpleasant.

She hit something that bent and corrected.

Not soft.  
Not solid.

A surface that accepted her weight late and then pitched her sideways anyway, sending her shoulder into a wall that didn’t line up with anything else. Pain arrived out of order—ribs, then hip, then shoulder again as if it wanted a second try.

Rachel lay there with her face pressed to the floor, palms scraping against a texture that felt like tile until it didn’t, grain shifting under her fingers like the surface was still deciding what it was supposed to be.

Behind her, the opening closed.

Not a slam.  
A decision.

The sound came twice.

Her lungs stuttered. Breath scraped shallow and fast, each inhale catching like it had to pass through something narrow before it was allowed in.

Stay down, it urged—

The words crowded in too close, not comfort, not command, and she hated that her body listened anyway, muscles locking while her heart battered against her ribs like it wanted out.

“No,” she said, but the word slid out weak and wrong, and she stayed there because the idea of standing felt like it would peel her apart.

Light crept in uneven strips overhead, not fluorescent, not warm, something in between that made the shadows smear instead of settle. The walls curved in ways that kept slipping when she tried to track them, angles drifting just enough to make her dizzy.

This wasn’t a hallway.  
It wasn’t a room.

The air smelled sharp and sweet, ozone and something like rain trapped underground, and every sound she made came back altered, breath returning half a beat late, the scrape of her sleeve echoing longer than it should have.

She pushed herself up and immediately regretted it as her vision washed pale, pressure inside her chest shifting forward in response, attentive in a way that made her skin crawl.

Careful, it warned.

Her ankle throbbed, swelling fast, heat blooming out of proportion to the injury. She grabbed it without thinking, fingers shaking, the shaking refusing to stop even after she noticed it.

“Where are we,” she whispered.

The space bent the words before giving them back.

No answer. Just the pressure holding steady, closer now, like it had leaned in to watch.

She tried to stand and failed halfway, body arriving late to its own decision, knees buckling as the floor dipped again—not opening, not collapsing, just *misbehaving*. She caught herself on a low rail that ran along the wall, metal warm under her palm.

The rail pulsed.

Once.

Rachel yanked her hand back like she’d touched something alive.

It’s listening, it warned—

“Everything is listening,” she snapped, anger punching through the fear sharp enough to hurt, and limped forward anyway, hand skimming the wall for balance, counting steps without meaning to—one, two—then stopping when the numbers slipped and refused to line up.

The space widened ahead of her into something that felt almost polite by comparison, ceiling lifting, lights spacing themselves more evenly, grids and arcs painted across the floor that overlapped without agreeing, some faded, some too bright, like several versions of the room had been mapped on top of each other and left there.

At the center stood a structure.

Not a machine.  
Not furniture.

A frame, but wrong—thinner, incomplete, angles bending inward where they should have flared, cables sinking into the floor and disappearing into seams that pulsed faintly, out of sync with her heartbeat.

Rachel stopped short, breath snagging.

“No.”

The pressure inside her tightened, not warning, not reassurance.

Not built for holding, it said.

Her ears rang as she edged around it, ankle screaming every time she put weight down wrong, the light inside the structure lagging behind her movement, shadows adjusting late like they were embarrassed to be caught out.

Something shifted inside it.

Not solid.  
Not stable.

The air folded inward and relaxed again.

Rachel backed away and nearly lost her balance as a sound reached her from behind.

Footsteps.

Not boots.

Bare feet, slapping lightly against the floor in a rhythm that didn’t quite repeat.

She turned too fast, pain tearing up her leg, and had to grab the wall to stay upright as a figure stepped into view from behind a curve in the wall—half seen at first, then all at once.

A person.

Young. Or not. It was hard to tell.

Barefoot. Gray uniform worn thin at the edges. Hair cut close, eyes too bright, pupils blown wide like they’d spent too long in bad light.

They stopped when they saw her.

The moment stretched awkwardly.

“You’re not supposed to be here,” the person said, voice echoing wrong, splitting and rejoining.

Rachel swallowed, throat dry.

“I could say the same.”

The person’s mouth twitched, not quite a smile, not quite anything.

“They’ll come,” they said. “They always do.”

Rachel looked back at the wall behind her. Smooth. No seam. No sign it had ever opened.

“I can’t go back.”

“No one does.”

The pressure inside her chest sharpened, alert now.

This one slipped earlier, it noted.

Rachel’s breath hitched.

“You can hear it too?” she asked before she could stop herself.

The person’s eyes widened a fraction.

“Oh,” they said quietly. “That’s bad.”

The lights rippled in sequence, a wave moving through the space like a signal being passed along something she couldn’t see. The wrong frame pulsed brighter.

The pressure leaned forward, interested.

They found the seam, it said.

Rachel closed her eyes for half a second.

“Then we move,” she said.

The other person hesitated, gaze flicking to her ankle, the frame, the walls that wouldn’t settle.

“Move where?”

Rachel didn’t have an answer.

The hum deepened. Layers stacked out of sync. The space waited.

# Chapter 43 — Shared Weight

They didn’t move right away.

Rachel leaned into the wall because it was there and because the floor still felt undecided about whether standing counted as a stable request. The ache in her ankle had spread upward into a heavy, nauseating throb that made her leg feel borrowed, like it belonged to a slightly misaligned version of her body and hadn’t been returned properly.

A few steps away, the other person shifted their weight without settling, bare feet testing the floor in small, uncertain adjustments. They watched the space more than they watched Rachel, eyes tracking the wrong frame at the center of the chamber and then flicking away again, like looking at it too directly caused problems they’d learned not to invite.

The hum thickened.

Not louder.  
Denser.

It pressed against Rachel’s ears until the air felt packed, resistant.

The pressure pressed a flat statement into her head: you’re carrying excess variance.

Rachel swallowed. “That’s bad,” she muttered, not bothering to ask.

The other person’s head snapped up—not toward the sound, but toward *her*. “What?”

“Nothing,” Rachel said too quickly, and immediately hated how thin it sounded.

The floor twitched.

Not a shake.  
A correction.

Rachel’s shoulder slid against the wall with a soft scrape that echoed from two directions at once. The pressure inside her chest shifted sideways, searching, as if trying to find a position that fit and failing.

The other person rubbed their arms, fingers digging into skin hard enough to leave pale crescents. “When it starts doing that,” they said, nodding at the floor, “you don’t argue with it. You make yourself smaller. Or farther away.”

Rachel snorted once, sharp and breathless. “That’s reassuring.”

The lights flickered in response, one strip dimming while another overcorrected, brightness rippling unevenly across the ceiling.

The wrong frame answered.

Its internal distortion tightened, light folding inward and then slipping back, the air around it bending just enough to make Rachel’s eyes sting.

Weight is misallocated, it noted.

The floor dipped under her good foot—not instantly, but delayed, as if it had waited to be sure—stealing her balance after she thought she’d found it. “Don’t touch,” they said sharply, eyes on the rail, not on Rachel.

Rachel froze.

The space reacted anyway.

# Chapter 55 — Clearance

The corridor opens without asking.

Rachel feels it before she sees it, the space easing around her shoulders, the low hum shifting into something closer to breath than machinery. She doesn’t have to slow. The floor doesn’t argue with her weight.

Lena does.

# Chapter 56 — Standing Room

The room doesn’t open so much as *already be there*.

Concrete underfoot, too smooth, walls curving wrong, ceiling low enough that Rachel feels it in her shoulders even before she notices the unlit fluorescent housings hanging there like dead things. Light leaks from seams that don’t cast shadows properly. It makes her eyes ache if she stares.

The floor takes her weight like it remembers her.

Lena steps in after and stops short.

Not a wall. Not a push. Just the floor failing to finish the job, like it forgets the last step of being solid.

You can feel it.

Another on a crate, knees too wide, legs too long, boots flat despite joints that shouldn’t allow it. Someone near the light seams whose face won’t stay finished, eyes catching brightness and throwing it back too sharp.

Human.  
Almost.

“Hey,” Lena whispers. “Rachel.”

One of them looks up.

“There you are.”

Lena flinches hard enough that Rachel feels it through the grip.

That won’t last.

You waited too long.

Rachel’s grip tightens until the fabric twists.

“How,” Rachel asks.

You’re standing in the wrong place, it said.  
It hurts her when you do.

“No.”

Barely.

See?

That was easier, it said.  
Not for her.

Rachel stands exactly where the room allows her to stand, holding exactly what it will tolerate her holding.

You can hold her again, it said.  
Just not the same way.

The room waits.

# Chapter 57 — Paper

No one moves.

Not because they’re waiting. Not because they’re being told to stay. The room just doesn’t give them anything else to do. It holds Rachel the way it already decided to. It holds Lena like it hasn’t finished deciding yet.

Lena is still pressed into Rachel’s side. Not slumped. Not falling. Just off. One knee bent without choosing to be, weight pitched slightly wrong, fingers twisted in Rachel’s sleeve hard enough that the fabric has gone warm and damp.

Rachel can feel it. The exact place where the room stops helping Lena. Not a line. Not an edge. Just a feeling like standing one inch too far from a curb you didn’t see.

“Okay,” Lena says. Then again. “Okay.”

It sounds steadier than before. That scares Rachel more than if she were shaking.

“I can stand,” Lena adds. “I just need— I need a second.”

The demons don’t look at her.

Someone against the wall shifts, elbows bending wrong. The one on the crate adjusts its seat like it’s settling in for a longer wait. None of them are in a hurry. None of them look concerned.

Rachel doesn’t loosen her grip.

She can’t keep standing like this, the voice says, close, familiar, like it’s always been there and Rachel just never noticed how often.  
You feel that, don’t you.

“That’s not normal,” Lena says, almost casually, like she’s reporting weather. “My thoughts don’t usually smear.”

Rachel squeezes her sleeve without meaning to.

A demon glances over, bored. “She’s staying longer than she should,” it says. “That’ll do it.”

Lena turns her head too fast and winces. “Do what.”

The demon doesn’t answer her. It looks at Rachel instead. “That.”

Silence drops again. Thicker this time.

Rachel’s shoulders ache. She realizes she’s bracing without moving.

Borrowing always leaves residue, the voice says.  
You’ve seen this part.

Rachel inhales too sharply.

Lena notices. Of course she does.

“You heard something,” Lena says. Not accusing. Certain. “You keep doing that. Pausing like someone just tapped you on the shoulder.”

Rachel doesn’t deny it.

“I’m not hallucinating,” Lena says. “That’s the worst part. I know what that feels like. This is different.”

“This feels like my thoughts are being rearranged,” she says. “Like something moved them and didn’t put them back in the same order.”

The demon on the crate hums, agreeable.

Rachel’s chest tightens.

There are ways to stop the drift, the voice says, not urgent, not cruel.  
You know that.

Rachel closes her eyes for half a second.

When she opens them, Lena is watching her too closely.

“You know something,” Lena says. “You’ve known something. Don’t do the face. I can’t do guessing right now.”

Rachel looks at the room instead. At the demons leaning, sitting, waiting like this is a lobby and they’ve been here before.

“I’ve seen this,” Rachel says. Her voice doesn’t sound like hers. “Not you. Just… this shape of wrong.”

Rachel hesitates.

The room doesn’t rush her. It doesn’t care.

“In the basement,” Rachel says finally. “Before everything. There were… agreements.”

Rachel nods.

Rachel swallows. “I didn’t think—”

“You didn’t think I’d end up like this,” Lena says. Not angry. Tired. “You thought you could just hold me.”

Rachel flinches like she’s been slapped.

Holding works until it doesn’t, the voice says quietly.  
You know what comes after.

One of the demons steps closer—not to Lena. To Rachel. “You’re late,” it says mildly. “But not wrong.”

Rachel looks at it, then at Lena.

“What happens,” Rachel asks, and the question feels like it’s tearing its way out of her chest, “when someone keeps borrowing alignment and it doesn’t… stop.”

The demon answers immediately. “It gets worse.”

“That’s it?” Lena says. “That’s the whole prognosis?”

Another demon shrugs. “Unless it’s stabilized.”

Lena turns slowly to Rachel. “Stabilized how.”

Rachel doesn’t answer.

The room feels heavier now. Not hostile. Attentive.

You remember the paper, the voice says, almost gently.  
You remember signing.

Rachel’s stomach twists.

Lena studies her face, and something shifts there—fear, yes, but also recognition.

“You’re not just asking,” Lena says quietly. “You already know what they’ll say.”

Rachel shakes her head. “I just want to know what the options are.”

Lena exhales, shaky. “Options for who.”

Rachel doesn’t say her name.

A demon finally looks at Lena—not really at her, more like checking an item that isn’t theirs. “It would stop hurting,” it says neutrally.

The demon tilts its head. “You already know.”

Lena’s grip loosens, not because she means to let go.

Because she’s tired.

Rachel nods immediately. “I know.”

The room hums. The light seams glow. The demons go back to waiting.

Nothing is offered. Nothing is taken back.

The option sits between them now, fully formed, not spoken out loud again, not denied.

And the room keeps holding them exactly the way it has been.

# Chapter 58 — Refusal

Lena doesn’t let go.

Not because she’s afraid Rachel will move. Because letting go would mean deciding something, and she isn’t ready to do that yet.

The room stays the same. The light seams don’t brighten. The demons don’t shift into anything like attention. They keep their places—leaning, sitting, waiting—like this conversation is happening in a public space and no one thinks it’s rude.

Rachel feels the wrongness in her arms, the quiet burn of holding too much for too long. She adjusts her stance without stepping, careful not to change where the room allows her to exist.

Lena swallows. “You asked about stabilization.”

Rachel nods. “I asked what it means.”

“No,” Lena says. Her voice is steadier than it should be. “You asked what it costs.”

Rachel opens her mouth.

Closes it.

One of the demons glances over, uninterested. “It varies.”

Lena laughs, short and sharp. “Of course it does.”

“It’s not a punishment,” another says, almost kindly. “It’s an agreement.”

Lena turns her head slowly, like too-fast movement would make something slip. “You don’t get to tell me what it’s not.”

The demon shrugs. “Then don’t sign.”

The words land with a dull thud. No drama. No threat.

Rachel’s chest tightens.

She’s choosing discomfort over alignment, the voice says, curious rather than disapproving.  
She always did that.

Rachel squeezes her eyes shut for half a second. When she opens them Lena is watching her again, too closely, like she’s trying to keep Rachel from drifting somewhere she can’t follow.

“What does it actually do,” Rachel asks, and hates that the question comes out calm.

“Bleed,” Lena repeats. “You all love that word.”

“It would stop you from borrowing her,” another adds, nodding at Rachel. “And it would stop the room from arguing with you.”

Lena exhales slowly. “At what price.”

The demon tilts its head. “You already know there’s a price.”

“That’s not an answer.”

“It’s the only one that matters,” the demon replies.

Rachel feels the ache in her arms deepen, the quiet pull of the room asking her to stand just a little differently. She doesn’t.

You could make this end, the voice says, not coaxing.  
You don’t have to keep holding.

Rachel ignores it.

Lena shifts, a small movement that costs her too much. Her fingers tighten again in Rachel’s sleeve. “Is this the part where you tell me I should do it for her,” she asks the room. “Because if that’s the pitch, it’s not landing.”

Lena snorts. “That’s easy for you to say.”

Lena’s gaze softens, just a little. “I don’t mean that the way it sounded.”

Silence stretches again. The room hums. Someone taps a finger against the crate, once, then stops.

Rachel nods. Once. “Okay.”

The room doesn’t change.

The demons don’t move.

Lena leans back into Rachel, not asking for balance, just contact, and Rachel holds her because that part is still allowed.

For now.

And somewhere in the quiet, something that had been waiting simply adjusts its expectations.

# Chapter 59 — Reallocation

Rachel feels it before she can name it, the way you feel a misstep coming half a second too late, not a warning exactly, just the sudden sense that something has finished deciding without her.

The floor corrects her.

That’s what it feels like — a small kindness, a shift so slight her body accepts it automatically, muscles adjusting, weight redistributing, and in that instant she realizes she’s no longer touching Lena.

“Rachel—” Lena says, or tries to, the word thinning out as it leaves her mouth like it had to travel too far to arrive intact.

Two steps.

Nothing dramatic. Nothing visible.

Too far.

That’s the part you kept fixing.

To the space itself.

A demon tilts its head, considering, not unkindly. “We can stabilize it.”

Close enough to hurt.

You waited too long.

It’s barely sound. It’s enough.

The demons turn away, already done.

And Rachel makes the choice she swore she wouldn’t make for someone else, because there is no version of this where waiting makes it better.

# Chapter 60 — Correction

Rachel doesn’t move.

That’s the first thing she notices — not the decision itself, just the fact of it, her feet planted, weight settled wrong on purpose, the dull familiar ache starting to creep into her knee the way it always did when she stood like this too long and didn’t fix it, and for a second there’s relief in that ache because it means she’s still allowed to be uncomfortable if she wants to be.

Lets the wrongness stay.

—and the ache softens.

# Chapter 61 — Registered

The paper doesn’t arrive.

It’s already there, tucked under the crate like it slid there on its own and never bothered to announce itself. Rachel only notices it when the demon’s hand goes for it, fingers hooking the edge, pulling it free with the carefulness of someone handling something that’s been used too many times to argue back.

“Wait,” Rachel says.

The word jumps out of her, sharp, unfinished.

The demon pauses anyway. Not startled. Not defensive. Just… considerate.

“This won’t take long,” it says, quieter than before. “And it hurts less if we don’t rush it.”

Rachel swallows and looks at Lena.

Lena is standing straight. Not rigid. Focused in a way that feels borrowed, like the clarity is being loaned out with interest. Her hands are clasped together hard enough that her knuckles have gone pale, fingers worrying each other in small, repetitive motions.

“You said it would stop the slipping,” Lena says.

“It will,” the demon replies. “Here.”

It sets the paper down on the crate between them. No flourish. No sound worth noting.

Just paper.

Rachel steps forward without meaning to, the impulse pulling her before she catches herself. She stops.

The floor doesn’t shift her this time. It doesn’t need to.

Her arms hang useless at her sides. She can feel the room watching, not leaning in, not judging, just… registering positions.

Lena notices her hesitation without looking up. “If you’re going to say something,” she says quietly, “say it.”

Rachel opens her mouth.

Nothing comes.

The demon exhales, a small sound of apology already baked into it. “She should read it.”

Lena nods and reaches out. Her fingers brush the paper once, recoil, then grip it properly, like she’s bracing for a texture she doesn’t expect.

The page has weight. Rachel can see it in the way Lena’s wrists dip.

The handwriting is ordinary. Slanted. Uneven in places. Further down the page, names appear and disappear under crossed-out lines, ink faded where time has worried at it.

Lena reads silently at first. Her lips move on a few words she doesn’t like. She stops halfway through, goes back, reads a section again slower.

“This says I’ll be… registered,” Lena says finally.

The demon nods. “Anchored.”

“Here,” Lena adds.

“Yes.”

Lena lifts her head. “Not owned.”

The demon shakes its head immediately, almost too fast. “No. Not owned.”

Rachel’s chest tightens. She knows the difference matters, even if she doesn’t know why yet.

“And I won’t—” Lena hesitates, frowns. “I won’t come apart like that again.”

“No,” the demon says. “The space will finish holding you.”

Rachel flinches. She can’t help it.

Lena notices.
