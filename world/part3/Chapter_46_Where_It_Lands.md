# Chapter 46 — Where It Lands

The slide didn’t stop so much as redistribute.

Rachel hit the floor hard on her side and kept moving, the surface beneath her shifting just enough to keep her from finding purchase, friction arriving late and uneven like the room was rationing it. Her shoulder screamed when it met resistance, pain blooming sharp and bright before splintering into smaller copies that chased each other down her arm.

She tasted blood.

The other person went down too, but not the same way—caught by the floor sooner, rolling once and stopping cleanly a few feet away, breath leaving them in a tight grunt that didn’t echo the way Rachel’s did.

The difference landed heavy.

Rachel lay there gasping, chest stuttering, vision swimming as the hum thickened until it felt like it was inside her skull instead of around it.

**It took the hit and kept the mess.**

“That’s—” she tried, and the word dissolved into a cough that tore at her throat. Her ankle flared again as she shifted, pain stacking, heat blooming too fast.

The lights overhead flared and then dimmed, not flickering now but draining, edges of the space smearing as if someone had wiped reality with a damp cloth.

The other person pushed themselves up on one elbow and froze when they saw her.

“Don’t move,” they said, voice tight. “You slid too far.”

Rachel laughed once, thin and hysterical. “I didn’t exactly get a vote.”

The floor dipped under her hip, delayed and pointed, as if agreeing.

**You went where it didn’t want you.**

Her stomach dropped.

“Stop saying things like that,” she snapped, though she knew it wouldn’t listen.

The wrong frame pulsed somewhere nearby, not in her direct line of sight but close enough that she could feel it through the soles of her feet, a low pressure beat that didn’t match her heart.

The other person crawled a step closer and then stopped, palm pressed flat to the floor like they were testing ice.

“Every time you move,” they said carefully, “it reacts harder. Like you’re—” They searched for the word and gave up. “Like you’re dragging something behind you.”

Rachel closed her eyes.

The pressure inside her shifted, not denying it.

**You didn’t leave it behind.**

Her breath came shallow and fast. “Then tell me how to get rid of it.”

The other person shook their head. “You don’t. You wait and hope it decides somewhere else is easier.”

“That’s not helpful.”

“No,” they agreed. “It’s just how this place works.”

Boots sounded again, closer now, the cadence distorted by whatever geometry they were passing through. A voice barked an order that arrived stretched and warped, syllables overlapping.

The hum deepened in response.

Rachel tried to roll onto her back and immediately regretted it as the floor dipped again, sharper this time, like a reprimand. She cried out before she could stop herself, the sound tearing free and coming back to her late, wrong.

The other person flinched and backed away instinctively.

The floor steadied under them.

Rachel felt the difference like a slap.

**It likes you farther away.**

“Oh, fuck you,” she whispered, tears blurring her vision. She wasn’t sure if she meant the voice, the room, or herself.

The lights dimmed further, shadows pooling in corners that hadn’t been there before. The wrong frame pulsed again, brighter now, and she could swear she felt something tug inside her chest in response, a pressure threading through her ribs like it was testing how much she’d give.

The other person’s jaw clenched. “They’re almost through,” they said. “When they breach, this place is going to panic.”

“Meaning?”

“Meaning it’ll grab the easiest thing and pull.”

Rachel dragged a breath in through clenched teeth. Her ankle throbbed in time with the hum, each pulse arriving just after the sound.

**It won’t take the hard thing first.**

Her heart stuttered.

“Say that again,” she demanded.

The pressure didn’t repeat itself. It only tightened, close and intent, like it was watching to see if she’d understood.

The other person looked between Rachel and the frame, calculation flickering across their face. “If I move farther away,” they said slowly, “it might settle.”

“No,” Rachel said immediately, panic spiking. “Don’t.”

They hesitated. “You heard what I said earlier. Staying close makes it worse.”

“I don’t care,” she shot back. “I’m not letting it choose for me.”

The floor shifted under her, a shallow dip that made her gasp.

**It already has.**

Boots hit the edge of the space, loud now, real, the sound snapping into place as if they’d finally found the right angle. A shadow moved across the wall, armor catching the dim light.

The wrong frame answered with a violent pulse that knocked the air from Rachel’s lungs. She cried out, clutching at her chest as the pressure spiked, sharp and invasive, like something had hooked under her ribs and pulled.

The other person shouted her name—too late, the sound stretched and torn as the space lurched again.

Rachel felt herself sliding, not across the floor this time but *into* it, the surface thinning beneath her spine like ice under weight.

“No,” she sobbed, and then there was no floor.
