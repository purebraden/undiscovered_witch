# Chapter 50 — The Way She Points

Rachel takes a step before she means to.

The ground slopes just enough to make it feel like momentum instead of choice, and Lena tightens her grip automatically, adjusting them both without asking. Rachel’s ankle flares hot and bright and she hisses through her teeth, fingers digging into Lena’s sleeve.

“Careful,” Lena says. “You’re going to—”

“I know,” Rachel snaps, though she doesn’t. The world feels a half-beat late again, sound lagging behind motion, rain touching her skin before she sees it fall.

They stop near the edge of the road where the pavement breaks and turns to churned mud. The lights behind them are louder now, closer, but still wrong—voices overlapping without shape, urgency without direction.

Lena looks back once, then forward. “We should get off the road.”

Rachel follows her gaze.

Trees crowd in close on the left, dark and wet and thick with undergrowth. On the right, the road curves away, narrowing, dipping out of sight.

Lena points left. “That way. It’ll hide us.”

The pressure inside Rachel tightens, sharp enough to make her breath catch.

**She doesn’t know that.**

Rachel swallows. “How do you know.”

Lena blinks. “Know what?”

“That it hides us.”

Lena’s mouth opens, closes. “I don’t. I just—Rachel, look at us. We can’t stay here.”

The rain ticks against the leaves like something counting.

Rachel stares into the trees. The dark there feels busy, layered, full of small sounds that don’t line up. The road, for all its exposure, at least stays straight for a few yards before it bends.

**She points where things gather.**

“That’s not—” Rachel stops herself. Her heart is beating too fast, every thud arriving a fraction late. “You don’t know what’s in there.”

Lena exhales hard. “Neither do you.”

“I know it’s not empty.”

The words come out before Rachel can stop them. Lena looks at her, confusion sharpening into concern.

“Rachel,” she says carefully, “you’re not making sense.”

The pressure leans closer, almost pleased.

**She wasn’t there when you fell.**

Rachel’s gaze snaps back to Lena. “You didn’t see it.”

“See what?”

“The way out,” Rachel says. “You didn’t see where I came from.”

Lena shifts her weight, uncomfortable. “I saw you on the ground. Bleeding. That was enough.”

“That’s not what I meant.”

Behind them, a light sweeps briefly across the road and then disappears again, too far away to belong to anyone paying attention.

Lena points left again, firmer this time. “We go now, or we don’t go at all.”

Rachel looks at her hand.

At the way Lena’s finger doesn’t shake.  
At how certain the gesture is.

**She’s already chosen without you.**

Something cold settles in Rachel’s stomach.

“If we go that way,” Rachel says slowly, “and it’s wrong—”

“It won’t be,” Lena says, too fast.

Rachel pulls her arm free.

The movement costs her. Pain spikes up her leg and she nearly stumbles, catching herself on nothing, breath tearing out of her in a sharp sound that makes Lena reach for her again.

Rachel steps back.

“Don’t,” she says.

Lena freezes. “What are you doing.”

Rachel turns to the road. The curve ahead is dark, wet, empty. It doesn’t promise safety. It doesn’t hide anything.

But it doesn’t close in either.

**She won’t follow you there.**

Rachel’s pulse roars in her ears.

“You don’t know that,” she whispers.

The pressure doesn’t argue.

Lena looks from Rachel to the road, then back to the trees. “Rachel. Please.”

Rachel takes one step toward the road.

The world steadies. Just a fraction. Enough to notice.

She takes another.

“Rachel,” Lena says again, sharper now.

Rachel doesn’t answer. She limps forward, each step deliberate, wrong in a way that feels intentional. The road curves and takes her with it, pulling her line of sight away from the lights, from the trees, from the place Lena is standing.

She hears Lena hesitate behind her.

Then footsteps.

Following.

Rachel doesn’t turn around.
