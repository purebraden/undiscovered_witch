# Chapter 50 — The Way She Points

Rachel took a step before she meant to.

The ground sloped just enough to make it feel like momentum instead of choice, and Lena tightened her grip automatically, adjusting them both without asking. Rachel’s ankle flared hot and bright and she hissed through her teeth, fingers digging into Lena’s sleeve.

“Careful,” Lena said. “You’re going to—”

“I know,” Rachel snapped, though she didn’t. The world felt a half-beat late again, sound lagging behind motion, rain touching her skin before she saw it fall.

They stopped near the edge of the road where the pavement broke and turned to churned mud. The lights behind them were louder now, closer, but still wrong—voices overlapping without shape, urgency without direction.

Lena looked back once, then forward. “We should get off the road.”

Rachel followed her gaze.

Trees crowded in close on the left, dark and wet and thick with undergrowth. On the right, the road curved away, narrowing, dipping out of sight.

Lena pointed left. “That way. It’ll hide us.”

The pressure inside Rachel tightened, sharp enough to make her breath catch.

**She doesn’t know that.**

Rachel swallowed. “How do you know.”

Lena blinks. “Know what?”

“That it hides us.”

Lena’s mouth opened, closed. “I don’t. I just—Rachel, look at us. We can’t stay here.”

The rain ticked against the leaves like something counting.

Rachel stared into the trees. The dark there felt busy, layered, full of small sounds that didn’t line up. The road, for all its exposure, at least stayed straight for a few yards before it bent.

**She points where things gather.**

“That’s not—” Rachel stopped herself. Her heart was beating too fast, every thud arriving a fraction late. “You don’t know what’s in there.”

Lena exhaled hard. “Neither do you.”

“I know it’s not empty.”

The words came out before Rachel could stop them. Lena looked at her, confusion sharpening into concern.

“Rachel,” she said carefully, “you’re not making sense.”

The pressure leaned closer, almost pleased.

**She wasn’t there when you fell.**

Rachel’s gaze snapped back to Lena. “You didn’t see it.”

“See what?”

“The way out,” Rachel said. “You didn’t see where I came from.”

Lena shifted her weight, uncomfortable. “I saw you on the ground. Bleeding. That was enough.”

“That’s not what I meant.”

Behind them, a light swept briefly across the road and then disappeared again, too far away to belong to anyone paying attention.

Lena pointed left again, firmer this time. “We go now, or we don’t go at all.”

Rachel looked at her hand.

At the way Lena’s finger doesn’t shake.  
At how certain the gesture is.

**She’s already chosen without you.**

Something cold settles in Rachel’s stomach.

“If we go that way,” Rachel said slowly, “and it’s wrong—”

“It won’t be,” Lena said, too fast.

Rachel pulled her arm free.

The movement cost her. Pain spiked up her leg and she nearly stumbled, catching herself on nothing, breath tearing out of her in a sharp sound that made Lena reach for her again.

Rachel stepped back.

“Don’t,” she said.

Lena froze. “What are you doing.”

Rachel turned to the road. The curve ahead was dark, wet, empty. It didn’t promise safety. It didn’t hide anything.

But it doesn’t close in either.

**She won’t follow you there.**

Rachel’s pulse roared in her ears.

“You don’t know that,” she whispered.

The pressure didn’t argue.

Lena looked from Rachel to the road, then back to the trees. “Rachel. Please.”

Rachel took one step toward the road.

The world steadied. Just a fraction. Enough to notice.

She took another.

“Rachel,” Lena said again, sharper now.

Rachel didn’t answer. She limped forward, each step deliberate, wrong in a way that felt intentional. The road curved and took her with it, pulling her line of sight away from the lights, from the trees, from the place Lena was standing.

She heard Lena hesitate behind her.

Then footsteps.

Following.

Rachel didn’t turn around.
