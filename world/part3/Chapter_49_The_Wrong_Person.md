# Chapter 49 — The Wrong Person

Rachel stayed down longer than she meant to.

Rain slid into her hair and down the side of her face, cold enough to sting, steady enough to count. Her ankle throbbed in a slow, ugly rhythm that felt solid, dependable in a way nothing else did. She pressed her palm into the mud and pushed, stopping halfway when the ground dipped under her weight and then settled again like it had changed its mind.

She breathed anyway.

In.  
Out.  
Not evenly.

“Are you—” Her voice cracked and she swallowed hard. “Are you still there.”

Nothing answered right away.

The pressure sat just behind her ribs, not gone, not forward, like something that had leaned back in its chair.

“Did anything come with me,” she whispered, because that felt like the most important question and also the one she least wanted answered.

Rain filled the space where a response should have been. Somewhere nearby, a light buzzed and cut out and buzzed back on.

Her jaw tightened.

“What did they break,” she said, sharper now, words pushed out without care.

The pressure shifted, late.

The voice slid in: you didn’t wait—

“For what,” she snapped, and the anger surprised her with how easy it came.

She wiped rain out of her eyes and forced herself to look properly. Grass torn to mud. A slope that didn’t line up with how she remembered the ground being. A fence farther off, leaning wrong, reflective tape snapping wetly in the wind.

Footprints.

Not hers.

Too many. Too light. Coming toward her instead of away.

Her stomach dropped.

“That’s wrong,” she murmured, and then louder, “No one else was—”

“Rachel?”

The sound hit her late.

She flinched hard, twisting toward it, pain tearing up her leg as she moved too fast. The world slid and then caught.

Lena stood a few yards away.

Not stepping into view.  
Just there.

Rain darkened her jacket. Her hair was plastered to her forehead. She was breathing hard, chest rising and falling too fast, like she’d been running longer than she should have. Mud already coated her shoes.

For a second, Rachel couldn’t make the image settle.

Lena wasn’t supposed to be here.

Not outside.  
Not now.  
Not past the doors.

“How are you out here,” Rachel blurted, the words coming out wrong, accusation bleeding through fear before she could stop it.

Lena blinked. “What?”

“How did you get out here,” Rachel said again, pushing herself upright despite her ankle screaming. The ground dipped and steadied and she ignored it, eyes locked on Lena. “I thought everything was sealed.”

Lena took a step closer and then stopped like she’d hit an invisible line. Confusion flickered across her face. “Rachel, I heard something break and then—” She waved a hand behind her, the gesture incomplete. “People started running. The outer doors were open. I just— I followed.”

Rachel shook her head, slow and small. “That doesn’t line up,” she said. “You shouldn’t have been able to just walk out.”

Rain drummed between them.

The pressure leaned in, curious.

She isn’t where you left her, it noted.

Rachel’s breath hitched.

Lena reached out and then pulled her hand back, fingers curling into her sleeve. “You’re bleeding,” she said instead. “We need to get you up.”

Rachel looked down like that was new information. Mud on her hands. Blood smeared along her sleeve she didn’t remember earning. Her hands shaking hard enough that she had to clench them to make it stop.

“Lena,” she said quietly, because loud felt dangerous now. “How did you get past the doors.”

Lena opened her mouth.

Metal clanged behind them—loud, wrong—followed by raised voices snapping into urgency, boots hitting pavement out of sync.

Lena’s head turned toward the sound.

Rachel didn’t move.

The pressure slid back, repositioning, like it had lost interest in the question and was already looking ahead.

You don’t get to finish this, it said.

Lena’s hand landed on Rachel’s shoulder.

The moment tore.
