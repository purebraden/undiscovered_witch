# Chapter 47 — After the Floor

There was weight and then there wasn’t, and the difference hurt more than the fall.

Rachel dropped through cold that had shape to it, edges catching at her clothes, her body turning without asking her to keep up. Something brushed her shoulder and slid away when she grabbed at it, fingers closing on nothing that stayed. Sound vanished mid-motion—no rush, no scream—just the pressure of going without air agreeing to be there.

Then sound came back wrong.

A low rushing, like breath dragged through a crack too small for it, followed by a wet thud that knocked the air from her chest when she hit again. This surface didn’t bend. It answered hard and stayed that way, pain blooming across her back and ribs and refusing to break apart into manageable pieces.

She lay still because moving required permission she didn’t have.
No answering voice followed her down.

The air tasted used. Not old. Handled. Like something had breathed it first and decided not to give it back.

Her lungs worked in shallow, scraping pulls. When she twitched her ankle by accident, pain flared bright enough to blur the edges of the dark and she bit down on the inside of her cheek until copper filled her mouth.

The dark wasn’t whole.

Light slid through in thin bands that didn’t agree on direction, crawling across uneven surfaces, catching on walls that didn’t quite meet the floor and a ceiling that sagged in places like it had given up pretending.

Rachel rolled onto her side and immediately regretted it, the motion dragging pain through her spine like a hooked wire. She gasped and pressed her face to the ground, grit biting into her skin, the surface warm in one place and cold an inch away.

You’re still in one piece, it said.

She laughed once, breathless and ugly. “Lucky me.”

The pressure didn’t move away.

This is where it puts what doesn’t fit, it said.

Her stomach clenched.

She whispered, “What,” and the word smeared, stretched thin when it came back to her, not quite hers anymore.

Something shifted.

Not close.  
Not far.

Just present.

Rachel froze so hard her muscles trembled. The sound wasn’t footsteps. It dragged, paused, dragged again, like effort was being considered and reconsidered between movements.

Her heart hammered loud enough she was sure it could hear it.

Not yet, it warned.

She didn’t know what that meant, but her body obeyed, breath caught halfway in, chest burning.

The light slid again and caught on something a few feet away—a section of wall peeled back to show layers underneath, concrete giving way to something softer that pulsed faintly, like it remembered having a rhythm once.

The dragging stopped.

Rachel counted breaths because counting felt safer than thinking. One scraped in. The next caught. The third hurt worse.

It knows you’re there, it said.

“No,” she breathed, the word barely sound.

The dark rearranged itself.

A shape pulled free from the floor where the light didn’t quite reach. Not a person. Not an absence either. It moved by folding itself forward, compressing and releasing in increments that didn’t match distance or time.

Rachel’s eyes slid off it. Looking straight made her dizzy, like the center of it refused to stay where she put it.

Don’t look straight, it warned.

She didn’t understand why, only that obeying made the thing smaller somehow—not clearer, just less everything at once. Too many joints. Not enough edges. A surface that caught light and then let it go like it hadn’t decided whether it wanted it.

It paused.

The pressure leaned closer.

It’s deciding if you’re worth it, it said.

Rachel swallowed a sound.

The thing dragged part of itself forward with a noise like fabric pulled across grit, then stopped again, angled wrong, attention snagging on her ankle.

Her pulse roared.

Pain shows, it warned.

She clamped her hand over her mouth, nails digging in.

The light above flared suddenly, bright enough to sting, and the thing recoiled, compressing inward like it had been surprised. It retreated a fraction, movements jerky and irritated, as if the interruption offended it.

Rachel didn’t wait to understand why.

She dragged herself backward in inches, palms scraping skin away, breath coming in ragged pulls. The ground resisted in places and then released without warning, throwing off her timing, making every movement feel like a mistake she hadn’t finished making yet.

Slow, it urged.

She froze again, heart trying to claw its way out.

The thing shifted, then settled, attention sliding away from her like an animal losing interest when the effort outweighed the reward.

Rachel sagged against the wall and let out a breath that shook.

Her body wouldn’t stop trembling.

The pressure eased—not gone, just less forward, like it had leaned back to watch.

This place forgets what stops moving, it said.

Tears leaked out despite her clenched jaw.

“Good,” she whispered. “I’ll practice.”

The light dimmed.

Somewhere not-here, something heavy moved, a distant groan rippling through layers of space that didn’t line up.

Rachel stayed very still and waited to see what would remember her next.
