# Chapter 42 — Baseline Break

Rachel ran and the hallway refused to decide what it was.

Red emergency strips pulsed overhead, not in a steady rhythm but in a pattern that kept forgetting itself, and every footfall landed before its sound caught up, a dull echo arriving late like it was embarrassed to be behind. The air burned metallic at the back of her throat, heat riding her breath until it tasted like she’d bitten down on a penny and kept it there.

Boots chased her—not one set, not even a line, too many feet hitting together, confident, synchronized, the sound of people moving the way the building expected them to move. The corridor liked that sound and answered it, floor and walls carrying the rhythm forward for them.

Her heel slid on stone that hadn’t been slick a second ago, balance pitching forward and correcting late, knees absorbing a fall that never fully happened. She gasped and the breath came out wrong, half-formed, and **Left** cut across the motion like a sharp notation pressed against the inside of her skull before she knew where left was supposed to be.

She didn’t turn because she didn’t know where left *was* yet, not in a space that kept rearranging its edges. She kept running straight and the corridor narrowed around her in increments too small to measure until her shoulder scraped stone that hadn’t been there, skin tearing, the sting arriving a beat after the contact and then staying too long.

Someone shouted behind her, the sound stretching, thinning, then snapping as if it had hit a seam in the air.

“Stop—”

Her lungs burned as the hallway dipped, and **Now** tangled with her breathing just as her body lurched left anyway, an ugly half-turn where her hips went first and her shoulders followed, foot planting on nothing and finding tile late as she slammed into a side passage that hadn’t existed when she looked. The turn crushed her ribs together and her vision paled at the edges, light washing thin.

A heavy door slammed somewhere above or below her, the sound coming twice—one version close, one far—each insisting it was the real one.

The passage sloped downward, not stairs, not a ramp, just enough of an angle to pull at her calves and make her stomach float as if the floor had decided gravity was optional. She kept running because stopping would mean standing still inside a place that had begun to anticipate her.

Her breath hitched. Her chest seized for half a heartbeat and then released too late to be helpful, something inside her tightening in response, a pressure like a hand closing, and **Don’t** threaded through the sensation without volume or urgency, too late to explain what she was doing wrong.

She was running. She was breathing. She was—

The floor arrived late under her next step and her ankle rolled hard.

Pain flashed white, sharp and immediate, then arrived again slower and deeper, a second copy of the same signal sliding in behind the first like it was trying to catch up. She bit back a cry that still leaked out of her throat as boots hit the turn behind her and the corridor answered with a dull crack, tile giving under weight that trusted it too much.

A shout cut off mid-syllable. Another kept going, layered over it.

She glanced back and regretted it immediately. One of them was down on a knee, one hand splayed against the wall as if he could brace the building into behaving, head jerking in small delayed motions while his other hand reached for his radio and passed over it twice, fingers closing on air that wasn’t there yet.

The others flowed around him without slowing. Procedure did not turn its head.

Rachel faced forward again and swallowed bile as **Faster** pressed into the back of her awareness.

“No,” she rasped, the word breaking apart before the breath finished forming, and **Not speed. Timing** slid in behind it, close enough to feel like it was leaning.

Her footfalls were wrong; she could feel it now, each step arriving half a beat out of phase with the hall, her body moving before her mind finished deciding, her thoughts chasing after her own motion like they were late to themselves.

The passage opened abruptly into a wider space that smelled like dust and oil and old paper, the scent landing all at once and then again, delayed.

Archive.  
No sign, no placard. The smell told her.

Metal shelving rose on either side, crates stacked too neatly, a desk bolted to the floor with a lamp that was off. A strip of light above it flickered bright-dim-bright, counting and forgetting where it was in the sequence.

Rachel ran between shelves and clipped a corner that hadn’t been there yet and then was, pain blooming late and multiplying into a hot throb that didn’t quite match the cut. She stumbled and caught herself on the desk, palm slapping wood that flexed under her weight like it was tired.

She froze with her hand still pressed flat, fingers splayed, the room going still in the way a held breath goes still.

Boots hit the threshold behind her.

“Hands visible! Stop moving!”

The voice was too close and she didn’t move because moving felt like it would tear something loose. Her heart hammered, the lamp trembling, cord shifting an inch across the desk before stopping, the overhead light flickering and steadying and flickering again, each change landing just after it should have.

The pressure in her chest leaned toward the voice, curious, and **Too many eyes** slid through her without sound.

Her mouth opened. No sound came.

They entered in numbers that wouldn’t stay fixed—three, then five, then more—faces smooth behind shields, movements practiced and careful, built for spaces that obeyed. They slowed when they saw her hand on the desk, not because of the desk itself but because she was touching something.

“Back away,” a man said through a speaker grille that flattened the words into something almost prerecorded.

She tried to lift her hand and her fingers stayed for a beat longer than they should have, skin dragging against the wood like the surface had teeth. When she jerked free, the desk creaked and then creaked again, the sound repeating itself a fraction late as the lamp slid half an inch.

One of them flinched and caught himself mid-step, boot hovering before settling too carefully, like the floor might punish commitment.

“Don’t approach,” another voice snapped. “Maintain baseline distance.”

Baseline.

Rachel laughed once, short and broken, the sound coming back to her late, echoing as if someone else had done it, and **They want you still** settled into the space the laugh left behind.

She stared at the nearest shield, at her own warped reflection in the curved plastic. She looked wrong. She looked late.

“What happens,” she asked, breath tearing in and out, “if I can’t?”

No one answered. They didn’t negotiate. They adjusted.

A flat panel unfolded from a case, its grid of dim lights pulsing in patterns too clean for this room, two of them holding it like a shield and angling it toward her without stepping closer. The grid brightened and the pressure in her chest reacted instantly, tightening like a muscle bracing, her breath catching as the air thickened and the space stiffened as if the room itself had been ordered to hold still.

Her knees trembled. She lowered herself onto the bolted stool behind the desk, her body arriving in the sit late, hips dropping after the decision, spine catching up.

The grid eased. Shoulders loosened a fraction.

“Good,” someone said. “Maintain that.”

Her hands shook on her thighs, the tremor shrinking but not stopping, twitching in tiny delayed pulses, the pressure inside her staying tight and unsatisfied.

A radio crackled. “Subject located. Initiate containment corridor. Bring frame.”

Frame.

Her stomach dropped and “Please” slipped out before she could stop it, the word tasting wrong as soon as it left her mouth.

A shield tilted—assessment, not sympathy.

“Don’t make it worse,” someone said softly.

**Ask the room** slid through her before she could reject it, and she stared at the desk, the lamp, the cord, the small rectangle of shadow beneath it that didn’t line up with the light.

“No,” she whispered.

**You already did.**

Her fingers twitched. The grid tightened. A boot slid a fraction on tile that had gone slick.

The room listened.

Rachel closed her eyes and didn’t reach for anything, didn’t picture moving the space, just shifted her weight inside her own body—forward, back—enough to be a choice, and the pressure snapped toward it as the lamp slid again, not far, just enough.

The grid stuttered, one light blinking out and coming back late as someone swore behind a shield.

“Stop—”

She opened her eyes. Nothing had resolved. The team was still there, still aiming, still trying to hold a space that refused to stay held, the pressure leaning closer, tight and interested, **Again** threading through the moment without volume.

She didn’t move.

The lamp kept sliding anyway, cord dragging, shadow lagging behind the base as if the world couldn’t decide which part mattered. A shield bumped a shelf and paper rasped, folders shifting, the sound arriving late and multiplying into a dry static that filled the room.

The grid flared, trying to correct, the air thickening until her lungs stuttered and heat breathed out of the wall, dust waking.

“Abort the grid!” someone shouted. “It’s feeding the desync!”

The panel went dark. The stiffness stayed.

Heavy boots gathered outside. The transfer team.

“Clear the archive,” a confident voice called from the hall. “We’re moving her.”

Too many bodies decided at once, relief and fear rippling through the room, and the pressure inside her tilted toward the doorway, drawn by cadence and authority and the promise of a clamp, **If they frame you again, it spills** threading through the thought before she could stop it.

Rachel stood in pieces, legs straightening before her torso finished, balance arriving late and overshooting, hands lifting with palms open—not surrender, not threat, just visible.

“Don’t,” someone said, but the word didn’t carry.

She backed between shelves that felt closer than they had been a second ago, paper smelling sharp, ink and dust and old glue, hit a shelf that wasn’t there yet, swore as a folder slid off and hit the floor, the papery slap arriving half a beat after it landed.

The doorway filled with heavier armor, a frame on wheels unfolding behind them, metal whispering as rings aligned with clean, merciless geometry.

The room was too full.

**Choose.**

“Where?” she whispered.

Nothing answered.

The frame finished unfolding.

Her vision slid sideways. Her weight shifted. The pressure snapped toward the movement like a held breath released as the archive light flickered and paper lifted in a slow, dry flutter, as if someone had exhaled through the shelves.

Rachel ran—not toward the door but into the narrow gap between two shelving units that should have been a dead end.

The space opened.

Not a door. Not a seam.

A mistake.

She fell through it shoulder-first, scraping skin and bone, breath knocked out of her as the world peeled away and the next one took too long to arrive. A shout followed her late and then cut off clean.

Rachel hit cold tile and rolled, lungs dragging for air.

The pressure inside her held tight.

Not guiding.  
Not apologizing.  
Waiting.
