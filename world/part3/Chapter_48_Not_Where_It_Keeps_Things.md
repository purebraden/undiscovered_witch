# Chapter 48 — Not Where It Keeps Things

The light changed first.

Not brighter. Not darker.  
Different.

Rachel felt it through her eyelids before she opened them, a pressure shift that didn’t belong to the place she was in, like someone had opened a door somewhere far above and the air had decided to notice. The thing near the wall stilled, its shape compressing in on itself, attention snapping away from her so fast it felt like being dropped.

The pressure inside her recoiled.

**That’s not meant to be open.**

Before she could ask what *that* meant, the floor lurched.

Not a slide.  
A failure.

The surface beneath her thinned all at once, no warning, no negotiation, and Rachel screamed as gravity returned with teeth, hauling her down and then up in the same breath, the sensation so wrong it turned her stomach inside out.

The dark tore.

Light punched through in a jagged column, white and hard and real, and the space around her folded inward like it was trying to close a wound. Something grabbed at her ankle—pressure, not hands—and then let go with a sharp, irritated snap.

**You’re in the way now.**

She hit ground that did not move.

Grass. Gravel. Something wet and cold.

Rachel rolled hard, breath exploding out of her lungs, shoulder slamming into earth that smelled alive and filthy and right. She lay there choking, palms sinking into mud, the night air cutting into her skin like it had edges.

Rain hit her face.

Not a memory.  
Not a projection.

Actual rain, cold and stinging, soaking through her clothes in seconds.

Above her, something boomed.

Not thunder.

Metal screaming under stress. A distant crack like concrete failing wrong. Shouts followed—human voices, panicked and overlapping, words she couldn’t separate but recognized as real because they didn’t smear when they hit her ears.

The pressure inside her chest pulled tight, disoriented.

**Outside isn’t quieter.**

“I don’t care,” she gasped, dragging air into her lungs like she’d been underwater too long. Her ankle screamed when she tried to move, pain sharp and grounding, proof she was back in a body that obeyed physics even when it hated her.

She rolled onto her side and vomited into the grass, bile burning her throat, hands shaking so badly she couldn’t keep herself upright afterward. Rain plastered her hair to her face. Mud coated her palms and knees.

The world stayed put.

Lights flared somewhere nearby—harsh, industrial, mounted too high—and silhouettes moved through them, figures running, slipping, shouting orders that overlapped and collided.

Someone yelled her name.

Or maybe just a name.

The pressure inside her shifted, retreating, like it had been shoved out of the room and was deciding whether to try the door again.

**They broke something important.**

Rachel laughed once, wet and hysterical, and pressed her forehead into the mud.

“Good,” she whispered.

Behind her, the ground shuddered.

Not collapsing.  
Sealing.

A deep, final sound rolled through the earth, like a vault door closing somewhere she couldn’t see. The lights steadied. The shouting changed pitch, urgency reorganizing itself.

Hands grabbed her shoulders.

Rachel flinched violently, gasping, swinging blind, nails scraping against fabric and skin until someone cursed and let go.

“Easy—easy—don’t—”

She curled in on herself instinctively, rain slicking her lashes, heart hammering so hard she thought it might tear free.

The pressure inside her didn’t answer.

Not gone.

Just… pushed back.

Rachel lay there in the mud and rain and noise and let the outside world prove itself by continuing to exist.

She was free of the space.

Whatever else had come with her was going to have to keep up.
