# Chapter 63 — Refusal

Lena didn’t let go.

Not because she was afraid Rachel would move. Because letting go would mean deciding something, and she wasn’t ready to do that yet.

The room stayed the same. The light seams didn’t brighten. The demons didn’t shift into anything like attention. They kept their places—leaning, sitting, waiting—like this conversation was happening in a public space and no one thought it was rude.

Rachel felt the wrongness in her arms, the quiet burn of holding too much for too long. She adjusted her stance without stepping, careful not to change where the room allowed her to exist.

Lena swallowed. “You asked about stabilization.”

Rachel nodded. “I asked what it means.”

“No,” Lena said. Her voice was steadier than it should have been. “You asked what it costs.”

Rachel opened her mouth.

Closes it.

One of the demons glanced over, uninterested. “It varies.”

Lena laughed, short and sharp. “Of course it does.”

“It’s not a punishment,” another said, almost kindly. “It’s an agreement.”

Lena turned her head slowly, like too-fast movement would make something slip. “You don’t get to tell me what it’s not.”

The demon shrugged. “Then don’t sign.”

The words land with a dull thud. No drama. No threat.

Rachel’s chest tightened.

**She’s choosing discomfort over alignment**, the voice said, curious rather than disapproving.  
**She always did that.**

Rachel squeezed her eyes shut for half a second. When she opened them Lena was watching her again, too closely, like she was trying to keep Rachel from drifting somewhere she couldn’t follow.

“What does it actually do,” Rachel asked, and hated that the question came out calm.

One of the demons answered without hesitation. “It would stop the bleed.”

“Bleed,” Lena repeated. “You all love that word.”

“It would stop you from borrowing her,” another added, nodding at Rachel. “And it would stop the room from arguing with you.”

Lena exhaled slowly. “At what price.”

The demon tilted its head. “You already know there’s a price.”

“That’s not an answer.”

“It’s the only one that matters,” the demon replied.

Rachel felt the ache in her arms deepen, the quiet pull of the room asking her to stand just a little differently. She didn’t.

**You could make this end**, the voice said, not coaxing.  
**You don’t have to keep holding.**

Rachel ignored it.

Lena shifted, a small movement that cost her too much. Her fingers tightened again in Rachel’s sleeve. “Is this the part where you tell me I should do it for her,” she asked the room. “Because if that’s the pitch, it’s not landing.”

“No pitch,” said the demon on the crate. “We don’t need one.”

Rachel looked at Lena. “You don’t have to decide anything now.”

Lena snorted. “That’s easy for you to say.”

Rachel flinched.

Lena’s gaze softened, just a little. “I don’t mean that the way it sounded.”

She swallowed. “I mean… you’ve already decided something. I can feel it.”

Rachel shook her head. “No. I’m just— asking.”

“That’s the same thing,” Lena said. “Here.”

Silence stretched again. The room hummed. Someone tapped a finger against the crate, once, then stopped.

Lena straightened as much as she could. It cost her. She did it anyway.

“I’m not signing anything,” she said. Not loud. Not dramatic. Just… placed. “Not here. Not like this. Not because the room wanted me to stop hurting.”

The demons didn’t argue.

One of them nodded, like this outcome was on a list somewhere. “Then you’ll continue to degrade.”

Rachel’s stomach dropped. “Stop.”

The demon looked at her, mildly surprised. “You asked.”

Lena let out a breath that shook. “I don’t care,” she said, and the words wobbled but didn’t break. “I don’t care if there’s paper. I don’t care if it’s easier. I’m not letting something else decide what I get to be just because it’s inconvenient.”

Rachel felt something twist in her chest. Pride. Fear. Something like loss.

**She’s choosing pain**, the voice said, thoughtful.  
**That’s inefficient.**

Rachel snapped, “Don’t.”

The voice goes quiet. Not gone. Just… listening.

Lena looked at Rachel, eyes bright and unfocused at the edges. “You don’t get to carry this for me,” she said softly. “Not this part.”

Rachel nodded. Once. “Okay.”

The room didn’t change.

The demons didn’t move.

The option remained exactly where it was—present, patient, unoffended by refusal.

Lena leaned back into Rachel, not asking for balance, just contact, and Rachel held her because that part was still allowed.

For now.

And somewhere in the quiet, something that had been waiting simply adjusted its expectations.
