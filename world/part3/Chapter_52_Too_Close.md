# Chapter 52 — Too Close

They heard him before they saw him.

A footstep that didn’t belong to either of them, heavier, more deliberate, the sound of someone who expected to be heard and expected that to be enough. Rachel’s shoulders locked instantly, her body reacting before her thoughts caught up, the ringing still faint but present behind her eyes like pressure waiting for an excuse.

“Hey,” a voice called out, close. Too close already. “You can’t—”

Lena’s hand tightened at Rachel’s back. Not steering. Warning.

Rachel turned.

The man was standing in the open stretch of road behind them, rain slicking his jacket dark, one hand half-raised in a gesture that was meant to calm, meant to stop them without chasing. He looked confused more than angry, breath fogging, eyes flicking from Rachel’s ankle to Lena’s face.

“You need to stop,” he said. “This area’s restricted.”

The word lands wrong.

Rachel felt it like a hook behind her ribs, a tug backward toward explanation, toward slowing down, toward letting this moment stretch long enough to trap them.

Her chest tightens.

“No,” she said, and it wasn’t loud.

The man stepped forward anyway.

Just one step.  
Just enough.

Something in Rachel snapped into alignment—not thought, not fear, but refusal so sharp it felt clean.

Not you.  
Not now.  
Not this close.

She didn’t raise her hands. She didn’t brace. She didn’t do anything that looked like action.

The world simply chose distance.

The man’s foot came down where the ground should have been and didn’t arrive with the rest of him. His body lurched forward without momentum, like the step forgot what it was supposed to do, and he stumbled hard, arms flailing as time caught up all at once.

He hit the road on one knee with a sharp, surprised sound, palms slapping wet pavement.

Rachel gasped.

The ringing slams into her skull a half-second later, high and sudden, a thin screaming tone that slices straight through her thoughts and makes the world tilt without moving. She squeezes her eyes shut, teeth clenched, the sound crowding everything else out.

**There it is.**

The voice was right there now.

Not distant.  
Not restrained.

Bright with it.

**You felt that.  
You didn’t wait.**

Rachel staggered, one hand flying up to her head, the ringing spiking as she moved. “Shut up,” she breathed, though she wasn’t sure if the words left her mouth or just bounced around inside her skull.

Lena swore under her breath and grabbed Rachel’s arm. “Rachel—what did you—”

“I didn’t,” Rachel said, too fast. “I didn’t do anything.”

Behind them, the man pushed himself up, blinking hard like he was trying to get his balance back, confusion etched deep across his face. “What the hell—”

The ringing surges again, pulsing in time with Rachel’s heartbeat.

**Again, if you want.**

Her stomach dropped.

Lena pulled her, sharp and urgent now. “We’re leaving. Now.”

Rachel didn’t argue.

They turned and moved, Lena half-dragging her this time, feet slipping on wet ground, breath tearing out of Rachel’s chest in ragged pulls. The road bent and took them, the man’s voice rising behind them, words blurring into noise she couldn’t separate from the ringing.

“What happened?” Lena demanded, low and tight. “He just—he wasn’t even touching you.”

“I know,” Rachel said, and the truth of it made her throat close. “I know.”

The ringing didn’t fade. It modulated, sharpened when she hesitated, softened when she committed to forward motion, like something tuning itself to her.

**You made space.  
That’s all this ever was.**

Rachel stumbled and Lena caught her, reality steadying around the contact even as her head screamed. She hated how that worked now. Hated how quickly the world behaved when she let someone else anchor her.

They cut off the road and into the darker stretch, branches clawing at their clothes, the night loud again, uneven, honest. Behind them, someone shouted her name—maybe the man, maybe someone else—but the sound came late, stretched thin.

The ringing peaks and then settles into a steady, invasive whine.

Rachel pressed her fingers hard against her temples and kept moving.

The voice stayed with her, close and pleased and no longer pretending to be patient.

**You’re allowed to do that now.**

She didn’t answer.

She just ran.
