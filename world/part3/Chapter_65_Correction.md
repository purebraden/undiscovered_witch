# Chapter 65 — Correction

Rachel didn’t move.

That was the first thing she noticed — not the decision itself, just the fact of it, her feet planted, weight settled wrong on purpose, the dull familiar ache starting to creep into her knee the way it always did when she stood like this too long and didn’t fix it, and for a second there was relief in that ache because it meant she was still allowed to be uncomfortable if she wanted to be.

She let it sit.  
Let the wrongness stay.

Her back tightened. Her calf pulled. The ache grew into something sharp enough that she had to breathe through it, jaw set, shoulders locked, and she thought, there, that’s it, that’s me still here—

—and the ache softened.

Not suddenly. Not dramatically. Just enough that she noticed halfway through a breath that it wasn’t biting the way it was, that her weight had shifted without asking her, muscles easing, posture settling, like someone straightened a picture she’d deliberately hung crooked.

Rachel exhaled hard, almost a laugh. “No.”

She twisted her hips a little, pushed the stance wider, forced the load back into the joint, tried to drag the pain back into place, and it worked for a moment, just long enough for her to feel the burn and cling to it—

—then it slid away again, quiet and thorough, leaving her standing comfortably in a shape she didn’t choose.

Her hands curled. Uncurled.

She tried stepping sideways, not toward Lena, not away, just elsewhere, trying to break the invisible lines she could feel but couldn’t see, and the step landed fine, the floor solid under her foot, until her balance followed and the floor followed that, smoothing her forward a fraction, correcting the angle, returning her to the same offset place she kept ending up.

She didn’t stop breathing, but she forgot how to breathe normally.

“Stop,” she said, and it came out thin, like the room swallowed part of it.

The demons didn’t react. Someone shifted against the wall. Someone else scratched at the crate with one clawed finger, absentminded. This wasn’t a moment to them. It was just the way things settled.

Rachel planted herself again, harder this time, arms loose at her sides, refusing to brace, refusing to compensate, refusing to help the correction along, and the ache crawled back in slow and mean, climbing her leg, tugging at her lower back, burning at the base of her neck, and she held it, teeth clenched, eyes watering, because this was what resistance felt like and she remembered it—

—and her breathing evened out.

Her shoulders dropped.

The pain drained away like it never belonged to her.

She swayed, just a little, not from weakness but from the sudden absence of effort, and that’s when the nausea hit, sharp and hot, because she didn’t choose to relax, didn’t decide to let it go.

Her body did.

“You’re fighting the part that already agreed,” the voice said, not loud, not pleased exactly, just present, like it had been waiting for her to notice. “That’s why it feels slippery.”

Rachel shook her head once, too fast. “I didn’t agree to this.”

She tried stepping back, slow and deliberate, watching her foot the whole way down, concentrating on every inch of contact, and the step completed, her heel hit, her weight followed, and then the floor nudged her again, subtle as breath, turning the landing into the same alignment she kept returning to.

Not force.

Inevitability.

Her chest tightened until it hurt.

She looked at Lena then, really looked, because something in Lena had changed again while Rachel wasn’t watching. The sharp edge of confusion was gone. Lena’s eyes were focusing cleanly now, movements landing where she meant them to, breath steady, body no longer slipping sideways through itself.

Rachel hadn’t touched her.

Rachel hadn’t done anything.

She took a half-step forward, instinctive, wanting to close the gap, wanting to be wrong in a way that mattered, and the floor slid her foot just past where she aimed, placing her neatly, efficiently, between Lena and the open space of the room.

Helping.

“I said no,” Rachel whispered.

“You said no to pain,” the voice answered, almost gently. “Not to being corrected.”

Lena shifted, steadier now, and looked at her. “Rachel?” she asked. “Why do I feel… better?”

Rachel opened her mouth.

Nothing comes out.

She was standing comfortably.  
Too comfortably.

Her feet hadn’t moved.  
The room hummed.

Whatever shape she was in now, it wasn’t one she remembered choosing.
