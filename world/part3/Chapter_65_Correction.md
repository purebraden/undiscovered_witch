# Chapter 65 — Correction

Rachel doesn’t move.

That’s the first thing she notices — not the decision itself, just the fact of it, her feet planted, weight settled wrong on purpose, the dull familiar ache starting to creep into her knee the way it always did when she stood like this too long and didn’t fix it, and for a second there’s relief in that ache because it means she’s still allowed to be uncomfortable if she wants to be.

She lets it sit.  
Lets the wrongness stay.

Her back tightens. Her calf pulls. The ache grows into something sharp enough that she has to breathe through it, jaw set, shoulders locked, and she thinks, there, that’s it, that’s me still here—

—and the ache softens.

Not suddenly. Not dramatically. Just enough that she notices halfway through a breath that it isn’t biting the way it was, that her weight has shifted without asking her, muscles easing, posture settling, like someone straightened a picture she’d deliberately hung crooked.

Rachel exhales hard, almost a laugh. “No.”

She twists her hips a little, pushes the stance wider, forces the load back into the joint, tries to drag the pain back into place, and it works for a moment, just long enough for her to feel the burn and cling to it—

—then it slides away again, quiet and thorough, leaving her standing comfortably in a shape she didn’t choose.

Her hands curl. Uncurl.

She tries stepping sideways, not toward Lena, not away, just elsewhere, trying to break the invisible lines she can feel but can’t see, and the step lands fine, the floor solid under her foot, until her balance follows and the floor follows that, smoothing her forward a fraction, correcting the angle, returning her to the same offset place she keeps ending up.

She doesn’t stop breathing, but she forgets how to breathe normally.

“Stop,” she says, and it comes out thin, like the room swallowed part of it.

The demons don’t react. Someone shifts against the wall. Someone else scratches at the crate with one clawed finger, absentminded. This isn’t a moment to them. It’s just the way things settle.

Rachel plants herself again, harder this time, arms loose at her sides, refusing to brace, refusing to compensate, refusing to help the correction along, and the ache crawls back in slow and mean, climbing her leg, tugging at her lower back, burning at the base of her neck, and she holds it, teeth clenched, eyes watering, because this is what resistance feels like and she remembers it—

—and her breathing evens out.

Her shoulders drop.

The pain drains away like it never belonged to her.

She sways, just a little, not from weakness but from the sudden absence of effort, and that’s when the nausea hits, sharp and hot, because she didn’t choose to relax, didn’t decide to let it go.

Her body did.

“You’re fighting the part that already agreed,” the voice says, not loud, not pleased exactly, just present, like it’s been waiting for her to notice. “That’s why it feels slippery.”

Rachel shakes her head once, too fast. “I didn’t agree to this.”

She tries stepping back, slow and deliberate, watching her foot the whole way down, concentrating on every inch of contact, and the step completes, her heel hits, her weight follows, and then the floor nudges her again, subtle as breath, turning the landing into the same alignment she keeps returning to.

Not force.

Inevitability.

Her chest tightens until it hurts.

She looks at Lena then, really looks, because something in Lena has changed again while Rachel wasn’t watching. The sharp edge of confusion is gone. Lena’s eyes are focusing cleanly now, movements landing where she means them to, breath steady, body no longer slipping sideways through itself.

Rachel hasn’t touched her.

Rachel hasn’t done anything.

She takes a half-step forward, instinctive, wanting to close the gap, wanting to be wrong in a way that matters, and the floor slides her foot just past where she aimed, placing her neatly, efficiently, between Lena and the open space of the room.

Helping.

“I said no,” Rachel whispers.

“You said no to pain,” the voice answers, almost gently. “Not to being corrected.”

Lena shifts, steadier now, and looks at her. “Rachel?” she asks. “Why do I feel… better?”

Rachel opens her mouth.

Nothing comes out.

She’s standing comfortably.  
Too comfortably.

Her feet haven’t moved.  
The room hums.

Whatever shape she’s in now, it isn’t one she remembers choosing.
