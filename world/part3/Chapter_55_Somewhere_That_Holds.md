# Chapter 55 — Somewhere That Holds

They kept moving without saying why, the kind of motion that didn’t feel chosen so much as required, like breathing through pain until it dulled into something you could carry. The trees thinned and thickened again, ground changing under Rachel’s feet—mud to gravel to something harder she didn’t want to look at too closely—and the ringing stayed tucked behind her eyes, not gone, just quieter, like it had learned where to sit without being noticed.

“We can’t just stay out here,” Lena said eventually, breath rough, words practical in the way people got when they were too tired to be scared properly.

Rachel nodded without really knowing what she was agreeing to. The idea of inside pressed at her ribs anyway, images stacking up without permission—walls too close, doors that decided when they opened, rooms that expected her to stay once she entered them.

“Somewhere dry,” Lena adds. “Somewhere we can lock.”

Rachel swallowed, the ringing tightening just enough to be felt. “I don’t want to lock anything.”

“That’s what doors are for,” Lena said, tired, reasonable.

They crested a small rise and the trees fell back, revealing a cluster of buildings below, half-lit and uneven, lights burning in a few windows while others sat dark and hollow like missing teeth. It wasn’t big enough to be a town or small enough to be nothing, just a place people stopped agreeing about all at once and never quite finished leaving.

The ringing shifts.

Not sharp.  
Not loud.

Interested.

Rachel slowed before she meant to, her body already resisting, and the ground steadied under her foot anyway, firming just enough to make the hesitation obvious. She hated that part most.

Lena noticed, of course. “You see something?”

“No.” Rachel forced herself forward, steps careful but committed, pain snapping bright through her ankle and fading again. “I just don’t like it.”

“That’s everywhere,” Lena said, but the words didn’t land with much confidence.

As they descended, the air changed—not temperature, but weight. Sound dulled slightly, rain quieter against surfaces that hadn’t decided what they were made of anymore. Rachel’s skin prickled, nerves stretched thin, tuned outward and inward at the same time.

They reached the edge of the buildings and she understood too late what was wrong.

Nothing reacts.

No windows brighten. No movement answers theirs. Even the night feels distracted here, like it has better things to do than keep track of who comes and goes.

The ringing eases.

Not relief.  
Approval.

Lena exhaled. “Okay. This is good. This—this works.”

Rachel’s stomach twisted hard. “It doesn’t.”

Lena frowned. “We’re out of sight. It’s dry. There are doors.”

Rachel looked at the nearest one. It hung crooked in its frame, paint blistered, lock either rusted shut or broken clean through. It looked like something that hadn’t kept anyone out in a long time.

Or in.

“We won’t be found here,” Lena said, trying again.

The words scrape.

The ringing hums, closer now, threading into Rachel’s thoughts without effort.

**This place doesn’t hold people.**

Rachel pressed her fingers into her temples. “Don’t.”

**That’s why it works.**

She dragged her hands down her face, palms smearing rain and dirt, pressure pulsing behind her eyes in time with her heartbeat. “I just want to stop,” she muttered. “Five minutes.”

The ground firmed under her foot when she stepped forward, the smallest assist, barely there, and she hated that she noticed it.

Lena noticed anyway. “Rachel.”

“I know,” Rachel said, because she did.

They chose a building that looked like it never wanted company—no lights, no intact glass. The door gave when Lena pushed it, wood creaking in complaint more than resistance. Inside smelled like damp and old dust and something metallic that made Rachel’s tongue taste wrong, and the ringing dropped another notch, settling into a low, steady thread that made the space feel quieter than it should.

Too quiet.

Lena closed the door out of habit, then hesitated before wedging a broken chair against it. “Just in case.”

Rachel didn’t argue, but her shoulders tightened as the room finished deciding how it wanted to exist around them.

“This isn’t safe,” she said, low.

Lena sat hard on the edge of a collapsed table, exhaustion finally winning. “It’s safe enough.”

The ringing flares, pleased.

**No one looks for what doesn’t register.**

Rachel closed her eyes and felt it then—the way the place didn’t line up with her properly, like she was standing half a step out of phase with the floor, the walls, the air. It didn’t push back. It didn’t pull her in.

It just doesn’t care.

And beneath the ache and the fatigue and the noise, the understanding lands heavy and unwelcome:

If she stays somewhere like this too long, nothing will notice when she leaves.

Or when she doesn’t.

She opened her eyes. “We can’t sleep here.”

Lena looked up, startled. “Rachel—”

“Not long,” Rachel said, softening it because Lena needed something workable. “Just… not long.”

The ringing settles again, patient, content.

**You’re learning.**

Rachel stayed on her feet, listening to a place that didn’t listen back, and knew they were still moving even now—just more slowly, in the wrong direction.
