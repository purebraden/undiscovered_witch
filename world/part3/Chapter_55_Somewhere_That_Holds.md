# Chapter 55 — Somewhere That Holds

They keep moving without saying why, the kind of motion that doesn’t feel chosen so much as required, like breathing through pain until it dulls into something you can carry. The trees thin and thicken again, ground changing under Rachel’s feet—mud to gravel to something harder she doesn’t want to look at too closely—and the ringing stays tucked behind her eyes, not gone, just quieter, like it’s learned where to sit without being noticed.

“We can’t just stay out here,” Lena says eventually, breath rough, words practical in the way people get when they’re too tired to be scared properly.

Rachel nods without really knowing what she’s agreeing to. The idea of inside presses at her ribs anyway, images stacking up without permission—walls too close, doors that decide when they open, rooms that expect her to stay once she enters them.

“Somewhere dry,” Lena adds. “Somewhere we can lock.”

Rachel swallows, the ringing tightening just enough to be felt. “I don’t want to lock anything.”

“That’s what doors are for,” Lena says, tired, reasonable.

They crest a small rise and the trees fall back, revealing a cluster of buildings below, half-lit and uneven, lights burning in a few windows while others sit dark and hollow like missing teeth. It isn’t big enough to be a town or small enough to be nothing, just a place people stopped agreeing about all at once and never quite finished leaving.

The ringing shifts.

Not sharp.  
Not loud.

Interested.

Rachel slows before she means to, her body already resisting, and the ground steadies under her foot anyway, firming just enough to make the hesitation obvious. She hates that part most.

Lena notices, of course. “You see something?”

“No.” Rachel forces herself forward, steps careful but committed, pain snapping bright through her ankle and fading again. “I just don’t like it.”

“That’s everywhere,” Lena says, but the words don’t land with much confidence.

As they descend, the air changes—not temperature, but weight. Sound dulls slightly, rain quieter against surfaces that haven’t decided what they’re made of anymore. Rachel’s skin prickles, nerves stretched thin, tuned outward and inward at the same time.

They reach the edge of the buildings and she understands too late what’s wrong.

Nothing reacts.

No windows brighten. No movement answers theirs. Even the night feels distracted here, like it has better things to do than keep track of who comes and goes.

The ringing eases.

Not relief.  
Approval.

Lena exhales. “Okay. This is good. This—this works.”

Rachel’s stomach twists hard. “It doesn’t.”

Lena frowns. “We’re out of sight. It’s dry. There are doors.”

Rachel looks at the nearest one. It hangs crooked in its frame, paint blistered, lock either rusted shut or broken clean through. It looks like something that hasn’t kept anyone out in a long time.

Or in.

“We won’t be found here,” Lena says, trying again.

The words scrape.

The ringing hums, closer now, threading into Rachel’s thoughts without effort.

**This place doesn’t hold people.**

Rachel presses her fingers into her temples. “Don’t.”

**That’s why it works.**

She drags her hands down her face, palms smearing rain and dirt, pressure pulsing behind her eyes in time with her heartbeat. “I just want to stop,” she mutters. “Five minutes.”

The ground firms under her foot when she steps forward, the smallest assist, barely there, and she hates that she notices it.

Lena notices anyway. “Rachel.”

“I know,” Rachel says, because she does.

They choose a building that looks like it never wanted company—no lights, no intact glass. The door gives when Lena pushes it, wood creaking in complaint more than resistance. Inside smells like damp and old dust and something metallic that makes Rachel’s tongue taste wrong, and the ringing drops another notch, settling into a low, steady thread that makes the space feel quieter than it should.

Too quiet.

Lena closes the door out of habit, then hesitates before wedging a broken chair against it. “Just in case.”

Rachel doesn’t argue, but her shoulders tighten as the room finishes deciding how it wants to exist around them.

“This isn’t safe,” she says, low.

Lena sits hard on the edge of a collapsed table, exhaustion finally winning. “It’s safe enough.”

The ringing flares, pleased.

**No one looks for what doesn’t register.**

Rachel closes her eyes and feels it then—the way the place doesn’t line up with her properly, like she’s standing half a step out of phase with the floor, the walls, the air. It doesn’t push back. It doesn’t pull her in.

It just doesn’t care.

And beneath the ache and the fatigue and the noise, the understanding lands heavy and unwelcome:

If she stays somewhere like this too long, nothing will notice when she leaves.

Or when she doesn’t.

She opens her eyes. “We can’t sleep here.”

Lena looks up, startled. “Rachel—”

“Not long,” Rachel says, softening it because Lena needs something workable. “Just… not long.”

The ringing settles again, patient, content.

**You’re learning.**

Rachel stays on her feet, listening to a place that doesn’t listen back, and knows they’re still moving even now—just more slowly, in the wrong direction.
