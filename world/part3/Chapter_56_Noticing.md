# Chapter 55 — Noticing

Rachel stays standing.

Lena sinks to the floor with a tired sound, back against the wall, head dropping forward like gravity finally remembered her. The building doesn’t answer that. Air presses in, damp and stale, holding its shape without reacting.

“Just a minute,” Lena says, eyes closed.

Rachel nods because it’s easier than speaking. She keeps her weight angled off her ankle, one foot braced, hands loose at her sides. The ringing sits behind her eyes, thinner than before, stretched tight like it’s being pulled somewhere else.

The room smells layered—wet wood, metal, dust that’s been damp long enough to lose its edges. The walls are close enough to touch without reaching, corners softened where they should meet cleanly.

Rachel shifts her weight.

The floor gives.

Not with sound. Just a brief, unmistakable softness under her foot, like pressure meeting pressure. She freezes, breath catching mid-draw.

The ringing tightens—

—and then slides away.

Not gone. Displaced.

There, it said.

The word lands cleanly.

No spike.  
No pain.  
No distortion.

Rachel’s stomach drops.

She doesn’t move. After a moment the floor firms again, ordinary, pretending nothing happened. She keeps her eyes fixed on the far wall, pulse loud enough to hear.

Lena rubs at her face, still not looking up. “You okay?”

“Yeah,” Rachel says. The word comes out steady, and that scares her more than if it hadn’t. “Just tired.”

She takes a careful step away from the wall.

The space answers faster this time. Air presses faintly around her shoulders, close enough to notice, and she stops instinctively.

The pressure eases.

You don’t need the noise anymore, the voice says, almost conversational.

Rachel swallows. “Stop.”

The voice doesn’t raise itself. It doesn’t push.

I am stopped, it said.

Her chest tightens. The ringing is barely there now, a distant echo, but the voice remains, settled somewhere nearer than it should be.

Rachel looks toward the door. The broken chair leans against it at a useless angle, wood splintered, lock hanging open. It doesn’t feel like an exit. It feels like a suggestion the building hasn’t bothered to argue with.

She shifts her weight again, testing.

The floor responds in the same place, the same fraction, like it remembers her. The ringing doesn’t spike.

The voice doesn’t wait.

You feel that faster now, it said.

Rachel closes her eyes, grounding herself in the smell, the damp, the quiet weight of the room. “It’s just a building.”

A pause.

Not distant.  
Not restrained.

So are you, it replied.

Lena opens her eyes and finally looks at her. “I don’t like this place,” she says. “It’s… empty.”

Rachel almost laughs. “It’s not.”

She presses her foot down deliberately, just enough to feel that wrong give again. The voice stays with her, calm and present.

No excitement.  
No hunger.

Just proximity.

She pulls her foot back.

“We can’t stay,” she says.

Lena exhales, relief cutting through exhaustion. “Good. Because I wasn’t planning on sleeping.”

They move for the door together. Rachel keeps her steps light, careful, aware of how quickly the room seems to register her leaving.

Outside, the night opens back up—rain louder, air thinner, sound less contained.

The ringing is almost gone.

The voice isn’t.

You don’t need it anymore, it says easily.

Rachel steps away from the building without answering.

She already knows that’s the cost.

They don’t go far before Lena stops again, this time without collapsing, hands rubbing together like she’s warming them even though the rain hasn’t changed. Rachel leans her shoulder against the building’s outer wall, careful with her ankle, listening to the way sound carries now that they’re back outside—rain louder, metal ticking somewhere nearby, the open night thinner than it should be.

“Okay,” Lena says. “We need a plan. Not a good one. Just something.”

Rachel nods. Her ankle throbs steadily, manageable, the kind of pain that gives you something solid to hold onto. Lena keeps talking, words stacking one on top of another in that practical way she gets when fear hasn’t finished deciding what to do with itself.

“Road’s bad. Trees aren’t great. We can’t double back.” Lena gestures vaguely, not looking behind them. “We head downhill. Less visibility. Fewer places to get boxed in.”

Rachel listens, following the rhythm more than the meaning, grateful for something ordinary. “Downhill,” she repeats, because repeating feels safer than adding.

The voice slides in.

You won’t make it far like that, it said.

Rachel flinches, not from pain, not from pressure, but because it lands in the same space Lena’s voice just occupied, clean and unmissable. She clamps her mouth shut and stares at the dark ground near her feet, breathing slow through her nose.

Lena keeps going, unaware. “People don’t like walking wet slopes in the dark. It buys us time.”

She’s thinking in straight lines, the voice says, almost fond. That won’t help you.

Rachel nods too fast.

Lena pauses. “You okay? You’re doing that thing again.”

“What thing.”

“Freezing.” Lena studies her face. “You do it when you’re in pain.”

“I’m fine,” Rachel says, because the word is already there, ready. “Just tired.”

The voice doesn’t wait.

You’re tired because you’re carrying it alone, it said.

Rachel’s fingers curl into her palms. She focuses on the scrape of wet leaves under Lena’s boots, the smell of rain and rust and something faintly electrical she doesn’t want to think about.

“We should keep moving,” Lena says. “Before someone notices we didn’t come back.”

They already did, the voice murmurs, calm enough to be cruel.

Rachel’s breath stutters.

Lena’s eyes sharpen. “What.”

“Nothing.” Rachel shakes her head, then forces the movement to slow, to look normal. “Sorry. Say that again.”

“Which part.”

“The—” Rachel stops herself. “Moving.”

Lena nods, relief flickering through her worry. “Okay. Good.”

They start walking. For a few seconds the sound of their steps lines up, rain steady, breathing even. Rachel counts her footfalls, lets the physical work fill as much space as it can.

“If we get separated—” Lena begins.

You won’t, the voice says immediately.

Rachel stops short.

Lena stumbles a step ahead, swears quietly, then turns. “Rachel?”

“I—sorry.” Rachel adjusts her stance, heart racing. “What were you saying.”

“I said we shouldn’t get separated.” Lena watches her carefully now.

Rachel nods. “Right.”

You don’t need to explain that to her, the voice adds. She’ll follow.

The words settle somewhere deep and unwelcome. Rachel presses her tongue to the roof of her mouth, grounding herself in the taste of rain, the ache in her ankle, the fact of her body still moving through space.

Lena steps closer, lowering her voice. “Hey. Talk to me. What’s going on.”

Rachel looks at her—mud on her jeans, rain in her hair, concern open and unguarded—and for a second the voice goes quiet.

Waiting.

“I’m fine,” Rachel says. “I just need to focus.”

Lena hesitates, then nods. “Okay. Just—don’t shut me out.”

Rachel nods again.

They walk.

The voice stays silent for several steps, close enough to feel even without sound.

Then, lightly, like it’s commenting on the weather—

You see how easy that was, it said.

Rachel keeps moving.

The rain keeps falling.

And even without pain, even without the ringing, the space inside her no longer feels empty enough to be her own.

They walk long enough that the rain changes character, thinning and then thickening again as the trees shift around them, branches catching it in uneven patches that leave Rachel soaked one minute and barely damp the next. Her ankle burns steadily now, not sharp enough to stop her, not dull enough to forget, every step a small negotiation she keeps having to win.

Lena keeps pace a half step behind her, quiet, listening. The silence stretches, not empty, just full of things neither of them wants to name yet.

The voice stays with Rachel.

Not speaking.  
Just present.

Lena lifts a hand without turning. “Stop.”

Rachel does, a beat late. Her boot slides before she catches herself. The sound behind them lands again, heavier now, too measured to be rain.

Lena lowers her voice. “That’s footsteps.”

Rachel waits for the ringing.

Nothing comes.

Her throat tightens.

Left, the voice says.

Lena whispers, “Uphill. Trees are thicker.”

Longer, the voice replies, already dismissing it.

Rachel stares into the dark to the left. The ground drops away sharply there, uneven and slick, the kind of descent that hurts if you misjudge it.

“There’s a break,” she says quietly. “We can cut down.”

Lena hesitates. “That’s not safer.”

Rachel turns anyway.

Mud gives under her boots immediately, ankle flaring hot enough to make her gasp. She catches herself on a low branch and keeps moving, heart hammering, breath ragged. Behind her Lena swears and slides, grabbing Rachel’s jacket for a second before letting go and finding her own balance.

They don’t stop until they reach the bottom, crouching behind a tangle of fallen trees and wet brush. Rachel presses her palm into the mud, grounding herself, rain ticking softly against bark overhead.

The footsteps don’t follow.

Lena stays crouched, breathing hard. “You could’ve warned me.”

Rachel nods. “I’m sorry.”

You didn’t stop, the voice says.

Lena looks up at her. “How did you know that was there?”

Rachel opens her mouth. Closes it. Her jaw tightens. “I didn’t. I just… went.”

Lena studies her face longer than she needs to, then looks away. “Next time, say something.”

They move again, slower now, ground flattening into something almost walkable. The rain thins under thicker cover, sound changing shape again. Rachel counts steps, keeps her weight light, lets the ache in her ankle pull her attention outward instead of in.

The voice breaks the quiet.

Not the path, it said.

Ahead, a narrow trail cuts through the trees, worn enough to be visible even in the dark.

“That’s the first clear thing we’ve seen,” Lena says. “That’s actual ground.”

Rachel hesitates.

They’ll expect it, the voice says, calm.

She angles left.

Lena stops short. “Rachel.”

Rachel keeps going, brushing past low branches, ground narrowing fast enough that Lena has to follow single-file.

“Hey.” Lena catches her arm. “Stop. You’re limping worse. You’re not thinking straight.”

The contact is warm, real. For a second the voice goes quiet.

Rachel breathes.

Rain.  
Mud.  
The weight of her body.

Then—

She’s slowing you down, it said.

Rachel pulls her arm free. Not hard. Just enough. “Trust me.”

Lena stares at her. “I am trusting you. I just need to know why you’re so sure.”

Rachel doesn’t answer. She turns back into the trees.

The ground narrows again, roots thick and close, branches catching at their clothes. Rachel moves faster than she should, guided by something that isn’t sight, not exactly, just a sense of where the ground will hold and where it won’t.

“Rachel,” Lena says behind her. “Slow down. I can’t see.”

Rachel stops and turns. The distance between them feels larger than it should.

Lena steps closer, rain dripping from her hair. “Don’t do that,” she says quietly. “Don’t disappear on me.”

Rachel nods. “I won’t.”

They start moving again, slower, closer together, but the direction doesn’t change.
Rachel keeps choosing turns she can’t explain.
Lena keeps following.
The night folds inward around them.

And every step after that feels shorter than it should have been.
