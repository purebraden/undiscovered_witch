# Chapter 61 — Reallocation

Rachel feels it before she can name it, the way you feel a misstep coming half a second too late, not a warning exactly, just the sudden sense that something has finished deciding without her.

The floor corrects her.

That’s what it feels like — a small kindness, a shift so slight her body accepts it automatically, muscles adjusting, weight redistributing, and in that instant she realizes she’s no longer touching Lena.

No step. No stumble. Just the absence where contact used to be.

“Rachel—” Lena says, or tries to, the word thinning out as it leaves her mouth like it had to travel too far to arrive intact.

Rachel lunges without thinking, momentum carrying her forward, hand outstretched—

—and the floor slides her foot away from where she meant to put it, smooth and accommodating, as if it’s helping her somewhere else, and she catches herself hard, breath knocking loose in her chest as she sees the distance.

Two steps.

Nothing dramatic. Nothing visible.

Too far.

Lena sways, weight tipping, her hand lifting in a searching motion that looks wrong even before it misses, fingers opening and closing on empty air where Rachel was a second ago.

“Oh,” Lena says, soft and surprised, like she’s just noticed gravity. Then again, quieter. “Oh.”

The pressure hits her all at once.

Not a shove. Not an impact. More like something letting go that had been quietly doing work this whole time. Her knees bend without asking permission, her body folding in on itself, not falling so much as losing the idea of how to stay upright, hands clawing uselessly at the space between them.

“Lena!” Rachel shouts, her voice tearing out of her.

Lena doesn’t look at her.

Her mouth moves. Nothing comes out. Then something does, but the pieces are wrong, order scrambled, words arriving without their beginnings.

“Was— I— it’s—”

Her eyes track something Rachel can’t see, lagging, chasing.

Rachel throws herself forward again, muscles burning, shoes skidding as every instinct in her body screams grab her, grab her now—

The room refuses.

Not violently. Not even firmly. It just doesn’t finish letting her move.

There, the voice says, warm now, unmistakably pleased, like it’s been waiting for this exact moment.  
That’s the part you kept fixing.

“Shut up,” Rachel snaps, the words coming out raw and breathless.

Lena laughs once, a short, broken sound that doesn’t match her face. “I can’t— the room— did we—”

She blinks too hard, lashes clumping, and when she looks up again her focus slides past Rachel’s face and doesn’t quite land, like her eyes are arriving late.

“Don’t leave,” Lena says suddenly.

Not to Rachel.

To the space itself.

Something in Rachel’s chest collapses inward.

One of the demons exhales softly, the sound of someone watching a glass finally tip after sitting too close to the edge. “That escalated.”

“She refused,” another says mildly. “The room adjusted.”

Rachel whirls on them, vision swimming. “Fix it.”

A demon tilts its head, considering, not unkindly. “We can stabilize it.”

Lena’s head jerks. “No.”

The word comes late, thin, already fraying.

“I said—” She tries again and fails, her mouth working around a sentence that won’t hold together. “I— I didn’t—”

Her hands shake. She presses them together, fingers interlacing, then pulls them apart again like she’s forgotten what hands are for and is trying to remember through repetition.

Rachel drops to her knees without choosing to. The floor allows down even if it won’t allow over.

“Lena,” she says, voice breaking. “Look at me.”

Lena looks near her.

Close enough to hurt.

“Something’s wrong,” Lena says calmly, terrifyingly calm, like she’s noting a detail she just noticed about a room she’s been standing in for a while. “I keep losing the start of things.”

Rachel’s eyes burn.

She’s slipping fast, the voice says, bright with interest.  
You waited too long.

Rachel doesn’t answer. She can’t spare the air.

“If I do this,” Rachel says, the words tumbling over each other, trying to stay together, “it won’t be the same. You know that, right?”

Lena nods, or thinks she does, the motion loose and uncertain. “I don’t—” She stops, brow furrowing. Tries again. “I don’t need same.”

Her gaze flickers, then sharpens for half a second, clarity punching through hard enough to knock the breath out of Rachel.

“I can’t hold the no,” Lena says, and the honesty of it is worse than fear. “It keeps… slipping.”

Rachel’s chest tightens until it hurts to breathe.

“Say it,” she whispers. “I need you to say it.”

Lena’s mouth opens. Nothing comes out.

She closes her eyes, face twisting with effort, as if the word is heavy and keeps sliding out of reach.

“Please,” she says finally.

It’s barely sound. It’s enough.

The room hums, satisfied.

The demons turn away, already done.

And Rachel makes the choice she swore she wouldn’t make for someone else, because there is no version of this where waiting makes it better.
