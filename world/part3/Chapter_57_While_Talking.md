# Chapter 57 — While Talking

They didn't go far before Lena stopped again, this time without collapsing, hands rubbing together like she was warming them even though the rain hadn't changed. Rachel leaned her shoulder against the building's outer wall, careful with her ankle, listening to the way sound carried now that they were back outside—rain louder, metal ticking somewhere nearby, the open night thinner than it should have been.

"Okay," Lena said. "We need a plan. Not a good one. Just something."

Rachel nodded. Her ankle throbbed steadily, manageable, the kind of pain that gave you something solid to hold onto. Lena kept talking, words stacking one on top of another in that practical way she got when fear hadn't finished deciding what to do with itself.

"Road's bad. Trees aren't great. We can't double back." Lena gestured vaguely, not looking behind them. "We head downhill. Less visibility. Fewer places to get boxed in."

Rachel listened, following the rhythm more than the meaning, grateful for something ordinary. "Downhill," she repeated, because repeating felt safer than adding.

The voice slid in.

**You won't make it far like that.**

Rachel flinched, not from pain, not from pressure, but because it landed in the same space Lena's voice had just occupied, clean and unmissable. She clamped her mouth shut and stared at the dark ground near her feet, breathing slow through her nose.

Lena kept going, unaware. "People don't like walking wet slopes in the dark. It buys us time."

**She's thinking in straight lines**, the voice said, almost fond. **That won't help you.**

Rachel nodded too fast.

Lena paused. "You okay? You're doing that thing again."

"What thing."

"Freezing." Lena studied her face. "You do it when you're in pain."

"I'm fine," Rachel said, because the word was already there, ready. "Just tired."

The voice didn't wait.

**You're tired because you're carrying it alone.**

Rachel's fingers curled into her palms. She focused on the scrape of wet leaves under Lena's boots, the smell of rain and rust and something faintly electrical she didn't want to think about.

"We should keep moving," Lena said. "Before someone notices we didn't come back."

**They already did**, the voice murmured, calm enough to be cruel.

Rachel's breath stuttered.

Lena's eyes sharpened. "What."

"Nothing." Rachel shook her head, then forced the movement to slow, to look normal. "Sorry. Say that again."

"Which part."

"The—" Rachel stopped herself. "Moving."

Lena nodded, relief flickering through her worry. "Okay. Good."

They started walking. For a few seconds the sound of their steps lined up, rain steady, breathing even. Rachel counted her footfalls, let the physical work fill as much space as it could.

"If we get separated—" Lena began.

**You won't**, the voice said immediately.

Rachel stopped short.

Lena stumbled a step ahead, swore quietly, then turned. "Rachel?"

"I—sorry." Rachel adjusted her stance, heart racing. "What were you saying."

"I said we shouldn't get separated." Lena watched her carefully now.

Rachel nodded. "Right."

**You don't need to explain that to her**, the voice added. **She'll follow.**

The words settled somewhere deep and unwelcome. Rachel pressed her tongue to the roof of her mouth, grounding herself in the taste of rain, the ache in her ankle, the fact of her body still moving through space.

Lena stepped closer, lowering her voice. "Hey. Talk to me. What's going on."

Rachel looked at her—mud on her jeans, rain in her hair, concern open and unguarded—and for a second the voice went quiet.

Waiting.

"I'm fine," Rachel said. "I just need to focus."

Lena hesitated, then nodded. "Okay. Just—don't shut me out."

Rachel nodded again.

They walked.

The voice stayed silent for several steps, close enough to feel even without sound.

Then, lightly, like it was commenting on the weather—

**You see how easy that was.**

Rachel kept moving.

The rain kept falling.

And even without pain, even without the ringing, the space inside her no longer felt empty enough to be her own.
