# Chapter 57 — While Talking

They don’t go far before Lena stops again, this time without collapsing, hands rubbing together like she’s warming them even though the rain hasn’t changed. Rachel leans her shoulder against the building’s outer wall, careful with her ankle, listening to the way sound carries now that they’re back outside—rain louder, metal ticking somewhere nearby, the open night thinner than it should be.

“Okay,” Lena says. “We need a plan. Not a good one. Just something.”

Rachel nods. Her ankle throbs steadily, manageable, the kind of pain that gives you something solid to hold onto. Lena keeps talking, words stacking one on top of another in that practical way she gets when fear hasn’t finished deciding what to do with itself.

“Road’s bad. Trees aren’t great. We can’t double back.” Lena gestures vaguely, not looking behind them. “We head downhill. Less visibility. Fewer places to get boxed in.”

Rachel listens, following the rhythm more than the meaning, grateful for something ordinary. “Downhill,” she repeats, because repeating feels safer than adding.

The voice slides in.

You won’t make it far like that, it said.

Rachel flinches, not from pain, not from pressure, but because it lands in the same space Lena’s voice just occupied, clean and unmissable. She clamps her mouth shut and stares at the dark ground near her feet, breathing slow through her nose.

Lena keeps going, unaware. “People don’t like walking wet slopes in the dark. It buys us time.”

She’s thinking in straight lines, the voice says, almost fond. That won’t help you.

Rachel nods too fast.

Lena pauses. “You okay? You’re doing that thing again.”

“What thing.”

“Freezing.” Lena studies her face. “You do it when you’re in pain.”

“I’m fine,” Rachel says, because the word is already there, ready. “Just tired.”

The voice doesn’t wait.

You’re tired because you’re carrying it alone, it said.

Rachel’s fingers curl into her palms. She focuses on the scrape of wet leaves under Lena’s boots, the smell of rain and rust and something faintly electrical she doesn’t want to think about.

“We should keep moving,” Lena says. “Before someone notices we didn’t come back.”

They already did, the voice murmurs, calm enough to be cruel.

Rachel’s breath stutters.

Lena’s eyes sharpen. “What.”

“Nothing.” Rachel shakes her head, then forces the movement to slow, to look normal. “Sorry. Say that again.”

“Which part.”

“The—” Rachel stops herself. “Moving.”

Lena nods, relief flickering through her worry. “Okay. Good.”

They start walking. For a few seconds the sound of their steps lines up, rain steady, breathing even. Rachel counts her footfalls, lets the physical work fill as much space as it can.

“If we get separated—” Lena begins.

You won’t, the voice says immediately.

Rachel stops short.

Lena stumbles a step ahead, swears quietly, then turns. “Rachel?”

“I—sorry.” Rachel adjusts her stance, heart racing. “What were you saying.”

“I said we shouldn’t get separated.” Lena watches her carefully now.

Rachel nods. “Right.”

You don’t need to explain that to her, the voice adds. She’ll follow.

The words settle somewhere deep and unwelcome. Rachel presses her tongue to the roof of her mouth, grounding herself in the taste of rain, the ache in her ankle, the fact of her body still moving through space.

Lena steps closer, lowering her voice. “Hey. Talk to me. What’s going on.”

Rachel looks at her—mud on her jeans, rain in her hair, concern open and unguarded—and for a second the voice goes quiet.

Waiting.

“I’m fine,” Rachel says. “I just need to focus.”

Lena hesitates, then nods. “Okay. Just—don’t shut me out.”

Rachel nods again.

They walk.

The voice stays silent for several steps, close enough to feel even without sound.

Then, lightly, like it’s commenting on the weather—

You see how easy that was, it said.

Rachel keeps moving.

The rain keeps falling.

And even without pain, even without the ringing, the space inside her no longer feels empty enough to be her own.
