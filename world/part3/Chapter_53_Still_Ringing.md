# Chapter 53 — Still Ringing

They didn't stop all at once.

Lena slowed first, breath rasping, hands braced on her knees like she was holding herself together by force, and Rachel kept moving another few steps before she realized she was doing it, ankle screaming every time it hit wrong ground. The trees thinned into a narrow break where the rain didn't reach as hard, branches bending low enough to make the air feel pressed down.

"Okay," Lena panted. "Okay—just—give me a second."

Rachel stayed upright because sitting felt like letting something arrive. The pressure was thinner now but constant, a high wire pulled tight behind her eyes, vibrating whenever she shifted her weight.

The world held.  
Uneven. Loud. Real enough to trust.

She pressed her palms hard against her temples, as if pressure might cancel pressure.

**You're listening now**, the voice said, right where the sound was worst.

Rachel dropped her hands. "I didn't ask you to stay."

A flicker of amusement rippled through the pressure.

**You didn't ask me to leave.**

"That's not—" She swallowed, breath catching. "That's not how this works."

A pause. Curious. Interested.

**Isn't it.**

Lena straightened slowly, eyes fixed on Rachel's face now instead of the ground. "Who are you talking to."

"No one." The word came too fast. Rachel hated that. "My head."

Lena watched her for a beat longer than necessary, rain ticking softly through leaves around them, then nodded like she was packing it away. "You scared him back there."

Rachel's stomach tightened. "I didn't touch him."

"I know," Lena said. "He just… missed himself."

The pressure sharpened, pleased.

**She noticed.**

Rachel turned her head away, staring into the dark between the trees where the light didn't reach properly. "We need to keep moving."

They did, slower now, careful, feet sliding on wet leaves and roots. Every few steps Rachel felt the world hesitate, like it was waiting to see if she'd change her mind.

"I don't want to do that again," she muttered, not sure if she was talking to Lena or the sound in her head.

**You will**, the voice said immediately, too certain.

Her skin crawled. "You don't know that."

The pressure tightened, bright and eager, threading into the back of her thoughts.

**You already crossed it.**

Rachel stopped without meaning to. Her breath caught halfway in and stayed there, chest burning as she stared too hard at the space ahead of her, like she could force it to make sense if she just looked long enough.

Lena slowed beside her, watching Rachel instead of the trail now. "Rachel," she said carefully, "you just stopped. What are you thinking about."

"Nothing." The lie tasted flat.

They stood there a beat too long, rain whispering, the space between them suddenly fragile in a way Rachel couldn't explain.

The trail ahead barely existed, just a suggestion pressed through wet undergrowth. Lena hesitated, looking left, then right, uncertainty flickering across her face.

Rachel stepped forward.

The ground firmed under her foot, roots flattening just enough to hold, and the pressure softened, almost approving, like something settling into place.

Lena's eyes flicked to Rachel's face, not the ground. "Did you just—"

"No." Rachel cut it off, jaw tight. She forced herself to stop. The earth stopped helping immediately, went back to being just earth, slick and uneven and uncooperative.

They stood there, the night breathing around them.

"Back there," Lena said after a moment, "when he came toward us… you didn't flinch."

Rachel laughed once, brittle. "I did. Inside."

"That's not what I meant."

The pressure pressed closer, intimate.

**She's asking the wrong question.**

Rachel winced. "Stop."

Lena frowned. "What."

"Nothing." Rachel rubbed her face hard, smearing rain and dirt across her cheeks. "If someone else comes near us, don't try to step in front of me."

Lena stiffened. "Rachel—"

"I need space," Rachel said, the words stacked too tightly together. "If they get close, let them."

Lena studied her, fear and trust tangling openly. "You're asking me to do nothing."

"I'm asking you not to fix it."

The pressure hummed, pleased again.

**She understands more than you think.**

Rachel started forward, slower now, deliberate. The trail resisted, then gave, the world not quite helping, not quite fighting her either.

The pressure never fully faded.

It stayed with her, thin and eager, threaded through every step, waiting for the next time she decided not to wait.
