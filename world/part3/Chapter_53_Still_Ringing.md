# Chapter 52 — Still Ringing

They don’t stop all at once.

Lena slows first, breath rasping, hands braced on her knees like she’s holding herself together by force, and Rachel keeps moving another few steps before she realizes she’s doing it, ankle screaming every time it hits wrong ground. The trees thin into a narrow break where the rain doesn’t reach as hard, branches bending low enough to make the air feel pressed down.

“Okay,” Lena pants. “Okay—just—give me a second.”

Rachel stays upright because sitting feels like letting something arrive. The ringing is thinner now but constant, a high wire pulled tight behind her eyes, vibrating whenever she shifts her weight.

The world holds, uneven and loud, real enough to trust.

She presses her palms hard against her temples, as if pressure might cancel pressure.

You’re listening now, the voice says, right where the sound is worst.

Rachel drops her hands. “I didn’t ask you to stay.”

A flicker of amusement ripples through the ringing.

You didn’t ask me to leave, it said.

“That’s not—” She swallows, breath catching. “That’s not how this works.”

A pause. Curious. Interested.

Isn’t it, it said.

Lena straightens slowly, eyes fixed on Rachel’s face now instead of the ground. “Who are you talking to.”

“No one.” The word comes too fast. Rachel hates that. “My head.”

Lena watches her for a beat longer than necessary, rain ticking softly through leaves around them, then nods like she’s packing it away. “You scared him back there.”

Rachel’s stomach tightens. “I didn’t touch him.”

“I know,” Lena says. “He just… missed himself.”

The ringing sharpens, pleased.

She noticed, it said.

Rachel turns her head away, staring into the dark between the trees where the light doesn’t reach properly. “We need to keep moving.”

They do, slower now, careful, feet sliding on wet leaves and roots. Every few steps Rachel feels the world hesitate, like it’s waiting to see if she’ll change her mind.

“I don’t want to do that again,” she mutters, not sure if she’s talking to Lena or the sound in her head.

You will, the voice says immediately, too certain.

Her skin crawls. “You don’t know that.”

The ringing tightens, bright and eager, threading into the back of her thoughts.

You already crossed it, it said.

Rachel stops without meaning to. Her breath catches halfway in and stays there, chest burning as she stares too hard at the space ahead of her, like she can force it to make sense if she just looks long enough.

Lena slows beside her, watching Rachel instead of the trail now. “Rachel,” she says carefully, “you just stopped. What are you thinking about.”

“Nothing.” The lie tastes flat.

They stand there a beat too long, rain whispering, the space between them suddenly fragile in a way Rachel can’t explain.

The trail ahead barely exists, just a suggestion pressed through wet undergrowth. Lena hesitates, looking left, then right, uncertainty flickering across her face.

Rachel steps forward.

The ground firms under her foot, roots flattening just enough to hold, and the ringing softens, almost approving, like something settling into place.

Lena’s eyes flick to Rachel’s face, not the ground. “Did you just—”

“No.” Rachel cuts it off, jaw tight. She forces herself to stop. The earth stops helping immediately, goes back to being just earth, slick and uneven and uncooperative.

They stand there, the night breathing around them.

“Back there,” Lena says after a moment, “when he came toward us… you didn’t flinch.”

Rachel laughs once, brittle. “I did. Inside.”

“That’s not what I meant.”

The ringing presses closer, intimate.

She’s asking the wrong question, it said.

Rachel winces. “Stop.”

Lena frowns. “What.”

“Nothing.” Rachel rubs her face hard, smearing rain and dirt across her cheeks. “If someone else comes near us, don’t try to step in front of me.”

Lena stiffens. “Rachel—”

“I need space,” Rachel says, the words stacked too tightly together. “If they get close, let them.”

Lena studies her, fear and trust tangling openly. “You’re asking me to do nothing.”

“I’m asking you not to fix it.”

The ringing hums, pleased again.

She understands more than you think, it said.

Rachel starts forward, slower now, deliberate. The trail resists, then gives, the world not quite helping, not quite fighting her either.

The ringing never fully fades.

It stays with her, thin and eager, threaded through every step, waiting for the next time she decides not to wait.
