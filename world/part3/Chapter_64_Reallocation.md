# Chapter 64 — Reallocation

Rachel felt it before she could name it, the way you felt a misstep coming half a second too late, not a warning exactly, just the sudden sense that something had finished deciding without her.

The floor corrected her.

That’s what it felt like — a small kindness, a shift so slight her body accepted it automatically, muscles adjusting, weight redistributing, and in that instant she realized she was no longer touching Lena.

No step. No stumble. Just the absence where contact used to be.

“Rachel—” Lena said, or tried to, the word thinning out as it left her mouth like it had to travel too far to arrive intact.

Rachel lunged without thinking, momentum carrying her forward, hand outstretched—

—and the floor slid her foot away from where she meant to put it, smooth and accommodating, as if it was helping her somewhere else, and she caught herself hard, breath knocking loose in her chest as she saw the distance.

Two steps.

Nothing dramatic. Nothing visible.

Too far.

Lena swayed, weight tipping, her hand lifting in a searching motion that looked wrong even before it missed, fingers opening and closing on empty air where Rachel had been a second ago.

“Oh,” Lena said, soft and surprised, like she’d just noticed gravity. Then again, quieter. “Oh.”

The pressure hit her all at once.

Not a shove. Not an impact. More like something letting go that had been quietly doing work this whole time. Her knees bent without asking permission, her body folding in on itself, not falling so much as losing the idea of how to stay upright, hands clawing uselessly at the space between them.

“Lena!” Rachel shouted, her voice tearing out of her.

Lena didn’t look at her.

Her mouth moved. Nothing came out. Then something did, but the pieces were wrong, order scrambled, words arriving without their beginnings.

“Was— I— it’s—”

Her eyes track something Rachel can’t see, lagging, chasing.

Rachel threw herself forward again, muscles burning, shoes skidding as every instinct in her body screamed grab her, grab her now—

The room refused.

Not violently. Not even firmly. It just didn’t finish letting her move.

**There**, the voice said, warm now, unmistakably pleased, like it had been waiting for this exact moment.  
**That’s the part you kept fixing.**

“Shut up,” Rachel snapped, the words coming out raw and breathless.

Lena laughed once, a short, broken sound that didn’t match her face. “I can’t— the room— did we—”

She blinked too hard, lashes clumping, and when she looked up again her focus slid past Rachel’s face and didn’t quite land, like her eyes were arriving late.

“Don’t leave,” Lena said suddenly.

Not to Rachel.

To the space itself.

Something in Rachel’s chest collapsed inward.

One of the demons exhaled softly, the sound of someone watching a glass finally tip after sitting too close to the edge. “That escalated.”

“She refused,” another said mildly. “The room adjusted.”

Rachel whirled on them, vision swimming. “Fix it.”

A demon tilted its head, considering, not unkindly. “We can stabilize it.”

Lena’s head jerked. “No.”

The word comes late, thin, already fraying.

“I said—” She tried again and failed, her mouth working around a sentence that wouldn’t hold together. “I— I didn’t—”

Her hands shook. She pressed them together, fingers interlacing, then pulled them apart again like she’d forgotten what hands were for and was trying to remember through repetition.

Rachel dropped to her knees without choosing to. The floor allowed down even if it wouldn’t allow over.

“Lena,” she said, voice breaking. “Look at me.”

Lena looked near her.

Close enough to hurt.

“Something’s wrong,” Lena said calmly, terrifyingly calm, like she was noting a detail she just noticed about a room she’d been standing in for a while. “I keep losing the start of things.”

Rachel’s eyes burned.

**She’s slipping fast**, the voice said, bright with interest.  
**You waited too long.**

Rachel didn’t answer. She couldn’t spare the air.

“If I do this,” Rachel said, the words tumbling over each other, trying to stay together, “it won’t be the same. You know that, right?”

Lena nodded, or thought she did, the motion loose and uncertain. “I don’t—” She stopped, brow furrowing. Tried again. “I don’t need same.”

Her gaze flickered, then sharpened for half a second, clarity punching through hard enough to knock the breath out of Rachel.

“I can’t hold the no,” Lena said, and the honesty of it was worse than fear. “It keeps… slipping.”

Rachel’s chest tightened until it hurt to breathe.

“Say it,” she whispered. “I need you to say it.”

Lena’s mouth opened. Nothing came out.

She closed her eyes, face twisting with effort, as if the word was heavy and kept sliding out of reach.

“Please,” she said finally.

It was barely sound. It was enough.

The room hummed, satisfied.

The demons turned away, already done.

And Rachel made the choice she swore she wouldn’t make for someone else, because there was no version of this where waiting made it better.
