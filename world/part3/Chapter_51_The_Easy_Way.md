# Chapter 51 — The Easy Way

Rachel’s foot came down and the ground answered it cleanly, no slip, no hollow delay, and the wrongness of that made her stumble anyway, breath hitching as her ankle flared hot and bright. She waited for the rest of it—for the lag, the sway, the world deciding whether to keep her upright—but it didn’t come. The road held. It kept holding.

She blinked and took another step, slower this time, and it still behaved.

Lena was close enough that Rachel could feel her through the damp fabric of her jacket, warmth and weight lining up in a way that shouldn’t matter and somehow did. Lena’s breathing was steady at her shoulder, human and ordinary, and the rain sounded like rain again instead of static tearing at the edges of Rachel’s hearing. The night pulled itself into something continuous.

Rachel swallowed.

She shifted her weight without thinking and the road smoothed under her foot like it was already expecting the move. The fence didn’t drift. The curve ahead didn’t warp when she looked at it too long. The trees stayed where trees were supposed to stay.

“That—” Her voice caught. She cleared her throat and tried again. “Did you feel that.”

Lena didn’t answer. Her arm tightened reflexively, not guiding, just there, and the world seemed to settle around the contact, pain blurring down from sharp to manageable, edges softening.

The pressure wakes up close and irritated.

**Don’t get used to that.**

Rachel almost laughs, the sound brittle in her chest. “Too late.”

She tried to step half a pace ahead, just to test it, and the ground grabbed her too soon, jarring her ankle hard enough that she gasped. Lena caught her instantly, fingers digging in with more force than necessary.

“Slow down,” Lena said. “You’re rushing.”

The road clicked back into place.

Rachel stood there shaking, breath tearing in and out of her lungs, hating how fast the relief came, how easily the noise in her head dulled when she let Lena take her weight. They started again, and the road opened where Lena angled them, not dramatically, just enough—fewer stones, fewer dips, shadows thinning as if brushed aside.

Rachel noticed without wanting to.

“You didn’t even look,” she said.

Lena glanced down, confused. “Look at what.”

“The ground.”

Lena shrugs. “Didn’t need to.”

The pressure presses harder, unpleasant now.

**She assumes.**

Rachel stumbled again, smaller this time, and Lena steadied her before she could decide whether she wanted it. The world obliged immediately, pain dulling, the air smoothing, the night loosening its grip.

Rachel exhaled and didn’t like the sound it made. “Why is it better,” she muttered.

Lena frowned. “Because you’re not fighting it.”

That lands wrong in a way Rachel can’t quite isolate.

They reached a place where the road changed its mind—not a fork, just a widening, one side dipping and cluttered, the other open and clean and easy to follow. Lena didn’t slow. She angled them toward the open side without comment, certainty in the motion.

The pressure spikes, sharp and immediate.

**That way keeps you quiet.**

Rachel dug her heel in on instinct. The world lurched, not collapsing, just slipping out of alignment enough to hurt, pain snapping bright up her leg, vision whitening at the edges.

Lena tightened her grip. “Stop doing that,” she said, breath sharp now. “You’re going to make it worse.”

The road steadied.

Rachel let out a thin, broken laugh. “You feel that,” she said. “You have to.”

“I feel you shaking,” Lena said, frustrated, scared.

The pressure hums, displeased.

**She likes when it’s easy.**

Rachel pulled her arm free and everything stuttered—not enough to drop her, just enough to make the night feel loud again, rain too present, ground honest and unforgiving under her foot. She swayed, catching herself on nothing, breath sawing out of her chest. Lena reached for her and stopped when Rachel shook her head.

“Don’t,” Rachel said. “Just wait.”

The open road ahead looked inviting now, smooth and reasonable, the kind of choice that explained itself. The other way looked worse—narrow, uneven, shadows piled thick where the light didn’t reach.

The pressure doesn’t rush her this time.

**You can walk where it hurts, or you can walk where you disappear.**

Rachel closed her eyes for half a second and stepped toward the worse ground. Pain answered immediately, loud and honest, the night pressing back in, rain sharp in her ears again.

But it stays real.

Behind her, Lena exhaled, frustrated and frightened. “Rachel. This is harder.”

Rachel nodded, jaw clenched. “I know.”

She kept moving, choosing discomfort over ease, and after a beat she heard footsteps again—not steadying, not leading.

Just there.
