# Chapter 51 — The Easy Way

Rachel’s foot comes down and the ground answers it cleanly, no slip, no hollow delay, and the wrongness of that makes her stumble anyway, breath hitching as her ankle flares hot and bright. She waits for the rest of it—for the lag, the sway, the world deciding whether to keep her upright—but it doesn’t come. The road holds. It keeps holding.

She blinks and takes another step, slower this time, and it still behaves.

Lena is close enough that Rachel can feel her through the damp fabric of her jacket, warmth and weight lining up in a way that shouldn’t matter and somehow does. Lena’s breathing is steady at her shoulder, human and ordinary, and the rain sounds like rain again instead of static tearing at the edges of Rachel’s hearing. The night pulls itself into something continuous.

Rachel swallows.

She shifts her weight without thinking and the road smooths under her foot like it was already expecting the move. The fence doesn’t drift. The curve ahead doesn’t warp when she looks at it too long. The trees stay where trees are supposed to stay.

“That—” Her voice catches. She clears her throat and tries again. “Did you feel that.”

Lena doesn’t answer. Her arm tightens reflexively, not guiding, just there, and the world seems to settle around the contact, pain blurring down from sharp to manageable, edges softening.

The pressure wakes up close and irritated.

**Don’t get used to that.**

Rachel almost laughs, the sound brittle in her chest. “Too late.”

She tries to step half a pace ahead, just to test it, and the ground grabs her too soon, jarring her ankle hard enough that she gasps. Lena catches her instantly, fingers digging in with more force than necessary.

“Slow down,” Lena says. “You’re rushing.”

The road clicks back into place.

Rachel stands there shaking, breath tearing in and out of her lungs, hating how fast the relief comes, how easily the noise in her head dulls when she lets Lena take her weight. They start again, and the road opens where Lena angles them, not dramatically, just enough—fewer stones, fewer dips, shadows thinning as if brushed aside.

Rachel notices without wanting to.

“You didn’t even look,” she says.

Lena glances down, confused. “Look at what.”

“The ground.”

Lena shrugs. “Didn’t need to.”

The pressure presses harder, unpleasant now.

**She assumes.**

Rachel stumbles again, smaller this time, and Lena steadies her before she can decide whether she wants it. The world obliges immediately, pain dulling, the air smoothing, the night loosening its grip.

Rachel exhales and doesn’t like the sound it makes. “Why is it better,” she mutters.

Lena frowns. “Because you’re not fighting it.”

That lands wrong in a way Rachel can’t quite isolate.

They reach a place where the road changes its mind—not a fork, just a widening, one side dipping and cluttered, the other open and clean and easy to follow. Lena doesn’t slow. She angles them toward the open side without comment, certainty in the motion.

The pressure spikes, sharp and immediate.

**That way keeps you quiet.**

Rachel digs her heel in on instinct. The world lurches, not collapsing, just slipping out of alignment enough to hurt, pain snapping bright up her leg, vision whitening at the edges.

Lena tightens her grip. “Stop doing that,” she says, breath sharp now. “You’re going to make it worse.”

The road steadies.

Rachel lets out a thin, broken laugh. “You feel that,” she says. “You have to.”

“I feel you shaking,” Lena says, frustrated, scared.

The pressure hums, displeased.

**She likes when it’s easy.**

Rachel pulls her arm free and everything stutters—not enough to drop her, just enough to make the night feel loud again, rain too present, ground honest and unforgiving under her foot. She sways, catching herself on nothing, breath sawing out of her chest. Lena reaches for her and stops when Rachel shakes her head.

“Don’t,” Rachel says. “Just wait.”

The open road ahead looks inviting now, smooth and reasonable, the kind of choice that explains itself. The other way looks worse—narrow, uneven, shadows piled thick where the light doesn’t reach.

The pressure doesn’t rush her this time.

**You can walk where it hurts, or you can walk where you disappear.**

Rachel closes her eyes for half a second and steps toward the worse ground. Pain answers immediately, loud and honest, the night pressing back in, rain sharp in her ears again.

But it stays real.

Behind her, Lena exhales, frustrated and frightened. “Rachel. This is harder.”

Rachel nods, jaw clenched. “I know.”

She keeps moving, choosing discomfort over ease, and after a beat she hears footsteps again—not steadying, not leading.

Just there.
