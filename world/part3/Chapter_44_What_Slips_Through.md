# Chapter 44 — What Slips Through

Rachel didn’t jump.

She stepped because her ankle failed first, because the floor behind her loosened its opinion about being solid, because the space in front of her felt crowded by intent even though nothing had touched her yet. Her heel crossed the seam and the moment stretched thin, tugged sideways, then let go all at once.

The floor didn’t drop so much as forget her.

She slid—half standing, half falling—cold air tearing up around her legs as the concrete edge skimmed her calf and vanished, the sound she made not matching fear so much as shock at being misplaced. Gravity hesitated. Her stomach did something unpleasant.

She hit something that bent and corrected.

Not soft.  
Not solid.

A surface that accepted her weight late and then pitched her sideways anyway, sending her shoulder into a wall that didn’t line up with anything else. Pain arrived out of order—ribs, then hip, then shoulder again as if it wanted a second try.

Rachel lay there with her face pressed to the floor, palms scraping against a texture that felt like tile until it didn’t, grain shifting under her fingers like the surface was still deciding what it was supposed to be.

Behind her, the opening closed.

Not a slam.  
A decision.

The sound came twice.

Her lungs stuttered. Breath scraped shallow and fast, each inhale catching like it had to pass through something narrow before it was allowed in.

**Stay down—**

The words crowded in too close, not comfort, not command, and she hated that her body listened anyway, muscles locking while her heart battered against her ribs like it wanted out.

“No,” she said, but the word slid out weak and wrong, and she stayed there because the idea of standing felt like it would peel her apart.

Light crept in uneven strips overhead, not fluorescent, not warm, something in between that made the shadows smear instead of settle. The walls curved in ways that kept slipping when she tried to track them, angles drifting just enough to make her dizzy.

This wasn’t a hallway.  
It wasn’t a room.

The air smelled sharp and sweet, ozone and something like rain trapped underground, and every sound she made came back altered, breath returning half a beat late, the scrape of her sleeve echoing longer than it should have.

She pushed herself up and immediately regretted it as her vision washed pale, pressure inside her chest shifting forward in response, attentive in a way that made her skin crawl.

**Careful.**

Her ankle throbbed, swelling fast, heat blooming out of proportion to the injury. She grabbed it without thinking, fingers shaking, the shaking refusing to stop even after she noticed it.

“Where are we,” she whispered.

The space bent the words before giving them back.

No answer. Just the pressure holding steady, closer now, like it had leaned in to watch.

She tried to stand and failed halfway, body arriving late to its own decision, knees buckling as the floor dipped again—not opening, not collapsing, just *misbehaving*. She caught herself on a low rail that ran along the wall, metal warm under her palm.

The rail pulsed.

Once.

Rachel yanked her hand back like she’d touched something alive.

**It’s listening—**

“Everything is listening,” she snapped, anger punching through the fear sharp enough to hurt, and limped forward anyway, hand skimming the wall for balance, counting steps without meaning to—one, two—then stopping when the numbers slipped and refused to line up.

The space widened ahead of her into something that felt almost polite by comparison, ceiling lifting, lights spacing themselves more evenly, grids and arcs painted across the floor that overlapped without agreeing, some faded, some too bright, like several versions of the room had been mapped on top of each other and left there.

At the center stood a structure.

Not a machine.  
Not furniture.

A frame, but wrong—thinner, incomplete, angles bending inward where they should have flared, cables sinking into the floor and disappearing into seams that pulsed faintly, out of sync with her heartbeat.

Rachel stopped short, breath snagging.

“No.”

The pressure inside her tightened, not warning, not reassurance.

**Not built for holding.**

Her ears rang as she edged around it, ankle screaming every time she put weight down wrong, the light inside the structure lagging behind her movement, shadows adjusting late like they were embarrassed to be caught out.

Something shifted inside it.

Not solid.  
Not stable.

The air folded inward and relaxed again.

Rachel backed away and nearly lost her balance as a sound reached her from behind.

Footsteps.

Not boots.

Bare feet, slapping lightly against the floor in a rhythm that didn’t quite repeat.

She turned too fast, pain tearing up her leg, and had to grab the wall to stay upright as a figure stepped into view from behind a curve in the wall—half seen at first, then all at once.

A person.

Young. Or not. It was hard to tell.

Barefoot. Gray uniform worn thin at the edges. Hair cut close, eyes too bright, pupils blown wide like they’d spent too long in bad light.

They stopped when they saw her.

The moment stretched awkwardly.

“You’re not supposed to be here,” the person said, voice echoing wrong, splitting and rejoining.

Rachel swallowed, throat dry.

“I could say the same.”

The person’s mouth twitched, not quite a smile, not quite anything.

“They’ll come,” they said. “They always do.”

Rachel looked back at the wall behind her. Smooth. No seam. No sign it had ever opened.

“I can’t go back.”

“No one does.”

The pressure inside her chest sharpened, alert now.

**This one slipped earlier.**

Rachel’s breath hitched.

“You can hear it too?” she asked before she could stop herself.

The person’s eyes widened a fraction.

“Oh,” they said quietly. “That’s bad.”

The lights rippled in sequence, a wave moving through the space like a signal being passed along something she couldn’t see. The wrong frame pulsed brighter.

The pressure leaned forward, interested.

**They found the seam.**

Rachel closed her eyes for half a second.

“Then we move,” she said.

The other person hesitated, gaze flicking to her ankle, the frame, the walls that wouldn’t settle.

“Move where?”

Rachel didn’t have an answer.

The hum deepened. Layers stacked out of sync. The space waited.
