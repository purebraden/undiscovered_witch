# Chapter 84 — Routing

The floor began to reorganize in sections.

Not beneath Rachel’s feet, but around them. Narrow bands slid past one another in precise increments, redistributing space without changing the room’s overall shape. She felt the adjustment as a tug at her balance rather than a visible shift, her body compensating before her eyes caught up.

She swayed.

The containment pressure in her chest tightened in response, not pushing, not pulling—simply removing the margin where she might have fallen.

The structure in front of her withdrew by degrees. Its surface dimmed as new lines of light threaded themselves into the floor, extending away from the chamber in a shallow arc that didn’t feel optional.

The hum followed, frequency stepping down and then stabilizing at a range that settled deeper in her body, no longer sharp enough to ache but heavy enough to register with every breath.

The presences remained where they were.

They did not watch her leave.

They didn’t need to.

Rachel took a step without deciding to.

Her foot landed cleanly on the newly resolved surface. No lag. No correction. The next step followed before she could stop it, her body carried forward by a sequence that didn’t feel like force so much as continuation.

“Routing engaged,” one of the presences said, voice neutral and already disengaging.

Rachel swallowed. “Routing to where.”

There was no response.

The space ahead narrowed—not by closing, but by committing. Distance resolved into a stretch that agreed to behave the same way twice, then a third time, removing the Between’s habitual uncertainty.

Rachel’s shoulders tensed.

The pressure adjusted instantly, compensating for the shift in posture.

*This is the transfer path,* the voice said, careful now. *Not the destination.*

“That’s worse,” Rachel muttered.

*Yes.*

She tried to slow.

The system noticed.

The pressure didn’t increase. It simply stopped accommodating her hesitation. Her muscles burned as the floor refused to support inefficiency, joints protesting as the Relay corrected her gait into something smoother and less forgiving.

She let herself move.

The corridor curved gently, not turning so much as redirecting priority. The surfaces around her remained featureless, planes that absorbed light instead of reflecting it. No seams. No doors. Nothing to mark progress except the steady repetition of her steps.

Her scar pulsed in time with the hum. Each beat felt warmer, spreading upward through her leg and settling low in her spine like something syncing.

Rachel’s breathing went shallow.

She lifted her foot and deliberately placed it half a step off the resolved path.

Pain snapped behind her eyes, immediate and contained, sharp enough to make her gasp. The pressure corrected her posture before she could fall, guiding her foot back where it belonged.

*Don’t test it,* the voice said. *That’s not resistance. That’s error.*

“Same difference,” Rachel said through clenched teeth.

*Not here.*

The corridor widened briefly into a node.

Not a room. A pause in prioritization.

The segmented floor slowed, then locked into place beneath her feet. The pressure eased just enough to let her stand without bracing.

Rachel dragged in a breath she hadn’t realized she’d been holding.

The hum modulated again.

Light gathered ahead, resolving into a vertical plane that shimmered faintly, its edges precise in a way the seam outside the Relay had not been.

Rachel stared at it, heart hammering.

Her stomach rolled. “Is that—”

*No,* the voice said quickly. *Not yet.*

The plane flickered and dimmed, withdrawing as the Relay recalibrated. The hum dipped, rose, and settled into a new band.

Rachel felt the change as a tightening behind her sternum.

The path resumed.

She moved again, the system carrying her forward while finalizing whatever sequence it had begun. The corridor narrowed further, attention compressing around her like hands that didn’t touch but didn’t let go either.

The pressure shifted.

Not alignment.

Direction.

*The window is closing,* the voice said, low and urgent. *Whatever we do next has to fit inside their movement, not interrupt it.*

Rachel swallowed hard. “And if it doesn’t.”

There was a pause—shorter than the ones she was used to.

*Then they stop moving you like a person.*

The floor adjusted beneath her feet.

The hum deepened.

Rachel kept walking, because stopping was no longer one of the options the Relay was willing to support.
