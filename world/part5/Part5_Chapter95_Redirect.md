# Chapter 95 — Redirect

The Relay's response was immediate and overwhelming.

The space around Rachel reorganized in a single, decisive motion, pressure planes collapsing and reforming into a configuration designed for extraction rather than containment. The hum sharpened into a focused pulse that locked onto her chest and began to pull.

Not at her.

At what she was carrying.

Rachel screamed.

The sound came out wrong, flattened by the density of the field around her. Her body arched against the containment, spine bowing backward as the system tried to separate her from the binding by force.

*Hold,* the voice said, and it was inside her now, bracing against the pull the way a hand braces against a current. *Don't let them open it.*

"I'm trying—"

The pull intensified.

Rachel felt the binding stretch, seams she hadn't known existed straining under pressure that wanted them to give. The warmth at her scar became heat, then fire, then something beyond temperature—a sensation of being torn from the inside out.

And beneath the pain, she felt the other vessel.

Close now. So close.

The prepared receiver, waiting in its chamber, hollow and ready. The Relay had already begun the transfer sequence. All it needed was for the binding to fail.

*They're opening me,* the voice said, and there was raw fear in it now. *They're pulling me toward—*

"I know," Rachel gasped. "I can feel them."

The other vessel's emptiness pressed against her awareness like a vacuum, hungry for what she was carrying. It would be so easy. Let the binding tear. Let the demon go. Walk out of here human and hollowed and free.

The pull eased for a moment, as if the system were offering her one last chance to comply.

Rachel hung in the containment field, gasping, the binding stretched thin between her and the waiting receiver.

*I could go,* the voice said quietly. *It would be easier.*

Rachel's eyes stung. "Is that what you want."

*I want…*

The voice trailed off.

The pull resumed, gentler now, almost coaxing. The Relay reading her hesitation and adjusting its approach.

*I want to be what you made me,* the voice said finally. *Even if that's harder. Even if it hurts. I don't want to be passed to something that won't remember how we got here.*

Rachel's breath caught.

"Then stay," she said.

*I can't. Not like this. They're pulling too hard. The binding is going to—*

"Stay with me."

She said it out loud. Not a command. Not a plea.

A choice.

And then she pulled.

Not outward. Not against the Relay's force.

Inward.

She pulled the binding tight around herself, around the voice, around every piece of the demon that had been living inside her since the circle broke. She grabbed hold of the thing the Relay was trying to extract and refused to let it go.

The system screamed.

Not sound. Pressure. The containment field convulsed around her, constraints failing and reforming in rapid succession as the Relay tried to compensate for something it hadn't anticipated: the subject actively completing the binding instead of resisting extraction.

*What are you doing,* the voice said, and it sounded terrified and exhilarated at once.

"Choosing you," Rachel said through clenched teeth.

The warmth at her scar exploded into something brighter, hotter, more present than anything she'd felt since the summoning. Light flooded through her bones, her blood, her breath. Not leaking out. Sinking in.

The binding closed.

The voice stopped being beside her thoughts.

It became them.

Rachel felt the moment of fusion—not a click, not a seal, but a settling. Two currents finding the same channel. Two patterns aligning into one.

The pull toward the other vessel snapped.

The transfer failed.

Rachel collapsed inside the containment field, body shaking, light steady beneath her skin for the first time since she'd arrived. Not flickering. Not surging.

Whole.

The Relay recalculated.

The hum shifted to a lower frequency, confused, searching for a variance that no longer existed. The pressure around her chest eased, not in release but in bewilderment.

*We did it,* the voice said, and it was her voice now. Or she was its voice. The distinction had stopped mattering.

Rachel tried to laugh. It came out as a sob.

"Now we leave," she said.

The containment field tightened again, new constraints forming as the system realized what had happened.

But something had changed.

The pressure that settled around her shoulders felt different. Less certain. The binding was complete now, and complete things didn't fit the Relay's protocols the same way incomplete ones did.

*They're not going to let us walk out,* the voice said.

"I know," Rachel said.

She lifted her hand.

It moved.

The pressure resisted, but it moved.

Rachel smiled with a mouth that felt like hers and not quite hers at the same time.

"We're not going to ask."
