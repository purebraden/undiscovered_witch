# Chapter 77 — Drift

The ground didn’t stay with her.

Rachel took three steps into the new direction and the space slid sideways, not fast, not dramatic—just enough that the basin she’d been walking toward ended up slightly to her left instead of straight ahead. Her body corrected before her eyes caught up. The correction made her knee buckle.

She hissed and slowed, breathing through it. Pain climbed her leg in dull, stacked layers. The injury hadn’t worsened, but it hadn’t forgiven her either.

The air thickened as she descended, pressing lightly against her skin like humidity without heat. Each breath felt measured, not restricted, just… observed.

The light beneath her skin pulsed low and steady. Dim. Contained.

*Hold that,* the voice said.

Rachel nodded once and kept moving.

The basin wasn’t a depression so much as a hesitation in the terrain, a place where the ground had tried several shapes and stopped halfway through choosing. The slope leveled, then dipped again in shallow steps that didn’t quite repeat. The surface here was smoother, marked with faint overlapping tracks that didn’t line up, as if many different paths had been taken through the same place without ever agreeing on where they were.

Rachel angled toward the edge.

The pressure nudged her inward.

She stopped.

“Why,” she asked.

*Because this is where things slow down,* the voice said.

Her stomach tightened. “Slow is bad.”

*Slow is visible.*

She took a careful step inward anyway, testing. The ground didn’t resist. The pressure stayed neutral, watchful.

The basin smelled faintly metallic, like old rain on stone. The air carried a low vibration she felt more than heard, a steady undercurrent that made her teeth hum.

Something ahead shifted.

Rachel froze.

The shape resolved slowly, not appearing so much as finishing a decision. A figure stood near the center of the basin, back turned, posture wrong in a way that made her skin crawl. Too upright. Too still.

Rachel swallowed. “Is that—”

*Yes,* the voice said. *And no.*

The figure turned.

Its face didn’t settle right away. Features slid, aligning and misaligning in quick, subtle ways until they landed on something almost human. Almost.

Its eyes found Rachel immediately.

The pressure in her chest tightened—not a command. A warning.

The figure lifted one hand, palm out. A gesture that might have meant stop. Might have meant wait.

Rachel didn’t move.

The light beneath her skin flickered, then steadied as she held the shared intent in place. Not to hide. Not to attack.

Just to pass.

The figure tilted its head.

“You’re leaking,” it said.

The words weren’t in her language. She understood them anyway.

Rachel’s throat went dry. “I’m trying not to.”

The figure’s mouth twitched. “That won’t last.”

It took a step closer.

The basin shifted in response, the ground smoothing beneath the figure’s feet, tracks aligning to favor its approach.

Rachel’s pulse spiked. The light beneath her skin surged in response, weak but urgent.

*Easy,* the voice said.

Rachel forced her breathing to slow. The surge settled, leaving her dizzy but upright.

“Who are you,” she asked.

The figure laughed softly. The sound didn’t echo.

“Finished,” it said. “Wrong.”

Rachel’s stomach dropped.

Up close, the figure’s skin had a faint translucence, light bleeding through in uneven patches. Not glowing. Leaking. Its movements lagged half a beat behind its intent, limbs following decisions already made.

“You shouldn’t be here yet,” it continued. “You’re early.”

“That keeps happening,” Rachel said.

The figure studied her, eyes flicking to her leg, to the faint pulse under her skin. “Incomplete,” it said. “But not unheld.”

The pressure in Rachel’s chest tightened sharply.

“What happens if I stay,” she asked.

The figure smiled, teeth slightly misaligned. “You slow. Then you stop. Then they find you.”

“And if I go.”

The smile widened. “They still might.”

Rachel nodded once. “Helpful.”

The figure stepped aside, gesturing vaguely toward the far edge of the basin where the ground thinned into uncertain distance. “Paths don’t like to be used twice,” it said. “Pick one and don’t look back.”

Rachel hesitated.

The basin responded by deepening, the air growing heavier around her ankles, as if the space itself preferred decisions.

*We can’t linger,* the voice said. *Markers will converge.*

Rachel met the figure’s gaze. “Can you hide me.”

The figure considered this. Its face slid again, features rearranging subtly. “For a moment,” it said. “But it will cost you.”

Rachel snorted weakly. “Everything costs me.”

“Not like this,” the figure said. It leaned closer, voice dropping. “I can smear you. Make you harder to resolve. But you’ll feel it. And it won’t come off clean.”

Rachel’s hands shook. The light beneath her skin flickered uncertainly.

*It increases noise,* the voice said. *But lowers signal.*

Rachel closed her eyes for half a second.

“Do it,” she said.

The figure didn’t touch her.

The basin did.

The ground beneath Rachel’s feet softened abruptly, not sinking but blurring. Her sense of balance shattered as the space around her lost edges. Nausea slammed into her hard enough to steal her breath.

She gagged, doubling over, vision tunneling.

The light beneath her skin scattered, breaking into uneven pulses that crawled instead of flowed. Her thoughts fractured, overlapping, like several near-identical versions of herself were trying to move at once.

*Hold on,* the voice said, suddenly closer, tighter. *Don’t resist.*

“I’m not—” Rachel choked. “I’m not doing anything—”

*That’s the problem.*

The pressure wrapped around her, not constricting but stabilizing, pinning her intent in place while everything else slid.

The basin hummed louder. The figure stepped back, its outline blurring as well.

“There,” it said. “That’s as much as I can do without breaking you.”

The sensation eased slowly, leaving Rachel shaking and soaked in cold sweat. Her skin prickled. The light beneath it settled into a mottled dimness that made her feel… unfocused.

She straightened with effort. The world around her looked slightly wrong, like she was seeing it through imperfect glass.

“Thank you,” she said, surprised she meant it.

The figure shrugged. “Don’t thank me if you finish.”

It turned away, already losing definition, dissolving back into the basin as if it had never fully separated from it.

The pressure in Rachel’s chest pulled gently toward the thinning edge of the basin.

She limped forward, every step feeling half-committed, like the ground wasn’t sure she was really there.

Behind her, something arrived.

Not the marker.

Something broader. Heavier.

The basin shuddered. The faint tracks erased themselves, smoothing over as if wiped clean.

Rachel didn’t look back.

She pushed into the thinning space ahead, holding the shared intent tight as the world tried, once again, to decide what to do with her.
