# Chapter 76 — Between

Rachel stepped out of the hold and the world didn’t finish forming.

Her foot came down on something that tried to be ground and missed. It firmed under her weight a heartbeat late, just enough to keep her upright, and she windmilled once before catching herself. The delayed impact sent a sick roll through her stomach.

She bent forward, hands braced on her thighs, breath tearing out of her chest in short pulls that didn’t feel like they reached all the way in. The air wasn’t cold. It was thin. Like it had been stretched and hadn’t settled back into place.

When she straightened, the sky was wrong.

Flat and pale, bright enough to see by without any visible source. No sun. No stars. Just an even gray that made it impossible to judge distance or direction. The horizon blurred, not fogged exactly—more like it refused to commit to being far away.

Behind her, there was nothing.

No doorway. No seam. No place where the hold had been.

The pressure in her chest shifted. Alert. Awake.

*You’re out,* the voice said.

“I know,” Rachel said, because if she didn’t speak the silence pressed too close.

She took a careful step forward.

The ground responded late again, her foot landing just after she expected it to. Her stomach lurched. She stopped and waited until the nausea eased to a manageable churn.

The light beneath her skin flickered weakly, sluggish, like it had to push through resistance to get out. She lifted her hand in front of her face.

The glow followed a fraction too slow.

Rachel clenched her fingers. It dimmed again, heavy and reluctant.

The pressure in her chest tightened slightly.

*That space amplified you,* the voice said. *This one doesn’t.*

“Great,” Rachel muttered.

She started walking.

The terrain shifted under her as she moved, not changing shape so much as changing agreement. Slopes appeared where there hadn’t been any. What looked level dipped when she stepped on it. Her body adjusted without asking her permission, hips and shoulders compensating to keep her upright.

Something moved at the edge of her vision.

Rachel stopped.

Nothing there.

She scanned the open space anyway, heart beating too loud. The flat light made everything feel equally close and far at the same time.

*Keep moving,* the voice said.

“What was that,” she asked.

A pause.

*Attention.*

Her mouth went dry. “From what.”

Another pause. Longer.

*Markers.*

Her breath caught. “Already?”

*They were waiting.*

The light beneath her skin pulsed in response, thin and weak. Rachel slapped her hand down over her forearm like she could physically smother it.

The ground ahead rippled faintly, as if reacting to the attempt.

She froze until the flicker died.

“Sorry,” she whispered, to the space or to herself.

She moved again, angling slightly away from a shallow depression ahead where the ground looked smoother, worn by passage. The moment she shifted course, pain lanced behind her eyes and nausea surged hard enough to make her stagger.

She dropped to one knee, gagging, breath coming in sharp, panicked pulls.

“Okay,” she hissed. “Okay.”

She turned back toward the worn path.

The pressure eased.

Her stomach settled into a sour, lingering churn. Sweat slicked her palms. She pushed herself upright and limped forward, jaw clenched so hard it ached.

*It tracks response,* the voice said. *Not distance.*

“So every time I react—”

*It learns.*

Her heart sank. “Can we lose it.”

A beat.

*Yes.*

Relief spiked too fast. The light beneath her skin flared reflexively, brighter than it had been since she left the hold.

The space around her shuddered.

Rachel sucked in a sharp breath and held still. The flare faded without compressing anything, without erasing anything—but the ground remembered it. One of the faint striations deepened, a shallow groove forming as if the space had decided this was a better way to be crossed.

She swallowed hard.

*We need to move without flaring,* the voice said. *No compression. No erasure.*

“That’s all I know how to do,” Rachel said.

*You have legs.*

She barked a short, breathless laugh and kept moving.

The ground rose into low ridges that broke line of sight without enclosing it. Rachel angled toward one and crested it carefully, favoring her injured leg.

On the other side, something followed.

Not close.

Not far.

It didn’t rush. It didn’t close distance. It matched her pace exactly, staying just at the edge of perception where her eyes refused to focus.

Rachel’s hands shook. Every instinct screamed to turn and do something—anything—but the memory of the weak, sputtering flare held her in place.

“What happens if it catches up,” she asked.

*It doesn’t catch,* the voice said. *It reports.*

Her throat tightened. “To who.”

*Everyone.*

She pushed harder, pain spiking up her leg in sharp bursts. The thing behind her adjusted without effort, never gaining, never falling back.

She couldn’t outrun it.

The thought landed clean and ugly.

*You don’t need to,* the voice said. *You need to disappear.*

Rachel laughed, breathless and bitter. “I’m glowing.”

*Less than before.*

She glanced down at her arms as she ran. The light beneath her skin was still there, but dimmer, uneven, like it was being filtered through something thicker than flesh.

“How,” she asked.

The voice hesitated.

That alone made her chest tighten.

*You have to agree with me,* it said. *Visibility is the risk.*

“I don’t disagree.”

*Not intellectually. In intent.*

Rachel slowed despite herself. The presence behind her slowed too, perfectly matched.

“What do you want,” she asked.

*To pass unnoticed.*

She focused on the open space ahead. On the way the ground lagged when she stepped. On how the light bent subtly toward her when she moved too sharply.

“I don’t want to be seen,” she said.

The pressure in her chest shifted—not tightening, not easing, but aligning.

The light beneath her skin dimmed further.

Not gone. Not hidden. Just quieter.

The nausea receded, leaving behind a strange, hollow steadiness. The world around her blurred slightly, edges losing interest in her presence.

Behind her, the attention faltered.

Just a fraction.

Rachel didn’t stop. She angled off the worn path and cut across uneven ground that twisted underfoot. The pressure guided without forcing. When fear spiked and the light tried to surge, she held the shared intent in place and felt it settle again.

Her thoughts felt crowded.

Not loud. Just fuller. Like she was borrowing someone else’s way of looking at things and hadn’t returned it yet.

“You’re still there,” she said.

*Yes.*

No pressure. No correction. Just presence.

The thing behind her slowed.

Then stopped.

Rachel kept moving until her lungs burned and her leg threatened to give out. Only when the ground leveled into something that felt almost stable did she risk looking back.

Nothing followed.

She bent forward, hands on her thighs, breath ripping out of her chest in ragged pulls. Her skin prickled as the light beneath it flickered uncertainly, then steadied at its dim baseline.

“That worked,” she said.

*For now.*

The ground beneath her feet darkened slightly, registering her pause.

Ahead, the terrain dipped into a shallow basin where the air felt heavier, thicker. Light curved subtly toward its center, drawn by something she couldn’t see yet.

Rachel reached into her pocket without thinking and pulled out the folded paper Lena had pressed into her hand.

She opened it.

Blank.

Her stomach dropped.

The pressure in her chest shifted, alert and uneasy.

*We’re off the path,* the voice said.

Rachel folded the paper carefully and tucked it away. She looked ahead at the basin, at the way the space bent wrong around whatever waited there.

“Then we find another one,” she said.

The ground ahead shifted, committing to a direction that hadn’t existed a moment before.

Rachel stepped into it because standing still was worse.

Behind her, somewhere she could no longer see, something recalibrated and began looking again.
