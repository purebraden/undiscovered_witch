# Chapter 86 — Carry

Movement resumed without acceleration.

Rachel noticed because her body stopped preparing for it. The pressure in her chest no longer tightened ahead of each step. It didn’t anticipate imbalance or correct posture. It simply held her in a configuration that moved when the space moved.

Her feet lifted and set down with the same rhythm, but the choice had drained out of the action. Each step completed itself before she felt the intent to take it, motion arriving fully formed instead of being initiated.

The hum flattened.

Not quieter. Uniform.

Rachel’s breathing fell into it without permission, shallow and even, her lungs matching a pattern the air had already decided on. When she tried to inhale deeper, the expansion completed halfway and stopped, pressure redistributing until the breath resolved into something acceptable.

She exhaled.

The hum did not change.

She tried again, slower this time, deliberate.

Her lungs complied only as far as the system allowed.

Rachel swallowed and focused on her legs. She tightened her thighs, calves, ankles—anything that might reassert control over the movement.

Her feet continued their sequence.

The corridor—if it still qualified as one—had lost its edges. Surfaces blurred into planes of pressure that defined where her body could exist without offering anything to orient against. Light remained even and unhelpful, shadows too thin to anchor distance or speed.

Rachel lifted her hand.

It rose cleanly, no lag, joints aligning as pressure equalized around them. She rotated her wrist slowly, watching her fingers obey with unnerving precision, every motion completing itself without resistance or feedback beyond correctness.

She lowered it.

The motion finished before she felt done deciding.

*They’re no longer evaluating movement,* the voice said carefully. *You’re inside an accepted pattern.*

Rachel’s mouth went dry. “Accepted how.”

There was a pause, shorter than usual.

*As sufficient.*

Her stomach dropped.

She tried to slow.

Not a stop. Just a fractional hesitation, the kind that would have registered earlier as uncertainty.

Nothing responded.

Her feet continued to lift and land, body advancing through space that no longer recalculated around her presence. The pressure at her chest held steady, not guiding or correcting—maintaining a state she hadn’t chosen.

Panic rose anyway.

She forced herself to breathe against the rhythm, pushing air deeper, faster, slower—anything to desynchronize.

The hum flattened her attempts.

Her lungs complied.

Warmth spread from her scar, dull and constant, threading upward through her leg and settling low in her spine. The sensation wasn’t painful. It was stabilizing in a way that felt disturbingly supportive, as if her body were being rewarded for staying inside the pattern.

Rachel clenched her jaw.

The space ahead thickened.

Not into a barrier. Into a region where pressure layered itself gradually, weight added a fraction at a time. Her steps shortened as the space itself reduced its movement, carrying her forward more slowly without offering resistance to push against.

She drifted.

The sensation made her dizzy. There was no sense of crossing from one place to another, only of remaining aligned within a segment that continued to advance.

Rachel curled her fingers into fists.

She could still feel that.

She focused on the scrape beneath her boots, faint but present, counting the rhythm of contact and release.

One.

Two.

Three.

The count dissolved when the floor adjusted its timing without warning.

*We’re approaching a handoff,* the voice said, quieter now. *Once that completes, I won’t be able to buffer the adjustments the same way.*

“Buffer what,” Rachel asked, voice thin.

*The parts of you that don’t match their carry model.*

The pressure redistributed.

Not tightening.

Cycling.

The space around her narrowed, widened, then narrowed again in smooth intervals that mimicked breathing she wasn’t controlling. Frequencies layered within the hum until the air felt dense with intention, each oscillation settling deeper into her body.

Rachel’s vision tunneled.

She tried to shake her head.

The motion completed, but the pressure corrected it immediately, returning her posture to neutral before dizziness could resolve.

Her steps stopped.

Not because she halted.

Because the space stopped moving her forward.

The pressure held her upright, body supported without strain, limbs fixed in a posture that felt carefully selected.

The hum deepened.

The space ahead resolved further, alignment refining into something prepared to accept her current configuration without modification.

Rachel stood there, breath shallow, heart hammering, aware in a distant way that time was still passing even though nothing she did interacted with it anymore.

*This is as far as I can slow it,* the voice said.

The pressure shifted again.

Rachel felt herself begin to change position, not forward or downward, but into a tighter alignment that stripped away the last sensation of initiating motion.

The carry did not stop.

It simply continued without her.
