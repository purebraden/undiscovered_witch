# Chapter 78 — Where the Wrong Ones Live

The ground changed without warning.

One step carried Rachel forward. The next landed too soon, her foot striking stone that hadn’t been there a moment ago. She stumbled, caught herself, and froze as the space ahead settled into something that looked—dangerously—finished.

Buildings.

Not clean. Not planned. Shapes pressed up out of the ground like the terrain had tried to imitate memory instead of architecture. Walls leaned into one another at uneven angles. Rooflines sagged, bent, or stopped halfway through forming. Nothing repeated cleanly. Nothing aligned.

The air smelled lived in.

Smoke without fire. Old water. Metal that had been handled too much.

Rachel stopped just inside the boundary, heart hammering.

The light beneath her skin dimmed further on its own, not gone but muffled, like it was being wrapped in layers she hadn’t agreed to. The pressure in her chest tightened, alert and wary.

*This place persists,* the voice said.

“People,” Rachel whispered.

Figures moved between the structures. Slowly. Carefully. No one ran. No one hurried. Motion here looked negotiated, each step taken with caution, as if the ground might object if pushed too hard.

A man crossed an open stretch ahead, body angled wrong, shoulders pitched forward like he was always leaning into wind that wasn’t there. Light leaked from the corners of his eyes in thin, steady lines. He did not blink.

Another figure sat against a wall that curved inward to cradle her shape. Her legs ended too soon, cut off at the knee by stone that had grown up around her and never let go. She watched Rachel pass with flat, incurious eyes.

Rachel swallowed and kept moving.

The settlement didn’t announce itself. No gate. No border. Just a place where the Between had stopped rearranging long enough for people to decide to stay.

The ground here held. Mostly.

Her bad leg protested with every step. She leaned harder on the shared intent, on not being noticed, and felt the pressure respond by dulling edges—hers and the world’s.

A child darted across a narrow path ahead, too fast for the space to like. The ground rippled under him, delayed, and he stumbled. He caught himself on a wall that flexed to meet his hands.

He didn’t cry.

He looked up at Rachel instead.

Light leaked from his mouth when he smiled.

Rachel’s stomach clenched. She looked away first.

*Don’t stare,* the voice said. *Recognition sticks.*

“I wasn’t—” She stopped. Adjusted. “Okay.”

She followed a path that felt worn without looking worn, the stone smoothed by passage that never quite repeated the same route twice. The structures crowded closer here, their surfaces bearing marks that weren’t decorative—scratches, burns, compressions where space had failed and been forced back into shape.

Someone watched her from a doorway that wasn’t fully open. His head lagged behind the turn of his shoulders when he shifted to follow her movement. His eyes glowed too bright, the wrong color, pulsing out of sync with his breath.

“You won’t last,” he said mildly.

Rachel didn’t slow.

A woman stepped into her path farther on, blocking it without touching the ground. Her feet hovered an inch above the stone, unmoving. The air around her wavered faintly, like heat.

Rachel stopped short, pulse racing.

The woman’s face was smooth in a way that didn’t read as young. Finished. Her skin held a dull sheen, light trapped under it without release.

“You’re early,” the woman said.

“That keeps happening,” Rachel said.

The woman’s lips twitched. “It does.”

Her gaze dropped to Rachel’s leg, to the faint circular scar that pulsed low and steady. “Incomplete,” she said. “But you’re carrying it.”

The pressure in Rachel’s chest tightened sharply.

“I’m passing through,” Rachel said. “I won’t stay.”

The woman considered her for a long moment. The air thickened slightly between them.

“No one stays,” she said finally. “Some of us just stop leaving.”

She stepped aside.

Rachel moved past before she could think better of it.

The deeper she went, the more wrongness layered in. A man whose shadow moved a fraction too slow. A pair of people whose bodies didn’t agree on where one ended and the other began, their outlines overlapping and separating with each step. A woman frozen mid-step, one foot lifted, face calm, eyes open, the space around her holding her exactly as she was.

No one screamed.

No one begged.

Life here had learned its limits and stayed inside them.

Rachel’s chest ached. Her breathing went shallow without her noticing until the pressure nudged her to slow.

*This place feeds on comparison,* the voice said. *Keep moving.*

A low structure ahead exhaled smoke from a seam near its roof. The smell made Rachel’s stomach turn—burnt copper and something organic underneath. She skirted wide.

A figure detached from the shadow near its base and fell into step beside her.

Rachel flinched hard enough to make her knee buckle.

“Easy,” the man said. His voice was steady. Too steady. “I won’t touch.”

She didn’t answer. The pressure stayed taut, ready.

He walked with a careful hitch, one leg dragging slightly behind the other as if it belonged to a different decision. His face was ordinary until you looked too long. Then the proportions started to slip.

“You’re headed east,” he said.

Rachel kept walking.

“That’s where they watch hardest,” he continued. “Because it’s where everyone thinks the answer is.”

“What answer,” Rachel asked before she could stop herself.

He smiled faintly. “Completion.”

Her grip tightened on nothing.

“You shouldn’t say that word,” he added. “It draws attention.”

“Everything draws attention,” Rachel said.

“Yes,” he agreed. “But some things ring.”

They passed a cluster of structures grown together into a single mass, doors opening into one another without regard for interior space. Voices drifted out, overlapping, out of sync.

“You won’t finish like us,” the man said, not unkindly. “You’re still negotiating.”

Rachel glanced at him. “And you’re not.”

He shook his head once. The motion lagged, then caught up. “No. I chose early. I chose wrong.”

The pressure in Rachel’s chest tightened, not in warning but in something like acknowledgment.

“I didn’t choose,” she said.

His smile thinned. “Neither did I.”

They reached the far edge of the settlement where the ground began to lose interest again, paths unraveling into suggestion. The air thinned. The hum faded.

The man stopped.

“They won’t follow you past this,” he said. “Not yet.”

“Why,” Rachel asked.

“Because this place confuses their instruments,” he said. “Too much noise.”

Behind them, something heavy shifted. A ripple passed through the settlement, subtle but deep enough to make the structures creak.

The man stiffened. “That’s not them.”

Rachel’s heart kicked hard.

“What is it,” she asked.

He didn’t answer. He stepped back, melting into the crowd with practiced ease, his outline dissolving among others who had learned how to blur.

The pressure in Rachel’s chest yanked her forward.

*Move,* the voice said. *Now.*

She didn’t argue.

The ground ahead thinned, losing texture with every step, distance pulling apart as soon as she tried to measure it.

Behind her, the settlement shifted. Structures leaned inward. People adjusted without looking at one another. The wrong ones knew how to brace.

The pressure in Rachel’s chest tightened hard enough to make her stumble.

*Now,* the voice said.

She went.

The space behind her folded, not closing so much as refusing to hold shape anymore, and the sound of it came late—stone remembering itself after the fact.

Rachel didn’t slow.

The light under her skin pulsed once, weak and contained, and then the ground ahead stopped pretending to be familiar.
