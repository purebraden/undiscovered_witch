# Chapter 92 — Transfer

The movement began without warning.

Rachel felt it as a change in pressure before she felt motion, a subtle tilt in how force resolved along her spine. The holding volume did not loosen. It adjusted, redistributing support so smoothly her body stayed upright as the space itself shifted.

Her stomach lurched anyway.

The hum deepened, frequency settling lower in her chest, and the floor slid out from under her without the sensation of falling. There was no drop, no acceleration—just a quiet, unmistakable sense that the space she occupied was no longer where it had been.

Rachel’s breath caught.

The pressure corrected it.

Air moved in and out of her lungs at a pace that didn’t change even as her inner ear protested, balance recalibrated before vertigo could fully form. Her feet stayed planted relative to the holding volume, boots never lifting, never dragging.

She was being carried.

Not like before.

This time there was direction.

The surrounding space elongated, boundaries stretching ahead of her while remaining close at her sides. The vertical presence she’d felt earlier resolved into a corridor-shaped absence, not an opening but a path defined by where pressure allowed movement to occur.

Rachel swallowed hard.

“Hey,” she said, the word barely audible.

*I’m here,* the voice answered immediately, closer than it had been. *They’ve engaged movement.*

“Can you stop it.”

There was a pause. Short. Final.

*No.*

The holding volume accelerated—not quickly, but decisively. The sense of relocation sharpened as the hum layered with a secondary rhythm that pulsed through her ribs and down into her legs, synchronizing her body to the motion whether she wanted it or not.

Rachel’s scar flared, heat blooming outward in response to the change. The warmth steadied her, anchoring sensation enough to keep panic from overwhelming her breath.

The corridor narrowed.

Not physically. Functionally.

Pressure increased along her shoulders and hips, ensuring she stayed aligned as the space carried her forward. Her arms remained held at her sides, hands relaxed and useless.

She tried to look around.

Her head turned a fraction and stopped, movement completed where the system allowed it and no further. The view ahead remained indistinct, light flattening distance into something unreadable.

“How long,” Rachel asked.

*Until they’re done,* the voice said.

That wasn’t an answer.

The motion continued, smooth and relentless. There were no turns she could feel, no landmarks, only the steady translation of the holding volume through a larger structure that never revealed itself.

Rachel’s breathing stayed shallow and even, chest rising and falling within limits she hadn’t set. Her pulse thudded in her ears, loud against the quiet hum.

The pressure shifted again.

This time it redistributed lower, supporting her knees and calves more firmly, preparing her body for a change she could sense coming without understanding it.

Her stomach clenched.

*We’re almost there,* the voice said, and this time it sounded less certain than before.

The corridor ahead darkened, pressure thickening as the space slowed. The hum modulated upward, not louder but tighter, as if resolution were increasing.

Rachel felt the movement decelerate.

The holding volume settled.

Not stopping.

Arriving.

The pressure held her upright as the surrounding space finalized around a new configuration, one that felt enclosed in a way the previous corridor had not.

Rachel stood breathing inside it, arms heavy, feet planted, heart racing, aware that whatever had been carrying her had decided where she belonged next.

The hum continued.

And the space did not offer a way back.
