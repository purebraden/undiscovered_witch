# Chapter 98 — Vessel

The frame shattered.

Not violently—the light simply stopped holding together, its structure collapsing inward as Rachel's presence displaced the system's certainty about what it was supposed to contain.

The vessel dropped.

Rachel caught them before they hit the floor, arms wrapping around a body that weighed less than it should have. They were light in a way that went beyond physical mass—hollowed out, prepared, shaped by months or years of the Relay's attention into something ready to receive.

They hadn't received.

The chamber convulsed around them, pressure planes shattering and reforming in chaotic patterns. The hum climbed to a shriek, frequency spiking as the system lost coherence in the space where its primary solution had just failed.

The vessel clutched at Rachel's arms, fingers weak but desperate.

"You—" Their voice cracked, unused, barely a whisper. "You broke it."

"I broke me loose," Rachel said. "You're coming with."

She pulled them upright. Their legs buckled immediately. Rachel caught them again, taking their weight against her shoulder.

*We need to move,* the voice said. *The system is redirecting everything it has toward this location.*

"Can you walk," Rachel asked the vessel.

They shook their head, eyes wide and unfocused. "I don't—they kept me—I haven't moved in—"

"Okay," Rachel said. "Hold on."

She shifted her grip, pulling the vessel's arm across her shoulders, bracing them against her side. The position was awkward, their body still trying to fold into the posture the system had enforced.

The chamber's walls began to close.

Not physically—the space itself contracted, pressure planes stacking inward, trying to compress everything inside into a configuration that could be contained.

Rachel walked toward the wall anyway.

"What are you—" the vessel started.

The wall yielded.

Not an opening. Not a door. The pressure simply parted as Rachel pushed through, her binding displacing the system's constraints the way she'd displaced everything else since completion.

They emerged into a corridor that hadn't existed a moment before—raw space, barely formed, the Relay improvising around a gap it couldn't close fast enough.

Rachel ran.

The vessel stumbled beside her, their legs remembering motion in fits and starts. Rachel half-carried, half-dragged them through the collapsing infrastructure, pressure waves chasing them as the system tried to seal the breach.

*Left,* the voice said.

Rachel turned left.

*Down.*

She found stairs that materialized under her feet as she needed them, the Relay's architecture reshaping around her passage, trying to direct her somewhere controllable.

She ignored the directions and kept moving.

"Who are you," the vessel gasped.

"Rachel."

"How did you—"

"Later."

The corridor widened, then narrowed, then widened again. The system couldn't decide what configuration to enforce, its protocols conflicting as it tried to contain a completed binding carrying an unreceived vessel through infrastructure designed for neither.

Alarms didn't sound. Presences didn't appear. The Relay was too busy recalculating to mount a coordinated response.

Rachel pushed through a final threshold and felt the pressure change.

The air thinned. The hum faded. The constraints around them loosened into something that felt almost like natural space.

The Between.

Rachel stopped, breathing hard, the vessel sagging against her side.

Behind them, the Relay's architecture groaned and shifted, sealing the breach, consolidating around the damage they'd caused.

In front of them, the uncertain terrain stretched in all directions, neither welcoming nor hostile.

"What is this place," the vessel whispered.

"Between," Rachel said. "Between where we were and everywhere else."

The vessel looked at her, really looked at her for the first time. Their eyes were still hollow, still carrying the shape the Relay had prepared in them, but there was something in them now. Awareness. Fear. Hope.

"They were going to put something in me," they said.

Rachel nodded. "They were going to put what's in me into you."

"And you stopped them."

"I chose something else."

The vessel stared at her for a long moment. Their hand, still gripping Rachel's arm, tightened slightly.

"Thank you," they said.

Rachel adjusted her grip on them, taking more of their weight.

"Don't thank me yet," she said. "We still have to get somewhere."

*The Relay will send trackers,* the voice said. *We should move.*

Rachel nodded.

She looked out at the Between—its uncertain horizon, its unreliable ground, its absolute lack of anything resembling safety.

Then she started walking, carrying the vessel with her, leaving the Relay behind.

One step at a time.
