# Chapter 87 — Classification

The pressure changed before anything else did.

It didn’t increase. It differentiated.

Rachel felt it as a subtle redistribution across her body, points of contact resolving with new priority. Her shoulders were held more firmly than her hips. Her spine was supported in a way that encouraged stillness rather than balance. Her feet remained planted, but the pressure no longer cared whether they were aligned.

The hum layered again.

Not louder. More specific.

Rachel tried to flex her fingers.

They moved.

Not freely.

The motion completed with a precision that felt assessed rather than allowed, joints settling into positions that satisfied an external expectation before she finished intending them.

She let her hands fall.

The space around her held.

The corridor—if it still existed—had thinned into a holding volume that didn’t offer direction or distance. Light remained even, shadows too faint to suggest edges. The only reliable feedback came from pressure and the hum’s subtle modulation as something progressed.

Rachel swallowed. Her throat felt tight, dry.

*This is classification,* the voice said quietly. *Not correction. They’re not trying to change you yet.*

“Yet,” Rachel whispered.

*They’re deciding what you count as.*

The pressure along her spine shifted.

Not pain. Not restraint.

Alignment.

Her posture adjusted by degrees she couldn’t see, vertebrae settling into a configuration that felt chosen rather than comfortable. The warmth from her scar spread again, responding to the hum with a faint pulse that traveled upward and then stalled, as if encountering a boundary.

Rachel’s breath hitched.

She tried to inhale deeply.

The breath completed, but the pressure at her ribs guided expansion unevenly, favoring one side over the other until her lungs settled into a shape that wasn’t symmetrical.

Her vision blurred at the edges.

She forced herself to focus on something—anything—but there was nothing to anchor against. The space refused to present reference points.

“Can they see me,” Rachel asked.

*They see parameters,* the voice replied. *Not experience.*

The pressure changed again, concentrating briefly at her joints, then releasing in a pattern that reminded her of being weighed without a scale. The hum shifted with it, frequencies aligning and separating in quick, quiet adjustments.

Rachel’s muscles trembled.

She didn’t feel tired.

She felt sorted.

A thin band of light resolved in front of her, not sweeping or scanning, just appearing fully formed. It hovered at chest height, motionless.

Rachel stared at it.

Her scar flared hot.

The band flickered, then stabilized.

The pressure at her sternum tightened sharply, compressing the light beneath her skin until her breath stuttered. Sensation sharpened everywhere at once, nerves responding as if each point of contact had been queried simultaneously.

Rachel cried out.

The sound came out flat, absorbed before it could echo.

*Easy,* the voice said, close and strained. *They’re sampling interaction, not intent.*

“It hurts,” Rachel gasped.

*Pain is not a disqualifier.*

The band of light shifted, splitting into two narrower lines that slid apart and then vanished. In their absence, the pressure redistributed again, settling into a configuration that felt newly decided.

Rachel’s hands shook violently.

She couldn’t stop it.

The hum deepened, then flattened, as if a setting had been locked.

The pressure held.

Rachel waited for something to change.

Nothing did.

Her breathing remained shallow and even, lungs completing each cycle without resistance or relief. The warmth from her scar stayed constant, neither fading nor intensifying, as if it had been assigned a stable value.

She shifted her weight.

The movement completed, posture corrected before imbalance could register, leaving her standing exactly as she had been before, only more precisely.

The space did not respond.

The thin band of light did not return.

Time passed without markers.

*I’m still here,* the voice said quietly, the words tucked close to her awareness rather than spoken aloud. *They haven’t pushed me out. They’ve just stopped caring whether I move.*

Rachel swallowed, throat tight.

The pressure did not loosen.

The hum did not change.

She stood held in place by decisions she could feel but not locate, body supported, aligned, and accounted for, while the Relay continued to treat her as something stable enough to leave exactly where it was.
