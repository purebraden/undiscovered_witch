# Chapter 81 — Intake

The seam closed behind her without sound.

Not sealed. Not erased. Just no longer available.

Rachel stood still and waited for the floor to decide what it was. It didn’t lag. It didn’t soften. It held her weight immediately, without negotiation, without interest.

That alone made her chest tighten.

The air inside the Relay felt filtered. Not clean, exactly—processed. Each breath arrived already measured, already sorted, like it had been passed through something that knew what it was looking for.

Light came from nowhere she could point to. It was even, shadowless, and unhelpful in the way that made depth feel optional. The walls—if they were walls—didn’t curve or angle so much as assert presence. They were close enough to matter and far enough not to touch.

The hum persisted, lower now, embedded.

Rachel took one step forward.

Nothing resisted her.

She took another.

Still nothing.

Her leg ached in steady pulses, pain arriving on time, predictable. The scar warmed faintly, responding to the underlying frequency like it had been waiting for it.

*They’ve got you,* the voice said.

Rachel swallowed. “You sound impressed.”

*They’re thorough.*

Ahead, the space widened into a chamber that didn’t announce itself with edges. The floor sloped down just enough to guide her. The walls didn’t lean. They didn’t flex. They stayed where they were put.

At the center of the chamber, a structure rose out of the floor. Not a desk. Not an altar. A surface shaped for interaction, flat on top, faceted underneath, its geometry resolving cleanly no matter how she looked at it.

She stopped a few paces short.

The pressure in her chest shifted, not warning or command, but expectation.

The light beneath her skin compressed further, pressed so tight it felt like it might bruise.

The structure responded.

Thin lines of light traced themselves across its surface, not glowing so much as revealing paths that had always been there. The hum modulated, the frequency adjusting in small increments that made her teeth ache.

Rachel’s stomach rolled.

“This is intake,” she said.

The word felt wrong in her mouth. Too procedural. Too calm.

The air in front of her thickened.

A presence resolved across from her, not stepping into the room so much as finishing an alignment she hadn’t noticed begin. Its shape held human proportions without committing to detail. The face stayed indistinct, features suggested rather than fixed.

It didn’t look at her.

It looked through her.

Rachel fought the instinct to flare and held still.

“Rachel,” the presence said.

Hearing her name inside the Relay made her pulse spike.

“Yes,” she said.

The presence didn’t respond to the answer.

Light swept over her in a narrow band, passing from her feet upward in a smooth, uninterrupted motion. Where it touched, sensation sharpened—not pain, not pressure, just clarity taken too far.

Rachel’s scar burned.

She gasped and staggered a half step forward before she caught herself.

The floor didn’t shift.

“Binding incomplete,” the presence said.

The words weren’t spoken so much as placed.

Rachel’s jaw tightened. “I know.”

Another sweep of light. Slower this time. It lingered at her chest, at the compressed glow beneath her skin.

“External alignment detected,” the presence continued. “Nonstandard.”

The pressure in Rachel’s chest tightened hard.

*Careful,* the voice said.

Rachel said nothing.

The presence tilted its head a fraction. The motion lagged just enough to feel intentional.

“Consent pathway unresolved,” it said.

The structure behind it brightened slightly, lines of light rearranging themselves.

Rachel’s mouth went dry.

“What happens now,” she asked.

The presence paused. Not for effect. For calculation.

“Stabilization,” it said. “Classification. Correction.”

Her stomach dropped.

“I didn’t ask for correction,” Rachel said.

The presence didn’t look at her.

“Request noted,” it said.

The hum deepened.

The floor beneath Rachel’s feet vibrated faintly, a precise oscillation that traveled up her bones and settled in her chest. The light beneath her skin stirred, straining against compression.

Rachel clenched her fists. “I’m not broken.”

The presence turned its head toward her then.

Not sharply. Not aggressively.

Just enough to register.

“Variance exceeds tolerance,” it said.

The words landed heavier than they should have.

Behind the presence, the structure extended a fraction, its surface reconfiguring into something narrower, more specific. Not a table. Not restraints.

A place to be put.

The pressure in Rachel’s chest tightened into something that felt dangerously close to a shove.

*Not yet,* the voice said. *Hold.*

Rachel forced herself to breathe, slow and shallow, keeping the light compressed, refusing to let it answer the pull of the hum.

“I’m not staying,” she said.

The presence’s face shifted, features sliding closer to resolution.

“Everyone stays,” it said.

The chamber adjusted around them, space tightening not by closing in but by committing. The walls felt closer without moving. The air grew denser, less forgiving.

Rachel’s vision tunneled.

The seam behind her did not reopen.

The structure waited.

And somewhere deeper in the Relay, something finished preparing for her.
