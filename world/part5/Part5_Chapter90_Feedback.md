# Chapter 90 — Feedback (Patched)

The interface did not react right away.

Rachel’s hand hovered inches from the surface, fingers spread, palm warm and faintly trembling. The pressure in her chest held steady, bracing without warning, as if the system had already decided that whatever happened next would need containment more than permission.

The hum remained even.

Rachel lowered her hand.

This time, when her palm touched the surface, the response lagged.

Not long. Just long enough to notice.

Pressure gathered at her sternum before the interface acknowledged contact, light compressing beneath her skin and then spreading outward in a shallow pulse that did not match the surface’s internal structure.

Rachel gasped.

The interface reacted late.

Internal patterns flared, then tightened abruptly, light pulling inward instead of expanding. The pressure spiked, dense and exact, locking her knees and snapping her posture upright before she could sway.

*Pull back,* the voice said, sharp. *Now.*

Rachel tried.

Her hand lifted, but the sensation didn’t release cleanly. The pressure clung for a fraction longer than it should have, as if the system had committed to a response before deciding it didn’t like the outcome.

The hum wavered.

Not fractured. Corrected.

Rachel’s scar flared hot, heat radiating outward in a wave that filled spaces the carry had left hollow. The warmth surged up her spine and across her ribs, sharp enough to steal her breath.

She cried out.

The interface dimmed hard.

Not retreating—constricting.

Its internal structures collapsed inward, light narrowing into a shallow band that no longer mirrored her hand. The surface stayed in place, but its responsiveness vanished, pressure locking down as if interaction had been downgraded rather than interrupted.

Rachel staggered. The system corrected her balance immediately, harsher this time, posture forced back into alignment without delay.

Her breath came in short, broken pulls.

The hum deepened, settling into a heavier frequency that pressed behind her eyes.

*That was too much,* the voice said, low and urgent. *They won’t let that happen again.*

“What did I do,” Rachel whispered.

*You crossed a line,* the voice said. *They noticed.*

The interface pulsed once.

Not a check.

A boundary being reinforced.

Pressure gathered at Rachel’s chest again, stronger than before, compressing the light beneath her skin until she felt the familiar warning tightness that meant containment was being prioritized over access.

She froze.

The interface dimmed further, its internal patterns rearranging into something simpler, flatter, less willing. Whatever depth it had briefly offered was gone.

The space around her tightened in response, boundaries clarifying as the Relay reduced tolerance across the holding volume. The pressure no longer felt provisional.

It felt fixed.

Rachel lowered her arm. This time, the system did not resist or respond. Her hand dropped to her side as if the interface were no longer meant for her to use at all.

The tingling in her fingers lingered.

*They’re done letting you touch it,* the voice said. *That means the next thing won’t involve your hands.*

Rachel swallowed, throat raw.

The interface remained in front of her, dim and inert.

The pressure held.

Nothing else opened.

Rachel stood inside the narrowed space, arm heavy at her side, aware only that whatever came next would not require her hands.
