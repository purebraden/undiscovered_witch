# Chapter 97 — Break

The presences arrived without warning.

One moment Rachel was pushing through the thickening corridor, pressure bending around her passage. The next, shapes resolved ahead of her, blocking the way. Not walls. Not barriers.

Handlers.

There were three of them, human-shaped in the way the intake presences had been—outlines that suggested features without committing to them, attention that weighed heavy against her skin. They stood spaced evenly across the corridor, their postures identical, their stillness coordinated.

Rachel stopped.

*They're anchored to the system,* the voice said. *Direct extensions of the Relay's will.*

"Can I go through them."

*Unknown.*

The presences didn't move. They didn't speak. They simply existed in her path, pressure concentrating around them like gravity around mass.

Rachel took a step forward.

The nearest presence raised one hand.

Pain lanced through her skull, sharp and immediate, the system using the handlers as focal points for correction. Her knees buckled. She caught herself against a pressure plane that materialized just long enough to support her weight.

*They're using concentrated protocols,* the voice said urgently. *Compliance enforcement at close range.*

Rachel gasped, vision blurring. "Suggestions?"

*Don't let them touch you.*

The handlers advanced in unison, each step synchronized, pressure building ahead of them in a wave that pushed against Rachel's chest. She stumbled backward, the corridor behind her narrowing, exits closing.

*We have to move,* the voice said. *Now.*

Rachel planted her feet.

The pressure hit her like a wall—not physical force, but constraint, her body being told to stop, to yield, to accept correction. Her muscles locked. Her breath shortened. The light beneath her skin compressed under the weight of the system's attention.

But it didn't go out.

Rachel focused on that light. On the binding that was hers now, not the Relay's. On the completion that meant she wasn't a variable to be corrected anymore.

She pushed back.

Not with force. With presence.

The light beneath her skin didn't flare outward. It expanded, filling the space she occupied, pressing against the constraints the way water presses against a container. Not breaking. Displacing.

The pressure wave faltered.

The handlers hesitated.

Rachel took a step forward.

*Again,* the voice said, and she felt it pushing with her, their combined intent focused on the same point.

She took another step.

The handlers' coordination broke. One adjusted its position a fraction too late. Another's outline flickered as the system struggled to maintain focus against interference it didn't understand.

Rachel walked through the gap.

The nearest handler reached for her.

Its hand passed through the air where her shoulder should have been and found nothing. Not absence—displacement. She was already a step past where it expected her to be, the binding adjusting her position in ways that didn't register on the system's sensors.

*You're learning to use it,* the voice observed, something like wonder threading through the thought.

"I'm learning to not be where they expect," Rachel corrected.

The handlers regrouped behind her, pressure building for another attempt. The corridor ahead remained thick with constraint, the Relay mobilizing everything it had to stop her progress.

Rachel kept moving.

She didn't try to overpower the system. She didn't try to destroy it. She simply refused to be held, refused to be corrected, refused to accept that the space around her had any right to tell her where she could and couldn't go.

The binding made it possible.

Completion made it true.

The corridor widened ahead. Rachel felt the vessel's presence growing stronger—still held, still waiting, close enough now that she could almost sense their shape.

The handlers pursued, but they couldn't catch her. The system adjusted, but it couldn't contain her. The pressure tried to redirect, but she pushed through it like walking against wind.

*Almost there,* the voice said.

The corridor opened into a chamber.

Rachel stopped at the threshold, breath catching.

The vessel was inside. A figure held in a frame of light, suspended the way she'd seen through the interface—young, still, eyes open and empty. The chamber hummed with a frequency designed to keep them ready, to keep them waiting, to keep the hollow space in their chest prepared for what had never arrived.

Rachel stepped inside.

The chamber's defenses responded immediately, pressure slamming down around her, trying to force her to the floor.

She didn't yield.

The light beneath her skin spread through her muscles, her bones, her stance. She stood upright against the weight of the system's full attention, completed binding blazing steady in her chest.

The vessel's eyes tracked toward her. Empty, but aware. Terrified.

"I'm getting you out," Rachel said.

The Relay screamed.

Not sound. Pressure. The entire chamber constricting, trying to crush her before she could interfere with its carefully prepared solution.

Rachel reached for the frame that held the vessel.

Her hand passed through the light.

And the Relay learned what a completed binding could do when it chose to break something instead of refusing to be held.
