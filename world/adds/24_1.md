# 24_1

They left the tray just inside the boundary.

Rachel watched it sit there, steam lifting faintly before vanishing into the room’s stale air. The pressure didn’t react. That felt deliberate.

A woman lingered longer than the others usually did. Not close. Close enough to be noticeable.

“It’s plain,” the woman said, then grimaced like she hadn’t meant to say anything at all.

Rachel nodded. The motion made the pressure stir.

“I won’t touch you,” the woman added quickly. Her hands folded into her sleeves like that had been a learned motion, not a courtesy.

The pressure leaned. Curious.

Silence stretched. It wasn’t empty. It was crowded with all the things neither of them was supposed to say.

“My sister,” the woman said, and stopped. Swallowed. Tried again. “She used to get sick when rooms were wrong. Not like this. Just—wrong.”

Rachel’s throat tightened.

The pressure spiked.

She gagged, the word she’d been about to say collapsing inward like it had hit a wall.

The woman flinched back. “I’m sorry. I shouldn’t—”

The floor creaked.

Not loud. Close.

The woman froze, eyes wide, breath too shallow to count as breathing.

Rachel shook her head hard, pain flashing behind her eyes. “Stop. Please.”

The pressure slammed down, heavy and furious, pinning her there, punishing the space between them.

The woman backed away in small steps, hands raised, mouth moving without sound.

The room relaxed as distance returned.

Rachel slid down to the floor, shaking, lungs burning.

The tray sat untouched, cooling where it had been left.

When the woman was gone, the pressure eased just enough for Rachel to breathe again.

It hadn’t reacted to the food.

It had reacted to recognition.
