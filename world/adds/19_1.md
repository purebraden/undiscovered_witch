# 19_1

The line on the floor didn’t move.

Rachel noticed because she was waiting for it to, because waiting had become a kind of stance she kept falling into without choosing. The pressure hovered in her chest, not leaning yet, not satisfied either, like something with its weight on the balls of its feet.

She stayed where she was.

Someone shifted behind her. A chair leg scraped. The sound felt too loud, but nothing reacted. The light held. The seams stayed closed.

“Don’t,” a man said quietly, and Rachel wasn’t sure who he was talking to until she felt the pressure twitch at the word and she locked up harder, breath snagging halfway in.

“I didn’t,” she said, then stopped talking because talking made the pressure feel like it was looking at her mouth.

The man exhaled. Paper rasped. Something was being written down, but she didn’t hear the stylus move. She felt it instead, the way the room seemed to register the act of recording even if it didn’t care about the content.

“Just—stay,” the man said. Not instruction. Observation. Like he was naming a condition already in place.

Rachel stayed.

The pressure eased a fraction. Not relief. Alignment. Like something heavy had stopped trying to decide where to settle.

Her shoulders burned. Her legs shook. She let them.

Across the room, someone crouched near the markings, head tilted like they were listening for something under the stone. They didn’t touch. No one touched anymore.

“If she anticipates,” the man said, and stopped there.

The pressure leaned.

Rachel clenched her jaw and let her thoughts scatter, anything but the next second, anything but preparing.

The light dimmed slightly, as if the room had exhaled.

Rachel did too.

The pressure surged at once, offended, snapping back into place, and the floor creaked under her boots.

“Don’t,” the man said again, sharper now.

Rachel swallowed the breath back down and stared at the line until her vision blurred.

The line held.
