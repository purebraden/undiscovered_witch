# Chapter 32

The shift change wasn’t announced.

Rachel only noticed because Lena moved—not away, not forward, just a subtle rebalancing, shoulders rolling back like she was preparing to step aside and didn’t like that she had to.

The pressure stirred at the adjustment, attention sharpening, then holding.

The door opened.

A woman stepped in, younger than Lena, hair pulled back too tight, eyes already moving before she’d fully crossed the threshold. She didn’t stop to take the room in. She skimmed it instead, fast and practiced, like she was checking whether something had drifted while no one was looking.

“Mara Kline,” she said. “Escort. Third rotation.”

Lena nodded and shifted half a step back. “She’s been here since last shift,” she said. “They told me to watch her.”

Mara’s eyes flicked to Rachel, then slid past her, already mapping space. “Managed?”

“That’s the word they used,” Lena said, not quite flat, not quite neutral.

Mara exhaled softly, calculation replacing whatever she’d expected to feel. “Okay.”

The pressure leaned toward her, curious, testing the shape of this new attention.

Mara moved farther into the room, stopping well short of Rachel. Her focus went to the furniture instead, the seams in the floor, the careful alignment of the chair that felt a little too careful now that Rachel knew how easily it could be wrong.

“Anything off?” Mara asked.

Lena hesitated, then shrugged slightly. “The chair wasn’t right. I fixed it.”

Mara nodded and crouched, palm flattening briefly against the floor seam near the chair leg. “Looks settled.”

Rachel’s stomach tightened.

Mara straightened and glanced at Rachel, her expression controlled but not unkind. “I’m not here to poke at you,” she said. “I’m here to keep things from escalating.”

The pressure shifted, unsettled by the word.

Mara reached for the chair.

“Don’t,” Lena said, sharp before she could soften it.

Mara nudged it anyway.

Just an inch.

The chair slid.

Rachel gasped before she could stop herself.

The pressure surged, offended and bright.

The chair slid again, slower this time, as if deciding how much it wanted to be seen.

Mara froze with her hand still out. “That shouldn’t—”

The chair creaked.

Mara stepped back, breath shallow now. “Okay,” she said, more carefully. “That’s not how these rooms usually respond.”

Rachel stayed seated. Stayed still. Let the pressure crowd her ribs without giving it anything to take.

“I didn’t touch her,” Mara said, glancing at Lena. “I didn’t go near her.”

“I know,” Lena said, voice low.

The pressure pressed closer, intent.

Mara looked from the chair to the seams to the wall plate, like she was triangulating a problem she didn’t have language for yet. “This isn’t about her moving,” she said. “It’s the space.”

The chair creaked again.

All three of them went still.

The pressure leaned forward.

Rachel’s hands clenched against her thighs, nails biting through fabric.

Mara straightened slowly. “Okay,” she said again. “I’m not calling this in yet.”

Lena shook her head once. “If you say it wrong, they’ll hear something else.”

The pressure stilled, listening.

Mara lowered herself into a crouch again, careful now, keeping her hands visible. “Rachel,” she said, “I need to ask you something.”

Rachel swallowed. “What?”

Mara hesitated, the pause stretching just long enough to feel deliberate. “When the room changes,” she said, “does it feel like it’s reacting to you—or like it’s reacting to us?”

The chair settled.

Not straight.

Not moving.

Waiting.

Rachel breathed once, slow. “I don’t know yet.”

Mara nodded. “That’s fair.”

Lena stared at the chair like it had crossed a line she couldn’t name.

The room stayed still.

But it didn’t feel finished.

Rachel sat very carefully and felt something shift—not power, not certainty.

Allowance.

And somewhere close, the pressure leaned in, attentive to what she might do next.
