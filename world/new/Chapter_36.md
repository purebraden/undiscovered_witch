# Chapter 36

The room stayed as it was, stone and seam and the chair too carefully aligned, the light holding steady in that flattened way that made depth feel optional. Mara remained near the wall, shoulders still squared toward the door that hadn’t reopened, attention drifting and snapping back like she didn’t trust it to stay anywhere too long.

The pressure stayed close, not pressing, not pulling, just occupying the space breathing needed.

The wall plate lit without a click, a soft glow that didn’t throw much shadow, and Mara turned toward it a fraction late, her mouth opening before she seemed sure what she was going to say. “Yes.”

“We are initiating a stabilizing protocol,” the voice said, even and distant, as if it were reading from a line that didn’t care who stood on the other side of it. “Remain in place.”

Rachel stayed seated.

The chair didn’t creak, and the absence sat there, unhelpful.

A hum built inside the walls, deeper than the earlier vibration and steadier, like something heavy had been engaged and told not to move, and the light cooled by a shade, flattening the room further so the corners felt less like corners and more like suggestions. Mara shifted her weight and stopped herself mid-step, one hand lifting and dropping again, the motion losing its purpose halfway through.

“Secondary presence removed,” the voice continued. “Environmental variance within acceptable—”

The pressure crowded closer.

Rachel’s breath finished and the air waited a beat too long before it pressed back, sliding into place like it had arrived late and hoped no one noticed.

Something unfolded overhead.

Not fast, not dramatic—panels and thin struts lowering with practiced slowness, stopping short of the chair, arranging themselves around it without touching, each segment locking into place with a soft mechanical confidence that didn’t match the room at all. The hum deepened as the structure settled into its own geometry.

Mara stared. “That isn’t—”

“Do not interfere,” the voice said.

The structure pulsed once, and the pressure shifted sideways, not away, not gone, just redirected. Rachel’s hands tightened against her thighs as the air thickened, movement through it taking more effort than it should have, and the chair answered with a faint vibration, not sliding, only responding.

The structure pulsed again.

Sound arrived late. Light followed after. The pressure came last, denser now, like it had been forced through something narrow and didn’t appreciate it.

“Stabilization relies on static reference,” the voice said, layered now, attention split. “Movement is not required.”

Mara’s back touched the wall before she seemed aware of stepping. “She isn’t static,” she said, and the sentence came out thin, like she knew it wouldn’t land cleanly.

A pause.

Long enough to stretch wrong.

“Monitoring will correct,” the voice said.

The structure brightened by a fraction, the hum sharpening, and the chair creaked—small, soft, insistent. The structure responded immediately, panels tightening, timing clean and exact, while the room lagged behind it, sound slipping first, light following, the pressure arriving late again, heavier, compressed.

Mara’s breath hitched. “This isn’t—”

The structure pulsed a third time.

The hum spiked.

Then stalled, like it had hit resistance it hadn’t been designed to name, and the light flickered in that in-between way that wasn’t failure but wasn’t steadiness either.

Mara said Rachel’s name, too quiet to matter.

The room dragged against the imposed shape and didn’t release, timing stretching and scraping, the structure shuddering and holding and adjusting as if it were learning the room’s rules by being forced to keep contact with them.

Rachel stayed where she was, breath shallow, hands flat, the pressure tight against her ribs as the space around her negotiated itself out of step with the thing meant to hold it, neither one yielding, neither one breaking.

Nothing snapped.

Nothing settled.

The room kept negotiating.
