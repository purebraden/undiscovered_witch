# Chapter 31

Lena noticed the chair first.

Not because it moved.

Because it didn’t belong where it was.

She didn’t say anything right away. She shifted her weight near the door, eyes moving from Rachel’s hands to the chair and back again, then to the floor seams, like she was checking herself before correcting someone else.

Rachel watched her notice.

The pressure stayed close, calm, already settled into the shape of this moment.

“Did you…” Lena started, then stopped. Cleared her throat. “Did you move?”

Rachel shook her head. “No.”

Lena frowned slightly and stepped farther into the room. She still didn’t look straight at the chair. She looked at the bed, the wall, the seams in the floor, as if the answer might be hiding there instead.

“It’s not where it was,” she said.

Rachel nodded.

The pressure pressed in, faintly pleased.

Lena crouched and ran two fingers along the floor seam, stopping where the chair leg sat just off the line. She hesitated, then nudged the chair back into alignment with a careful push.

It slid.

Too easily.

Rachel’s breath caught before she could stop it.

Lena froze with her hand still on the chair. She lifted it slowly and wiped her fingers on her pants, like she’d touched something she didn’t want to name.

“Did you feel anything?” she asked, not looking at Rachel.

“Yes,” Rachel said.

Lena straightened and stepped back toward the wall by the door. A small metal plate was set into the stone there, flush enough to miss unless you knew it was there. She pressed her thumb to it and leaned in slightly, shoulders tight.

“Quarters check,” she said. “Furniture alignment discrepancy.”

The wall clicked softly.

A pause stretched just long enough to feel deliberate.

“Discrepancy?” a voice said, thin and flattened by stone.

“The chair,” Lena said. “It was off.”

Another pause. Longer this time.

“Is it off now?”

Lena glanced at it. Perfectly squared. Innocent. “No.”

“Then correct and monitor,” the voice said. “Log if repeated.”

The plate went dark.

Lena stepped back, arms folding too tight across her chest.

“They’ll note it,” she said.

Rachel swallowed. “They didn’t ask how.”

Lena’s jaw set. “They don’t.”

The pressure shifted, subtle and content, like it had learned a new rule.

They stayed like that for a while. Rachel seated. Lena upright. The chair between them, aligned now, pretending.

Rachel’s chest tightened. “If it happens again—”

“We’ll log it,” Lena said quickly.

“That’s not what I meant.”

Lena closed her eyes for a beat, then opened them again. “Rachel. Listen.”

Rachel waited.

“If you tell them you moved it without moving,” Lena said carefully, “they won’t hear what you mean. They’ll hear something else.”

Rachel nodded. She could feel the pressure listening too.

“So what do I do?” she asked.

Lena hesitated. Her eyes went to the chair again, then away. “You wait,” she said. “You tell me. And you don’t… show them.”

Rachel nodded.

The chair creaked.

Soft. Almost nothing.

Both of them froze.

The sound faded before either of them moved.

Lena stared at the chair.

It didn’t move.

The pressure leaned forward.

Rachel stayed still.

Lena let out a breath she’d been holding and pressed her palm briefly against the wall plate again, like she needed the contact. “That didn’t happen,” she said.

Rachel closed her eyes.

The pressure settled, satisfied.

Lena stepped back into position near the door. “Shift ends soon,” she said, not looking at Rachel. “We’ll get you reassigned. New quarters, maybe.”

Rachel opened her eyes. “And if it keeps happening?”

Lena didn’t answer at first.

When she did, her voice was quiet. “Then we find out what they’re actually managing.”

The chair stayed where it was.

The room didn’t.

Rachel waited.

The pressure waited with her.

Not patient.

Learning.
