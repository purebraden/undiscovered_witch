# Chapter 29

They didn’t use doors.

That was the second thing Rachel noticed, right after the way everyone waited before moving.

The seam opened the same way it had before—stone folding inward, patient and reluctant—but this time the older woman didn’t touch it until Lena and Rachel were standing exactly where she wanted them. The gray-haired man stayed back with Miri. He kept his eyes down. Rachel felt the pressure lean toward him once, curious, and then withdraw.

“Slow,” the woman said.

Rachel nodded.

She stood when she was told to stand. The chair scraped louder than it should have. She stopped halfway up, knees bent awkwardly, heart racing, waiting for the pressure to surge.

It didn’t.

It adjusted, sliding into the new angle like it had expected this all along.

They went through one at a time.

On the other side, the corridor was wider and cleaner than the one they’d come from. The stone had been cut more carefully here, edges straight, the floor smoothed to the point of slipperiness. Chalk marks ran along the walls at shoulder height—broken lines, arrows, symbols layered over one another where someone had changed their mind and never fully erased the old one.

“Don’t touch the walls,” the woman said without looking back.

Rachel hadn’t meant to.

The pressure leaned toward the markings anyway, tugging lightly at her attention, the way an eye keeps catching on a word you’re trying not to read. She fixed her gaze on Lena’s shoulder instead, the crooked seam of her jacket, the faint stain near the cuff.

They walked.

Not fast. Not slow.

Just fast enough that stopping would have been noticeable.

The corridor sloped downward. The lighting shifted by degrees, bulbs spaced farther apart, leaving longer pockets of dimness between them. Each time they passed one, the light behind them softened, not off, just less—like it was losing interest.

Rachel counted steps without deciding to.

Lena’s breathing stayed steady but shallow, the sound brushing Rachel’s ear every time the pressure shifted.

“You okay?” Lena murmured, barely moving her mouth.

Rachel nodded. Then hesitated. Then nodded again.

The pressure twitched, responsive to the indecision.

They passed another seam. This one stayed closed, but Rachel felt something brush her awareness as they went by, the sensation of being catalogued without being addressed.

Her throat tightened.

At a junction where three corridors met, the older woman stopped so abruptly Rachel nearly ran into her back. Lena caught herself at the last second, boots squeaking faintly on the smooth stone.

“Left,” the woman said.

Lena hesitated just long enough to matter.

“Left,” the woman repeated.

They turned.

The air changed. Cooler. Drier. It smelled faintly of metal filings and old paper. The chalk markings thickened here, layered so heavily in places the wall looked scabbed with corrections.

Rachel’s foot slid on the polished floor. She corrected without thinking, weight shifting sharply to compensate.

The pressure snapped toward the movement.

“Careful,” the woman said, sharp.

Rachel froze mid-step, balance off, breath locked in her chest. Her calf burned. Lena reached out instinctively and stopped herself inches from Rachel’s arm, fingers curling back on themselves.

“I’m fine,” Rachel said too quickly.

The pressure leaned forward at the lie, pressing close enough to make her skin itch.

They moved again.

The corridor narrowed, then widened into a long rectangular hall lined with doors. Real doors this time—metal-faced, edges scarred from use. Each had a small placard mounted beside it. Most were blank. A few weren’t.

ARCHIVE. INTAKE. HOLD.

Rachel’s gaze snagged on the last one. The pressure did too, lingering there like it was tasting the word.

INTAKE snagged too, for a different reason. Intake was what you called it when you didn’t want to say summoned, when you didn’t want to say dragged.

The older woman stopped in front of a door without a label. She unlocked it with a key that looked worn thin, the grooves shallow from use.

Inside, the room felt finished in a way the others hadn’t. Smooth floor. Even lighting. A narrow bed bolted to the wall. A chair placed precisely across from it, legs squared to the lines in the stone.

Rachel’s stomach tightened.

“Temporary quarters,” the woman said.

The pressure pressed close, almost eager, then settled when Rachel didn’t react.

“You’ll stay here until shift,” the woman continued. “No wandering. No testing. No adjustments.”

Rachel nodded.

Lena stood in the doorway, not stepping in, not stepping back.

“And you,” the woman said, turning to her, “don’t leave.”

Lena stiffened. “I thought I was escort.”

“You are,” the woman said. “Escorts don’t leave.”

That landed harder than Rachel expected.

Rachel stepped into the room.

The pressure followed her in and then slowed, circling, like it was reading the boundaries. The doorframe creaked softly as she passed through.

The woman pulled the door mostly closed. Not latched. Not open. A deliberate gap.

“If something changes,” she said, eyes on Rachel through it, “you tell us.”

Rachel nodded.

“And if you feel the urge to move,” the woman added, “you wait.”

Rachel swallowed. “How long?”

The woman tilted her head, listening to something Rachel couldn’t hear. “Until it passes,” she said.

The door clicked shut.

Lena exhaled and leaned her forehead against the metal for a second before straightening.

Rachel sat on the bed slowly, testing the give, the resistance. The pressure settled around her ribs, contained but alert, like it had been assigned a seat.

They hadn’t chased her here.

They hadn’t dragged her.

They’d placed her.

Rachel stared at the blank wall and waited for the urge to move.

It didn’t come right away.

But she could feel it gathering, patient, learning the room.
