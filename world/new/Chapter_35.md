# Chapter 35

The chair stayed where it was, legs aligned with the seam that had been wrong earlier, the light overhead holding steady in that slightly too-even way that made it feel watched rather than lit.

Lena stood beside the bed, close enough that Rachel could see the tension traveling through her hands as her fingers flexed and stopped, flexed again, like the signal to do something had arrived without instructions attached. Mara stayed back near the wall, weight shifting and settling, her attention sliding between Lena and the door without ever landing long enough to feel useful.

The pressure stayed close, not heavy, not active, just present enough that breathing had to bend around it.

The wall plate clicked.

Mara turned toward it, then hesitated, as if the space between her and the stone mattered. “Yes,” she said after a beat that stretched thin.

The voice on the other side didn’t adjust for the delay. “Proceed. Secondary escort reassigned.”

Proceed meant they’d decided the room could keep its damage as long as it stayed in the right place.

The plate went dark again.

Lena didn’t move, and for a moment it wasn’t clear whether the words had reached her at all, because her mouth opened and closed without sound, her head tilting slightly like she was trying to catch something that had already passed.

“No,” she said finally, but the word landed wrong, detached from anything it could anchor to.

The chair creaked, soft but insistent, and all three of them went still without meaning to, waiting for the room to decide whether that counted.

Nothing followed.

The pressure leaned closer, not reacting so much as paying attention.

Footsteps sounded outside the door, more than one set, their timing off just enough to scrape. The lock released with that same softened sound, and the door slid open only partway, letting a hard stripe of corridor light cut across the floor and stop short of Rachel’s feet.

A man stood in the doorway, younger than the others had been, face already settled into something neutral and practiced. He looked straight at Lena.

“Come on,” he said.

Lena stared at him, her shoulders shifting before her feet did, then stopping, then trying again like her body was running slightly behind whatever decision she hadn’t finished making.

Mara took a step forward. “She—”

The man lifted a hand without looking at her, not aggressive, just final. “Now.”

Lena stepped back instead of forward, the movement small but unmistakable, and the pressure tightened in response, the air feeling suddenly closer to Rachel’s ribs. The light flickered once and steadied again.

Lena froze.

Her eyes moved, not to the man, but across the room — Rachel’s hands pressed flat against her thighs, the chair that hadn’t moved when it should have, the seam in the floor that still felt wrong even aligned.

Rachel didn’t speak. Didn’t move.

The pressure waited.

“Lena,” Mara said, and the name cracked, just slightly, before she could pull it back.

Lena lifted one hand halfway, fingers spread as if she meant to touch something and couldn’t remember what it was. The gesture stalled, hanging there without completing itself.

The man shifted his weight.

That was enough.

Lena stepped forward, the motion sudden and uneven, and the pressure surged in response — not loud, not fast, just exact. The room didn’t answer. No slide, no lurch, no sound at all, which felt worse than anything that had happened before.

She reached the doorway and hesitated, just long enough for the moment to drag, for something to pull against the timing and refuse to let go.

The pressure leaned in.

Lena didn’t turn around. Her hand dropped. She stepped out.

The door slid shut behind her, cutting off the corridor light and leaving the room exactly as it had been.

Mara stood where she was, staring at the closed door like it might reopen and undo the last few seconds.

It didn’t.

Rachel stayed seated, the pressure easing back into place, not gone, not satisfied — settled, like something had been removed that the room no longer needed to account for.
