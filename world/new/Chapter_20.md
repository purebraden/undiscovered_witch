# Chapter 20

The gap wasn’t a tunnel so much as a mistake the wall hadn’t corrected yet.

Stone bowed inward, then out again, its surface uneven in a way Rachel couldn’t keep her eyes on for long. If she stared, her vision slipped; if she didn’t, the sense of movement stayed anyway, like the place was remembering how it should have been shaped.

Lena went first, not pausing, not checking.

Rachel followed and felt the pressure crowd her chest, heavy and unhelpful, not guiding her so much as waiting to see what she’d do wrong. The air on the other side smelled scraped clean—dry metal, old heat, something burned out and never replaced.

Crates filled the room, stacked with too much care for a place this temporary. Chalk lines crossed the floor in shapes that almost closed, their edges blurred where boots had scuffed them away. Rachel stepped over one without thinking and felt the pressure tighten, sharp and irritated, like she’d kicked a live wire.

Someone shifted in the dark.

A woman stepped forward, short and thick through the shoulders, gloves darkened at the fingertips. She raised a handheld scanner, its casing cracked, its buzz uneven and insect-thin.

She looked at Lena.

Then at Rachel.

The scanner screamed.

“Fuck,” the woman said, already moving back.

Lena snapped, “Cut it.”

The woman didn’t argue. She smashed the scanner against the wall until it went silent, the sound echoing too long, too clean.

“You didn’t say it was like this,” she said, eyes still on Rachel. “You said injured. You said manageable.”

Rachel felt her leg pulse under her skin and hated that her body had learned to announce itself.

“I said she moves,” Lena replied. “That’s all.”

The woman let out a short laugh that held no humor. “She doesn’t move. She ruins.”

The floor shifted under Rachel’s feet, not enough to throw her, just enough to make her stomach flip. One of the chalk lines cracked and burned away to bare stone.

The hum wasn’t there yet, but the room felt thinner, like it had been marked.

Lights dimmed, brightened, settled wrong.

“We won’t stay,” Rachel said, her voice louder than she meant it to be.

“You won’t,” the woman said. “I won’t either if you leave right now.”

Lena grabbed Rachel’s arm and pulled her toward the far wall, boots scraping as the floor pitched again, a fraction steeper this time.

“No,” the woman said. “You’re burning my node.”

The pressure swelled without direction, dense and clumsy. A crate slid an inch across the floor and bumped another with a hollow sound.

“Stop,” Lena said, not looking at Rachel.

Rachel tried to still herself and failed. The pressure didn’t care about intent.

“You know what happens next,” the woman said. “You know how fast they trace this.”

“I know,” Lena said, too quickly.

The hum arrived like the start of a migraine, low and present, close enough to feel in Rachel’s teeth.

The woman moved fast, ripping open a crate and shoving a bundle into Lena’s hands. “Route three is dead. Don’t touch doors. If the walls start sweating, you run.”

“We always run,” Lena said.

The woman’s gaze slid back to Rachel. “Not fast enough.”

The crate beside Rachel split with a sharp crack, metal spilling across the floor.

The woman bolted.

Lights snapped off as she ran, plunging the room into uneven emergency glow. Somewhere deeper, a door sealed hard enough to shake dust loose from the ceiling.

Lena dragged Rachel into the dark and didn’t slow when Rachel stumbled.

They ran through a passage that narrowed too quickly, Rachel clipping her shoulder hard enough to tear skin. The walls pulsed faintly as they passed, heat bleeding through stone like a fever coming on.

The pressure crowded her forward, dragging her through turns she hadn’t seen yet, leaving her breathless and angry and unable to tell which part of her was driving.

The hum thickened.

Rachel’s leg burned, then didn’t. The pain didn’t leave; it slid somewhere duller, less urgent, and she hated the quiet more than the ache.

They burst into another chamber.

Smaller. Cleaner. A man waited there with one hand on a lever set into the wall, eyes already glassy like he’d been listening too long.

He looked at Lena, then at Rachel, and shook his head once.

“No.”

Lena didn’t slow. “Open it.”

“You’re too loud,” he said, voice thin.

The pressure spread, sloppy and wide, brushing the room. The lever rattled in its housing as the wall behind him bowed inward, stone groaning.

“I can’t,” he said. “If I do, they follow me.”

“They follow you anyway,” Lena said. “She’s here.”

Rachel took a step forward without meaning to, and the pressure surged with her, uncontrolled. A seam in the ceiling lit up.

The man’s mouth worked. He swallowed.

“Vent,” he said. “Don’t touch the floor plates.”

He yanked the lever.

A panel shrieked open and heat rolled out, dry and biting. Lena shoved Rachel toward it without slowing.

Rachel dropped to her hands and knees and crawled, metal scraping skin, the panel slamming shut behind them with a vibration she felt in her bones.

The hum filled the chamber outside.

Rachel felt it through the vent, not sound so much as resonance, tapping at the inside of her skull. The pressure coiled, disorganized and angry, as they crawled until the vent gave way and Rachel spilled into a narrow shaft lined with old insulation.

She landed wrong and waited for her leg to fail.

It didn’t.

Her hands stopped shaking faster than they should have. Her breathing evened before she was ready for it.

Lena watched her closely. “It’s faster.”

Rachel nodded. “It’s spreading.”

Lena didn’t argue.

They moved again, following scratches carved into the walls by different hands, different tools, some old, some fresh, all pointing deeper.

“Why did she run?” Rachel asked.

Lena hesitated just long enough to matter. “Because you don’t pass clean. No one does after you.”

The hum echoed above them, closer now.

Rachel stopped. “You knew.”

“I know how long places last,” Lena said, meeting her eyes.

The pressure crowded her chest, sharp and demanding.

“Where does this go?” Rachel asked.

“Another handoff,” Lena said. “If they’ll take us.”

“And if they won’t?”

Lena looked away. “Then we keep breaking things.”

Ahead, the wall shimmered faintly. Rachel’s palm tingled before she touched it, like the stone was already aware of her.

The pressure surged forward.

Not waiting.

Not asking.
