# Chapter 24

They didn’t find the place.

The place took them.

The corridor looked like all the others until the floor changed under Rachel’s boots—not a step or a slope, just stone worn smoother in a strip wide enough to notice too late. Lena slowed without deciding to. Rachel didn’t and nearly ran into her, breath catching hard as she checked herself.

“There,” Lena said, and it wasn’t relief.

Rachel looked for a door and didn’t see one. Just a seam in the wall that didn’t line up with anything around it, stone patched wrong, like a repair that had been rushed and then forgotten. A chalk line ran down the seam and stopped at ankle height, the mark broken off mid-stroke.

Lena lifted her hand and let it hover.

The pressure crowded Rachel’s ribs, tight and restless, then slid away from the seam as if it had brushed something unpleasant.

Lena tapped the stone once.

Nothing.

She tapped again, harder this time, knuckles whitening.

A small click answered from inside the wall, neat and final, like the end of a mechanism. The seam folded inward with stubborn reluctance. Warm air spilled out—soap, old coffee, something chemical underneath that burned faintly at the back of Rachel’s throat.

Lena went in.

Rachel followed and felt the pressure recoil, then tighten again a half-second later, undecided.

The seam closed behind them with a quiet drag of stone that made Rachel flinch anyway.

The room was low-ceilinged and badly lit, bare bulbs hanging too close together, throwing overlapping shadows that never quite settled. Tables lined the walls. Crates stacked in corners. A kettle hissed softly on a hot plate, steam curling up and vanishing before it reached the ceiling. Someone had tried very hard to make it ordinary.

Three people looked up.

A gray-haired man sat at a table with both hands around a mug he wasn’t drinking, shoulders hunched as if he were cold.

A young woman stood near the far wall with a toolbox at her feet, sleeves rolled up, eyes too bright, like she’d been awake longer than she should have been.

An older woman stood closest to the seam, small and wiry, already turning her body so she wasn’t directly in line with the door. She didn’t block it. She listened to it, palm lifted toward the stone without touching.

Her gaze moved from Lena to Rachel and stayed there.

“Route?” she asked.

Lena opened her mouth. Closed it again.

“Name,” the woman said.

“Lena.”

“And you.”

Rachel swallowed. The pressure crowded her chest.

“Rachel.”

The woman nodded once, then pointed with two fingers. “Sit.”

The chair was near the wall, away from the table, away from the seam.

Rachel moved toward it carefully. One of the bulbs flickered, steadied, then flickered again.

She sat. The chair scraped. She shifted it a fraction to stop the sound.

The scrape got louder, sharper, like it was arguing.

Rachel froze.

“Don’t,” the woman said, and this time it wasn’t gentle.

Rachel lifted her hands slowly, palms open, heat prickling across her skin. “I’m not—”

“You’re not the one who decides when things stop,” the woman said, eyes never leaving Rachel’s chest.

The gray-haired man finally drank from his mug. His hand shook. He kept the mug close to his mouth to hide it.

Lena leaned against the wall near the seam, breathing hard, eyes tracking the ceiling braces, the bulbs, the cracks between them.

“What’s on you?” the woman asked.

Rachel blinked. “On me?”

The woman’s gaze didn’t move. “That.”

“I don’t know,” Rachel said.

The woman nodded, like that answer had weight. “Good.”

She turned her head. “Miri.”

The young woman dropped to her knees by the wall and pulled a panel free. Inside, wires were bundled too neatly, taped down in strips. Her hands shook as she worked. She didn’t look at Rachel.

“What is this place?” Rachel asked, her voice sounding too loud in the low room.

“A stop,” the woman said. “Sit still.”

Miri’s screwdriver slipped. Sparks jumped. One bulb dimmed. Another flickered.

Miri hissed and jerked her hand back, then forced it in again, jaw clenched.

“It’s not the wire,” the gray-haired man said without looking up.

The woman watched Rachel. “When you crossed the seam, it pulled.”

Rachel’s skin prickled. “Pulled what?”

The woman didn’t answer.

Silence settled in layers. Not calm. Listening.

The kettle clicked off.

The pressure crowded Rachel’s ribs, restless, like it wanted to surge and couldn’t find room.

The woman stepped back a pace. Then another, careful not to turn her back.

“We don’t touch you,” she said. “We don’t stand close. We don’t close you into smaller spaces than we have to.”

Lena let out a short breath that might have been a laugh. “Safehouse.”

The word sat badly in the room.

“Drink,” the woman said, pointing to a bottle on the table. “Slow.”

Rachel leaned forward in pieces. The pressure pressed close, impatient. Her fingers closed around the bottle.

The table shuddered.

The gray-haired man hissed as his mug tipped, coffee spilling across the surface and dripping onto the floor. He yanked his hands back and stared at the mess like it had accused him of something.

Rachel unscrewed the cap and drank. The water tasted faintly of plastic and something medicinal.

Her heartbeat slowed too fast.

The room didn’t.

Miri shoved the panel closed and sat back on her heels, breathing hard, sweat beading along her hairline.

The woman pressed her palm to the seam again and stayed there longer this time, eyes closed.

“Is it close?” the man asked.

The woman didn’t answer at first. Then, quietly, “It’s here.”

Rachel’s stomach dropped.

“But not inside,” the woman added, and that wasn’t comfort. It was accounting.

The bulbs flickered in a wave down the line.

Miri made a sound that might have been a laugh and might have been a sob. “You brought it right to my hands.”

Lena turned toward Rachel. “We can’t stay.”

“You can’t leave yet,” the woman said.

Lena’s jaw tightened. “We don’t have a choice.”

The woman looked at Rachel. “You do.”

Rachel tightened her grip on the bottle. The plastic creaked and sprang back.

The pressure crowded her ribs and stayed there.

Not moving her.

Not leaving.

Just waiting.
