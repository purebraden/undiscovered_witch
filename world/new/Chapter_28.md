# Chapter 28

They didn’t hurry.

That was the first thing Rachel noticed.

Miri had been moved again, farther from the center of the room, mat slid inch by inch until the older woman was satisfied with the distance. No one spoke while it happened. The gray-haired man kept his hands folded, eyes fixed on the floor. Lena hovered near the seam, starting forward once and stopping herself when the chalk dust shifted under her boot.

The pressure stayed close to Rachel’s ribs, calm now, almost polite. It shifted when people moved, like it was tracking turn order.

The older woman took a clipboard from a crate Rachel hadn’t noticed before. The metal corner was bent, the paper clipped to it yellowed and soft from handling. She didn’t sit. She didn’t lean.

She stood where she could see everyone.

“State your name again,” she said.

Rachel blinked. “You know it.”

“Yes,” the woman said. “Say it anyway.”

“Rachel.”

The pen scratched. The sound cut through the room sharper than it should have. Rachel’s shoulders tightened before she caught it. The pressure twitched at the noise, then settled, disinterested.

“Origin,” the woman said.

Rachel hesitated. “I don’t know.”

The pen paused. Then continued.

“Method of arrival.”

“I woke up.”

The woman nodded as if she’d expected that. The pressure drifted a fraction closer, curious.

“Agreement.”

“No.” Rachel swallowed. “I never agreed to anything.”

The pen lifted. “Confirmed?”

Rachel shook her head.

The woman wrote smaller this time, letters pressed tight together.

“Duration of exposure.”

“I don’t know,” Rachel said. “Time’s been—”

“Unreliable,” the woman finished, already writing it down.

Lena shifted her weight. “What is this?”

The older woman didn’t look up. “Classification.”

“She just got here.”

“Yes,” the woman said. “That’s why we’re doing it now.”

Rachel adjusted her posture without thinking. The chair creaked.

She froze.

The pressure leaned in, attentive.

“Don’t,” the woman said, not unkindly.

Rachel nodded and forced the tension back into her shoulders, breath shallow.

The pen moved again.

“Observed effects,” the woman said, eyes lifting now. “Environmental destabilization. Non-consensual redirection. Secondary injury.”

Rachel flinched. “I didn’t—”

“I know,” the woman said. “That doesn’t change the line item.”

Miri made a small sound. Not pain. Something thinner. The gray-haired man shifted closer to her mat, stopping just short, his shadow stretching across her legs.

Another line appeared on the page.

“Response to intervention,” the woman continued. “Escalatory.”

Lena’s voice cracked. “You’re writing her like a weapon.”

The woman finally looked at her. “I’m writing her like a variable.”

Rachel felt something cold open under her ribs that had nothing to do with the room.

The pressure stayed close, intent on the exchange.

“Current status,” the woman said, pen hovering. “Unstable. Self-aware.”

She underlined the second word.

Rachel’s stomach dipped.

“What does that mean?”

The woman capped the pen. The click echoed. “It means you noticed you could choose.”

Silence spread unevenly, catching on edges.

Lena shook her head. “She didn’t hurt Miri on purpose.”

“No,” the woman said. “She hurt Miri deliberately.”

Rachel’s mouth opened. Closed.

“That’s not—” Lena started.

The woman cut her off. “Intent applied to test outcome. Outcome confirmed. That’s deliberation.”

The pressure leaned forward, faintly pleased, like a concept had resolved.

“What happens now?” Rachel asked.

The woman flipped the clipboard around.

Rachel didn’t lean forward. She didn’t stand. She just looked.

Most of the page was boxes. Symbols. Notes crossed out and rewritten. At the bottom, stamped heavier than the rest, was a single word.

**MANAGED**

The woman took the clipboard back.

“You don’t move without escort,” she said. “You don’t attempt redirection. You don’t test thresholds.”

Rachel nodded once.

“And,” the woman added, “you don’t leave Railroad supervision.”

Lena stepped forward. “You can’t keep her.”

The woman looked tired now. “We’re not keeping her. We’re keeping everyone else.”

Rachel’s hands curled slightly against her thighs. The pressure stirred in response, then settled again, indulgent.

“Who escorts her?” Lena asked.

The woman didn’t hesitate. “You.”

Lena froze. “Me?”

“You brought her in,” the woman said. “You know her rhythms. And”—her eyes flicked to Rachel—“she stabilizes when you’re present.”

Rachel hadn’t noticed that.

Now she did.

The pressure softened a fraction when Lena stood closer.

“And if I say no?” Lena asked.

The woman met her gaze. “Then we assign someone who doesn’t care if you survive.”

That sat.

Lena stepped back beside Rachel, jaw tight.

The woman clipped the clipboard back onto the crate. “We move at shift,” she said. “You’ll have quarters. Temporary.”

Rachel nodded. “Where?”

The woman hesitated. Just long enough to matter.

“Down,” she said.

The pressure leaned forward at the word.

Rachel stayed seated.

For the first time since she’d woken up in this world, the danger wasn’t chasing her.

It was filing her.

And it wasn’t done yet.
