# Chapter 30

Nothing happened.

Rachel sat on the edge of the bed with her hands where she’d put them, fingers loose, palms warm against her thighs. Lena stood near the door, weight set back on one heel, eyes fixed on Rachel’s hands the way she’d been taught to watch—waiting for movement, not listening for it.

The room held steady. The light didn’t flicker. The thin line under the door didn’t change.

Rachel breathed in shallow, let it out slower.

The pressure stayed close. Not heavy. Not urgent. Just there, a presence pressed up behind her ribs like someone standing too near in a line.

She adjusted her breathing again.

The pressure followed, late.

Rachel frowned and stopped counting. Counting made it feel like there was a right answer, and she had learned the hard way this place didn’t care what felt right.

The chair across from the bed hadn’t moved. Its legs were still squared to the seams in the floor. One of them sat just a fraction closer to a hairline crack in the stone than the others, the way things did when they’d been placed carefully by someone who didn’t trust symmetry.

Lena didn’t look at it.

Rachel kept her eyes on the wall instead.

Back home, a chair was a chair. It didn’t have rules. It didn’t have consequences.

The smell in the room sharpened when she inhaled too deeply—metal, cleaner, something dry and old underneath. She eased her breath back again and let her shoulders sink a fraction.

The pressure shifted.

Rachel felt it before she saw anything change, a tug at the edge of her attention that made her jaw tighten. She didn’t look at the chair. She didn’t move.

Her fingers twitched anyway.

Lena’s gaze snapped down at the twitch, sharp and immediate, then held.

Rachel pressed her fingers flat and stared harder at the discoloration on the wall, the faint outline where something had once been mounted and removed without patching the stone properly.

The chair leg scraped.

Not loud.

Just enough.

Rachel’s heart jumped.

Lena didn’t react, but Rachel saw her swallow.

The pressure crowded closer, curious now.

Rachel swallowed.

She stayed seated.

The chair creaked.

The sound settled into her teeth, a vibration she felt more than heard. Her breath caught and she forced it out slowly, careful not to change her posture.

The pressure leaned forward.

Rachel’s hands lifted a fraction off her thighs before she noticed. She shoved them back down, nails digging into fabric.

Lena shifted her weight in response, attention tightening on Rachel, not the room.

“You don’t talk to it,” Lena said, voice rough and quiet. “You don’t ask it what it wants. You don’t bargain. You just—” She stopped, like the rest of the sentence had teeth.

The chair tilted.

Not falling. Just testing, one leg lifting and setting back down again like something had put weight on it and changed its mind.

Rachel’s pulse spiked.

She stayed still.

The light flickered once and steadied.

Lena glanced up at the bulb, then back to Rachel.

Rachel closed her eyes.

She focused on the feel of the bed under her, the edge pressing into the backs of her legs, the way the floor stayed cool through her boots.

The chair slid.

Slow.

Deliberate.

Not far. Just enough that the alignment was wrong now, one leg no longer matching the seams in the stone.

Rachel gasped and bit it back.

The pressure surged, tight and offended, then eased again, satisfied.

The chair stopped.

Silence snapped into place around it.

Rachel opened her eyes.

The room looked the same.

The chair didn’t.

Lena was still watching Rachel.

Rachel waited.

Nothing else moved.

Her breath came fast and shallow, chest tight, ribs aching from holding still too long. She let it slow a fraction at a time, afraid of what would happen if she let it slow all the way.

The pressure stayed close, quieter now, like it had settled into something.

Rachel stared at the chair.

She didn’t reach for it.

She didn’t speak.

She didn’t stand.

She stayed where she was while the room stayed wrong.

Lena didn’t say anything.

Something clicked in the wall beside the door—soft, administrative—and the thin line of light under the door widened by a hair.

Lena’s head came up fast. “No,” she said, and the word came out like a reflex.

The pressure pressed in and stayed.
