# Chapter 40

The structure stayed where it was.

Panels held their distance from the chair, neither tightening nor easing, the hum flattened into a narrow band that didn’t scrape anymore, didn’t hunt for correction. It ignored the faint vibration under the chair and the small changes in Rachel’s breath, as if it had already decided what counted.

Rachel stayed seated.

Her breath moved in and out without catching, shallow but even, the air no longer pushing back halfway through. The pressure stayed close to her ribs, dense and quiet, no longer compressing inward, just occupying the space like it had found somewhere to rest.

Mara watched it and didn’t trust it.

Her eyes moved from Rachel’s shoulders to the panels and back again, waiting for the response that didn’t come, for the correction she’d learned to expect. She shifted her weight once and stopped herself, heel lifting and settling again like the floor might object.

The wall plate lit.

The glow spread wider than before, flattening the stone around it.

“Baseline achieved,” the voice said.

The hum didn’t change.

Rachel stayed still.

Mara let out a breath she hadn’t meant to release and caught herself before stepping forward, hands curling and uncurling at her sides, proximity suddenly feeling like a thing she could misuse.

The structure didn’t react.

The light flickered.

Not sharp. Not failing. Just long enough to register.

A sound followed from behind the wall, a low click that didn’t line up with anything Mara had just done.

Her head came up. “What was that.”

The wall plate stayed lit.

“Environmental correction underway,” the voice said, attention already angled elsewhere.

Rachel’s breath stayed even.

The pressure stayed quiet.

The structure held.

Mara pushed off the wall.

Not fast. Not urgent. Just a step, weight shifting the way it always had, her foot moving forward before she thought about it.

Her foot landed before the floor did.

The impact shot up her leg sharp and wrong, her knee buckling as the stone arrived a fraction late and caught her unevenly. She gasped, the sound tearing out of her before the breath that should have made it, and grabbed for the wall.

Her hand hit nothing.

Then stone snapped into place under her palm, hard enough to sting, the surface arriving late and unapologetic, like it had waited to see where she’d reach.

The room lurched.

Not collapsing. Not dramatic. Just enough.

Mara swore and dragged in a breath, balance off by inches that didn’t exist before, her body scrambling to catch up to a space that refused to agree with it. Her knee throbbed, heat blooming where the timing had gone wrong.

The structure didn’t move.

Rachel stayed seated.

The pressure stayed close to Rachel’s ribs, unchanged.

Mara straightened slowly, one hand still flat against the wall, fingers spread like she didn’t trust it to stay there. “That wasn’t her,” she said, louder this time, and the words came out wrong, consonants landing before the sound had finished forming.

The wall plate brightened by a degree.

“No subject variance detected,” the voice said.

The light flickered again.

Longer.

The hum stayed flat.

Something deeper in the wall slid, a soft scrape that didn’t belong to any mechanism she could picture.

Rachel’s breath stayed even.

Mara took another step without meaning to, testing the floor this time, careful and slow.

The structure didn’t react.

The pressure didn’t surge.

The room hesitated.

Her foot settled, and the sound followed late, a dull thud arriving after the weight had already committed. She froze, heart hammering now, the timing wrong enough to feel like it might keep getting worse.

“Open the door,” she said.

The wall plate stayed lit.

“Stand by,” the voice said.

Time slipped.

Not forward.

The hum stayed flat.

The sound of Mara’s breath reached the room before her chest finished rising.
