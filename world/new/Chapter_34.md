# Chapter 34

They came for the room.

Rachel understood that before anyone crossed the threshold.

The lock released with a soft sound that didn’t belong to it, followed by a pause long enough to stretch wrong. Light spilled in through the gap, hard and unfamiliar, cutting across the floor at an angle the room hadn’t used before. Voices stacked there—too many, not raised, just layered, instructions colliding before they decided who was meant to hear them.

The pressure tightened at Rachel’s ribs. Not alarmed. Sharper. Like it had felt a change in posture.

Mara stepped back without meaning to, palms lifting, body giving ground she hadn’t planned to give. Lena shifted toward the wall, eyes tracking the doorway instead of Rachel for the first time since the chair had moved.

“Hold,” someone said.

“No, we’re already past that,” someone else answered.

Equipment passed through the opening—edges of metal, reflective surfaces, things with weight and intent. The floor answered with a faint hum, heavier than before, a vibration that didn’t ask permission.

Rachel stayed seated.

A man entered first. Older than Mara, hair clipped close, expression already worn thin. He didn’t look at Rachel. He went straight to the wall and pressed his palm flat against the stone, eyes closing like he was listening for something he already expected to hear.

“Desync,” he said.

The word sat there, heavy and administrative.

The pressure leaned toward him, curious, then slid away again.

Another figure followed, carrying something flat and rectangular that hummed softly, its surface pulsing a fraction out of time with the lights. She moved around the room in a slow arc, frowning when the readings refused to line up cleanly.

“This should have triggered containment,” she said.

Mara opened her mouth.

The man lifted a hand without turning. “Later.”

Rachel felt the timing slip again. Her breath ended before the room noticed. The pressure answered late, then crowded closer, compensating.

The woman with the device circled the chair without touching it. The hum deepened when she passed too close. She adjusted her grip and tried again from farther back.

“It’s anchored wrong,” she said.

“It wasn’t touched,” Mara said, too fast.

The man opened his eyes and finally looked at Rachel.

Not at her face.

At the space around her.

The pressure stiffened.

“Of course it wasn’t,” he said. “She’s not the actuator.”

The words landed sideways.

Lena made a sharp sound under her breath and stopped it before it became anything else.

The man turned back to the wall. “The room’s responding to attention,” he said, already moving past the thought. “That’s why it lagged.” He glanced over his shoulder. “Did you log?”

Mara hesitated.

That was enough.

“Right,” the man said. “Of course.”

The pressure thickened, pleased with the friction.

The woman with the device stepped closer, emboldened now. The hum spiked. The light flickered—not enough to fail, just enough to scrape.

Rachel gasped.

“Don’t,” Lena said, too loud.

Everything stopped.

The device whined, its surface flashing erratically. The floor vibrated—not strong enough to shift anything loose, just enough to feel wrong in the bones.

The man swore softly. “Back up.”

They did. Not together. Not cleanly.

The pressure surged, bright with the confusion.

The chair slid.

Not far.

Enough.

Rachel’s hands pressed hard against her thighs. Breath locked in her chest. She didn’t reach. She didn’t choose.

The room lurched out of time and snapped back again.

The man swore again, louder. “We’re moving her.”

“No,” Lena said.

The word cut across the room, sharp and wrong.

The man looked at her like he’d just realized she was there. “You don’t get a say.”

“She’s not the problem,” Lena said. “You’re crowding it.”

The pressure pressed closer, tight and alert.

The woman with the device shook her head. “If she stays, the room won’t settle.”

“If we move her,” Mara said, voice thin, “we take the lag with us.”

Silence fell unevenly.

The pressure waited.

The man decided without looking at Rachel again. “Clear it,” he said. “Strip the room. She stays.”

Lena’s breath left her in a rush she didn’t hide.

Rachel didn’t move.

Hands went to the walls. The device powered down. Extra bodies backed toward the door, arguments dropping into mutters about thresholds and sequencing.

The pressure eased as the room emptied—not calm. Sated.

The door slid shut.

The corridor light vanished.

Mara stood very still near the wall, color drained from her face.

Lena crossed the room and stopped short of touching Rachel, hands curled into fists at her sides.

The room felt quieter now.

Not fixed.

Left alone.

Rachel swallowed and understood something cold and certain.

They hadn’t solved anything.

They’d just decided where the damage was allowed to remain.
