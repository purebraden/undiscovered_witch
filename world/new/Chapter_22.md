# Chapter 22

They didn’t slow down so much as lose the floor.

The passage narrowed, dipped, and then ended where part of it had fallen away. Rachel pitched forward, caught herself on her hands, and slid the rest of the way down on her knees, breath knocked loose from her chest.

Lena hit the wall beside her and swore.

Nothing followed.

That sat wrong in Rachel’s gut.

She stayed where she was, palms stinging, waiting for the hum, for the vibration in her teeth, for the pressure to shove her upright again. Instead it crowded her chest and stayed there, tight and close, like it had pressed its ear to the stone.

Water dripped nearby. The air smelled cracked open.

“Don’t move,” Lena said.

Rachel didn’t.

The pressure shifted.

Not forward.

Sideways.

Rachel’s balance went strange, just a fraction off, and her eyes slid toward the wall where the collapse had peeled back layers that didn’t belong in open air. Stone had torn away, leaving metal exposed, twisted inward like it had been pulled instead of struck.

A door sat there.

Bent. Half-seated in a frame that no longer matched it.

Lena saw it and stopped breathing for a second.

“That’s not sealed,” she said.

Rachel opened her mouth. Nothing came out.

The door flexed.

Not opening.

Giving.

The pressure snapped.

Rachel’s weight shifted hard to the left. Her knees skidded on damp stone and her shoulder slammed into the wall.

The door tore free.

Metal shrieked as it folded inward, frame ripping loose in a way that sounded too easy.

Something inside screamed.

High. Short.

It stopped.

Rachel tasted blood and swallowed hard.

Lena shouted her name.

The pressure surged again, wider now, brushing past Rachel and into the space she hadn’t meant to uncover.

Silence dropped heavy.

Rachel pushed herself upright. Her legs answered immediately. She was standing before she remembered standing.

“I—” she said.

Nothing followed it.

Lena grabbed her shoulders and shook her once. “What did you just do?”

Rachel’s heart hammered, but her hands were still. Her breathing had already evened out.

Lena turned toward the room.

Light hummed softly inside. Clean panels. A body against the far wall, limbs bent where they shouldn’t bend. Blood spread slowly, following the grooves cut into the floor.

Rachel stared at the blood, not the person.

“I didn’t touch them,” she said.

Lena didn’t turn. “You didn’t have to.”

The pressure shifted again, tight and restless.

“That room wasn’t empty,” Lena said. “It wasn’t supposed to be.”

Rachel took a step forward before she noticed she’d moved. The pressure stayed close to her ribs.

“No,” Lena said. “Stay.”

Rachel stopped.

The room smelled clean under the blood.

Prepared.

“I didn’t—” Rachel tried again.

Lena cut her off. “Don’t.”

They stood there too long.

The hum came back, thin and distant, threading through the walls like a sound remembered instead of heard.

Lena grabbed Rachel’s wrist and pulled. “We’re gone.”

They ran.

The passage bent in on itself. Rachel stumbled, caught herself, and kept moving, feet landing where she expected them to be without checking first.

“Did it—” she started.

Lena didn’t answer.

They burst into a maintenance bay cluttered with stripped braces and broken equipment. Heat sat heavy in the air. Lena shoved a rack aside and tore open a crawlspace behind it.

“Inside.”

Rachel hesitated.

The pressure crowded her chest.

She crawled.

The space forced her onto her side, metal pressing into her ribs, vibrating faintly. Lena slammed the panel shut behind them and wedged it with something heavy.

They lay there.

Rachel’s heartbeat slowed.

Too quickly.

Her breathing evened out. Her muscles loosened.

She waited for the shaking.

It didn’t come.

“I should—” she whispered.

Lena’s voice came thin through the dark. “Don’t.”

The hum passed overhead and kept going.

The pressure stayed close, tight against Rachel’s ribs, not pushing, not leaving.

“When did it start doing that?” Rachel asked.

Lena didn’t answer.

Somewhere deeper in the structure, metal shifted with a soft, final sound.

Rachel stared into the dark, listening to her own breathing, waiting for something to catch up to her that didn’t.

Nothing did.
