# Chapter 33

Enough time passed that the room lost its edge.

Mara stopped moving. Not deliberately—just the way people did when they decided nothing else was going to happen. She leaned back against the wall, arms loose, attention drifting in short, habitual passes instead of the tight scans from earlier. Lena shifted her weight in the corner, one boot braced against the stone, eyes on Rachel without counting her breaths anymore.

Rachel stayed seated.

The chair hadn’t moved again. The seams in the floor held their lines. The pressure stayed close to her ribs, not pressing, not pulling—present in the way a sound was present even after you stopped listening for it.

Nothing happened.

Rachel let her breathing loosen a fraction at a time. The bed pressed evenly behind her knees. The light stayed steady. The wall plate remained dark.

The waiting part felt finished.

The sound came in sideways.

Not loud. Not sudden. Just a thin vibration that didn’t belong to anything Rachel could see. She noticed it because the pressure shifted at the same moment, not reacting—adjusting.

Lena straightened slowly.

Mara lifted her head. “Do you—”

The sound dipped, then returned late, like it had missed its place. A moment after that, the light followed, brightening just enough to register before settling again.

Mara pushed off the wall.

The pressure leaned closer.

Rachel felt her breath reach the end before the room caught up with it. The pressure answered a fraction behind, then slid into place like it hadn’t noticed the delay.

Something clicked inside the wall.

Not the plate. Lower. Duller.

It clicked again, not quite in the same place.

Lena’s mouth tightened. She didn’t speak.

Mara crossed the room carefully, stopping short of Rachel, palm flattening against the stone. She closed her eyes, listening through contact instead of sound.

The light dimmed a shade.

The pressure tightened, dense and attentive.

The vibration stuttered, slipping further out of time. Rachel felt it stack—sound after movement, light after sound, the pressure after her breath—each piece arriving just late enough to scrape.

“It feels—” Rachel started, then stopped.

Both of them were already looking at her.

The chair creaked.

Not shifting. Just protesting.

No one moved.

Another click answered from farther away, late enough to feel wrong. The sound in the room spread, as if the space couldn’t decide which version of itself to use.

Mara took a step toward the door. The handle resisted for a beat longer than it should have, then turned easily in her hand.

She didn’t open it.

The vibration cut out mid-cycle.

Silence rushed in too fast.

The light steadied. The wall went quiet. The chair stopped complaining.

Everything looked normal.

Mara drew in a breath and held it.

The pressure surged.

The chair slid.

Just enough.

Rachel gasped and caught it, hands pressing flat against her thighs as the room lurched a fraction out of step and snapped back again.

The pressure stayed.

No one spoke.

The space held its shape like it was waiting to see if anyone would argue.

Rachel sat very still.

She understood it then—not as a thought, not cleanly.

It didn’t need her to do anything.

It just needed time where no one was watching closely enough.

And it had been given that.
