# Chapter 13

Rachel slept in fragments, the dark closing over her and peeling back again without warning, her body snapping awake whenever the pressure in her chest shifted just enough to remind her it was still there.

The room did not change.

The light stayed thin and steady in the slits near the ceiling. The frame remained where it had been left. The sealed passage stayed sealed.

Rachel lay on her side with her knees drawn up, the bandage on her leg tight and warm, listening to her own breathing and the drip of water that never quite landed in the same place twice.

The drip slowed.

Rachel opened her eyes.

For a moment everything looked the same. Stone walls. Bare floor. The frame standing empty. Cool air against her skin.

The drip stopped.

Rachel pushed herself upright, moving slowly, one hand braced on the floor, her leg pulling where the bandage tightened. She waited for the nausea, the sideways pressure, the familiar correction.

Nothing came.

The basin was empty.

The stone beneath it was dry.

No trail. No spill. Nothing left behind.

Rachel stood.

Her balance held.

That was worse.

She took a step toward the frame.

Metal shifted.

Not loud. Not sudden. A faint, wrong sound, like something adjusting under weight it had not been built to carry.

Rachel stopped.

The frame answered.

One upright creaked where it met the floor, the sound thin and strained, vibrating through the stone.

Rachel stepped back.

The sound followed, metal scraping lightly as if it were trying to find her again.

Her breath shortened, quick and shallow, the room shrinking around her despite the unchanged walls. The light in the ceiling slits thinned unevenly, one strip dimming while another stayed bright, the edges of the room losing their alignment.

Rachel pressed her shoulders to the wall.

The stone was cold.

She slid sideways without fully choosing to move, feet scuffing the floor as she edged away from the frame, eyes locked on it while her body searched for space.

The metal groaned.

One corner lifted, barely, just enough to cast a warped shadow beneath it before settling again with a dull, unfinished thud.

Rachel's hands shook.

She kept moving sideways until the wall met her back and the corner of the room closed in around her, her breath scraping in and out of her chest.

The frame twisted.

Bolts shrieked as they tore free from the stone, the sound sharp and ugly, metal bending where it should not bend. The structure folded inward, collapsing piece by piece until it slumped against the wall in a tangled heap.

The noise echoed once.

Then stopped.

Rachel slid down the wall and sat hard on the floor, fingers clenched together in her lap, breath coming too fast, too shallow, her leg throbbing beneath the bandage.

The room stayed still.

No footsteps.

No voices.

No correction.

After a while, the stone beneath the collapsed metal darkened. Hairline cracks spread outward from the torn bolt holes, branching slowly, deliberately, the surface changing in ways she could not undo by closing her eyes.

Rachel did not move.

The cracks spread until they stopped.

The room did not fix itself.

The frame did not rise again.

Rachel stayed where she was, back against the wall, watching the shape of the space fail to return to what it had been.
