# Chapter 10

Rachel came up through a hatch that resisted at first, the metal warped and stiff enough that she had to brace her shoulder against it and lean her weight forward until it gave with a dull scrape that sounded too loud in the confined space below.

She paused halfway out, listening, then pulled herself fully into a narrow courtyard wedged between two buildings, stone walls rising close on either side with their upper windows shuttered tight. The hatch settled back into place when she let go, metal touching stone without a clang, the sound absorbed before it could travel.

The air smelled different here, not cleaner so much as arranged, smoke lingering without heat, like something that had burned earlier and been dealt with.

Rachel stood still and waited.

The pressure in her chest loosened.

She stepped forward, slow and careful, testing the ground with each movement, and felt nothing resist her as she crossed the courtyard and reached the narrow street beyond.

The buildings lining it were low and plain, their fronts unadorned, no banners or signs hanging from the walls, no lamps burning despite the early hour. The street curved gently out of sight in both directions, the stone underfoot worn smooth and patched in places where repairs had been made recently.

Rachel limped into it, her steps sounding louder than she liked until she adjusted her pace, letting her weight settle more evenly despite the ache spreading through her leg.

A doorway passed on her left with boards freshly nailed across it, pale wood standing out against older stone, a simple line painted across the boards in dull white. Farther along, a window stood open with its curtains pulled back but unmoving in the still air, the room beyond empty.

She slowed.

The pressure stirred, then eased again when she stopped moving.

Rachel stayed where she was.

At the far end of the street, a man appeared carrying a crate against his chest, his pace unhurried, his attention on his footing until he looked up and saw her. He paused, shifted the crate slightly, then turned without a word and stepped into a side alley, disappearing from view.

Rachel waited, breath shallow, hands loose at her sides.

The pressure remained low and steady.

She moved on.

The street widened gradually into a small square paved with uneven stones, a dry fountain at its center with a cloth draped over the basin. The same simple line marked the fabric, brushed on with deliberate strokes. Benches lined the edges of the square, their surfaces clean and empty.

Rachel crossed slowly, scanning doorways and windows as she went, noting the shutters drawn tight, the locks newly set, metal bright against worn wood. When she reached the far side, she stopped again.

The pressure tightened when she turned back.

It eased when she faced forward.

She swallowed and kept walking.

Beyond the square, the buildings grew taller and older, their facades crowded with signs and lamps that burned steady and bright. Voices carried faintly from farther down the road, layered and indistinct.

Rachel slowed at the edge where the quiet ended.

Her foot hovered over the change in stone.

The pressure coiled.

She pulled her foot back.

Behind her, the street she had come from remained still, holding its shape without sound or movement.

Ahead, the city went on.

Rachel turned sideways and followed the edge instead, keeping to the narrow strip where light thinned and sound dulled, moving without drawing notice as people went about their business a few steps away.

A woman sweeping her doorstep glanced up as Rachel passed, eyes sliding over her and away again, the broom never pausing in its rhythm.

Two guards stood talking quietly at an intersection farther on, one gesturing down a side street as Rachel approached, the other nodding without looking up. They continued their conversation as she passed between them.

The pressure eased.

Rachel walked until her leg shook and her mouth tasted of metal, then stopped in a narrow lane where the buildings leaned close together and the air felt heavier. She sank down against the wall and closed her eyes.

The pressure settled.

Somewhere above the rooftops, a bell rang once, then again.

When Rachel opened her eyes, the street she had come from was no longer visible.

She stayed where she was, listening to the city move around her, and did not try to follow the sound.
