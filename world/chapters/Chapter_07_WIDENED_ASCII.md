# Chapter 7

Rachel did not get far underground before her breathing went wrong, pulling too fast for how slowly she was moving, her chest working harder than it should have while the corridor sloped gently forward as if it were easing her along.

The ache in her leg dulled, then sharpened again without pattern, heat blooming and fading as she stepped, sweat breaking out along her spine despite the cool stone underfoot. She tried to slow down. She tried to count her breaths. Neither helped for long.

The corridor widened.

Here the blocks were pale and fitted tightly together, seams clean, surfaces smoothed by long use. Light leaked from narrow cuts in the walls, even and flat, thinning the shadows until depth became difficult to judge. Her boots scraped louder against the stone, the sound carrying farther than it should have.

Rachel slowed.

Then stopped.

Nothing happened.

Her leg burned. Then it didn't. Then it did again, sharp enough to pull a sound from her teeth. She shifted her weight, testing it, waiting for the nausea or the sideways pull.

It didn't come.

She stood still.

The sensation eased, not gone, just pulled back enough to make space.

At the far end of the corridor, a man stood with his hands loose at his sides, dark coat hanging straight, boots clean enough to catch the light.

"Rachel," he said.

The sound of her name landed wrong in the space, too clear, too confident.

She didn't answer.

Her breath settled, shallow but steady, her pulse loud in her ears.

"You're bleeding," the man said.

Rachel looked down. Blood seeped through the fabric at her calf, dark and unhurried, trailing toward her heel as if it had all the time in the world.

"How do you know my name," she asked.

"We keep records," he said.

She took one step back.

The floor tipped just enough to throw her off balance, her vision shifting sideways with it, the walls seeming to lean in. Rachel stopped short, heart jumping.

The floor leveled.

"I'm leaving," she said.

The man nodded once, as if she had said something expected. "That's normal."

He stepped forward and stopped again, gaze dropping briefly to the stone between them before lifting back to her face.

Thin grooves crossed the floor there, shallow cuts worn smooth, intersecting at uneven angles. Old marks. Used marks.

"What happened below is finished," he said.

Rachel shook her head.

"You burned them."

"Yes," he said. "And we would do it again."

Heat flared at the mark on her leg, sharp and sudden. Rachel hissed and shifted her weight, breath catching as the sensation climbed and then settled into a deep, heavy throb.

The man's eyes followed the movement.

"Don't resist that," he said. "It won't help."

"I'm not," she said, though the words felt thin even to her.

A low vibration settled into the stone beneath their feet, not loud enough to hear so much as feel, steady and continuous, running up through her boots and into her bones.

"If I go," Rachel said, "what happens."

The man watched her for several seconds, his expression unchanged, the vibration holding steady under them both.

"We wait," he said. "And the thing bound to you acts."

The pressure in her chest tightened.

Her stomach lurched. Nausea rose fast and thick, forcing her hand to the wall as her breath tore out in short bursts she couldn't slow. Her vision blurred at the edges.

The man didn't move.

"No one is touching you," he said.

Rachel slid down the wall and sat on the stone floor, hands clenched together in her lap, shoulders shaking as the nausea ebbed in uneven waves.

The vibration remained.

The man released a slow breath, barely audible.

"Good," he said.

He shifted his weight and glanced past her, listening to something she could not hear. After a moment, he nodded once, almost to himself, then stepped back, retreating toward the end of the corridor without hurry.

As he moved away, the vibration in the stone thinned, the steady pressure loosening in a way it had not before. The light dulled slightly along the walls, the seams losing their sharp edge as the corridor seemed to ease.

Rachel kept her eyes on the floor.

Her breathing slowed.

The corridor did not close.

It did not open.

It waited.
