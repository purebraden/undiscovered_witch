# Chapter 7

Rachel didn't get far underground before her breathing went wrong, pulling too fast for how slowly she was moving, her chest working harder than it should have while the corridor sloped gently forward.

The ache in her leg dulled, then sharpened again without pattern, heat blooming and fading as she stepped, sweat breaking out along her spine despite the cool stone underfoot.

The corridor widened.

Here the blocks were pale and fitted tightly together, the seams clean, the surface smoothed by long use. Light leaked from narrow cuts in the walls, even and flat, thinning shadows until depth became difficult to judge. Her boots scraped louder against the stone.

Rachel slowed, then stopped.

Nothing changed.

Her leg burned. Then it didn't. Then it did again, sharp enough to pull a breath from her teeth.

She stood still.

The sensation eased.

At the far end of the corridor, a man stood with his hands loose at his sides, dark coat hanging straight, boots clean enough to catch the light.

"Rachel," he said.

She didn't answer.

Her breath settled, shallow but steady.

"You're bleeding," the man said.

Rachel looked down. Blood seeped through the fabric at her calf, dark and unhurried, trailing toward her heel.

"How do you know my name," she asked.

"We keep records," he said.

She took one step back, heel sliding slightly as the floor tipped just enough to throw her off balance, her vision shifting sideways with it.

Rachel stopped.

The floor leveled.

"I'm leaving," she said.

The man nodded once. "That's expected."

He stepped forward and stopped again, his gaze dropping briefly to the stone between them before lifting back to her face.

Thin grooves crossed the floor there, shallow cuts worn smooth, intersecting at uneven angles.

"What happened below is finished," he said.

Rachel shook her head.

"You burned them."

"Yes," he said. "And we would do it again."

Heat flared at the mark on her leg, sharp and sudden. Rachel hissed and shifted her weight, breath catching as the sensation climbed and then settled.

The man watched her leg.

"Don't resist that," he said. "It won't help."

"I'm not," she said.

A low vibration settled into the stone beneath their feet, not loud enough to hear so much as feel, steady and continuous.

"If I go," Rachel said, "what happens."

The man looked at her for several seconds, his expression unchanged.

"We wait," he said. "And the thing bound to you acts."

The pressure in her chest tightened.

Her stomach lurched. Nausea rose fast and thick, forcing her hand to the wall as her breath tore out in short bursts she couldn't slow.

The man didn't move.

"No one is touching you," he said.

Rachel slid down the wall and sat on the stone floor, hands clenched together in her lap, shoulders shaking as the nausea ebbed.

The vibration remained.

The man released a slow breath.

"Good," he said.

The man shifted his weight and glanced past her, listening to something Rachel could not hear.

He nodded once, almost to himself, then stepped back, retreating toward the end of the corridor without hurry. As he moved away, the vibration in the stone thinned, the steady pressure loosening in a way it had not before.

The light dulled slightly along the walls, the seams losing their sharp edge as the corridor seemed to relax around her.

Rachel kept her eyes on the floor.

Her breathing slowed.

The light in the corridor stayed the same.
