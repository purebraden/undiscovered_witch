# Chapter 32

The door clicked.

Rachel jerked hard enough to bite her tongue, copper blooming in her mouth as she pressed herself into the wall. Her heart slammed, loud and panicked, the sound of it filling her head.

The click came again.

Smaller than the first. Wrong somehow.

"No," she whispered. "No."

The pressure tightened, then loosened, then tightened again, unsettled, like it was waiting for something else to happen before it decided what to do.

The door stayed closed.

A line of light cut through the darkness instead, thin and sharp, slicing the floor in half. It flickered once, then steadied, as if the door had stopped halfway through remembering how it worked.

Rachel stared at it, breath shallow, chest tight.

The hum crept back into the walls in pieces, not all at once. A vibration rolled through the stone, faint but wide, followed by a dull thud somewhere she couldn't place. Dust drifted down and caught in the light.

The etched lines buzzed under her feet.

She took a step without meaning to.

The pressure flared hot and sharp and she froze mid-movement, weight wrong, muscles shaking. Her vision dimmed at the edges and she swallowed hard, fighting the urge to move again just to make it stop.

"Okay," she said, to no one. "Okay."

The light widened.

Not much. Just enough to show shadow beyond it, sliding past in a way that made her stomach twist. The corridor outside felt longer than it should have been, stretched thin and restless.

The door shuddered.

Rachel felt it through the floor before she heard it, a low vibration that rattled her teeth. The pressure surged inward, compressing tight enough to steal her breath.

"Stop," she said, louder, the word tearing out of her. "Please."

The door twisted.

Not opening. Not closing. Metal groaned as the gap widened another fraction and stuck there, caught on something unseen.

Cold air spilled through, sharp and stale, carrying the smell of oil and stone and something underneath that made her gag.

Rachel stumbled back, heel catching on one of the etched lines. The buzz under her skin spiked and the pressure snapped tight, pinning her in place hard enough that she cried out.

The light flickered.

For a second it went dark.

Then it flared brighter than before.

Heat ripped through her chest as the pressure reacted all at once, wild and unfocused. The etched lines beneath her hands burned bright through the stone and Rachel screamed as the force slammed back into her, knocking her sideways.

She hit the floor hard.

The door shrieked.

Metal warped. The gap widened another breath and something scraped on the other side, slow and uneven, not a step, not quite anything she could name.

Rachel crawled backward on instinct, palms slipping on stone as she dragged herself away from the door, heart pounding, breath coming in short, panicked pulls.

The door stopped.

The light held.

The pressure eased just enough that she could curl in on herself, shaking, every muscle screaming as it let go all at once.

She waited.

The gap didn't change.

The door didn't finish what it had started.

It stayed wrong, light bleeding through the seam, a wound that refused to close.

Rachel pressed her forehead to the floor and squeezed her eyes shut.

The building didn't settle.

And whatever had started responding to her wasn't done yet.
