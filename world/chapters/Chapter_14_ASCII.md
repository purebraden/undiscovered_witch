# Chapter 14

The room failed quietly, not all at once, but in small uneven ways that made it hard for Rachel to tell where to look first, one of the thin slits near the ceiling dimming and then brightening again, the light losing its steadiness while the stone around it darkened in patches.

She stayed where she was, back against the wall, breath coming in short pulls that scraped in her chest.

The cracked stone beneath the collapsed metal frame shifted, a faint grinding sound moving through the floor as the fractures widened by a hair, dust lifting and settling again without drifting far.

Rachel swallowed and waited.

The sealed passage did not open.

For a while, nothing else happened.

Then the pressure changed, not tightening, not easing, but pulling sideways, an off-angle insistence that made standing still uncomfortable in a new way, like her weight was suddenly wrong for the room.

Rachel pushed herself upright, palms sliding against the stone, her leg flaring hot as she put weight on it, the bandage pulling tight enough to make her hiss through her teeth.

She took one step.

The pull softened.

She took another, angling across the room, boots crunching lightly on fallen grit as she moved away from the twisted metal and toward the far corner, her balance lagging behind her intention.

The pull steadied.

Rachel stopped.

The wall in front of her shifted, not opening so much as separating, a thin seam appearing in the stone where there hadn't been one before, widening slowly until it outlined a narrow door she could not remember seeing.

Cool air slipped through the opening and brushed her face.

Rachel didn't move.

The pressure pressed sideways again, firmer now, her chest tightening as if her lungs had been nudged out of alignment.

She stepped forward.

The door stayed open.

Behind her, the fractured stone beneath the frame settled again with a dull sound, dust lifting in a thin cloud that hung for a moment before dropping.

Rachel crossed the threshold.

As soon as both feet were in the corridor, the pull loosened, her shoulders dropping a fraction before she caught herself.

The door closed behind her.

Stone met stone without echo.

The corridor sloped downward, narrower than the one she had left before, the walls close enough that her shoulders brushed them when she drifted too far to one side. Light seeped from nowhere she could see, thin but steady, enough to keep her from tripping.

Rachel limped forward, one hand dragging along the wall for balance, her leg burning with each step as the floor dipped and the angle pulled at her hips.

The air smelled clean.

Too clean.

Her footsteps carried farther than they should have, the sound stretching ahead of her and coming back wrong.

The corridor opened into another space, not a room, not quite, the floor dipping into a shallow channel lined with smooth stone, faint ridges running along its length like something had worn a path there over time.

Metal rails were set into the walls at shoulder height, polished dull where hands had gripped them before.

Rachel stopped at the edge.

The pressure nudged her forward.

She stepped into the channel.

The stone beneath her boots vibrated, low and steady, the sensation crawling up her legs and settling in her knees. She reached for the rail and caught it, her grip tightening as the vibration deepened.

The channel began to move.

Not fast. Not slow.

Just enough that the walls slid past her vision in a steady glide, the space carrying her forward without warning or pause.

Rachel shifted her stance, her injured leg protesting as the floor moved beneath her, the bandage pulling tight as she fought to keep her balance.

She did not fall.

The corridor bent and descended, the light thinning and strengthening again in long stretches, the air cooling further as the channel carried her deeper, the sound of movement constant and dull.

Rachel swallowed and kept her eyes forward.

Openings passed along the walls, narrow alcoves sliding by too quickly to see inside. Some were empty. Some held shapes that blurred before she could focus on them.

The vibration stayed the same.

After a time she could not measure, the channel slowed.

The vibration softened.

Rachel loosened her grip on the rail as the floor eased to a stop, her hand tingling as she let go.

Ahead, the corridor widened again.

The pressure shifted, urging her forward.

Rachel stepped out of the channel, her leg shaking now from the strain, breath loud in her ears.

Behind her, the stone floor slid back into place, sealing the channel without sound, leaving nothing but smooth wall where it had been.

Rachel stood still for a moment, bent slightly forward, hands on her thighs as she caught her breath.

The pressure settled.

She was not where she had been.

She did not turn around.
