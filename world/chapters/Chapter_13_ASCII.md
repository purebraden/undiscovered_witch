# Chapter 13

Rachel slept in short pieces, the dark closing over her and letting go again without warning, her body jerking awake whenever the pressure in her chest shifted enough to pull her back into herself.

The room did not change.

The light stayed thin and steady in the slits near the ceiling. The frame remained where it had been left. The sealed passage stayed sealed.

Rachel lay on her side with her knees drawn up, the bandage on her leg tight and warm, listening to her breathing and the drip of water that never quite landed in the same place twice.

The drip slowed.

Rachel opened her eyes.

For a moment, everything looked the same. Stone walls. Bare floor. The frame standing empty. Cool air against her skin.

The drip stopped.

Rachel pushed herself upright, moving slowly, one hand braced on the floor, her leg pulling where the bandage tightened.

The basin was empty.

The stone was dry.

No trail. No spill. Nothing left behind.

Rachel stood.

She waited for the familiar spike, the roll in her stomach, the clamp that followed certain steps.

Nothing came.

She took a step toward the frame.

The sound of metal shifting reached her ears, faint and wrong, like something adjusting under weight it had not been built to carry.

Rachel stopped.

The frame made another sound, one upright creaking where it met the floor.

Rachel stepped back.

The sound followed, metal scraping lightly against stone.

Her breath shortened, quick and shallow, the room feeling suddenly smaller despite the unchanged walls.

The light in the slits near the ceiling thinned unevenly, one strip dimming while another stayed bright, the edges of the room losing their balance.

Rachel pressed her shoulders to the wall.

The stone was cold.

She slid sideways without fully choosing to move, feet scuffing the floor as she edged away from the frame, keeping her eyes on it while her body searched for space.

The metal groaned.

One corner lifted, just enough to cast a warped shadow beneath it before settling again with a dull thud.

Rachel's hands shook.

She kept moving sideways, breath scraping in and out of her chest, until the wall met her back and the corner of the room pressed in around her.

The frame twisted.

Bolts shrieked as they tore free from the stone, the sound sharp and ugly, metal bending where it should not bend. The structure folded inward, collapsing on itself piece by piece until it slumped against the wall in a tangled heap.

The sound echoed once.

Then stopped.

Rachel slid down the wall and sat hard on the floor, fingers clenched together in her lap, breath coming too fast, too shallow, her leg throbbing beneath the bandage.

The room stayed still.

No footsteps. No voices. No movement beyond the stone.

After a while, the floor beneath the collapsed metal darkened, hairline cracks spreading outward from the torn bolt holes, branching slowly, deliberately, the stone surface changing in ways she could not undo by looking away.

Rachel did not move.

The cracks spread until they stopped.

The room did not correct itself.

The frame did not rise again.

Rachel stayed where she was, back against the wall, watching the shape of the space fail to return to what it had been.
