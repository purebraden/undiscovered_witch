# Chapter 4

Rachel stepped forward.

Not because she wanted to.

Because the pull tightened until her chest felt like it was going to cave in on itself, because the horns above were screaming now, close enough that the stone vibrated, because every other option ended with her bleeding out on the stairs.

Her boot crossed the line.

The circle reacted.

Light slammed upward like a trap snapping shut. The chains screamed as they pulled tight, metal grinding against stone, sparks spitting as the air warped and folded inward.

Rachel screamed too.

The sound tore out of her before she could stop it, sharp and raw, as pain ripped through her chest and spine and hooked hard behind her eyes. She dropped to her knees inside the circle, hands slapping uselessly against the stone as the light crawled up her arms, her throat, her face.

Something latched on.

Not her body.

Her.

She gasped, choking, as memories flashed white-hot and disjointed—hands, voices, heat, cold—things ripped loose and pressed flat like they were being inspected.

The figure in the center watched calmly.

“Easy,” it said. “You’ll tear if you fight it.”

“I didn’t agree to this,” Rachel snarled, though the words came out slurred and wrong, her tongue thick in her mouth.

The figure smiled.

“No,” it said. “You agreed when you crossed.”

The chains shuddered again.

The circle bit.

Rachel felt it then—the shape of it. The way the magic wasn’t flowing through her so much as measuring her, compressing and aligning, shaving off edges that didn’t fit.

Something inside her screamed.

Something else answered.

The light under her skin darkened.

Not dimmed. Deepened. Thickened into something heavier, slower, that pulsed in time with her heartbeat and dragged at her thoughts when she tried to move.

Rachel collapsed forward, retching, bile burning her throat. Black flecks spattered the stone inside the circle and steamed faintly where they landed.

The cages erupted.

Chains rattled violently as the things inside pressed forward, claws and hands and too-many-jointed limbs thrusting through the bars as far as they could reach.

“She’s binding!”
“Not sealed—look at her—”
“Careful,” a voice purred. “That one might stick.”

Rachel forced herself up onto her hands and knees, vision swimming.

“What did you do,” she rasped.

The figure finally stepped closer, stopping just short of touching her.

“What your kind always does,” it said mildly. “You fell into a contract.”

“I didn’t sign anything.”

The figure crouched, meeting her eyes. Up close, the human mask slipped just a little—pupils too wide, reflection wrong, something vast and patient looking back out.

“You bled,” it said. “You crossed a circle. You survived the break. That’s a signature where we’re from.”

The chains slackened slightly.

Not released.

Waiting.

Above them, stone cracked.

A thunderous impact shook dust from the ceiling. Shouts echoed down the stairwell now, sharp and triumphant.

“They’re here,” someone hissed from a cage. “Oh, this will be ugly.”

The figure straightened, listening, then smiled again.

“They’ll burn this chamber,” it said. “They always do.”

Rachel’s heart slammed against her ribs. “Then let me out.”

The figure tilted its head.

“No.”

The word was gentle. Final.

“But,” it continued, “I can teach you how not to die when they try.”

The circle flared again, tighter this time, squeezing until Rachel cried out and curled in on herself, light searing behind her eyes.

“Lesson one,” the figure said calmly, as the first stones overhead began to crack apart, “you don’t belong to them anymore.”

Rachel screamed as something inside her locked into place, and above them the ceiling started to fall.
