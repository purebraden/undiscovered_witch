# Chapter 23

They didn't come back into the room.

The floor lines brightened instead, the pale glow sharpening until it made Rachel squint, and the hum under her feet deepened into something she felt in her teeth. The pressure inside her tightened in response, alert and irritated, like it didn't appreciate being ignored.

Rachel shifted her weight and immediately regretted it. The lines flared brighter under her boots and a pulse of force rippled up through the stone, not enough to knock her down but enough to remind her that standing still was being noticed now too.

"Okay," she muttered. "I get it."

The voice returned, flatter than before, closer now, as if it had moved into the room without needing to pass through it.

"Remain upright."

Rachel laughed, sharp and breathless. "You don't want that."

The pressure surged at the sound, hot and reactive, and the glow spiked again. Somewhere overhead, stone shifted with a grinding complaint.

"Stability compromised," the voice said.

Rachel swallowed and forced herself still, shoulders tight, every muscle braced. The pressure coiled heavy in her chest, not pushing, not pulling, just there, like it was waiting to see how this played out.

The floor lines shifted.

Not toward the corridor she'd come from.

Toward a different seam she hadn't noticed before, a long narrow break in the stone that widened as she watched, light bleeding up from below. Cold air spilled out carrying a sharp metallic smell that made her eyes water.

Rachel took an involuntary step back.

The pressure slammed into her chest, a solid shove that stole her breath and sent her stumbling forward instead. She caught herself on the edge of the opening, palms burning as the stone vibrated under her hands.

"Transport initiated," the voice said.

"No," Rachel said, panic cracking through her control. "Wait."

The opening widened another inch.

The pressure leaned hard, not subtle now, a full-body insistence that made her legs shake as she fought it. Heat flared under her skin and she bit down on a cry, refusing to give it sound.

A second voice cut in, sharper, closer, overlapping the first.

"Hold relocation. Documentation incomplete."

The pressure hesitated.

Rachel sagged, gasping.

The first voice answered immediately. "Containment insufficient. Subject active."

"I said hold," the sharper voice snapped. "We don't know where she-"

The pressure surged again, hotter this time, and Rachel cried out despite herself as it tore sideways through her ribs, the sensation sharp and disorienting. The floor lines flared blinding white.

"See?" the first voice said. "We're already behind."

Rachel's vision blurred. The opening yawned wider under her hands and she felt the pull now, not just pressure but a downward insistence that made her stomach drop.

"Downgrade safeguards," the sharp voice said tightly. "No stabilizers. No sedation."

Rachel's head snapped up. "What?"

"Do it," the first voice said. "We can't keep her here."

The pressure coiled and then released all at once.

Rachel's feet slid out from under her.

She fell.

The drop wasn't long enough to scream, just long enough for the air to tear past her ears and for the pressure inside her to spike, furious and bright, as if it had been waiting for this exact moment.

She hit hard, shoulder first, the impact knocking the breath from her lungs and sending pain flaring down her side. Stone scraped her skin as she slid the rest of the way down and came to a rough stop on a slanted surface that vibrated beneath her.

The world tilted.

Light streaked past in broken bands as the surface beneath her began to move, carrying her downward through a narrow shaft that felt too small and too fast, the walls close enough that she could almost touch them if she dared.

Rachel curled in on herself, arms over her head, teeth clenched against the pain as the pressure surged and twisted inside her, not aligned with the movement, not helping, just reacting violently to being dragged along.

Voices echoed faintly above, distorted by distance and motion.

"This is a mistake," the sharp voice said.

"Log it," the first voice replied. "She's already in transit."

The shaft bent sharply and Rachel was thrown sideways, her body slamming into the wall with a dull crack that made stars burst behind her eyes. She screamed then, unable to stop it, the sound ripped away by the rushing air.

The pressure flared in answer, hot and eager, and for a terrifying moment Rachel felt it lock with the motion, like it had found a rhythm it liked.

She clawed at the stone, skin tearing, desperate for friction, for anything to slow her down.

The movement didn't stop.

It only sped up.

Wherever they were sending her, they were doing it fast.

And whatever rules had existed when Lena was there hadn't survived the handoff.
