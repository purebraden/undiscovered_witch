# Chapter 9

Rachel did not hear the doors close.

She felt it instead, a thinning of space behind her, a subtle change in the air as she moved deeper underground, the pull in her chest tightening and loosening in small, careful increments as the passages narrowed and bent away from the corridor she had left behind.

The light was gone now. Not all at once, but in stretches, long sections of stone swallowed by shadow between isolated seams that still glowed faintly, like embers buried under ash.

Her steps echoed differently.

She slowed.

Then stopped.

Nothing followed.

The pressure eased when she stood still, settling into something low and constant that made it easier to breathe as long as she did not turn around.

She didn't.

The tunnel sloped downward, the air cooling, dampness seeping into her clothes. The clean stone smell gave way to something stagnant. Old water. Rot held at bay.

Her leg ached again. Not sharp. Not urgent. A deep soreness that spread when she leaned into it and receded when she rested.

She adjusted her pace without thinking.

After a while, she came to a junction marked with paint.

Three symbols, simple and rough, brushed onto the stone in dull white. One was crossed through hard enough to scar the rock beneath it. One was half-smeared, the lines blurred as if someone had tried to wipe it away in a hurry. The third remained intact.

Rachel stared at them longer than she meant to.

The pressure in her chest tightened when her eyes lingered on the crossed symbol. Her stomach rolled, nausea pricking sharp and fast.

When she looked at the intact mark, the pressure loosened.

Not relief.

Permission.

She took that path.

The passage narrowed, then opened into a low chamber stacked with old crates, most of them split or empty, their lids warped by damp and time. A rusted grate lay half-buried in debris at the far end, bent inward as if something had forced its way through and never bothered to come back.

Rachel moved past the crates and crouched, lowering herself onto a patch of dry stone.

Her hands shook when she pressed them to the floor.

She leaned back and closed her eyes.

For a few seconds, nothing happened.

Then the pressure shifted.

Not sharper. Not stronger.

Closer.

Like something had leaned in, just enough to see if she would react.

Rachel opened her eyes.

The chamber hadn't changed.

Her breathing had.

Too fast. Too shallow.

She forced it slower, matching the steady pull in her chest, riding it the way she had learned to ride pain, letting it set the rhythm instead of fighting it.

She stayed there until her hands stopped shaking.

When she stood again, the ache in her leg had dulled to a heavy throb.

She moved on.

Time blurred underground. The tunnels pinched and widened, bent back on themselves, split and rejoined. She walked until standing hurt, then crawled until her knees burned, then walked again.

She drank from a shallow pool that tasted of minerals and rust and regret. She slept in short, broken stretches, waking whenever the pressure changed, heart jumping as if she'd been touched.

Each time she woke, the pull was still there.

Steady.

Waiting.

When she finally climbed toward the surface again, hours later, the pressure tightened in anticipation, guiding her toward a different exit than before.

Not the one she wanted.

The one that remained.

Above her, the air felt heavier.

The city had already adjusted.
