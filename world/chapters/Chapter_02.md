# Chapter 2

Rachel ran until the corridor bent, then bent again, and then stopped bending altogether and just went on and on like it had no intention of ever letting her out.

Her leg screamed every time her foot hit the stone. The bolt was still in there. She could feel it move when she moved, a wet dragging sensation that made her stomach lurch. Blood ran down into her boot, warm at first, then slippery.

The air tasted wrong. Sharp. Metallic. Like a storm that hadn’t happened yet.

Behind her, something hit the walls hard enough to shake dust loose from the ceiling.

Not close. Not far either.

She limped harder, nearly pitching forward when her bad leg buckled. Her hand slapped against the wall to keep her upright, and the stone felt warm under her palm. Too warm.

She yanked her hand back.

The light under her skin flared.

The wall puckered inward where she’d touched it, stone dimpling like clay pressed by a thumb. A crack raced outward with a dry snapping sound.

Rachel stared at it for half a second too long.

“Shit,” she breathed, and then she was moving again because stopping felt worse.

The corridor forked ahead.

No signs. No markings she understood. Just left or right, both of them narrow, both of them dark, both of them wrong.

Boots thundered somewhere behind her. Shouts echoed, overlapping, sharp and urgent.

“—don’t let it corner—”
“—bleeding, it’s bleeding—”
“—keep it moving—”

It.

Rachel took the right-hand passage and immediately regretted it.

The floor dipped. Water slicked the stone, old and stagnant, and her bad foot slid out from under her. She went down hard, breath whooshing out of her lungs as pain ripped up her leg so fast she saw spots.

She screamed again. Couldn’t stop it this time.

Hands shaking, she rolled onto her side and grabbed the bolt. The shaft was slick with blood. Her blood. She wrapped both hands around it and pulled.

White exploded behind her eyes.

She shrieked and yanked harder, teeth clenched so tight her jaw popped. The bolt came free with a wet sound that turned her stomach, and blood poured out fast, too fast.

“No no no,” she muttered, pressing her hands to the wound. “Stop. Stop.”

It didn’t.

The light surged without asking.

The stone beneath her rippled.

The water froze solid in an instant, not into ice but into something cloudy and brittle that cracked under its own weight. The floor bulged upward, lifting Rachel with it, pitching her back onto her feet as the corridor warped and groaned around her.

The bleeding slowed.

Not stopped. Slowed. Like something had pinched it closed from the inside.

Rachel stood there panting, hands slick and shaking, staring at her leg.

The wound looked wrong.

The flesh around it had gone pale and tight, pulled together like it had been stitched by an impatient hand. Thin, dark lines spidered out from it under the skin, fading as she watched.

She swallowed hard.

Behind her, something howled.

Closer now.

She ran again.

The passage spat her out into a wider hall lined with doors—cells, maybe, or rooms meant to be locked from the outside. Some of them were dented. One was torn halfway off its hinges, claw marks gouged deep into the metal.

Rachel didn’t slow down.

A figure lunged out of one of the open doorways.

She screamed and swung without thinking.

The light snapped outward in a tight, ugly pulse.

The figure slammed back into the wall with a sound like a sack of meat hitting stone and slid down, boneless. It didn’t get up.

Rachel stumbled past without looking.

More boots. More voices.

Someone blew a horn, the sound low and vibrating, setting her teeth on edge. Something answered it from deeper in the facility, a long, echoing call that raised goosebumps along her arms.

The building felt awake now.

Watching.

She burst through a final doorway and into open air.

Cold hit her like a slap.

Night, or something like it—dark sky smeared with slow-moving clouds, the air thick with smoke and the smell of burning oil. Towers rose around her, angular and crowded, windows glowing with uneven light. Bells were ringing everywhere, clanging and overlapping, turning the city into one huge alarm.

People were running.

Not toward her. Away.

Someone saw her stumble out into the open, blood-soaked and glowing faintly under the skin, and screamed. The scream spread. Others joined it.

“Outsider!”
“Break-runner!”
“Get back—get back—”

A man grabbed a child and hauled them behind a cart. A woman slammed shutters closed. Somewhere above, a window banged open and something heavy was thrown, shattering at Rachel’s feet.

She limped into the street, dizzy now, the world tilting at the edges.

Her vision blurred. The light under her skin pulsed in time with her heartbeat, each thud a little stronger than the last.

She felt it then—the pull.

Not the same as before. This one was outward. Thread-thin but insistent, tugging at her chest, pointing her somewhere she didn’t want to go.

Rachel staggered in the opposite direction.

The pull sharpened.

She gasped, clutching at herself like she could hold it in. The street stones cracked beneath her boots, fractures racing outward as the light flared again, uncontrolled and angry.

High above, something answered.

A flare of pale light arced across the sky, hanging there for a breath too long before snapping downward like a finger pointing straight at her.

Rachel swore and ran.

Behind her, horns blared again—different this time, sharper, faster.

She had made it out.

And the whole city had noticed.
