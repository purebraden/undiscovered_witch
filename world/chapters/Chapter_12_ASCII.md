# Chapter 12

Rachel woke before the pressure changed, lying on the stone floor with her cheek against the wall, eyes open, breathing shallow and uneven while water dripped somewhere in the room and her pulse beat heavy in her ears.

Her leg ached in a dull, spreading way, the pain sinking deeper when she shifted and easing slightly when she stayed still.

The room looked the same.

Light held steady in the slits near the ceiling. The basin still held water. The cloth and the bread sat where she had left them, folded and untouched.

Rachel did not move.

After a while, the pressure in her chest tightened, not sharply, not enough to force her upright, but enough to draw her attention toward the sealed passage, the air near that wall thickening as she watched it.

She pushed herself up slowly, palms sliding against the stone.

Her joints protested. Her leg shook when she bent it, skin pulling where the blood had dried. She paused.

The pressure did not spike.

A sound came from the other side of the wall.

Footsteps.

Slow and even. More than one set.

Rachel stood with her back to the wall, hands hanging at her sides, fingers curling and uncurling without direction.

The stone panel slid back into the wall without warning, moving smoothly until the passage stood open again.

Two people stood there.

They did not look at her.

One was tall and broad-shouldered, dressed in dark clothing that blended into the stone behind him. The other was shorter, carrying a bundle tucked under one arm. They stepped into the room together and stopped just inside, leaving space between themselves and the walls.

The pressure stayed low.

The shorter one crossed the room and knelt beside the basin, setting the bundle on the stone. She unfolded it piece by piece, laying out clean bandages, a small jar, a narrow blade that caught the light when she turned it.

Rachel stayed where she was.

The taller one remained near the entrance, gaze fixed on the far wall, shoulders loose, feet planted.

The shorter one dipped her fingers into the basin, tested the water, then turned and looked at Rachel.

Her eyes moved over Rachel's face and down to her leg.

"Sit," she said.

Rachel did not answer.

The pressure remained unchanged.

The woman waited.

Rachel lowered herself onto the bench, movements stiff and uneven, jaw tight as she extended her injured leg. The stone felt cold under her hands.

The woman cut away the blood-stiffened fabric and cleaned the wound, her movements efficient, the sensation sharp and lingering. Rachel gripped the edge of the bench and breathed through it, eyes fixed on the woman's hands.

Behind her, the taller one shifted his weight once.

That was the only sound.

When the bandage was finished, the woman packed her tools away, folding the cloth, securing the blade, returning each piece to the bundle.

She stood and stepped back without looking at Rachel.

"Don't walk on it too hard," she said.

Rachel opened her mouth.

The pressure tightened.

She closed it again.

The two of them left the room without another word. The stone panel slid shut behind them, sealing the passage.

The pressure remained.

Rachel sat on the bench and watched as a darker stain crept into the clean white bandage at the edges.

She stayed there.

After some time, the pressure shifted again, drawing her attention away from the sealed passage and toward the metal frame bolted into the floor.

Rachel stood.

Her leg held.

She took one step toward the frame.

The pressure loosened.

She stopped.

The room stayed quiet.

Rachel backed away until her shoulders met the wall and slid down, sitting hard on the stone, breath coming faster than she could slow it.

The frame remained where it was.
