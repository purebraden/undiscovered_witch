# Chapter 5

The ceiling came down in pieces first.

Stone cracked and split overhead, dust pouring down in choking sheets as heat rolled through the chamber in a sudden, violent wave. Somewhere above, something screamed—not human, not quite—and then cut off abruptly.

Fire followed.

Not a rush. Not a blast. A deliberate flooding, pouring down the stairwell and through the cracks in the stone like something being carefully applied.

Rachel curled tighter inside the circle, arms over her head, lungs burning as the air grew thick and hot. The light under her skin pulsed sluggishly now, heavy as syrup, dragging at her thoughts.

The cages screamed.

Metal shrieked as iron bars glowed red, then white. The things inside howled and clawed and begged in voices that twisted words into sounds Rachel couldn’t untangle.

“Open it!” one shrieked.
“Let us out—let us out—”
“We helped you—”

They burned anyway.

Rachel smelled it. Sweet and rancid and thick, coating the back of her throat until she gagged and retched again, black flecks splattering the stone.

She tried to crawl.

The circle held.

Chains rattled violently as the heat intensified, the anchors screaming in protest. One of them snapped loose with a sound like a gunshot, whipping free and carving a deep groove through the stone.

The figure stood unmoving at the center of it all.

Fire washed over its back and broke around it like water around a rock.

“Breathe,” it said calmly. “If you pass out, you die.”

“I thought—” Rachel choked. “You said—”

“I said I would teach you how not to die,” it replied. “This is part of it.”

The floor lurched.

Stone gave way beneath one side of the chamber, collapsing inward as the ceiling finally failed. A section of wall tore loose, exposing a yawning shaft beyond, black and deep and swallowing heat as fast as it came.

The pull inside Rachel twisted.

Not toward the figure.

Toward the hole.

She screamed as the circle loosened—not released, just weakened—and the light surged outward in a ragged, desperate pulse.

Chains tore free.

The circle ruptured.

Rachel was thrown forward as the stone beneath her feet vanished, tumbling end over end into open air. She hit hard, rolled, and slid down a steep incline of broken masonry, skin scraping raw, lungs screaming.

She didn’t stop until something caught her by the ankle.

Rachel shrieked and kicked blindly, pain flaring bright and hot.

The grip loosened.

She scrambled away on hands and knees, coughing, eyes streaming, the heat finally fading behind her as she slid into darkness.

The tunnel leveled out and spat her onto cold stone slick with moisture.

She lay there gasping, chest heaving, every nerve screaming.

Behind her, the chamber collapsed fully.

The sound rolled through the earth like thunder.

Rachel laughed once. A broken, hysterical sound she couldn’t stop.

Then she sobered.

Her leg burned.

Not pain. Something deeper.

She pushed herself up and looked down.

The wound where the bolt had been was gone.

Not healed. Gone.

The flesh was smooth and darkened, the skin marked by a faint, circular scar that pulsed slowly, almost lazily, in time with her heartbeat.

She touched it.

The light under her skin answered immediately, flaring low and heavy.

Rachel jerked her hand back, breathing hard.

Footsteps echoed faintly somewhere above. Voices shouted, distant now but spreading fast.

She staggered to her feet and limped into the darkness, smoke still pouring from the tunnel mouth behind her.

She had escaped.

Behind her, the demons burned.

And whatever she was now walked on alone.
