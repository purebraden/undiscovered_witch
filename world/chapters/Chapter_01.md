# Chapter 1

The pain came first.

Not sharp. Not clean. Just wrong, like being poured into a container that was already full and still filling, sloshing everywhere it wasn’t supposed to go.

Stone slammed into Rachel’s ribs. Hard enough to knock the air out of her. Cold grit scraped her cheek. Lines carved into the floor burned white-hot against her skin, too bright to look at, too hot to ignore, as the world snapped sideways and then tried—badly—to stay that way.

Someone screamed nearby. High and cracking, the sound of it already breaking apart. Another voice cut through it, barking orders, fast and angry, in a language that felt sharp in the mouth even when Rachel didn’t understand a word of it.

“No—don’t anchor it yet—”

Something yanked at her spine.

Not muscle. Not bone. Something behind the eyes, hooked deep and pulling forward like it wanted her inside out.

Rachel tried to breathe and tasted blood. Thick and coppery. She gagged and swallowed it anyway.

Hands grabbed her arms. Rough hands. Gloved. Fingers digging in too hard, like they were afraid she might dissolve if they didn’t hold tight enough. A face leaned into view, young and pale and splattered with dark spots that looked wet.

“You’re not supposed to be *this early*,” the man said.

His voice shook. So did his hands.

The circle flared.

Light crawled along the carved lines, ugly-colored, wrong-colored, the kind that made her teeth ache and her stomach turn. The pull intensified, dragging at her chest like it wanted everything inside her out at once, heart and lungs and all of it.

Someone shouted, “One anchor is dead—cut it, cut it—”

The dead man lay half inside the circle.

His legs were gone. Just gone. Ended cleanly, like they’d been sliced away with something impossibly thin. The rest of him twitched when the light pulsed, small useless movements that made Rachel’s stomach flip.

She screamed.

It wasn’t words. It wasn’t even a sound she recognized. Just pressure breaking loose, panic tearing its way out through her throat because it didn’t have anywhere else to go.

Something answered.

The air bent.

For a fraction of a second, the room flipped—floor above, ceiling below—and then gravity slammed back into place wrong. Bones cracked. Someone hit the wall hard enough to leave a red smear that slid slowly downward. A woman was lifted off her feet, spun once, and folded backward around nothing, her scream cutting off in the middle like someone had pinched a wire.

The circle shattered.

Stone blew upward in chunks. The pull snapped, recoiling like a broken tendon, and Rachel went with it, thrown clear, skidding across the floor until her shoulder hit a column hard enough to turn the edges of the room black.

She lay there gasping, ears ringing, lungs burning, light still crawling over her skin. It didn’t fade. It sank in, slow and heavy, like it meant to stay.

The quiet didn’t last.

Alarms started howling. Deep ones. Layered ones. The kind that crawled into your teeth and wouldn’t leave. Doors slammed open somewhere nearby. Boots pounded closer, too many of them, coming fast.

“Containment breach!” someone yelled. “It’s unstable—don’t let it touch the—”

Too late.

Rachel pushed herself up, slipping in blood that wasn’t all hers. Her hands shook so badly she almost fell again. Her chest hurt in a way that felt wrong, deep and structural, like something inside had been knocked loose and hadn’t settled back right.

Across the chamber, the young summoner stared at her.

Horror hollowed his face out, left it empty.

“You’re not supposed to survive the break,” he said, very quietly.

Crossbow bolts slammed into the stone around her.

One of them punched through Rachel’s leg like it barely noticed the bone on the way through. Pain detonated, bright and blinding, and she screamed again as she staggered sideways, nearly going down.

She didn’t think.

She didn’t plan.

She just wanted it to stop.

The light surged outward.

The nearest guard vanished.

Not burned. Not torn apart. Just gone, like someone had erased him too hard. The wall behind where he’d been folded inward with a shriek as stone compressed into itself, cracking and collapsing like it was made of clay.

Dust and fragments blasted through the chamber. People went down. Someone was crying. Someone else was praying.

“Kill it now!” a voice shouted.

Another voice cut in, older and steadier, like this wasn’t its first ruined room.

“No. It’s breached but coherent. If it gets out—”

The iron doors at the far end of the chamber buckled. Cracks raced through the metal like veins as something on the other side slammed into them again and again, hard enough to bow the frame.

Drawn by the alarms. By the rupture. By the sudden scent of *new*.

The young summoner backed away, shaking so badly he nearly dropped the knife in his hand.

“We have to collapse the hall,” he said. “We have to bury it—”

Rachel looked at the doors.  
Then at the bodies.  
Then at the light burning under her skin, shifting, changing, like it was paying attention.

She ran.

Bolts chased her down the corridor, clattering and sparking. One grazed her neck, hot and close. Another shattered a torch, plunging the hall into smoke and falling embers.

Behind her, the doors finally gave way.

Something screamed, long and hungry.

Rachel didn’t look back.

She didn’t slow down.

And somewhere deep inside her, the thing that had answered her panic shifted and settled, like it had been waiting for this exact moment.
