# Chapter 8

The corridor did not change after the man left.

The light stayed even along the walls, pale and flat, thinning the shadows without erasing them, and the vibration in the stone continued at a low, steady pitch that Rachel felt more in her teeth than her ears, a hum that never quite faded no matter how still she tried to be.

She sat with her back against the wall, knees drawn up, hands laced together until her fingers went numb and then hurt again when the feeling came back. Each time she shifted her weight the pressure in her chest stirred, a brief tightening that loosened when she stilled. When she stayed very still, it eased further, as if rewarding her for not testing it.

Her breathing slowed to match it.

Time stretched without markers. No footsteps. No voices. Just the hum in the stone and the ache in her leg, dull and deep, sitting under the skin instead of flaring the way pain was supposed to.

When footsteps came again, they were wrong.

Uneven. One heavier than the other. A rhythm that didn't belong to someone walking alone.

Rachel lifted her head and waited.

A woman came into view at the far end of the corridor, moving carefully, her eyes tracking the seams in the stone and the shallow grooves cut across the floor as if she had been warned about them. She carried nothing in her hands. A boy followed a step behind her, smaller, restless, his attention skittering ahead of him, fingers brushing the wall as he walked.

"Don't touch anything," the woman said quietly.

"I'm not," the boy said, already reaching.

The pressure in Rachel's chest tightened.

The woman saw Rachel and stopped, breath catching, one hand lifting toward the boy without quite touching him.

"It's all right," the woman said, though her voice thinned as she spoke. "She's hurt."

Rachel opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

Her throat felt thick, her tongue slow and clumsy, as if the words were lined up wrong.

The boy leaned around the woman's side, eyes dropping to Rachel's leg, to the dark stain spreading through the fabric and the mark just visible beneath it.

"Did it bite you?" he asked.

The pressure spiked.

Nausea surged fast and sharp, her vision blurring at the edges as her fingers dug into the fabric at her sleeves. The corridor seemed to tilt, the light smearing along the seams in a way that made distance hard to judge.

"Stay back," the woman said, louder now.

The boy stepped forward anyway.

Rachel pushed herself up from the floor too quickly. The world pitched. Her leg buckled and she caught herself against the wall, breath tearing out of her chest.

"I can-" she started.

The sound came out wrong.

The stone under the boy's foot shifted, not much, just enough to steal his balance. He pitched forward with a startled cry and the woman lunged to catch him.

Her hand brushed Rachel's sleeve.

The vibration deepened.

The stone shuddered beneath their feet. The air compressed, thick and heavy, forcing the breath from Rachel's lungs in a harsh gasp as the space seemed to fold inward.

The woman was thrown sideways, striking the wall hard enough to rattle the seams. She dropped out of sight with a sharp, broken sound.

The boy hit the floor and didn't move.

Rachel screamed.

The sound vanished into the ringing in her ears as she staggered backward, boots skidding, hands scraping against the wall without finding purchase. The light flared brighter, then dimmed, then settled again into its earlier flatness.

"Don't move," a voice shouted from farther down the corridor.

Footsteps followed, fast and measured, the cadence wrong for panic.

Rachel dropped to her knees beside the boy, hands hovering inches above him, fingers shaking, unsure where to land. His chest moved, shallow and fast, a wet sound rattling in his throat.

Behind her, the woman groaned and tried to rise, one arm hanging at an angle that did not match the rest of her.

Rachel backed away.

The pressure eased immediately, the tightness in her chest loosening as she put distance between herself and the bodies.

The vibration settled back into its earlier rhythm.

Three people entered the corridor, coats dark, movements precise and economical. One knelt beside the boy without hesitation, hands already working. Another went to the woman, voice low and steady, words lost under the hum in the stone.

The third stopped in front of Rachel.

"Stay where you are," he said.

Rachel pressed her back to the wall and slid down until she was sitting again, shaking hard enough that her teeth clicked.

"I didn't-" she said.

The man raised a hand.

"I know," he said.

The boy coughed, wet and ragged, and the kneeling figure leaned closer, murmuring something Rachel couldn't hear. The woman cried out as her arm was set, the sound sharp and ugly.

The pressure in Rachel's chest remained low and watchful, not easing, not tightening, just there.

The man in front of her didn't raise his voice. He didn't step closer.

"This space is no longer usable," he said.

Rachel laughed once, the sound short and rough in her throat, and then stopped when no one reacted.

Down the corridor, the light dimmed one seam at a time, retreating slowly and methodically until the edges of the space softened and blurred.

The pressure tightened again, urging Rachel away from the injured, away from the hands and voices gathering behind her.

She stood.

No one stopped her.

As she limped past, the boy's eyes fluttered open. His mouth worked soundlessly, trying to form something that didn't come.

Rachel didn't slow.

The corridor bent away from them, stone swallowing light and sound until only her breathing and the steady pull in her chest remained, drawing her forward into darker, narrower space.

Behind her, the lights went out.
