# Chapter 3

Rachel ran until the street narrowed and the buildings leaned in close, until the bells faded behind her and the horns stretched thin and distant, like something losing interest.

The pull didn’t fade.

It tightened.

Not a yank. Not a shove. Just a steady, patient pressure in her chest, like a finger hooked gently into her sternum and pulling her sideways.

“Stop it,” she muttered, breath ragged. “Stop.”

It didn’t.

She tried to cut left, away from it.

Pain flared behind her eyes. Not sharp. Not clean. A dull crushing pressure that made her vision tunnel and her knees wobble.

Rachel swore and staggered back the other way, heart hammering.

The pressure eased.

“Fuck,” she whispered, and followed it.

The streets changed as she went.

The stone underfoot grew uneven, older, patched and repatched until it looked like scars laid over scars. The buildings thinned, their windows dark and narrow, doors reinforced with iron bands and old symbols carved deep enough to weather centuries.

No one ran here.

They watched.

Eyes from behind shutters. From cracks in doors. From alleys that smelled like rot and damp fur. The light under Rachel’s skin pulsed brighter with every step, and with it came whispers—not voices exactly, more like pressure changes in the air, like something breathing just out of sync with her.

She limped past a narrow square with a dry fountain at its center. Something dark stained the basin. Old. Flaked.

The pull sharpened again, tugging her toward a stairwell cut into the side of a building that looked less built than piled together.

The door at the top hung open.

Rachel slowed, dread crawling up her spine.

Behind her, far off now, horns sounded again. Closer than she liked. Faster.

She looked back once, just long enough to see pale light flicker at the far end of the street, figures moving in practiced formation.

Then she turned and went down the stairs.

The air changed immediately.

Cooler. Damp. Heavy with the smell of mold and something faintly sweet underneath, like spoiled fruit. The stairs spiraled down farther than they should have, stone slick under her boots, the walls close enough that her shoulders brushed on both sides.

The pull grew almost gentle.

Encouraging.

Rachel hated it.

At the bottom, the stairwell opened into a low chamber lit by guttering lamps. Iron cages lined the walls, some empty, some not.

Something shifted inside one of them.

Rachel froze.

Chains rattled softly. A shape moved closer to the bars, tall and thin, joints bending the wrong way. Pale eyes opened in the dark and fixed on her, pupils widening.

It smiled.

“Oh,” it said, voice soft and pleased. “You can see us.”

Rachel backed up a step.

The pull yanked.

Hard.

She gasped as her chest seized, pain blooming sharp and sudden, and she stumbled forward despite herself. The light under her skin flared bright enough to cast warped shadows across the chamber.

The thing in the cage laughed.

Another voice joined it. Then another.

From the shadows, from behind bars, from places Rachel hadn’t even noticed, shapes stirred and leaned closer, eyes gleaming, mouths stretching too wide.

“Fresh,” someone murmured.
“Uncut,” said another.
“Not sealed,” breathed a third, reverent and hungry.

Rachel’s leg buckled. She caught herself on a post slick with old grime, heart slamming so hard she thought it might tear loose.

“What is this place,” she whispered.

The pull answered.

It dragged her gaze to the far end of the chamber, where something large lay half-submerged in shadow. A circle—older than the one she’d broken, carved deeper, worn smooth by time and use. Chains anchored it at eight points, each one disappearing into the stone.

At the center sat a figure.

Not bound. Not caged.

Waiting.

It looked human at first glance. Too human. Naked feet on stone. Hands folded loosely in its lap. Long dark hair hanging in tangled ropes down its back.

When it lifted its head, Rachel felt the light inside her recoil.

“You followed,” the figure said, voice calm and amused. “Good.”

Rachel shook her head. “I didn’t.”

“You did,” it said gently. “You just didn’t know you were choosing.”

The chains around the circle trembled.

Somewhere above them, horns blared again—louder now, closer, the sound echoing down through the stone.

The figure smiled wider.

“They’ll be here soon,” it said. “And when they are, they’ll burn this place to keep you from us.”

The pull tightened one last time, sharp enough to make Rachel cry out.

“So,” the figure continued, rising smoothly to its feet, light glinting wrong across its eyes, “you can run back up those stairs and die screaming.”

It stepped closer, just to the edge of the circle.

“Or,” it said softly, “you can step inside and learn why they’re so afraid of what you are.”

The cages rattled.

The things inside pressed forward, eager.

Rachel stood there shaking, blood soaking her boot, light burning under her skin, the horns above and the monsters below.
