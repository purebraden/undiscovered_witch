# Chapter 34

Rachel lay on the floor until the shaking stopped.

It took longer than she wanted to admit.

Her teeth finally unclenched first. Her hands followed, fingers uncurling in slow, reluctant increments as the tremor drained out of her arms and left them aching and weak. The pressure stayed close but dull, not pressing, not pulling, just there, like a weight she'd grown used to carrying wrong.

She focused on breathing.

In.
Out.

When she shifted her legs, nothing flared. No heat. No spike. Just soreness and a dragging heaviness that made her feel older than she should have been.

Rachel swallowed and let her head rest back against the wall.

The door didn't move.

The light under it stayed thin and steady.

For a while, that was all that happened.

---

Time passed.

She didn't know how much. Long enough for the ache in her shoulder to settle into something bearable. Long enough for her breathing to even out. Long enough that the silence stopped feeling like it was about to explode.

Rachel slid down the wall until she was sitting, knees drawn up, arms wrapped loosely around them. The floor was cold, but she didn't bother trying to move away from it. Cold was better than guessing.

The pressure felt distant.

Not gone. Just quiet. Spread thin enough that she could almost forget it if she didn't move too fast or think about it too hard.

She closed her eyes.

Nothing happened.

No hum. No vibration. No voices bleeding through the walls.

For the first time since the door had gone wrong, the room felt like a room again.

Rachel let her forehead rest against her knees and stayed there, waiting for the catch.

---

The hum came back wrong.

She felt it before she heard it, a low vibration through the floor that didn't belong to any rhythm she recognized. Her eyes snapped open as the stone beneath her shuddered once, then again, the movement traveling sideways instead of up.

Rachel lifted her head slowly.

The light under the door dimmed.

Not flickered. Not surged.

Dimmed, like something had turned it down on purpose.

The etched lines brightened in response.

Heat rolled through her chest a second later, late and sluggish, and she gasped as the pressure tightened belatedly, squeezing around pain that had already arrived.

"Okay," she said, the word coming out thin.

The vibration deepened.

Somewhere beyond the door, something shifted, not a step, not a scrape, just the sound of space changing shape. The hum dropped lower, settling into a pitch that made her bones ache.

Rachel braced her hands on the floor and pushed herself upright.

Her vision swam. The pressure lagged again, clamping down after she was already unsteady, and she had to stop halfway, breathing hard, waiting for the room to stop tilting.

The door didn't open.

It didn't break.

It adjusted.

The light under it slid sideways, no longer centered in the seam, as if the frame itself had shifted. The etched lines pulsed once, brighter than before, and the pressure reacted too late to stop the heat that tore through her ribs.

Rachel cried out and dropped back to the floor, curling instinctively as the wave passed.

When it eased, she stayed still, afraid to move, afraid not to.

The hum held steady.

The light did not return to what it had been.

Whatever the building was doing now, it wasn't reacting to her anymore.

It was reorganizing.

Rachel lay there, breath shallow, the pressure hovering wrong and delayed inside her chest.

She had the awful, sinking sense that the quiet hadn't been a mercy.

It had been preparation.
