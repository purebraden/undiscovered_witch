# Chapter 8

The corridor stayed the same after the man left.

The light remained even along the walls, pale and flat, thinning the shadows without removing them entirely, and the vibration in the stone continued at a low, steady pitch that Rachel felt more in her teeth than her ears.

She sat with her back against the wall, knees drawn up, hands laced together until her fingers went numb. When she shifted her weight the pressure in her chest stirred, then eased again as she stilled. When she stayed still, it loosened further.

Her breathing slowed to match it.

Time passed without markers.

When footsteps sounded again they were not the same as before - uneven, one heavier than the other, the rhythm wrong for someone walking alone.

Rachel lifted her head.

A woman came into view at the far end of the corridor, moving carefully, her eyes tracking the seams in the stone and the shallow grooves cut across the floor. She carried nothing in her hands. A boy followed a step behind her, smaller, his attention skittering ahead of him, fingers brushing the wall as he walked.

"Don't touch anything," the woman said quietly.

"I'm not," the boy said, already reaching.

The pressure in Rachel's chest shifted.

The woman saw Rachel and stopped, breath catching, one hand lifting toward the boy without quite touching him.

"It's all right," the woman said, voice thin. "She's hurt."

Rachel opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

The boy leaned around the woman's side, eyes dropping to Rachel's leg, to the dark stain spreading through the fabric and the mark just visible beneath it.

"Did it bite you?" he asked.

The pressure spiked.

Rachel's vision blurred at the edges as nausea rose fast and sharp. Her fingers dug into the fabric at her sleeves.

"Stay back," the woman said, louder now.

The boy stepped forward instead.

Rachel pushed herself up from the floor too quickly. The corridor tilted. Light smeared along the seams.

"I can-" she started.

The sound came out wrong.

The stone under the boy's foot shifted, just enough to steal his balance. He pitched forward, arms flailing, and the woman lunged to catch him.

Her hand brushed Rachel's sleeve.

The vibration deepened.

The stone shuddered. The air compressed, thick and heavy, forcing breath from Rachel's lungs in a harsh gasp. The woman was thrown sideways, striking the wall hard enough to rattle the seams. She dropped out of sight.

The boy hit the floor and stayed there.

Rachel screamed.

The sound vanished into the ringing in her ears as she staggered backward, boots skidding, hands scraping against the wall without finding purchase. The light flared brighter, then dimmed, then steadied again.

"Don't move," a voice shouted from farther down the corridor.

Footsteps followed, fast and measured.

Rachel dropped to her knees beside the boy, hands hovering inches above him, fingers shaking, unsure where to land. His chest moved, shallow and fast.

Behind her, the woman groaned and tried to rise, one arm hanging at an angle that didn't match the rest of her.

Rachel backed away.

The pressure eased as soon as she did.

The vibration settled back into its earlier rhythm.

Three people entered the corridor, coats dark, movements precise. One knelt beside the boy without hesitation, hands already working. Another went to the woman, voice low, steady, words lost under the hum in the stone.

The third stopped in front of Rachel.

"Stay where you are," he said.

Rachel pressed her back to the wall and slid down until she was sitting again, shaking hard enough that her teeth clicked.

"I didn't-" she said.

The man raised a hand.

"I know," he said.

The boy coughed, wet and ragged. The sound tore through the corridor and the kneeling figure leaned closer, murmuring something Rachel couldn't hear.

The woman cried out as her arm was set.

The pressure in Rachel's chest remained low and watchful.

The man in front of her didn't raise his voice. He didn't step closer.

"This space is no longer usable," he said.

Rachel laughed once, the sound short and rough in her throat.

The man didn't respond.

Down the corridor, the light dimmed one seam at a time, retreating slowly, methodically, until the edges of the space softened and blurred.

The pressure tightened, urging Rachel away from the injured, away from the noise and hands and voices gathering behind her.

She stood.

No one stopped her.

As she limped past, the boy's eyes fluttered open. His mouth worked soundlessly.

Rachel didn't slow.

The corridor bent away from them, stone swallowing light and sound until only her breathing and the steady pull in her chest remained, drawing her forward into darker, narrower space.

Behind her, the lights went out.
