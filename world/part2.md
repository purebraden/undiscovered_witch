# Chapter 17 — Light Before Sound

The light reached her boots before the sound reached her ears.

A narrow band slid across the floor, pale and steady, cutting the dark into measured sections. It stopped when it touched the toe of her left boot and stayed there, unwavering, as if waiting for something to line up.

Rachel froze.

Pressure gathered under her ribs. Not pain. Not yet. Just enough weight to warn her that stillness was the wrong answer.

She shifted her weight.

The light brightened a fraction.

Something hummed behind it. Low and even, the sound of a system running exactly as intended.

Rachel stepped back. The stone beneath her heel warmed, heat blooming too fast to be natural. The pressure tightened, nausea rising sharp and fast, and she lurched sideways instead, catching herself on the wall as the light slid forward and settled where she had been standing.

The unit rolled into view.

It stayed low to the ground, a wide ring moving without visible joints. The light came from inside it, sweeping outward in slow arcs that never crossed the walls, never rushed ahead.

It followed at a fixed distance.

Rachel limped down the corridor, favoring her bad leg, every step negotiated between pain and the pressure urging her onward. The walls narrowed ahead, seams faintly glowing as systems woke and adjusted.

The unit followed.

Not closer.

Close enough.

The pressure shoved her right at the junction before she registered the turn. She scraped her shoulder on stone and bit back a sound as nausea hit hard enough to steal her breath. Black crept at the edges of her vision. She gagged and kept moving.

Behind her, the hum deepened by a note.

The corridor dipped into a service run lined with pipes. Water dripped steadily into a channel cut down the center of the floor. Rust flaked under her fingers as she crossed, boots slipping on damp stone.

The pressure flared as her foot slid. She went down on one knee, the impact jolting pain up her leg, and braced herself against the wall.

The unit stopped at the threshold.

Its light swept the space again, slower now, as if taking inventory.

Rachel dragged herself upright and limped toward the far end of the run. Her leg burned, then went strangely numb, sensation thinning until it felt distant and hollow. The pressure settled, dense and focused, bracing her instead of driving her.

Overhead, a pipe rattled as she passed. Another shuddered, vibrating in a way that made her teeth ache. The pressure twisted, sharp and insistent, and the pipe burst with a crack, spraying hot water into the channel.

Steam filled the run.

The unit rolled forward.

Its light cut through the steam and dimmed, just slightly, before correcting.

Rachel ran.

Not fast. Not clean. Her body moved because it was already moving, because stopping felt worse than falling. The corridor bent twice and sloped downward, the floor slick beneath her boots.

The pressure surged ahead of her, yanking her left at the next split before she could think about it. She slammed a hand into the wall to stay upright, palm skidding over stone.

The surface puckered under her touch.

A crack raced outward and vanished around the bend.

She pulled her hand back and kept going.

The hum behind her changed pitch.

She burst into a wider passage and skidded to a halt.

A man stood near a stack of crates, hands lifting slowly as he saw her. A dull white line marked his vest, the same one she had seen painted on doors. His gaze flicked to her leg, to the faint pulse beneath her skin, and his throat worked.

“Don’t,” he said.

Rachel opened her mouth.

The pressure slammed down and stole the word.

The hum grew louder.

The man’s eyes slid past her shoulder. Color drained from his face.

“Go,” he said, louder now. “Go now.”

Rachel tried to move past him.

The pressure flared sideways.

The floor between them dipped, just enough to knock him off balance. He stumbled into the crates and went down hard, a sharp cry torn from his throat.

“No,” Rachel gasped. “I didn’t—”

The unit entered the passage.

The light locked onto the fallen man.

The pressure recoiled, tight and offended, then braced hard against Rachel’s ribs.

She turned and ran.

The corridor narrowed into a crawl. She ducked and scraped and dragged herself through, the hum echoing too loud behind her, too close. A door slid open as she hit it shoulder-first, and she tumbled through.

The door slammed shut with a clang that rattled the walls.

The hum cut off.

Rachel lay on the floor, chest heaving, forehead pressed to cold stone. Feeling rushed back into her leg in a hot, punishing wave. She waited for the pressure to move her.

It didn’t.

It held.

She pushed herself up and looked around.

The room was small and square. A single light strip hummed overhead. A bench sat against one wall. Near the ceiling, a narrow grate spilled in cooler air that smelled of oil and dust.

Lena crouched beside the bench.

She looked thinner. One arm was wrapped in a stained bandage. Her eyes flicked to the door, then back to Rachel, sharp and quick.

“Don’t stand there,” Lena said. “It’ll find the heat.”

Rachel stared. Her mouth opened, closed.

“You’re alive,” she said.

Lena snorted softly. “I noticed.”

The pressure twitched under Rachel’s ribs.

“How did you—”

“Later.” Lena stood and grabbed Rachel’s arm, fingers digging in hard enough to hurt. “Move.”

She dragged Rachel toward a low panel set into the wall. It slid aside at her touch, revealing a dark slot barely wide enough for one person.

“Go.”

Rachel hesitated.

The pressure leaned forward.

She dropped to her knees and crawled, scraping shoulder and hip, biting back a sound as pain flared. Lena followed and slammed the panel shut behind them.

They crawled in the dark. The space stank of old metal and damp stone. Rachel’s breath rasped loud in her ears. The pressure stayed heavy and still, like it was listening.

The slot opened into a maintenance shaft. Lena rolled out and hauled Rachel after her, bracing her weight without comment.

They moved.

Down ladders. Through side runs. Past places Rachel remembered being shoved away from before. Lena didn’t slow. She moved like someone who had memorized where things failed.

“Why were you there?” Rachel asked between breaths.

“Because they put me where things break.”

Behind them, the hum started again.

Farther away.

Rachel stumbled. Lena swore and pulled her upright.

“That thing’s tighter now,” Lena said.

“It locked on,” Rachel said.

Lena’s mouth flattened. “I figured.”

They reached a junction marked with three scratched symbols. Lena paused, head tilted, listening.

The pressure twitched toward the left passage.

Lena turned right.

The pressure flared hot and sudden, nausea tearing up Rachel’s throat. She gagged and grabbed the wall, vision swimming.

“Stop,” Rachel gasped.

Lena hesitated.

The hum deepened behind them.

“Trust me,” Lena said.

The pressure surged again, harder this time. Rachel cried out as her knees buckled.

Lena cursed and dragged her into the right-hand passage anyway.

The nausea eased, settling into a dull, workable throb. Rachel could see again. She could keep her feet.

They ran.

The corridor opened into a low passage lined with old doors. Some stood open. Some were sealed. Pale light leaked from beneath a few of them, thin and wrong.

“We don’t take doors,” Lena said, slowing. “We take gaps.”

She kicked a loose panel. It fell inward, revealing another narrow run.

They slipped through as the hum rounded the corner behind them. The panel slid back into place.

Rachel slumped against the wall, shaking.

“Why help me?” she said. “They took you because of me.”

Lena watched the seam in the panel, listening. When she spoke, her voice stayed low.

“They take people all the time,” she said. “You just make it faster.”

The pressure shifted, uneasy.

“You’re not from here,” Rachel said.

Lena glanced at her. “Neither are you. Not after a circle spits you out.”

“Where are you from?”

Lena snorted. “Someplace that doesn’t matter anymore.”

The hum passed on the other side of the wall, light bleeding briefly through the seam before fading.

Silence followed.

“There are others,” Lena said. “Routes. People who move cargo.”

“Cargo,” Rachel said.

“That’s us.”

The pressure tightened, not in warning but in agreement.

“There’s a place,” Lena continued. “Past the routes. Past the lines. Where they stop looking.”

Rachel swallowed. “Free.”

Lena’s mouth twitched. “Alive,” she said. “Sometimes that’s close enough.”

The pressure leaned forward, not toward the wall, but toward the dark ahead.

Lena stood. “We move before it recalibrates.”

Rachel pushed herself up. Her leg protested, then held.

They slipped deeper into the dark as the stone behind them began, quietly, to warm.

# Chapter 18 — A Mistake in the Wall

The gap wasn’t a tunnel so much as a mistake the wall hadn’t corrected yet.

Stone bowed inward, then out again, its surface uneven in a way Rachel couldn’t keep her eyes on for long. If she stared, her vision slipped; if she didn’t, the sense of movement stayed anyway, like the place was remembering how it should have been shaped.

Lena went first, not pausing, not checking.

Rachel followed and felt the pressure crowd her chest, heavy and unhelpful, not guiding her so much as waiting to see what she’d do wrong. The air on the other side smelled scraped clean—dry metal, old heat, something burned out and never replaced.

Crates filled the room, stacked with too much care for a place this temporary. Chalk lines crossed the floor in shapes that almost closed, their edges blurred where boots had scuffed them away. Rachel stepped over one without thinking and felt the pressure tighten, sharp and irritated, like she’d kicked a live wire.

Someone shifted in the dark.

A woman stepped forward, short and thick through the shoulders, gloves darkened at the fingertips. She raised a handheld scanner, its casing cracked, its buzz uneven and insect-thin.

She looked at Lena.

Then at Rachel.

The scanner screamed.

“Fuck,” the woman said, already moving back.

Lena snapped, “Cut it.”

The woman didn’t argue. She smashed the scanner against the wall until it went silent, the sound echoing too long, too clean.

“You didn’t say it was like this,” she said, eyes still on Rachel. “You said injured. You said manageable.”

Rachel felt her leg pulse under her skin and hated that her body had learned to announce itself.

“I said she moves,” Lena replied. “That’s all.”

The woman let out a short laugh that held no humor. “She doesn’t move. She ruins.”

The floor shifted under Rachel’s feet, not enough to throw her, just enough to make her stomach flip. One of the chalk lines cracked and burned away to bare stone.

The hum wasn’t there yet, but the room felt thinner, like it had been marked.

Lights dimmed, brightened, settled wrong.

“We won’t stay,” Rachel said, her voice louder than she meant it to be.

“You won’t,” the woman said. “I won’t either if you leave right now.”

Lena grabbed Rachel’s arm and pulled her toward the far wall, boots scraping as the floor pitched again, a fraction steeper this time.

“No,” the woman said. “You’re burning my node.”

The pressure swelled without direction, dense and clumsy. A crate slid an inch across the floor and bumped another with a hollow sound.

“Stop,” Lena said, not looking at Rachel.

Rachel tried to still herself and failed. The pressure didn’t care about intent.

“You know what happens next,” the woman said. “You know how fast they trace a summoning spill.”

“I know,” Lena said, too quickly.

The hum arrived like the start of a migraine, low and present, close enough to feel in Rachel’s teeth.

The woman moved fast, ripping open a crate and shoving a bundle into Lena’s hands. “Route three is dead. Don’t touch doors. If the walls start sweating, you run.”

“We always run,” Lena said.

The woman’s gaze slid back to Rachel. “Not fast enough.”

The crate beside Rachel split with a sharp crack, metal spilling across the floor.

The woman bolted.

Lights snapped off as she ran, plunging the room into uneven emergency glow. Somewhere deeper, a door sealed hard enough to shake dust loose from the ceiling.

Lena dragged Rachel into the dark and didn’t slow when Rachel stumbled.

They ran through a passage that narrowed too quickly, Rachel clipping her shoulder hard enough to tear skin. The walls pulsed faintly as they passed, heat bleeding through stone like a fever coming on.

The pressure crowded her forward, dragging her through turns she hadn’t seen yet, leaving her breathless and angry and unable to tell which part of her was driving.

The hum thickened.

Rachel’s leg burned, then didn’t. The pain didn’t leave; it slid somewhere duller, less urgent, and she hated the quiet more than the ache.

They burst into another chamber.

Smaller. Cleaner. A man waited there with one hand on a lever set into the wall, eyes already glassy like he’d been listening too long.

He looked at Lena, then at Rachel, and shook his head once.

“No.”

Lena didn’t slow. “Open it.”

“You’re too loud,” he said, voice thin.

The pressure spread, sloppy and wide, brushing the room. The lever rattled in its housing as the wall behind him bowed inward, stone groaning.

“I can’t,” he said. “If I do, they follow me.”

“They follow you anyway,” Lena said. “She’s here.”

Rachel took a step forward without meaning to, and the pressure surged with her, uncontrolled. A seam in the ceiling lit up.

The man’s mouth worked. He swallowed.

“Vent,” he said. “Don’t touch the floor plates.”

He yanked the lever.

A panel shrieked open and heat rolled out, dry and biting. Lena shoved Rachel toward it without slowing.

Rachel dropped to her hands and knees and crawled, metal scraping skin, the panel slamming shut behind them with a vibration she felt in her bones.

The hum filled the chamber outside.

Rachel felt it through the vent, not sound so much as resonance, tapping at the inside of her skull. The pressure coiled, disorganized and angry, as they crawled until the vent gave way and Rachel spilled into a narrow shaft lined with old insulation.

She landed wrong and waited for her leg to fail.

It didn’t.

Her hands stopped shaking faster than they should have. Her breathing evened before she was ready for it.

Lena watched her closely. “It’s faster.”

Rachel nodded. “It’s spreading.”

Lena didn’t argue.

They moved again, following scratches carved into the walls by different hands, different tools, some old, some fresh, all pointing deeper.

“Why did she run?” Rachel asked.

Lena hesitated just long enough to matter. “Because you don’t pass clean. No one does after you.”

The hum echoed above them, closer now.

Rachel stopped. “You knew.”

“I know how long places last,” Lena said, meeting her eyes.

The pressure crowded her chest, sharp and demanding.

“Where does this go?” Rachel asked.

“Another handoff,” Lena said. “If they’ll take us.”

“And if they won’t?”

Lena looked away. “Then we keep breaking things.”

Ahead, the wall shimmered faintly. Rachel’s palm tingled before she touched it, like the stone was already aware of her.

The pressure surged forward.

Not waiting.

Not asking.

# Chapter 19 — Not a Place That Helps

The next place didn’t look like a place that helped.

It looked like a throat.

The passage narrowed unevenly, walls sweating faintly where the stone met old metal braces. The air tasted sour, like insulation that had burned once and never been replaced. Someone had scraped symbols into the wall with a tool that skipped, lines gouged too deep and then abandoned.

Lena slowed.

Not because she wanted to.

Because the pressure did something strange.

It didn’t surge. It didn’t crowd. It slid sideways, like it had noticed something Rachel hadn’t yet.

Rachel’s foot caught on a raised seam and she swore, the sound bouncing wrong in the narrow space. Her leg held, but she felt the delay now—the fraction of time between impact and response stretching, rubbery and unpleasant.

“That’s bad,” Lena muttered.

“What?” Rachel asked.

Lena didn’t answer.

They reached the end of the passage and stepped into a room that wasn’t finished deciding what it was. Half concrete, half framed, wiring exposed like veins. A single lamp hung from a cord, swaying gently even though nothing had touched it.

A man sat at a folding table with his hands flat on the surface.

He didn’t stand when he saw them.

He looked at Lena first, then at Rachel, and something in his face closed.

“No,” he said.

Lena stopped short. “We just need—”

“No,” he said again, firmer this time. “You can’t be here.”

Rachel felt the pressure press outward, unfocused, brushing the edges of the room. The lamp flickered. The cord creaked.

“We won’t stay,” Rachel said. The words came out fast, overlapping themselves. “We just need to pass through.”

The man’s gaze stayed on her, steady and unhappy. “That’s what the last one said. The last off-worlder.”

Lena’s jaw tightened. “She’s injured.”

“Rooms don’t stay the same after you,” he replied.

The word hit wrong.

Rachel felt it in her chest, not fear exactly, but a kind of recognition, like when someone names a problem you’ve been pretending is temporary.

The pressure shifted again, jittery now, tapping at the room in uneven pulses. A screw worked loose from the ceiling brace and dropped, clinking on the concrete.

The man flinched.

“I won’t open it,” he said. “I won’t mark another route.”

Lena stepped forward. “If you don’t, they’ll catch her in the open.”

“If I do, they’ll catch all of us,” he said. “You know how this works.”

Rachel glanced around, trying to anchor on something solid. The table wobbled slightly under the man’s hands. One leg was shorter than the others. She fixated on that, on the uneven scrape it made when he shifted his weight.

The pressure coiled tight, crowded and irritated.

“I can be careful,” Rachel said, and hated how small it sounded.

The man shook his head. “You can’t. That’s the point.”

The lamp flickered again, brighter this time. The walls gave off a faint, skin-prickling warmth.

Lena swore under her breath. “We’re out of time.”

The hum arrived without ceremony.

No buildup. No warning.

Just a low vibration threading through the room, setting Rachel’s teeth on edge and making the lamp swing wider, cord twisting as if it were trying to unwind itself.

The man stood up too fast, chair scraping loudly across the floor. “You brought it here?”

Rachel felt the pressure snap inward, hard enough to steal her breath. Not a push. A clamp.

Her vision tunneled. The room tilted.

She staggered and caught herself on the table. The surface bowed under her palm, wood fibers creaking as if they were softening.

“I didn’t—” she started.

The pressure surged before she finished the thought.

Not outward.

Down.

The concrete floor cracked with a sharp, echoing pop. A fissure raced from beneath Rachel’s hand to the far wall, splitting around the man’s feet.

He shouted and stumbled back, hitting the wall hard enough to rattle the exposed wiring. Sparks spat and died.

Lena grabbed Rachel around the ribs and hauled her back. “Stop fighting it!”

“I’m not!” Rachel gasped.

The hum deepened.

Not louder. Denser. Like something had decided this room was no longer optional.

The man backed toward a metal door set into the far wall, fumbling for the latch. “Get out,” he said, voice breaking. “Get out now.”

The pressure twisted, confused and angry, then did something new.

It moved Rachel.

Not a shove. Not a pull.

Her weight shifted without her choosing it, balance snapping forward as if her body had skipped a step. She lurched, collided with Lena, and both of them went down hard.

The door behind the man buckled inward.

Metal screamed.

The latch tore free and skittered across the floor.

The man stared at it, then at Rachel, horror finally overtaking caution. “What are you?”

Rachel didn’t have an answer.

The pressure surged again, wider this time, sloppier, brushing everything at once. The lamp tore free from the ceiling and smashed against the wall, glass exploding outward in a bright, sharp spray.

The hum cut off.

For half a second, everything went wrong-quiet.

Then Lena was dragging Rachel up by the collar. “Move. Now.”

They ran through the only opening left, not a door so much as a split where the wall had failed. Rachel’s foot slipped on loose concrete and she nearly went down again, hands scraping raw as she caught herself.

Behind them, the room collapsed inward with a heavy, final sound.

They didn’t stop running.

They didn’t talk.

Rachel’s heart hammered too fast, breath tearing in and out of her chest. Her leg held. Her hands steadied faster than they should have. The pressure stayed close, crowded and sharp, like it was braced for another mistake.

They ducked into a narrow side run and Lena slammed a panel shut behind them. The hum didn’t follow.

Rachel slid down the wall, shaking.

“That wasn’t supposed to happen,” she said.

Lena crouched in front of her, face pale and furious. “No,” she said. “That was.”

Rachel looked up. “You said there were routes.”

“There are,” Lena said. “And then there are limits.”

The pressure pressed against Rachel’s ribs, restless, offended.

“What did you do?” Rachel whispered.

Lena didn’t answer right away. When she did, her voice was tight. “I brought you somewhere that could say no.”

Rachel swallowed, throat burning. “And?”

Lena stood. “And it couldn’t.”

The walls around them creaked softly, warming as if something deeper had shifted its attention.

Lena wiped her hands on her pants and looked down the passage ahead. “We don’t get many of those.”

Rachel pushed herself up, legs unsteady but working. Her body felt wrong—lighter somehow, quicker to respond, like the space between thought and motion had thinned.

The pressure leaned forward.

Already moving.

# Chapter 20 — Losing the Floor

They didn’t slow down so much as lose the floor.

The passage narrowed, dipped, and then ended where part of it had fallen away. Rachel pitched forward, caught herself on her hands, and slid the rest of the way down on her knees, breath knocked loose from her chest.

Lena hit the wall beside her and swore.

Nothing followed.

That sat wrong in Rachel’s gut.

She stayed where she was, palms stinging, waiting for the hum, for the vibration in her teeth, for the pressure to shove her upright again. Instead it crowded her chest and stayed there, tight and close, like it had pressed its ear to the stone.

Water dripped nearby. The air smelled cracked open.

“Don’t move,” Lena said.

Rachel didn’t.

The pressure shifted.

Not forward.

Sideways.

Rachel’s balance went strange, just a fraction off, and her eyes slid toward the wall where the collapse had peeled back layers that didn’t belong in open air. Stone had torn away, leaving metal exposed, twisted inward like it had been pulled instead of struck.

A door sat there.

Bent. Half-seated in a frame that no longer matched it.

Lena saw it and stopped breathing for a second.

“That’s not sealed,” she said.

Rachel opened her mouth. Nothing came out.

The door flexed.

Not opening.

Giving.

The pressure snapped.

Rachel’s weight shifted hard to the left. Her knees skidded on damp stone and her shoulder slammed into the wall.

The door tore free.

Metal shrieked as it folded inward, frame ripping loose in a way that sounded too easy.

Something inside screamed.

High. Short.

It stopped.

Rachel tasted blood and swallowed hard.

Lena shouted her name.

The pressure surged again, wider now, brushing past Rachel and into the space she hadn’t meant to uncover.

Silence dropped heavy.

Rachel pushed herself upright. Her legs answered immediately. She was standing before she remembered standing.

“I—” she said.

Nothing followed it.

Lena grabbed her shoulders and shook her once. “What did you just do?”

Rachel’s heart hammered, but her hands were still. Her breathing had already evened out.

Lena turned toward the room.

Light hummed softly inside. Clean panels. The kind of room you woke up in when the summon held and nobody panicked. A body against the far wall, limbs bent where they shouldn’t bend. Blood spread slowly, following the grooves cut into the floor.

Rachel stared at the blood, not the person.

“I didn’t touch them,” she said.

Lena didn’t turn. “You didn’t have to.”

The pressure shifted again, tight and restless.

“That room wasn’t empty,” Lena said. “It wasn’t supposed to be.”

Rachel took a step forward before she noticed she’d moved. The pressure stayed close to her ribs.

“No,” Lena said. “Stay.”

Rachel stopped.

The room smelled clean under the blood.

Prepared.

“I didn’t—” Rachel tried again.

Lena cut her off. “Don’t.”

They stood there too long.

The hum came back, thin and distant, threading through the walls like a sound remembered instead of heard.

Lena grabbed Rachel’s wrist and pulled. “We’re gone.”

They ran.

The passage bent in on itself. Rachel stumbled, caught herself, and kept moving, feet landing where she expected them to be without checking first.

“Did it—” she started.

Lena didn’t answer.

They burst into a maintenance bay cluttered with stripped braces and broken equipment. Heat sat heavy in the air. Lena shoved a rack aside and tore open a crawlspace behind it.

“Inside.”

Rachel hesitated.

The pressure crowded her chest.

She crawled.

The space forced her onto her side, metal pressing into her ribs, vibrating faintly. Lena slammed the panel shut behind them and wedged it with something heavy.

They lay there.

Rachel’s heartbeat slowed.

Too quickly.

Her breathing evened out. Her muscles loosened.

She waited for the shaking.

It didn’t come.

“I should—” she whispered.

Lena’s voice came thin through the dark. “Don’t.”

The hum passed overhead and kept going.

The pressure stayed close, tight against Rachel’s ribs, not pushing, not leaving.

“When did it start doing that?” Rachel asked.

Lena didn’t answer.

Somewhere deeper in the structure, metal shifted with a soft, final sound.

Rachel stared into the dark, listening to her own breathing, waiting for something to catch up to her that didn’t.

Nothing did.

# Chapter 21 — Slowed Steps

Rachel slowed her steps until the effort made her calves burn.

The passage sloped downward, walls damp where old braces showed through the stone, the floor uneven in a way that punished hesitation. Lena moved ahead of her, shoulders tight, counting under her breath without realizing she was doing it.

Rachel shortened her stride again, forcing her weight to settle where her foot landed instead of pitching forward into the next step. It worked badly. Her knee locked for half a beat, then released too late, sending her hand into the wall as she caught herself.

The stone was warm.

“That’s not helping,” Lena said, not turning around.

“I’m not running,” Rachel said, breath already out of sync with the pace she’d chosen.

The pressure crowded her chest without direction, dense and present, the way air feels before a storm breaks.

They moved on.

Rachel kept counting, letting the numbers stack up in her head, focusing on the scrape of her boots and the ache blooming behind her knee. The passage bent, shallow at first, then sharper, and the shift in angle arrived early, her foot already committed before her eyes caught up.

She stumbled and clipped the corner with her shoulder. Something inside the wall cracked low and ugly.

Lena swore and grabbed her arm. “You can’t change pace like that.”

“I didn’t,” Rachel said. “I went slower.”

“That’s the problem.”

The corridor opened into a wider chamber where the ceiling sagged between failed braces and rubble lay scattered in uneven drifts. Old equipment lay half-buried, metal edges catching faint light.

Rachel stopped moving forward and didn’t move backward either. She stayed exactly where she was and focused on the space between breaths, the way her lungs pulled air without instruction.

Her heart slowed.

The room didn’t.

Her balance slid downward, knees buckling as if the floor had dropped a fraction she couldn’t see. She hit hard, pain arriving late and wrong, muted like it had taken a detour.

Rachel pushed herself upright before she remembered falling. Her hands were steady. Her breath had already evened out.

Lena stared at her. “You didn’t brace.”

“I didn’t have time.”

The pressure stayed close, tight and unsettled, like something crowding into a space it didn’t quite fit.

They crossed the chamber faster than Rachel meant to, her steps stretching despite the effort she put into shortening them. She watched her hands instead of the floor, the way they lifted and reached a half-second before she finished deciding to move.

At the far end, a narrow stairwell dropped away into shadow.

Rachel slowed again, not stopping, just easing back against the forward pull of motion. Her foot slid farther than she meant it to, heel skidding on loose grit.

She yanked it back.

Her weight followed anyway.

Rachel lurched toward the stairwell and caught the rail with both hands, metal biting into her palms as her boots scraped uselessly for purchase.

Lena lunged and wrapped an arm around her waist. “Let go!”

“No,” Rachel said, the word tearing out of her.

The rail tore free.

Metal screamed as it ripped from the wall, and the world tipped sideways as Rachel and Lena went down together, tumbling the first few steps before slamming into the landing below.

They lay there, stunned, breath ragged.

Rachel waited for the crash to catch up to her.

It didn’t.

Her leg answered when she tested it. Her hands stopped shaking almost immediately. Her heart slowed faster than it should have.

Lena pushed herself up, face pale and furious. “You see it now?”

Rachel nodded.

“It’s not speed,” she said, voice flat. “It’s timing.”

The pressure pressed close without correcting her.

They didn’t argue after that.

They moved.

Rachel stopped trying to adjust her pace. Instead, she watched the lag between thought and motion, the way the decision arrived after the movement had already begun.

Every attempt to interfere bent the space around her just enough to make things worse.

By the time they reached the next run, Rachel was breathing evenly, sweat cooling on her skin, fear settling somewhere distant and manageable.

That scared her more than the fall.

They slipped into the dark, stone warming behind them, the pressure already leaning forward, lining her up with whatever came next.

# Chapter 22 — Not Found

They didn’t find the place.

The place took them.

The corridor looked like all the others until the floor changed under Rachel’s boots—not a step or a slope, just stone worn smoother in a strip wide enough to notice too late. Lena slowed without deciding to. Rachel didn’t and nearly ran into her, breath catching hard as she checked herself.

“There,” Lena said, and it wasn’t relief.

Rachel looked for a door and didn’t see one. Just a seam in the wall that didn’t line up with anything around it, stone patched wrong, like a repair that had been rushed and then forgotten. A chalk line ran down the seam and stopped at ankle height, the mark broken off mid-stroke.

Lena lifted her hand and let it hover.

The pressure crowded Rachel’s ribs, tight and restless, then slid away from the seam as if it had brushed something unpleasant.

Lena tapped the stone once.

Nothing.
