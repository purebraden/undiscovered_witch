# Chapter 64 — What the Room Remembers

Rachel noticed the others when she stopped trying not to.

They hadn’t arrived. That assumption didn’t hold up under inspection. The space didn’t work that way. Things existed without announcing themselves, and the mistake was thinking awareness caused presence instead of the other way around.

She walked the interior in slow loops, the way you did when you didn’t want to look like you were looking. The floor held shallow impressions where people sat the same way for long stretches. Heat lingered in those pockets long after the bodies moved. The room remembered weight.

Others were scattered through it — human-shaped and not, some seated, some standing in stillness that felt more like a decision than rest. A figure with too many joints moved across a seam and didn’t acknowledge the seam at all. A woman leaned against a low rise with her eyes closed, breathing slow.

Rachel knew, suddenly, that she could tell which places had held people for years. The air was different there. Heavy with the friction of answered questions.

An older woman sat near one of those spots, thin and straight-backed, hands folded in her lap. Her hair was more silver than not. The space didn’t adjust around her; it had already decided how to keep her.

Rachel stopped a few feet away. “Can I ask you something.”

The woman opened her eyes. “You just did.”

Rachel felt a tight smile pull at her mouth and disappear. “Do you know why I’m here.”

The woman considered her for a beat, then shook her head. “You’re not asking the first question.”

Rachel looked at Lena across the room. The distance between them felt like a rule. “What’s the first one.”

“Ask what wants you dead,” the woman said. “Then ask why it hasn’t managed yet.”

Rachel’s chest tightened. The pressure inside her shifted, alert.

*That one knows,* the voice said.

Rachel swallowed. “Why would something want me dead.”

“You’re carrying it,” the woman said, soft and direct. “And things want what they can’t get.”

Rachel looked down at her hands. They were steady. The room was steady. The steadiness felt earned by someone else.

She turned to go and felt the weight of the space settle around her, not resisting, not helping. Just watching.

By the time she found Lena again, the words had rooted. The binding wasn’t just keeping her alive.

It was keeping something off her long enough for her to ask the right thing.
