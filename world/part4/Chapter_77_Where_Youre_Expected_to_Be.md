# Chapter 77 — Where You’re Expected to Be

The space didn’t hesitate anymore.

Rachel noticed it because she kept bracing for something that didn’t arrive — that soft pause, the moment where the floor used to decide whether to cooperate — and instead her foot just came down wrong, weight redistributed without warning, balance adjusted after the fact like she’d misremembered where she was supposed to land.

Lena shifted. The floor moved with her.

Rachel shifted too, or thought she did, and found herself standing somewhere adjacent to where she’d aimed, not far enough to comment on, not close enough to ignore.

You’re doing it again, the voice said, mildly.

Doing what, Rachel thought back, already stepping, correcting the correction, body making small unconscious adjustments that didn’t add up the way they should have.

Her foot slid a fraction sideways before it touched down.

She adjusted. The floor adjusted back.

The exchange continued, quiet and stubborn, like two people correcting each other at the same time and never quite syncing up.

“Did you mean to—” Lena started.

“No,” Rachel said, and took another step that landed forward and not forward at all, weight carried somewhere else before she could catch it, calves tightening, then releasing when the floor finished the movement for her.

You keep assuming it’s reacting to you, the voice said. It isn’t.

Lena turned fully now. “Rachel.”

“I’m fine,” Rachel said, breath already off. She tried stepping back first, then forward again, like resetting the sequence would matter.

Backward was easy.

Forward wasn’t.

Her body veered just enough that she ended up parallel instead of beside, the space maintaining the gap with patient consistency. She stopped, then didn’t, then found herself still again without remembering deciding to be.

“You don’t have to keep correcting,” someone said nearby.

“I’m just walking,” Rachel said.

That’s optimistic, the voice said.

The floor thickened under her next step, not blocking it, not pushing back, just changing the effort required until motion started to feel expensive. She pushed anyway, momentum carrying her half a step farther before the space slid her placement sideways again, neat and unbothered.

Her hand came up without her meaning to, fingers closing on air where Lena’s arm should have been.

Lena swore softly. “What is it doing.”

Rachel tried circling wide, moving away instead of toward, feet landing cleanly when she wasn’t aiming for anything in particular.

See, the voice said. It likes you better when you don’t want things.

She turned.

Stepped.

The correction returned immediately, placement nudged back into the same loose pocket she’d been orbiting, not identical, just familiar enough to feel deliberate.

She laughed once, sharp, and stopped when no one else did.

“You’re overcompensating,” someone said.

“I’m not compensating,” Rachel said, and tried stepping faster, slower, diagonal, letting frustration pick the pattern instead of thought.

The floor answered every variation the same way.

Lena took a step toward her.

The space moved with her.

Rachel stepped at the same time and felt the difference immediately — the way Lena’s movement resolved cleanly while Rachel’s stalled mid-motion, effort climbing without progress, like she’d hit water where Lena hit air.

There it is, the voice said, not impressed. That lag you pretend isn’t judgment.

Rachel froze there, weight half-committed, then let herself settle back where the floor clearly preferred her.

Lena stared at the space between them. “Why does it keep doing that.”

No one answered right away.

“It’s easier if she stays there,” someone said eventually.

Of course it is, the voice said. You’re inefficient.

“There where,” Rachel asked.

The answers overlapped.

“Where it stabilizes.”

“Where adjustments don’t spread.”

“Where she resolves.”

That’s rude, the voice added. But accurate.

Rachel took a step back.

The floor accepted it immediately.

Sideways.

Fine.

Forward again.

The same correction, clean and efficient, nudging her back into the pocket she kept being returned to.

She stopped moving and felt the space ease, tension draining out of it like she’d finally stopped interrupting.

Lena’s voice was tight. “You’re not staying there.”

Rachel opened her mouth and couldn’t find anything that wasn’t a lie or a promise she couldn’t keep. She tried stepping forward again anyway, anger lending her weight.

The floor responded harder this time — not violently, just decisively — momentum redirected enough that she had to abort the step or fall.

She chose not to fall.

The space softened immediately.

See, the voice said. It prefers you compliant.

Lena didn’t move. The space stayed calm around her.

Rachel stood where the floor no longer argued with her, breath uneven, muscles buzzing with the afterimage of resistance.

Nothing stopped her from trying again.

Nothing encouraged it either.

You can keep testing, the voice said, almost bored. But this is where you resolve.

Rachel stayed.

Not because she agreed.

Because motion had started to feel like escalation, and escalation no longer felt like something she controlled.
