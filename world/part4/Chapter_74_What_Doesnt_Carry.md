# Chapter 74 — What Doesn’t Carry

Rachel noticed it because everything else still sounded right.

Voices overlapped and separated again without effort, the low ambient noise of people who knew how to exist together in a room, a laugh somewhere behind her, a question asked and answered without anyone needing to repeat it, fabric shifting, breath, movement. She turned toward Lena without thinking.

“Did you hear that,” she said.

Lena’s mouth moved.

Nothing came with it.

Rachel frowned and laughed at the same time. “Okay. What.”

Lena said something again, slower, more deliberate, her mouth shaping the words carefully like pronunciation might matter.

Rachel heard nothing.

She tilted her head, changed the angle between them like that might shake something loose. The room didn’t pause. No one corrected anything. Conversation continued around them like this wasn’t happening.

“That’s not funny,” Rachel said, and felt the words land wrong.

Lena stepped closer, said Rachel’s name.

Silence.

Rachel felt a cold pressure bloom behind her eyes. She could hear the woman on the floor breathing. She could hear a chair scrape slightly. She could hear someone murmur a comment too low to catch the words.

Lena opened her mouth again.

Nothing.

Rachel took a step forward. “Stop.”

Lena froze, confusion sharp and immediate, then reached out without thinking.

Her hand didn’t touch Rachel.

It didn’t stop. It didn’t slide away. It just failed to register, like the space between them had decided not to report it.

Rachel’s breath caught. “Say it again.”

Lena shook her head hard, then said something anyway, louder now, the words forced, jaw tight.

Rachel heard air move.

She didn’t hear Lena.

The woman on the floor made a small sound and Rachel flinched toward it, relief spiking at the wrong target.

“I can hear everyone else,” Rachel said too fast. “I just— I can’t hear her.”

No one contradicted her.

Lena’s lips pressed together. She tried again, slower, exaggerating each syllable, hands lifting as if they could carry sound when her voice couldn’t.

Rachel watched and felt panic crawl up her spine.

“Tell her to stop,” Rachel said to the nearest voice she could hear. “Tell her to stop doing this.”

“We aren’t,” someone said.

Rachel laughed, sharp and breathless. “Bullshit.”

“This is what leaving sounds like,” the voice said, and that was the first time the word arrived without invitation.

Lena stiffened. She said Rachel’s name again, clearly, angrily.

Rachel watched her say it.

She couldn’t hear it.

“Why,” Rachel said, the word thinning out. “I haven’t gone anywhere.”

“No,” they said. “But you aren’t fully here anymore.”

Lena shook her head, words spilling out now, fast and angry, hands moving in a way that made Rachel’s chest ache. Rachel heard none of it. Not even the shape.

Something cracked open behind her ribs, clean and frightening. “Say it to me,” she said, and hated the sound of it. “Say it louder.”

Lena stopped.

Her shoulders dropped.

She said one last thing, quietly.

Rachel heard nothing.

The silence wasn’t empty. It was specific. Like something had been removed with intention.

“That’s not fair,” Rachel said.

“No,” they agreed. “It isn’t.”

Rachel pressed her palms together hard enough to hurt. “Fix it.”

A pause, not long, not careful.

“This is the first thing that goes,” someone said. “Proximity.”

Rachel turned on them. “Then tell me how to get it back.”

“You finish what you started,” they said. “Somewhere it can close.”

Rachel looked at Lena, who was watching her with a focus that hurt worse than shouting would have, like she was trying to memorize Rachel’s face while it was still possible.

“I can’t even hear you,” Rachel said, words tipping toward panic now. “How am I supposed to leave like this.”

“You don’t leave like this,” the voice said. “This is leaving.”

Lena stepped closer again, stopped herself this time, fists clenched at her sides. She mouthed something slow, deliberate, unmistakable.

Rachel read her lips.

I’m here.

Rachel nodded, breath shaking. “I know.”

Lena mouthed something else, smaller, angrier, like it hurt to give it away.

Go.

And it landed then — not as a realization, not as a thought she finished — that hearing and knowing were already two different things, and one of them was slipping out of reach whether she moved or not.
