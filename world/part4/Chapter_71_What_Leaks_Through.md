# Chapter 71 — What Leaks Through

Rachel noticed the lanterns first.

They hung from curved brackets that grew directly from the walls — not mounted, grown, the metal branching like frozen vines before cradling glass bulbs filled with something that wasn't fire. The light inside them moved slowly, a pale amber that shifted when she looked directly at it and steadied when she didn't.

One of them flickered as she passed.

She stopped. The lantern's glow contracted, dimmed to a thin line, then slowly expanded back to its original warmth. No one else seemed to notice.

*That was you,* the voice said.

Rachel's chest tightened. "I didn't do anything."

*You decided something. You always do.*

"I was just walking."

*You were thinking about the exit. The light answered.*

She looked back at the lantern. It pulsed once, gently, like a held breath releasing.

Lena was sitting across the space, near a low table that seemed carved from a single piece of something between stone and bone — pale, smooth, warm to the touch when Rachel had brushed it earlier. The surface held shallow bowls filled with a thick liquid that smelled like honey and minerals. Food, maybe. Sustenance. Rachel hadn't been hungry since arriving, but she'd watched others drink from similar bowls, their movements slow and deliberate, like ritual.

"You're staring," Lena said, not looking up.

"Sorry."

"It's fine." Lena lifted her bowl, drank, set it back down with a soft click that the table absorbed without echo. "You've been standing there for a while."

Rachel hadn't realized. She shifted her weight and felt the floor respond — not smoothly, not the way it did for Lena. There was a lag, a negotiation, and then a faint ripple that traveled outward from her feet and dissipated against the nearest wall.

Someone across the space looked up. A being with features that kept rearranging themselves — eyes that migrated slowly across a face like drops of oil on water, mouth widening and narrowing as if testing different configurations. It watched the ripple fade, then returned to whatever it had been doing before.

Rachel forced herself to breathe.

*You're leaving marks,* the voice said. *The floor remembers where you've been.*

She looked down. The surface beneath her feet had darkened slightly — not stained, not damaged, just… registered. A faint impression that faded as she watched, but slowly. Too slowly.

Lena noticed her looking. "What."

"Nothing."

The lie sat badly between them.

Rachel took a step toward her. The floor resisted — not blocking her, just making the motion cost more than it should. Her calf muscles tightened. Her balance shifted. And behind her, one of the lanterns dimmed again, its light pulling inward like something had drawn its attention elsewhere.

*You're pulling,* the voice said. *You don't know how to stop.*

"I'm not doing anything," Rachel said, louder than she meant to.

Lena's head came up. "Okay."

"I'm just—" Rachel gestured at nothing. "Walking."

A being near the far wall turned. This one was more solid than the others — humanoid, almost, with skin the color of wet clay and eyes that glowed faintly green. It watched Rachel the way someone might watch a pot beginning to boil.

"You think loudly," it said.

Rachel's hands curled into fists. "That's not a thing."

"It is here." The being's mouth curved, not quite a smile. "When you decide something, things answer before you act. The light. The floor. The temperature."

Rachel felt heat prickle along her arms. She looked down and saw it — a faint shimmer beneath her skin, barely visible, the same wrongness she'd carried since the circle broke.

The being nodded. "Like that."

"I can't control it."

"No," the being agreed. "That's the problem."

Lena stood slowly, the space adjusting around her without hesitation — floor smoothing, table shifting a fraction to let her pass, light warming where she moved. When Rachel stepped to meet her, the lanterns flickered in sequence, one after another, and the being with the oil-drop eyes made a sound like wind through reeds.

"You're making it hard," someone said from behind her.

Rachel spun. A tall figure stood where nothing had been a moment before — not arrived, just suddenly present. Its body was wrapped in something that might have been cloth or might have been shadow, and when it moved, the fabric trailed like smoke.

"Hard for who," Rachel said.

"For the space." The figure gestured with hands that had too many joints. "It's trying to accommodate you. But you keep changing the shape you need."

Rachel felt the pressure in her chest tighten. "I'm not changing anything."

"You're always changing things." The figure tilted its head — the motion too smooth, too controlled. "You leave impressions. Demands. Unfinished thoughts that the space tries to answer."

*That's accurate,* the voice said. *I've been telling you this.*

"Then tell me how to stop."

The figure studied her for a long moment. "You don't stop," it said finally. "You're not built to stop. You're built to move. To want. To reach." It paused. "This place is built to hold. The two don't fit."

Lena stepped closer — or tried to. Rachel watched the space thicken between them, air gaining weight, distance stretching despite neither of them moving.

"What is that," Lena said, voice tight.

The figure smiled. The expression looked borrowed. "That's what incomplete looks like."

Rachel's skin prickled. She looked down at her hands and saw the shimmer intensifying — light gathering beneath the surface, not bright enough to illuminate anything but present enough to see. The floor beneath her feet darkened another shade.

"I can be careful," she said.

"You've been trying," the figure replied. "It hasn't worked."

Across the space, a bowl cracked. Not the one Lena had been using — another one, three tables away, sitting untouched. The liquid inside it bubbled once and went still.

No one moved to clean it up. No one looked surprised.

"That wasn't me," Rachel said.

The figure's smile widened. "Everything you do ripples. You just don't see where they land."

Lena's hand came up — reaching for Rachel, maybe, or just reaching. The space between them flexed and held, refusing to close.

Rachel stayed where she was, skin glowing faintly, floor darkening beneath her feet, lanterns flickering in her peripheral vision.

*Now you see it,* the voice said quietly. *Now you know what they've been adjusting for.*

Rachel swallowed and didn't move.

Moving had started to feel like a declaration.

And declarations, she was learning, had consequences here.
