# Chapter 76 — The Part That Doesn’t Come With You

Rachel stood longer than made sense.

Not because she was deciding anything. Because movement had stopped feeling like a neutral act, and she needed a second to remember what it felt like when standing up didn’t mean volunteering information.

Around Lena, the space stayed calm.

That was the worst part.

People continued to talk, quieter now, the conversation losing urgency the way it always did once something resolved itself. Someone adjusted a partition and didn’t bother explaining why. Another person shifted a seat closer to Lena’s left, then reconsidered and moved it back half an inch, like fine-tuning a picture frame.

Lena noticed none of it.

Rachel did.

She took a step.

The floor resisted, not sharply, not enough to make anyone comment, just enough that her foot landed a fraction later than expected, weight distributing wrong, calves tightening to compensate.

She stopped.

No one looked at her.

Lena shifted in her seat, stretching her leg out in front of her. The space moved with her without hesitation, the floor smoothing, the angle correcting, the adjustment invisible unless you were looking for it.

Rachel tried again, slower this time, moving when Lena moved, letting the motion overlap so it wouldn’t stand out.

The space followed Lena.

It followed Rachel after.

The delay was small.

It wasn’t ignorable.

There, the voice said, low. That’s it.

That’s nothing, Rachel thought.

That’s everything, the voice said.

She turned toward Lena. “Can we walk.”

Lena looked up, surprised. “Where.”

“Just—” Rachel gestured vaguely.

Lena hesitated, then nodded easily. “Sure.”

She stood.

The space stood with her.

Rachel felt it before she saw it — the way the floor drew itself up under Lena’s feet, ready, cooperative, as if it had been waiting for the cue.

Lena took a step forward.

The space flowed.

Rachel stepped after her.

The floor caught too late, then too much, the correction clumsy enough that her balance pitched forward and she had to grab for it, fingers scraping air before she steadied herself.

Someone nearby glanced over, then away again, expression smoothing back into neutrality.

Lena stopped immediately. “Hey.”

“I’m fine,” Rachel said, too fast.

Lena watched her for a moment. “You didn’t look fine.”

Rachel forced a smile. “I just wasn’t ready.”

You were, the voice said. The space wasn’t.

Rachel tried again, smaller steps, careful placement, focusing on the mechanics the way she always had when terrain turned unreliable.

It didn’t help.

Every movement required negotiation. Every step arrived with a slight argument attached, a sense of being corrected after the fact, like she was constantly interrupting something already in progress.

Lena walked ahead a few paces, then stopped, frowning. “It feels weird back here.”

Rachel nodded. “Yeah.”

“No,” Lena said slowly. “Not like before.” She turned, eyes narrowing. “It feels… fine.”

Rachel laughed, short and sharp. “Of course it does.”

Lena opened her mouth, then closed it. She took a step back toward Rachel.

The space responded instantly.

When Rachel stepped back to meet her, the floor lagged again, the distance stretching between them without either of them meaning it to.

They stopped, standing a few feet apart.

Rachel hadn’t noticed the gap opening until it was already there.

That’s the part you don’t get to take with you, the voice said conversationally.

Stop, Rachel thought.

I’m not doing anything, the voice replied. This is just how it works.

Lena frowned, unsettled now. “Why does it feel like you’re farther away.”

Rachel shook her head. “I don’t know.”

“That’s not—” Lena started, then stopped, looking around as if expecting the space to clarify. It didn’t.

Rachel took another step forward.

The floor resisted more clearly this time, not enough to stop her, but enough that she felt the effort travel up her legs, into her hips, her spine, like she was pushing against water that had decided to thicken.

She stopped again.

People were watching now. Not openly. Not staring. Just aware in the way people became aware when a pattern broke and didn’t immediately repair itself.

Lena glanced over her shoulder, then back at Rachel. “Do you want me to come back.”

Rachel’s mouth opened.

She didn’t know how to answer without choosing something.

Careful, the voice said, almost fond. This is where people pretend it’s mutual.

Rachel forced herself to breathe. “Just—stay there.”

Lena hesitated, clearly uncomfortable, then nodded. “Okay.”

She stayed where she was.

The space relaxed.

Rachel felt it happen, the subtle easing, the way tension drained out of the floor now that Lena wasn’t moving unpredictably.

Rachel hadn’t moved at all.

That seemed important.

“I don’t like this,” Lena said quietly.

“I know,” Rachel said.

Do you, the voice asked.

Rachel didn’t answer.

She took one more step, just to see if it had changed.

It hadn’t.

The resistance was immediate now, unmistakable, the space responding to her motion like a system that had already accounted for her and found her inefficient.

She stopped, heart pounding, breath shallow.

Lena looked at her, really looked, and for the first time since they’d arrived here, uncertainty crept into her expression.

“Rachel,” she said. “What’s happening.”

Rachel stared at the floor between them, at the invisible line she hadn’t meant to draw but couldn’t seem to cross without consequence.

“I think,” she said slowly, “I don’t move the same way you do anymore.”

Lena shook her head. “That doesn’t make sense.”

Rachel let out a breath that felt like it had been sitting in her chest for hours. “I know.”

The space remained quiet, cooperative, aligned — just not with her.

You can still follow, the voice said gently. It just won’t come with you.

Rachel closed her eyes.

When she opened them, Lena was still where she’d stopped, the distance between them unchanged, clean, polite.

Rachel didn’t try to cross it again.

She stayed where the space would tolerate her.

And waited to see what the cost of that would be.
