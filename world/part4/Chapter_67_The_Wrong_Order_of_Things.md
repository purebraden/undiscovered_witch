# Chapter 67 — The Wrong Order of Things

Rachel missed the first one because Lena was still talking, circling her knee again, how folding it didn't pull the way it used to except sometimes except maybe that was habit, she laughed and waved it off like she'd said too much about something that no longer mattered, and Rachel nodded in a place that didn't quite line up with the sentence.

People were everywhere and nowhere at once, movement without destination, pauses without arrival, the space rearranging itself while pretending it hadn't, and Rachel kept trying to track it the way she always had—edges, exits, threats—except there weren't any of those now, just drift.

Someone was suddenly close enough that Rachel noticed the pause before the person.

"Oh," Lena said. "Hey."

The person smiled too quickly, like they'd been waiting for that permission. "You're calmer."

Lena blinked, surprised by the idea. "Am I?"

"Yes. Yesterday you were—" They stopped, glanced at Rachel, then finished anyway, voice smoothing itself. "—pulling away from yourself."

The phrase landed before Rachel could catch it, already familiar, already wrong, because she'd thought it earlier and hated the way it had sounded even then, melodramatic and sticky, and she'd pushed it aside without saying it to anyone.

"I didn't," she said, then stalled out because there wasn't a verb that fit, because she hadn't done anything, she'd just—

The person winced. "Sorry. That wasn't—"

It was, the voice said, not interrupting so much as sliding into the space where her thought had been. You thought it hard.

Rachel clenched her jaw. Stop.

"You think loudly," the person said, hands folding and unfolding like they were trying to put them somewhere safe.

Lena laughed, startled and easy, then stopped when Rachel didn't. "She does."

"That's not a thing," Rachel said, the words coming out sharper than she meant.

"It is here," the person said, and then, as if reconsidering, "I think."

"When you decide something," they continued, and Rachel felt the space lean before she felt her own weight shift, "things answer first."

"That doesn't make sense," she said, but the sentence slid sideways when Lena moved and the floor adjusted under her without comment, without friction, while Rachel shifted a half-step and felt the resistance negotiate with her calves before letting her settle.

The person noticed. Their smile thinned. "That used to be me."

"What," Rachel said.

"That feeling," they said. "Like you're leaving fingerprints everywhere you go."

You do, the voice said mildly. You never wipe them off.

Another person stopped before Rachel could answer, not looking at Lena at all this time, gaze settling on Rachel's hands, her shoulders, the way she was holding herself like bracing counted as stillness.

"You should unclench," they said.

"I didn't ask—"

"I know," they said easily. "I wasn't answering."

Lena frowned. "Answering what."

The second person smiled at her, softer than the first. "You don't pull anymore."

Lena stiffened. "Pull what."

"Anything," they said. "It's nice."

Rachel felt heat climb her neck as the space shifted again, subtle and unmistakable, her balance pitching forward and then being corrected without her consent, the sensation arriving before the explanation every time.

There, the voice said. That lean you pretend is thinking.

Rachel pressed her palms into her thighs. They were shaking. She hadn't noticed when that started.

A third voice passed behind them, close enough to catch on the ear. "Careful."

No one turned. The first person was already disengaging, face gone neutral, attention sliding away. "You'll get tired," they said, not quite to Rachel, not quite to anyone.

"Tired of what," she asked, and someone—she wasn't sure who—said, "Echoing," and then the space folded around absence again and they were gone.

Lena sat very still.

"That was strange," she said eventually.

"They were guessing," Rachel said, because she needed it to be projection, inference, coincidence, anything that wasn't pattern.

Lena looked at her longer this time, not accusing, just measuring. "Guessing about what."

Rachel tried to answer and couldn't find the start of the sentence, everything she reached for sounding like something she hadn't meant to offer.

The floor creaked softly to her left.

She hadn't moved.

Oh, the voice said, almost conversational. You're not hiding it now.

"They can't hear you," Rachel whispered.

The pause was brief but real.

No, the voice said. But they can tell when I'm leaning.

Rachel swallowed. "Why did you come here. Through me."

The pressure shifted, settling into something heavier. You weren't supposed to be the destination. You were supposed to be the door.

"Door to what."

Somewhere I could move. The voice didn't waver. Demons don't cross easily. Humans do. A circle, a binding, a vessel that walks where I can't. That was the arrangement.

"Whose arrangement."

The voice paused. A group that trades in things like me. They call themselves the Convocation. They paid for the circle. Funded the summoning. Lost control when it broke.

Rachel's chest tightened. "And now?"

Now they want me back. You're the seal they didn't mean to make.

Her breath hitched. She closed her eyes and opened them again, like that might reset the order of things, but Lena was still watching her with an expression she didn't recognize.

"You're doing that thing again," Lena said.

Rachel laughed without humor. "What thing."

"The thinking," Lena said. "The loud kind."

The space shifted, expectant, and Rachel's chest locked up for a second before air forced its way back in, sharp and humiliating. She tried to flatten her thoughts, scatter them, think about nothing at all.

Nothing changed.

That felt worse.

You're going to have to stop pretending I wait my turn, the voice said, almost cheerfully.

Rachel stayed where she was.

She didn't speak.

She waited to see what would answer next.
