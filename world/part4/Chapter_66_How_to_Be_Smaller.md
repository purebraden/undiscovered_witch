# Chapter 66 — How to Be Smaller

Rachel decided to be careful.

Not all at once. It wasn’t a plan so much as a collection of small adjustments she didn’t announce even to herself: keeping her shoulders loose, not bracing before she shifted her weight, letting Lena finish sentences without anticipating the end of them. She tried to sit the way other people sat, as if sitting were a neutral act and not a series of negotiations.

It felt fake immediately.

Lena didn’t notice. That was worse.

“You don’t have to do that,” Lena said, halfway through a thought about nothing in particular, about how the light here didn’t buzz the way it used to in other places.

“Do what,” Rachel said, too quickly.

Lena shrugged. “You look like you’re trying not to spill.”

Rachel forced herself to relax her hands. She hadn’t realized they were clenched.

That’s not subtle, the voice said. That’s a performance.

I’m helping, Rachel thought.

You’re curating, the voice said. Those aren’t the same.

Rachel didn’t answer. She watched the floor instead, tracking the faint seams that appeared and disappeared depending on where she put her attention. When she softened her gaze, the seams faded. When she focused, they sharpened.

She softened.

Nothing happened.

That felt promising, until it didn’t.

Someone passed nearby and didn’t slow. Another followed the same path a moment later and did. A third veered slightly, as if avoiding something Rachel hadn’t placed there on purpose.

She shifted her weight back, then forward again, careful to keep the motion small.

The floor adjusted anyway.

Rachel exhaled and tried again, this time moving when Lena moved, mirroring her timing instead of leading it. The space accommodated Lena first, then corrected itself around Rachel with a faint resistance that made her calves tense before she could stop them.

Lena frowned. “Did you feel that.”

“No,” Rachel said.

Lena looked at her. “You did.”

Rachel smiled. It felt wrong on her face. “I’m just tired.”

“That’s not what tired looks like on you,” Lena said.

Rachel let the smile drop.

You see, the voice said, not unkind. You can’t be careful without being noticed.

Rachel pressed her palms flat against the floor, grounding herself the way she’d learned to do when things started slipping. The surface felt warmer than it should have, responsive in a way she didn’t like.

She pulled her hands back.

Across the space, someone paused. Not looking at Rachel directly, but not not looking either. Their posture shifted, then stilled, like they were waiting for a cue that didn’t come.

Rachel swallowed and forced her shoulders to drop again.

“I’m fine,” she said, this time to the room as much as to Lena.

The person across the space nodded, satisfied, and moved on.

That felt worse than if they’d stayed.

Lena leaned closer, voice low. “You’re making it weird.”

Rachel closed her eyes for a second. “I know.”

“Then stop.”

Rachel opened her mouth and almost laughed. She didn’t know how to explain that stopping was the thing she was trying to do, that effort itself was the problem, that even the intention to be smaller seemed to register before the action.

“I just don’t want to—” She trailed off.

Don’t want to what, the voice prompted, interested.

Don’t, Rachel thought back.

The voice hummed, thoughtful, like it was turning the word over.

Another person stopped nearby, this one closer than Rachel liked. “You should pick a rhythm,” they said, conversational, like advice about walking pace. “The stuttering makes it harder.”

Rachel stared at them. “Harder for who.”

They blinked. “For you,” they said. Then, after a beat, “For us.”

Lena stiffened. “Us who.”

The person gestured vaguely, encompassing the space without pointing to anything specific. “Everyone who has to adjust after you do.”

Rachel felt heat rise behind her eyes. “I’m not doing this on purpose.”

“I know,” the person said, and sounded sincere. “That’s why it keeps happening.”

They moved on without waiting for a response.

Rachel sat back hard, the motion unplanned, and the floor caught her too late, then too much, the correction sloppy in a way that felt almost irritated.

She stayed seated, breathing shallowly, trying not to think about the way too late and too much had felt intentional.

Lena watched her. Not frightened. Not angry. Just… recalculating.

“You don’t have to manage it,” Lena said. “Whatever it is.”

Rachel shook her head. “I do.”

“Why.”

Because if I don’t, it will.
Because it’s already doing it.
Because I don’t know where the edges are anymore.

None of those came out.

“I just need to be careful,” Rachel said instead.

The voice laughed, soft and surprised. You keep saying that like it’s a setting.

Rachel ignored it and stood slowly, deliberately, moving one piece at a time. The space reacted in segments, a half-beat behind each adjustment, like it was trying to follow instructions written in a language it didn’t quite speak.

Someone nearby sighed, impatient.

Rachel froze.

The sigh wasn’t aimed at her, exactly. It was the sound people made when something disrupted a pattern they’d already adapted to.

She sat back down.

“That’s better,” someone said, not unkindly.

Rachel’s hands trembled. She folded them together and focused on Lena’s breathing, steady and untroubled, a rhythm she could borrow without altering.

For a moment, it worked.

The space smoothed. Movement resumed. Attention drifted away.

Rachel let herself believe she’d found the trick.

You didn’t, the voice said gently. You just stopped pulling long enough for it to reset.

Rachel’s chest tightened. Reset what.

The voice didn’t answer.

A seam appeared in the floor near Rachel’s knee, thin and precise, like a line drawn to mark where something would be separated later.

She shifted away from it instinctively.

The seam stayed where it was.

Rachel swallowed and forced herself to look away.

Being careful, she realized too late, didn’t make her smaller.

It made her legible.
