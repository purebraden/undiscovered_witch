# Chapter 73 — What Others Carry

The woman volunteered before Rachel understood what she was offering.

Her name wasn't given — names worked differently here, Rachel had noticed, used sparingly and earned rather than assigned. But she was young by the standards of this place, with skin that still held warmth and eyes that hadn't yet learned to settle into fixed positions. She stepped forward in a chamber filled with cascading light, the walls embedded with crystals that split the lantern glow into scattered rainbows.

"I can take some of it," she said. "I've done it before."

Rachel didn't know what she meant until she sat down beside Lena.

The effect was immediate. Rachel felt something shift — not in herself, but in the space around her. The floor stopped pulsing. The nearest lantern steadied. For the first time since arriving, Rachel could breathe without feeling like her breathing was being observed and catalogued.

Then she looked at the woman.

The woman's hands were shaking.

Not violently. Not obviously. Just a fine tremor running through her fingers, visible when she pressed them flat against her thighs. She smiled, an expression that flickered at the edges like a lantern about to go out.

"It's fine," the woman said. "I just need to adjust."

"What are you doing," Rachel asked.

"Absorbing the differential." The woman's voice came out steady, but her shoulders had started to creep upward, tension building where there shouldn't be any. "You generate variance faster than the space can process it. So someone else has to help carry the load."

Rachel looked at Lena. Lena's face had gone carefully blank.

"She volunteered," someone said from nearby. A being with crystalline structures growing from its joints, their surfaces catching the scattered light. "We rotate. It's manageable."

"Rotate," Rachel repeated.

"No one can hold it for long." The being gestured with hands that clinked gently with each movement. "But together, we can keep it distributed. Keep it from pooling."

Rachel watched the woman's hands tremble harder. Her knee had started to bounce — a quick, repetitive motion that looked involuntary. Her breathing had changed too, gone shallow and fast like she was running from something she couldn't see.

"Stop," Rachel said.

The woman looked up, confusion flickering across her features. "What?"

"Whatever you're doing. Stop."

"I can't just—" The woman's voice caught. "If I let go now, it rebounds. It has to settle somewhere."

"Where."

The woman glanced at Lena. Didn't answer.

Rachel felt cold spread through her chest. "It goes into her?"

"It goes into the nearest stable point," the crystalline being said. "Right now, that's her."

Lena's jaw tightened. "I didn't feel anything."

"Because someone was holding it." The being's tone carried no judgment, just explanation. "That's what we've been doing since you arrived. Distributing the cost so neither of you noticed."

Rachel's hands curled into fists. "Why didn't you tell us?"

"Would it have changed anything?"

The question sat there, heavy and unanswerable.

The woman on the floor made a small sound — not pain exactly, but something close to it. Rachel saw light shimmer beneath the woman's skin, faint and wrong, the same shimmer she'd seen on her own arms. The woman was absorbing more than variance. She was absorbing whatever Rachel was becoming.

"That's enough," Rachel said.

"It's not—" The woman shook her head, teeth gritting. "I can do more."

"You're shaking."

"It passes." The woman tried to smile again. The expression collapsed halfway through. "It always passes."

A man stepped forward — older, steadier, with patterns scarred into his skin that looked like they might have been letters once. He crouched beside the woman and reached for her wrist.

"Don't touch," someone warned.

The man froze, hand hovering. "Why not?"

"Contact spreads it faster. You'd be carrying part of it before you knew."

The man withdrew slowly. The woman's breathing had turned ragged now, her whole body vibrating with something she couldn't release. Her eyes found Rachel's and held.

"I wanted to help," she said. "I thought I could handle more."

"Why."

The woman's laugh came out broken. "Because watching you hurt is worse than this."

The words landed somewhere Rachel couldn't identify. She watched the woman tremble and felt something in her own chest pull tight and not release.

*You see now,* the voice said quietly. *You see what you cost.*

Rachel turned away from the woman, toward the assembled beings who had been watching without intervening. "How do I stop this?"

No one rushed to answer.

"You don't," the crystalline being said finally. "Not here. Not while you're incomplete."

"Then where."

"There's a place where these things finish." The words came from somewhere to her left — the shadow-figure from before, its form barely distinguishable from the darker patches of wall. "Where incomplete bindings are drawn to ground out."

"The Relay," Rachel said. The name felt different now. Not just a destination. An answer.

"Yes."

Rachel looked at Lena, who was watching the woman with something between horror and helplessness. Then at the woman herself, still shaking, still refusing to let go because letting go meant the cost would land on someone else.

"If I go," Rachel said slowly, "does this stop?"

"If you complete the binding, the variance stops generating. The space stabilizes. Everything you've been diffusing returns to you and stays there."

"And her?" Rachel gestured at Lena. "What happens to her?"

A pause. Long enough to feel deliberate.

"She remains," the shadow-figure said. "Stable. Held. The way she was meant to be."

"Without me."

"Without the part of you that breaks things." The shadow-figure's voice carried something that might have been sympathy. "The part she can't follow is the part that's hurting her."

Rachel closed her eyes. The woman's trembling had become audible now — a soft chattering sound that filled the crystalline chamber.

"Okay," Rachel said.

When she opened her eyes, everyone was watching her.

"Tell me how to get there," she said. "Tell me how to finish this."

The shadow-figure drifted closer, its form solidifying slightly, taking on something almost like humanity.

"First," it said, "you need to say goodbye. Before you can't anymore."

Rachel nodded.

She crossed the space toward Lena, feeling the floor resist every step, watching the gap between them widen despite her movement.

The goodbye had already started.

She was only just noticing.
