# Chapter 72 — Someone Else Holds It

Lena stayed where she was, upright and still, hands flat on her thighs, shoulders set in a way that looked practiced rather than comfortable, like she’d already learned which movements were a waste and wasn’t interested in proving it again. People moved around her carefully, not circling exactly, not avoiding either, just adjusting paths a fraction too late and then again, like the room itself hadn’t quite agreed on how wide the space around her needed to be.

A woman Rachel hadn’t noticed before stepped closer, younger than most of them, her posture loose in a way that suggested she still expected cooperation. She paused, glanced at Lena, then at Rachel, hesitated just long enough that the choice registered as one.

“I can take it,” she said, then amended, quieter, “I can.”

No one asked what she meant. Someone nodded. Someone else didn’t object. That was enough.

“That’s fine,” a voice said. “Just for now.”

The woman smiled automatically and sat down near Lena, close enough that Rachel felt the shift before she saw it, the room adjusting in a way that wasn’t clean, wasn’t finished. The woman straightened, didn’t stay straight, adjusted again, her smile thinning as her shoulders crept upward like they were answering a question she hadn’t asked.

Lena turned. “You don’t have to.”

“It’s okay,” the woman said, swallowing. “I just—”

She stopped and pressed her palm flat to the floor, then pulled it back like it surprised her, laughing once under her breath. “That’s weird.”

Rachel stepped forward without thinking and stopped just as fast.

*No,* the voice said, sharp enough to cut. *You’re doing it again.*

The woman’s hand trembled. She tucked it under her thigh like it hadn’t. Her knee bounced once, then again, then stilled with effort that showed in her jaw.

“It’ll settle,” someone said nearby, already looking away.

“Yeah,” the woman said too quickly. “I just feel kind of—” She broke off, shoulders tightening, loosening, tightening again, breath going shallow and then deeper like she was trying to find a rhythm that wouldn’t trigger whatever had noticed her.

Lena frowned. “You’re shaking.”

The woman looked down at herself, blinked. “Am I.”

Rachel felt her mouth open and close without sound. The woman shifted, then shifted again, unable to land anywhere long enough to rest, her breathing hitching and smoothing and hitching again, hands curling and uncurling between her knees.

“I can hold it,” she said, quieter now, more determined. “It’s fine.”

“You shouldn’t have to,” Lena said, and for the first time there was something sharp under it.

A pause followed that wasn’t for Lena.

“If she moves now,” someone said, careful, “it’ll slide back.”

“Slide where,” Lena asked.

The woman answered before anyone else could, voice thin but steady. “Through you.”

Lena went still.

Rachel felt heat climb up her neck, felt her feet shift before she told them not to, felt the room tighten in response like a held breath.

The woman gasped, a short sound she cut off immediately, hands flying to brace herself as her shoulders hunched and her breath came hard and fast. “Sorry,” she said quickly. “Sorry, that was—”

Rachel stopped moving.

The room loosened.

The woman folded forward slightly, forearms pressed to her thighs now, breath still uneven as she tried to pull it back under control. She didn’t look up again.

Lena stood abruptly and the movement rippled outward, people adjusting late, then again. “No,” she said. “You’re not okay.”

“I am,” the woman said, and laughed again, wrong this time, her hands shaking openly now. “I just feel stretched.”

Someone said, “We can rotate,” like it was an answer.

Lena stared. “Rotate.”

“Yes,” they said. “It doesn’t have to be her.”

The woman nodded immediately, relief flashing across her face and disappearing just as fast. “I can do longer.”

Rachel took another step before she decided to, the room snapping tight in response, the woman choking on a sound she swallowed as her shoulders drew in and her breath stuttered.

Rachel froze.

The room eased.

Lena looked at Rachel.

Rachel looked back.

*See,* the voice said, low and satisfied in a way Rachel hated. *It doesn’t stay put.*

No one moved the woman. No one asked Rachel anything. The woman stayed where she was, breathing shallow, hands clenched together between her knees, waiting for it to stop while the room quietly reorganized itself around the fact that it hadn’t.
