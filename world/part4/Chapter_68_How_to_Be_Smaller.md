# Chapter 66 — Lena’s Question

Rachel found Lena in a common space that wasn’t quite a room. The floor curved up into a low rise and flattened again, a shape that made sitting feel like a choice the space approved of.

Lena sat with her legs folded, posture easy. The air between them held weight, the same soft resistance Rachel had felt when she’d tried to touch her before. Not a barrier. A reminder.

“Have you figured out what you’re supposed to do yet,” Lena asked.

Rachel shook her head. “I know what I am. Not what I’m for.”

Lena nodded once, slow. “That’s… not nothing.”

Rachel watched her hands. They were steady. The room made sure of it.

Lena let the silence sit for a beat and then said, “I can’t go with you.”

Rachel looked up. “I know.”

“No,” Lena said, shaking her head. “I mean I can’t. Even if I wanted to tear the paper up. The anchor isn’t in the signature. It’s in me now.”

Rachel’s throat tightened. She’d known it. Hearing it out loud made it real in a way knowing hadn’t managed.

Lena tried to smile and missed. “So whatever you’re supposed to do—you have to figure it out. Because I can’t help you find it, and I can’t follow you to it.”

The pressure in Rachel’s chest stirred, not in warning. In recognition.

Rachel pressed her palm to the floor and felt the faint warmth the space kept there for Lena. She didn’t reach across it.

“I don’t want to leave you,” she said.

“I know,” Lena replied. “That’s why I’m saying it.”

Rachel took a breath and let it out slow. The room listened.

Lena glanced away, eyes tracking something Rachel couldn’t see. “If you stay, you won’t get answers,” she said quietly. “If you go, I don’t get you. That’s not fair, but it’s the shape of it.”

Rachel looked at the space between them, at the way it thickened when she tried to close it and softened when she didn’t.

She realized, with a cold clarity that hurt more than panic, that Lena wasn’t asking her to leave.

She was telling her it was already true.
