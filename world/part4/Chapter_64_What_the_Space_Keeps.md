# Chapter 64 — What the Space Keeps

Rachel noticed the others when she stopped trying not to.

They hadn’t arrived. That assumption didn’t hold up under inspection. The space didn’t work that way. Things existed without announcing themselves, and the mistake was thinking awareness caused presence instead of the other way around.

Someone moved across the interior, not toward them, not away. The path didn’t line up with the floor the way Rachel expected it to. Each step landed a fraction early, like gravity had made a decision she hadn’t been consulted on.

Lena’s head turned, tracking the motion.

“I don’t remember that one,” she said.

Rachel frowned. “They were here.”

Lena glanced at her. “No. I would’ve noticed.”

The disagreement settled between them, small and unresolved.

The figure paused near a low partition that hadn’t been there when Rachel last checked that direction. Its outline kept adjusting, never quite finishing. When it turned, its face resolved into something almost human—eyes, mouth, expression arranged correctly but proportioned in a way that made Rachel’s gaze slide instead of stick.

It looked at Lena.

Not curious. Not hungry.

Recognizing.

Lena straightened without seeming to realize she’d done it. “Hi,” she said.

The figure smiled. The expression stayed longer than it should have. “You don’t ache anymore,” it said.

Lena blinked. “I—”

“That’s not a warning,” the figure added. “It’s an observation.”

Rachel stepped closer. The space suggested she reconsider. She didn’t.

“Do you know her?” Rachel asked.

The figure’s eyes flicked to her and away again. “I know what she’s like now.”

Lena shifted. “What are you.”

The figure laughed, surprised. “I don’t remember,” it said. “Does that matter.”

Rachel felt the pressure in her chest stir.

*Listen,* the voice said. *Not to the words.*

“Were you like me,” Lena asked, tentative.

The figure nodded. “Before it stopped scraping.”

Lena’s shoulders loosened, just slightly. “So it gets better.”

The figure hesitated. “It gets quieter.”

Rachel stepped forward again. This time the space resisted more clearly — not a barrier, just friction.

“What did it cost you,” Rachel asked.

The figure studied her, expression shifting with effort. “You leave impressions,” it said.

“That’s not—”

“I don’t mean your voice,” it continued. “I mean the way you keep carrying things past the point where they’re useful.”

Lena frowned. “Like what.”

“Guilt,” the figure said. “Other people’s unfinished thoughts.” It smiled, almost kindly. “You’ll wear yourself thin if you don’t stop.”

Rachel felt heat rise behind her eyes. “You don’t know me.”

The figure shrugged. “I sounded like you.”

That sat wrong.

“What happened,” Rachel asked.

The figure looked past them, toward a section of the space where depth layered over itself until distance became suggestion. “People stopped waiting for me to answer.”

Lena inhaled sharply.

“That’s not—” Rachel started.

“It’s fine,” the figure said. “I don’t miss it.” Its attention returned to Lena. “You’ll understand.”

The space shifted around them — not closer, not farther — just enough that Rachel had to adjust her footing.

The figure stepped away, dissolving into the movement of the interior. When it passed behind a partition, its outline blurred, then softened, then failed to reappear.

Lena sat very still.

Rachel turned toward her. “You okay.”

Lena nodded too fast. “Yeah. It’s just… nice. Knowing there’s a point where it stops pulling.”

Rachel swallowed. “Stops pulling what.”

Lena didn’t answer right away. “Everything,” she said finally.

Movement stirred elsewhere. Another figure paused near the edge of sight. Then another. Not approaching. Not retreating. Watching without focus, the way people did when they recognized something familiar but didn’t want to claim it.

Rachel realized, slowly, that none of it was aimed at her.

It was aimed at Lena.

The pressure in her chest shifted, unsettled.

*They’re comparing,* the voice said. *Not you.*

Rachel clenched her hands. “We should go.”

Lena shook her head. “Why. Nothing’s happening.”

Rachel opened her mouth to argue and found nothing she could point to. No alarm. No narrowing corridor. No reason that didn’t sound like fear.

The space continued on.

Someone laughed in the distance. Someone else sighed.

Rachel sat back down beside Lena, keeping the margin she’d learned not to cross.

She had the growing sense that the space was tracking her questions.

And that some answers were already circulating — just not through her.
