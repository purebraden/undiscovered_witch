# Chapter 73 — What It Costs

Lena stood up and then didn’t go anywhere, weight shifting once like she’d forgotten what she meant to do with it, shoulder rolling forward and back, not stretching so much as checking.

“I’m going to sit,” she said, already doing it, already halfway down before anyone answered.

The space made room for her the way it always did now, smooth and unquestioning, and for a second it looked like that might be enough. She leaned back, exhaled, eyes closing and opening again almost immediately.

“You okay,” Rachel said, too fast.

“Yeah,” Lena said, and then didn’t add anything, adjusted again instead, hip shifting, then shifting back, like the movement hadn’t quite finished following her.

*That’s not a thing,* the voice said. *She’s just tired.*

Lena tried lying down. Hands folded. Breath slow. The space answered politely and then stopped answering, leaving the rest unfinished.

Her foot twitched.

Then again.

She opened her eyes. “Okay.”

She sat up instead of finishing the motion.

Rachel felt her jaw tighten and didn’t know when it had started.

A conversation nearby stalled for half a beat, resumed somewhere else. Someone else changed position, then changed it again, correcting for something that didn’t have a shape.

“It’s fine,” Lena said, still not looking at Rachel. “I just need a minute.”

She stood again, slower now, as if the speed might matter, took a step, then another, stopped with her hand lifted like she might need to brace.

Rachel moved without thinking, a half-step forward, then caught herself.

*Don’t,* the voice said, sharp. *That’s worse.*

Lena noticed anyway. “Why are you doing that.”

“I’m not,” Rachel said, and hated how thin it sounded even to her.

Lena shook out her hands. “Okay. No. I need water.”

A container appeared near her knee, not close enough to be helpful, not far enough to refuse. Lena frowned, reached anyway, drank, paused, drank again.

Her shoulders dropped a fraction.

Rachel let herself breathe.

Then Lena winced, just slightly. “It’s still tight.”

“Where,” Rachel said.

Lena gestured, vague and directional at the same time, not pointing, just indicating space.

Rachel felt people around them adjust, attention bending without anyone looking directly at her.

*Coincidence,* the voice said, too quickly.

Lena set the container down and rubbed at her neck. “It won’t stop.”

Rachel stepped sideways, putting distance between them without leaving.

A throat cleared nearby. “It’ll keep doing that.”

Lena looked up. “Doing what.”

“Failing to settle,” they said, like it was a weather pattern.

Rachel laughed once, short. “Then stop letting it.”

No one answered her.

Lena tried sitting again, slower, careful, like she was negotiating with something that kept revising the terms. The space accommodated her and then left her there, the job half-done.

Her breathing hitched and smoothed out again without quite resolving.

Rachel moved again without deciding to, then stopped, then didn’t remember stopping.

*They’re letting it travel,* the voice said. *Or maybe they’re letting you see it.*

Lena closed her eyes, pressed her palms into her thighs, opened them again with a tired sound that wasn’t quite a sigh.

“This shouldn’t be this hard,” she said.

“No,” someone said nearby. “It usually isn’t.”

“You keep saying that,” Rachel said.

A pause. Not for her.

Lena turned toward Rachel, fully now. “Why does it feel better when you’re farther away.”

Rachel froze.

“That’s not—” she started.

“It’s how it distributes,” someone said, gentle, already halfway through the explanation. “When it doesn’t have a focal point.”

“So it’s bouncing through me,” Lena said, flat.

No one corrected her.

Rachel swallowed and felt her hands shake. “I didn’t choose this.”

“No,” someone said. “But you’re sustaining it.”

Lena leaned back again, then stopped halfway, abandoning the motion like she already knew how it would end. She let out a breath and didn’t try again.

“So,” she said quietly, not accusing, not pleading. “As long as you stay like this, I don’t get to rest.”

Rachel shook her head. “That’s not fair.”

“No,” they said again.

Lena didn’t move. The space stayed calm.

Rachel stayed where she was because leaving would make it worse and moving closer would make it worse and staying felt like the only option that didn’t immediately add cost.

Lena shifted once, like she might try again, then didn’t. Her eyes stayed open.
