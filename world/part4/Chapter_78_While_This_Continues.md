# Chapter 78 — While This Continues

No one said what time it was, and Rachel stopped trying to figure it out because the space didn’t behave like time was something you moved through so much as something that pooled. People thinned out, came back, stood in different places without remarking on the change. Nothing ended. Nothing reset. It felt less like waiting than like something being maintained.

Something warm was placed into Lena’s hand without anyone asking first, the movement clean enough that Rachel noticed it only because it didn’t interrupt anything. A surface shifted a fraction so Lena didn’t have to lean.

“That should help,” someone said, already half-turned away.

“With what,” Lena asked, blinking down at it.

“Staying comfortable,” came the answer, easy. “It’s simpler if we don’t keep compensating.”

Rachel stayed still and tried to decide who we included, but the conversation slid past the question and into something else, voices bending around each other, the way people talked when they assumed agreement.

Sleeping came up without attaching itself to a night. Light. Spacing. Someone said it was better once things stopped moving around too much, then paused and amended it—no, that wouldn’t be necessary here.

“Why not,” Lena asked, frowning.

“You’re already aligned,” someone said, like that settled it. “Moving you would just introduce drag.”

The explanation ended there, cleanly, as if it had reached its intended audience and had nowhere else to go.

Rachel shifted her weight and felt nothing respond, nothing correct her, nothing object. As long as she didn’t aim anywhere.

Someone crossed the space and altered their path at the last second, a quiet detour that cost them a few extra steps. They didn’t look annoyed, just aware, the way people got when they learned where not to put their foot.

“Sorry,” they said reflexively as they passed Rachel, even though she hadn’t moved.

“It’s fine,” Rachel said, because that was what went there.

The person nodded and kept going, already adjusting for the next thing.

It occurred to her then that no one had asked her to move.

They were moving instead.

Lena leaned back, stretching, and the space accommodated her without comment. Someone murmured about sightlines. Another person nudged something closer so Lena wouldn’t have to reach later. It all happened quickly, smoothly, like a habit no one remembered forming.

Rachel stayed where she was, careful not to make stillness look intentional.

“Later,” someone said to Lena, not urgently, not quietly, “we can talk through how you want this to feel.”

“Talk through what,” Lena asked.

“Patterns,” they said. “Nothing pressing.”

Later again. The word settled and stayed.

Rachel felt it take weight.

Nearby, another voice layered in, still aimed at Lena. “Most people don’t even notice the adjustment phase. It usually smooths itself out.”

“Usually,” Lena echoed.

“Yes,” they said. “This is just… noisier.”

Rachel felt her shoulders tighten and forced them to drop. She shifted her stance a fraction and felt the space remain neutral, permissive, as long as she wasn’t going anywhere in particular.

A container appeared near her knee. Not offered. Just there. Close enough to be useful, far enough to avoid acknowledgment. She didn’t touch it. No one noticed that either.

A conversation stalled briefly as someone waited for a movement to finish, the pause practiced and unremarkable, then resumed exactly where it had left off, no apology necessary.

“I can move,” Rachel said, not loudly, not sharply.

The words landed in a gap that closed almost immediately.

A glance, then another, not aimed at her for long. “You don’t need to,” someone said. “This works.”

“For who,” Rachel asked.

The answer came sideways, overlapping something else. “It keeps things from propagating.”

Another voice added, softer, like smoothing a wrinkle. “We just don’t want it to get harder than it has to be.”

Harder for whom sat in the air, unanswered.

Lena shifted again, uneasy now, the ease faltering. “What would make it easier.”

Rachel felt the question before she registered it as sound.

A pause followed. Not silence. Consideration.

“Well,” someone said slowly, choosing the kind of words meant to calm, “most people don’t carry this much variance with them.”

Variance again. The word was starting to feel familiar in the wrong way.

“But there are ways it settles,” they continued, still not looking at Rachel. “Eventually.”

The sentence stopped there.

Eventually pressed in from the edges and stayed unfinished.

Rachel waited for someone to say the rest of it.

No one did.

There it is, the voice said quietly, not pleased, not alarmed. Just noting.

Lena looked at Rachel then, confusion tipping into something tighter. “Are you making this worse.”

Rachel shook her head. “I don’t think so.”

No one contradicted her.

No one agreed.

Someone shifted a little farther away, weight redistributing across the room like caution spreading thinly. Another person took the long way around without comment.

“We can manage it,” someone said to Lena. “It’s just… slower.”

Slower. No urgency in it. No warning.

Just time being spent.

Rachel felt the shape of the room settle around that assumption, the idea that this was workable, that the friction was acceptable, that nothing needed to be solved quickly.

That something could change later, if it needed to.

She stayed quiet.

The space stayed calm.

And without anyone asking her for anything at all, Rachel understood that nothing here was pushing her toward agreement—

only showing her how much effort it took not to drift into it.
