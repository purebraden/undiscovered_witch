# Chapter 71 — The Original Destination

Rachel went back to the presence with sharper questions.

"The Relay," she said. "Tell me what it is."

The presence considered her for a moment before answering. "A facility. The Convocation's primary binding center. Where transfers are completed and vessels are finalized."

"Where is it?"

"Outside this space. Several days' travel, if you moved without interruption." The presence paused. "You wouldn't move without interruption."

"Because they're looking for me."

"Because the moment you leave here, you become visible again. And they have ways of tracking incomplete bindings."

Rachel felt the voice stir, wary.

*That's true,* it said. *I can feel the pull. It's faint here, but it's there.*

"The binding was incomplete because the circle broke," Rachel said. "The demon was being transferred from somewhere. A holding space?"

"Yes," the presence said. "A prison, of sorts. Where demons are kept until the Convocation is ready to deploy them."

"And it was supposed to go to the Relay."

"To a prepared vessel at the Relay. Someone who'd been made ready to receive it." The presence's expression flickered. "The transfer was supposed to be clean. Direct. The vessel would have been compliant because compliance had been... ensured beforehand."

Rachel's stomach turned. "Broken."

"Prepared," the presence corrected, though its tone suggested the distinction was thin. "The Convocation prefers vessels that don't fight. The binding settles faster. The demon has less to overcome."

"And me?"

"You fought. You're still fighting." Something like respect crossed the presence's features. "That's why the binding locked wrong. The demon couldn't settle because you wouldn't stop resisting."

*That's not entirely accurate,* the voice said. *You weren't resisting. You were surviving. It's different.*

Rachel ignored it. "If I go to the Relay, what happens?"

The presence was quiet for a moment. "That depends on how you arrive."

"Explain."

"If you arrive as a captive, they extract the demon and you die. The binding is broken, the demon is re-contained, and they try again with a proper vessel."

"And if I arrive on my own terms?"

The presence smiled, slow and knowing. "Then you have a choice. You can complete the binding yourself, in a place designed for completion. The facility has what's needed — the geometries, the anchors, the structural support for a binding to finalize."

"And then?"

"Then you and the demon become one thing. Inseparable. The distinction between you dissolves." The presence leaned closer. "They can't extract something that doesn't have edges. They can't separate something that was never really two things."

Rachel felt a strange calm settle over her. "That's what I want."

"Is it?" The presence tilted its head. "You'd stop being you. You'd become something else. Something that carries what you were but isn't limited by it."

"I'm already something else," Rachel said. "I've been something else since the circle broke."

The presence studied her. "Yes. You have."

*She's testing you,* the voice said. *Or showing you something. I can't tell which.*

"What would completing the binding mean for you?" Rachel asked the voice directly, not caring that the presence could hear.

A pause. Then: *Finishing. Placement. Becoming what I was meant to be, but not in the way they intended.*

"Do you want that?"

*I want to stop being passed around.* The voice's tone was flat, honest. *I want to be placed in something that chooses me. Not something that's been broken to accept me.*

Rachel turned back to the presence. "How do I get there?"

"To the Relay?"

"Yes."

The presence considered this. "We can show you the direction. Give you what knowledge we have about the approaches, the defenses, the layout." It paused. "We can't protect you once you leave."

"I know."

"And you can't come back. Not the way you are now. Once you leave, this space won't recognize you anymore."

Rachel felt that land, heavier than expected. "Because I'll be different."

"Because you'll be complete," the presence said. "And complete things don't need holding spaces. They become their own containers."

The voice stirred, something that might have been anticipation.

*That's the destination,* it said. *That's where I was being dragged. Before you.*

"Do you want to go there?" Rachel asked.

*I want to be finished,* the voice said. *One way or another.*

Rachel stood, feeling the space adjust around her without the usual resistance.

"Show me," she said. "Show me where I need to go."
