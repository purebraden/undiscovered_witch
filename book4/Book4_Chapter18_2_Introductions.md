Chapter 18.2 — Introductions

Naelis does not step aside.

She steps with Allison.

Not across the threshold—not yet—but close enough that the distinction between edge and interior begins to feel less like a wall and more like a posture. Allison notices the shift immediately. The Fairy World responds to Naelis’s proximity the way a room responds when someone expected arrives: not rearranging itself, just making space.

“This is far enough,” Naelis says, not to Allison alone but to the space itself.

Tomas remains where he is. The line between them holds, clear and intact. Allison feels it at her back—not pressure, not restraint. A reference point.

Naelis turns to her. “You may look,” she says. “Not to inventory. To notice.”

Allison nods once. The permission—if that’s what it is—feels carefully shaped. She lets her attention soften rather than sharpen, widening instead of reaching.

The Fairy World resolves into people.

Not all at once. Not dramatically. One presence at a time, like figures emerging from peripheral vision when she stops trying to focus directly on them. A small cluster nearby pauses in conversation, their attention flicking toward Naelis before settling briefly on Allison.

No one stares.

No one ignores her either.

“This is Lira,” Naelis says, indicating a fairy whose wings—if that’s the right word—fold and unfold with a rhythm that seems less biological than expressive. Lira inclines her head, eyes bright and assessing.

“You are the human who stands carefully,” Lira says.

Allison blinks. “I try to.”

“That is sufficient,” Lira replies, already turning back to her companions.

Naelis continues, unhurried. “This is Corien. He shapes boundaries.”

Corien looks at Allison with open curiosity. “You stop where others lean,” he observes.

“I was told it mattered,” Allison says.

“It does,” Corien agrees. “But not for the reason you think.”

Before Allison can ask, Naelis moves on.

Introductions here are not invitations to conversation. They are acknowledgments—names placed carefully, like markers on a map that doesn’t belong to her.

As they walk a short distance—still within the margin Naelis has defined—Allison becomes aware of how often eyes flick toward her and then away again. Not wary. Curious. Measuring something that isn’t visible.

“She is not here to fix anything,” Naelis says at one point, not loudly, but clearly enough that those nearby hear.

The effect is immediate. A subtle easing. Conversations resume. Attention redistributes.

Allison absorbs that with a quiet jolt. Even without action, even without crossing fully in, her presence has weight.

A fairy with skin like polished stone—smooth, matte, faintly reflective—steps closer. “You are early,” she says.

Allison hesitates. “Early for…?”

“For obligation,” the fairy replies. “But not for learning.”

Naelis nods. “This is Maera. She keeps records that do not belong to time.”

Maera studies Allison with interest that feels archival rather than personal. “You understand that being seen creates narrative,” she says.

“I’m starting to,” Allison replies.

“That is how it usually begins,” Maera says, and steps back.

The introductions continue in this manner—brief, precise, never overwhelming. Names. Roles. Impressions offered without explanation. No one asks Allison where she is from. No one asks what she intends to do.

They ask how she is standing.

“She listens,” one fairy notes.

“She does not reach,” says another.

“She notices when she is noticed,” says a third, with what might be approval.

Allison feels the strangeness of being known in pieces, none of them intrusive, all of them accurate enough to unsettle her.

At no point does Naelis frame this as welcome.

Nor does she frame it as trial.

“This is what it means to arrive without a case,” Naelis says quietly, as if answering a question Allison hasn’t asked aloud. “You are not evaluated for usefulness. You are observed for alignment.”

“Alignment with what?” Allison asks.

Naelis considers her. “With consequence.”

The word settles between them.

Allison becomes acutely aware of Tomas again—not because he moves, but because he doesn’t. He remains steady at the boundary, allowing this extension without following it. She understands then that this, too, is a signal. He trusts the shape of this moment.

Naelis slows and turns back toward the threshold. “This is as far as introductions go,” she says. “Beyond this, people will begin to expect things of you.”

“I don’t want that yet,” Allison says.

Naelis’s mouth curves slightly. “I know.”

They stop. The space holds.

Around them, the Fairy World continues—not paused, not altered. People move. Voices rise and fall. Life happens in a way that does not center Allison, and yet now includes her.

She feels something settle into place inside her—not certainty, not resolve. Orientation.

When Naelis steps back, the margin closes gently, like a held breath released. Allison finds herself once again clearly at the threshold, Tomas beside her, the Fairy World just beyond.

But something is different now.

She has names.

And names, she is beginning to understand, are a kind of responsibility all their own.
