# Chapter 2 — Assigned Use

Allison learned where she fit by noticing where she was not asked to stand.

She was not placed at the center of rooms.  
Not positioned beside decision-makers.  
Not framed as an authority, or even as a representative.

Instead, she was scheduled.

The message arrived without preamble while she was still in the corridor outside the Registrar’s Office, the folded paper from Iria’s desk resting warm against her side as if it had weight beyond its mass.

**Observation Block — Tiered Access**  
**Duration: Flexible**  
**Escort: Tomas Calder**

No urgency marker.  
No explanatory paragraph.

Just a line added to her day, the way maintenance tasks were added to infrastructure once they became reliable enough to depend on.

Allison stopped walking.

Not because she was unsure—but because stopping had become one of the few actions that still clearly belonged to her.

She read the message again, slower this time.

Observation.  
Tiered.  
Escort.

The Confluence was not subtle when it mattered. It did not pretend neutrality where classification already existed.

When she looked up, Tomas was already waiting at the junction where the west corridors bent toward the internal archives. He hadn’t summoned her. He hadn’t waved.

He was simply *there*, positioned where the system assumed he would be.

“Allison,” he said when she approached. “Good morning.”

“Morning.”

His eyes moved to her bag—not the way Evan’s did, checking for emotional state, but with professional acknowledgment. Documentation. Context. Readiness.

“You received the reassignment,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Good.” He turned and began walking without waiting for her to follow, the invitation implicit rather than enforced.

She followed anyway.

They moved deeper into the Confluence than Allison usually did, past spaces that felt public-adjacent and into corridors where the walls themselves seemed to absorb sound. This wasn’t secrecy. It was focus.

Here, conversations were meant to be remembered.

“Observation block,” Allison said after a while. “That’s new language for me.”

Tomas nodded. “It’s not new. You’re just now positioned to encounter it.”

That distinction mattered.

“So I’m not… participating,” she said.

“You are,” Tomas replied. “Just not *acting*.”

Allison frowned slightly. “That sounds like a contradiction.”

“It’s not,” Tomas said calmly. “It’s infrastructure logic. Your presence changes the system even if you do nothing. The question is whether you do so intentionally.”

They entered a long room lined with open alcoves, each one occupied by tables, chairs, and layered storage systems that combined physical recordkeeping with enchantments Allison had learned not to probe. The magic here was old, stable, and uninterested in reaction.

No one looked up when they entered.

Not because Allison was invisible—but because this room was designed not to respond to novelty.

Tomas stopped near the perimeter, deliberately outside the flow of work. “Before we begin,” he said, “we establish scope.”

Allison turned fully toward him. She’d learned that when Tomas said *before we begin*, it mattered.

“You will be present,” he said. “You will observe. You may ask questions of me, quietly. You will not intervene, comment, or interpret unless explicitly invited.”

“And if I notice something?” Allison asked.

“Then you notice it,” Tomas said. “That’s allowed.”

“And after?”

“You record it privately. You bring it to me. We decide together whether it becomes institutional knowledge.”

That last part settled something in her chest she hadn’t realized was braced.

“Nothing moves forward without calibration,” she said.

“Correct,” Tomas replied. “Recognition is not alignment.”

The words carried weight. Threshold language.

They took seats at a small side table that allowed a clear view of one alcove without intruding into it. Allison sat carefully, aware of how much space she occupied, how visible even stillness could be.

A quiet review was underway nearby—two Confluence members cross-referencing records, their conversation economical, almost ritualized.

“What am I being evaluated for?” Allison asked softly.

Tomas placed a thin slate between them and turned it so she could see.

**Evaluation Focus:**  
– Response to classification  
– Stability under contextual load  
– Recognition without alignment

Her breath caught slightly at the last line.

Recognition without alignment.

She felt it as a physical idea—like standing near something resonant and deliberately choosing not to attune to it. Not suppression. Just… restraint.

“I won’t be exposed to foreign systems today,” she said, needing to confirm it.

“No,” Tomas agreed. “This is preparatory.”

Preparatory was not *practice*.  
Not *training*.

It meant *being ready to remain unchanged*.

They sat in silence.

Time moved differently here—not slower, but less marked. Allison watched patterns emerge: how questions were framed to avoid presumption, how decisions were deferred rather than rushed, how uncertainty was documented instead of erased.

This was how the Confluence stayed calm.

Not by avoiding destabilization—but by refusing to solve it too quickly.

“Do you feel pressure?” Tomas asked quietly.

Allison checked herself carefully.

Not temptation.  
Not fear.

“Yes,” she said. “But it’s internal.”

“From what source?”

“From being accounted for,” she replied. “From knowing that my presence is now… measurable.”

Tomas nodded. “That’s expected.”

Across the room, one of the archivists glanced up and looked directly at Allison—not startled, not wary. Just acknowledging that she existed *within scope*.

Allison felt the attention register faintly along her skin, like warmth without heat.

She did not respond.

She breathed.

The sensation passed.

Tomas didn’t comment. His silence was deliberate—a confirmation that she had stayed within bounds.

Later, a woman approached their table. She moved with the contained confidence of someone used to ethical weight.

“Allison,” she said. “I’m Lysa Merewen.”

Allison rose automatically, then stopped herself and inclined her head instead. Presence without submission.

“Hello.”

“You’re listed as an observer today,” Lysa said.

“Yes.”

Lysa’s eyes flicked to Tomas, seeking confirmation rather than permission.

“Within bounds,” Tomas said.

Relief crossed Lysa’s face before she masked it. “Good. I wanted to ask if you’d be willing to sit in on a procedural review later. No participation. Just… presence.”

Allison felt the internal check engage—consent, scope, consequence.

“Yes,” she said. “If it remains observation-only.”

“It will,” Lysa said quickly. “It matters that you’re seen choosing.”

Choosing.

Not acting.  
Not deciding for others.

Choosing restraint.

When Lysa returned to her work, Allison sat slowly.

“They’re learning how to use me,” she said under her breath.

Tomas corrected her gently. “They’re learning how to *include* you without destabilizing themselves.”

Allison watched the room again—the records, the patience, the deliberate refusal of urgency.

“Is there a difference?” she asked.

“There can be,” Tomas said. “If you continue to choose not to exceed what is asked of you.”

Allison nodded.

For the first time since *Reintegration* had appeared in her file, she understood her usefulness clearly.

She was not here to change the Confluence.

She was here to be **counted**—

And to learn, quietly, what that counting cost.
