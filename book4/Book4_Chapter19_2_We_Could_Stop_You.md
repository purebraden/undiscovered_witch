Chapter 19.2 — “We Could Stop You…”

They do not move.

That is how Allison knows Tomas is not finished.

The space remains what it was—overlooked, transitional, deliberately unremarkable. The Confluence hum continues at the edges, unchanged. If anything, it feels quieter now, as though the system itself has registered that something has already been said that cannot be unsaid.

Allison waits.

She does not prompt him. She understands, suddenly, that if she asks the obvious question, she will be choosing the timing of the answer. And timing, right now, matters.

Tomas watches her for a moment longer than strictly necessary. Not assessing. Confirming.

“There’s one more thing,” he says finally. “And if I don’t say it now, then I’m choosing for you.”

She nods once. “Okay.”

“We could stop you,” Tomas says.

He doesn’t emphasize the words. He doesn’t brace them with justification. He offers them the same way he offered everything else tonight: plainly, because accuracy matters more than comfort.

Allison lets the sentence exist between them.

“Yes,” she says. “I know.”

Tomas exhales—not in relief, exactly, but in recognition. “Most people don’t,” he says. “They assume restraint means inability.”

“And you’re saying it doesn’t.”

“I’m saying it’s a choice,” Tomas replies. “One we are still capable of making.”

“Not forever,” Allison says.

“No,” he agrees. “And not cleanly. But there are mechanisms. Constraints. Ways to close paths. Not because you’ve done anything wrong—”

“But because I might,” Allison finishes.

“Yes.”

She considers that carefully. “That’s…oddly reassuring.”

Tomas’s mouth curves slightly. “It should be.”

Silence settles again, heavier this time. This is usually where institutions stop talking—where authority lets implication do the work. Tomas does not do that.

“We don’t want to,” he says.

The words land with more weight than the first statement did.

Allison looks at him. “Because you trust me?”

Tomas shakes his head. “Trust is too fragile to build policy on.”

“Then why?”

“Because stopping you now would require us to decide for you,” Tomas says. “And that would undo everything we’ve just acknowledged.”

Allison feels the shape of it immediately. “You’d be taking responsibility back.”

“Yes.”

“And pretending that awareness is only acceptable up to the point where it becomes inconvenient.”

“Yes.”

The Confluence hum feels different now—not louder, but closer. As if the system is aware of being named without being accused.

“If we intervened,” Tomas continues, “we would be saying that obligation exists only when it aligns with institutional tolerance. That responsibility is conditional. And once we say that, none of this means what we claimed it did.”

Allison nods slowly. “So restraint on your part would actually be avoidance.”

“Yes,” Tomas says. “And avoidance is not protection.”

She lets that settle. It aligns too cleanly with everything Naelis showed her to be coincidence.

“So you could stop me,” she says.

“Yes.”

“But you won’t.”

“We won’t,” Tomas confirms. “Unless you force us to.”

Allison meets his gaze. “By doing something reckless.”

“By acting as if you don’t understand what you now understand,” Tomas corrects.

That distinction matters. It shifts the burden exactly where it belongs.

“If I don’t act yet,” Allison says, “that’s still a choice.”

“Yes.”

“And when I do—”

“That will be a choice too,” Tomas says. “One we will respond to, not preempt.”

Something settles inside Allison then—not resolve, not urgency.

Recognition.

It feels heavier than she expected. Not dramatic. Not sharp. More like something lowering into place and changing the balance of everything around it.

She exhales slowly, more sound than breath, and for the first time since the conversation began, she doesn’t rush to fill the silence.

“I keep hearing myself talk like I’m still trying to be small,” she says at last. “Like if I stay careful enough, this will stay theoretical.”

Tomas does not interrupt. He doesn’t nod either. He lets the space hold.

“I keep framing this as if I act,” Allison continues. “As though attention is something I can put down again. As though seeing doesn’t already count.”

She looks at her hands, resting loosely in her lap. They aren’t clenched. They aren’t steady either.

“That stopped being true awhile ago,” she says quietly. “I just didn’t want to admit when.”

“Yes,” Tomas says.

The single word lands with weight. Confirmation, not correction.

Allison lifts her gaze back to him. “I don’t actually think you’re worried I’ll do something reckless,” she says. “I think you’re worried I’ll do something careful in a system that depends on people staying unexamined.”

Tomas studies her for a long moment. There is no defensiveness in his expression. Only recognition.

“That’s part of it,” he says.

She nods once. “Because careful choices don’t collapse neatly. They spread.”

“Yes.”

Allison leans back slightly, letting her shoulders settle instead of curving inward the way they have for most of her life when she’s trying not to be a problem. The shift is small. It changes everything.

“I’m not going to pretend I don’t see what I see,” she says. “And I’m not going to perform ignorance to make other people more comfortable.”

Tomas’s attention sharpens—not alarmed, not approving. Witnessing.

“That doesn’t mean I’m about to start intervening,” she adds. A faint edge of dry humor touches the words, but it doesn’t soften them. “I understand restraint. I understand timing.”

“I know,” Tomas says.

“But I’m done framing myself as something that needs to be contained preemptively,” Allison continues. “I’m not dangerous because I’m impulsive. I’m dangerous because I notice patterns and don’t forget them.”

The word dangerous sits between them, unsoftened.

Tomas nods once. “Yes.”

Allison closes her eyes briefly, then opens them again. There is a cost here, and she is finally letting herself feel it.

“I know this doesn’t end with a promise,” she says. “I know you don’t get to reassure yourselves by having one.”

“That’s true,” Tomas replies.

“And I don’t get to reassure myself either,” she adds. “I don’t get to pretend that staying still will keep things clean.”

“No,” Tomas says gently.

She exhales again, slower this time. When she speaks, her voice is steady—not because she’s certain, but because she’s finished negotiating with the reality of the moment.

“I can’t promise I’ll always make the right choice,” Allison says. “But I can promise I won’t make the quiet one just because it lets me disappear again.”

That lands.

Tomas inclines his head. Not approval. Acknowledgment.

They remain where they are. No ceremony marks the end of the conversation. No permission is granted. No rules rewritten.

But something fundamental has shifted.

Allison is no longer trying to reduce her impact to make the world easier to manage.

And the most consequential permission she has been given is not the freedom to act—

—but the refusal to stop her from becoming fully responsible for what she already understands.
