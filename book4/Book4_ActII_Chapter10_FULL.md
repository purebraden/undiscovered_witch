# Chapter 10 — Quiet Prohibition

## Scene 10.0 — Institutional Framing

The room was built to look agreeable.

Allison noticed that before anyone spoke. The light was warmer than elsewhere in the Confluence, angled to soften shadows rather than eliminate them. The table—round, not long—sat low enough that no one needed to look up to speak. The chairs were identical, spaced so no seat could claim priority without moving first.

Consent optics, Allison thought, and then stopped herself. Thinking in those terms felt dangerously close to analysis.

Tomas entered with her and stopped just inside the doorway. He did not take a seat. He did not gesture for her to do so either. The omission felt deliberate.

“Allison,” a woman said, standing as they approached the table. Her voice was calm, practiced without sounding rehearsed. “Thank you for coming.”

Allison nodded and took the chair nearest to her without being directed. It felt important not to hesitate, not to appear uncertain. She folded her hands in her lap and waited.

Two others were already seated. Neither introduced themselves.

Tomas remained behind her left shoulder. Close enough to be present. Far enough to be clearly excluded.

“We’ll keep this brief,” the woman said. “This is a framing conversation, not a review.”

Allison absorbed that. *Framing* meant boundaries. *Not a review* meant nothing would be decided here.

The woman sat. No one else moved.

“You are not in trouble,” she continued, as if anticipating the question. “There are no violations associated with your conduct to date.”

Allison felt the small, instinctive release in her chest—and immediately distrusted it.

“This conversation exists,” the woman said, “because observed conditions have changed.”

Not *you*.
*Conditions*.

Allison nodded once.

Tomas shifted slightly behind her. Not a warning. A presence.

“We are speaking carefully,” the woman went on, “because precision matters. Your participation in observation sessions has produced outcomes that differ from baseline expectation.”

Allison felt the familiar tightening. She did not look at Tomas.

“These outcomes are favorable,” the woman said. “Consistently so.”

The word *favorable* sounded strange in the room. Not celebratory. Just… catalogued.

“However,” the woman continued, “favorability is not the sole metric by which stability is assessed.”

There it was.

Stability.

Allison recognized the structure of the argument before it completed itself. Favorable outcomes increased scrutiny. Increased scrutiny narrowed space.

“We are not prepared,” the woman said, “to expand the scope of your exposure at this time.”

Allison waited. The phrasing was careful. *Not prepared*. *At this time*. She could hear the door in it—closed, but not sealed.

“This is not a prohibition,” the woman added smoothly. “It is a deferral.”

Allison nodded again. She had learned the difference. Deferral meant waiting without timeline.

Tomas spoke then, for the first time. “For clarity,” he said, his voice even, “this does not alter her current restrictions.”

“That’s correct,” the woman said. “Existing constraints remain appropriate.”

Appropriate. Another word that smoothed edges without removing them.

Allison felt the urge to ask what *would* be appropriate. She recognized the urge as action.

She did not indulge it.

“We want to be explicit,” the woman said, turning her attention back to Allison, “about what this means for you.”

Allison met her gaze. The woman’s eyes were kind in the way professionals learned to be kind: attentive, not intimate.

“You will continue supervised observation,” the woman said. “You will not pursue independent inquiry. You will not attempt to deepen recognition beyond current parameters.”

Allison’s breath remained steady.

“This request is made,” the woman added, “for stability.”

The phrase landed with quiet finality.

Allison understood exactly what was being asked of her. Not to stop noticing. Not to forget what she had already seen. Only not to go further.

She also understood—without anyone saying it—that going further was possible.

The recognition settled in her chest like weight.

Tomas did not speak.

“We are aware,” the woman continued, “that restraint carries its own cost. That is noted.”

Not addressed. Not mitigated. Noted.

“If, in the future,” the woman said, “conditions change sufficiently to warrant reassessment, we will initiate that conversation.”

Allison heard the careful absence of *you may request*.

“Do you have questions?” the woman asked.

The table waited.

Allison felt the room’s attention narrow—not pressure exactly, but focus. This was the moment where response would be measured, not for content but for implication.

“No,” Allison said.

The woman smiled, just slightly. Relief or approval—Allison couldn’t tell which.

“Thank you,” the woman said. “That will be all.”

No dismissal. No formal close. The meeting ended because there was nothing more to say.

As Allison stood, Tomas placed a hand lightly at her back—not steering, just grounding.

In the corridor outside, the Confluence’s hum returned to its usual register, steady and impersonal. Allison realized only then how quiet the room had been.

She did not ask Tomas what he thought.

He did not offer.

Behind them, the agreeable room remained exactly as it was—warm light, round table, equal chairs—ready to contain the next careful conversation.


## Scene 10.1 — The Prohibition

They did not return to the agreeable room.

That was the first difference Allison noticed, and it mattered more than the change in corridor. The space Tomas guided her toward was smaller, less curated. Still calm. Still human-scaled. But without the warmth that suggested someone wanted her to feel comfortable about agreeing.

There was no table this time. Only three chairs arranged in a shallow arc, all facing the same direction.

Facing a wall panel that wasn’t lit yet.

Consent optics again, Allison thought, and felt the reflexive discomfort at how quickly the pattern presented itself. She was learning to recognize design the way she recognized magic: structure first, intention second.

Tomas stopped just outside the doorway.

He didn’t enter immediately.

That pause was not for her benefit. It felt like a timing check—an internal alignment, not magical, but procedural. Then he stepped in with her, and the door sealed behind them with a soft sound that wasn’t quite a lock.

A man stood near the panel. He looked unremarkable by Confluence standards: calm posture, neutral expression, presence tuned to avoid dominance. If he had been chosen for anything, it was his ability to make restriction sound like care.

“Allison,” he said, as if they had already met. “Thank you for your flexibility.”

Allison sat in the chair closest to the door without being directed. Tomas remained behind and slightly to the side, where his presence was unmistakable and his authority clearly limited.

“This will be brief,” the man said. “We’re issuing operational guidance. Not commentary.”

Operational guidance meant it would apply whether she agreed with it or not.

The wall panel illuminated without fanfare. Not data. Not graphs. A short list of statements, arranged like policy but written like conversation.

The man didn’t look at the panel when he spoke. He watched Allison instead.

“We are continuing observation under current parameters,” he said. “That remains appropriate.”

Appropriate.

“And we are limiting exposure to non-instructional contexts,” he continued, “until review concludes.”

Allison held her face steady. The phrasing was clean enough that she could feel the edges of what it contained.

“Specifically,” he said, “you will not pursue additional alignment pathways.”

The words were simple.

Allison felt them land like something physical.

She did not ask what he meant. She already knew. She had felt the pathways. She had recognized their shape in her own awareness, the way possibility gathered at the edges of a moment.

She kept her hands folded.

The man continued, tone unchanged. “You will not attempt to deepen recognition beyond supervised sessions. You will not attempt to reproduce or refine what has been observed.”

Refine.

As if she had been doing this on purpose.

“As we stated previously,” the man added, “your compliance is not in question. This is not responsive. It is precautionary.”

Precautionary was one of those words that sounded reasonable in any context. It made restriction feel like maturity.

Tomas spoke once, a narrow correction. “For clarity,” he said, “recognition events may still occur involuntarily.”

The man inclined his head, as if grateful for the precision. “Correct,” he said. “Recognition may occur. The constraint is on pursuit.”

Pursuit.

Allison felt the word tighten around her chest.

She was being told she could not want something too openly. She could not lean toward it. She could not ask herself the next question in a way that became observable.

The man’s gaze remained steady. “This guidance is being issued for stability.”

Allison heard the repetition of that word as a kind of seal. Not explanation. Not justification. A category that overrode discussion.

“You are not being punished,” the man said, as if reading her thoughts. “You are being protected from premature escalation.”

Allison almost asked: protected by whom? from whom?

The questions rose and fell inside her without reaching her mouth.

“Premature escalation,” the man continued, “creates interpretive conflict. It forces classification before we are prepared to classify.”

The admission was quiet and unsettling: they didn’t know what she was, not in their own terms. And they were solving that uncertainty the only way institutions solved uncertainty.

By narrowing.

The panel updated slightly—one line shifting down as if someone had inserted it after the meeting began.

“Any deviation from this guidance,” the man said, “will result in suspension of exposure until further review.”

Allison’s pulse ticked up.

She did not react.

Further review could mean anything. Further review was a hallway that didn’t end.

The man’s tone softened by half a degree. Not warmth. Just care in delivery. “We will reassess,” he said, “when conditions support it.”

When conditions support it.

Not when she was ready. Not when she asked. When conditions—external, institutional, undefined—shifted.

Allison nodded once. A small motion. Minimal, safe.

“Thank you,” the man said immediately, as if her nod had completed a contract. “That is the response we expected.”

Allison felt the quiet pressure of being thanked for agreement she had not yet given.

Tomas remained silent.

The man glanced briefly toward him, then back to Allison. “Tomas will continue to supervise your sessions,” he said. “And he will continue to stop them early when necessary.”

As if ending her access to experience were a routine safety feature. As if Tomas’s caution were part of the design, not a personal intervention.

Tomas’s jaw tightened once—so small Allison almost missed it.

“Yes,” Tomas said.

The man smiled faintly. “We appreciate your cooperation,” he said to Allison. “It helps maintain stability for all parties.”

All parties.

Allison understood the phrase as a quiet reminder: the Fairy World was an interested party. The Confluence was an interested party. She was not the only one affected by her recognition.

Maybe she wasn’t even the primary one.

The panel dimmed.

The meeting ended without ending. No dismissal. No closing words. Just the cessation of instruction.

Allison stood because there was nothing else to do.

Tomas placed himself beside her—not touching, not leading, simply aligning his position with hers like a shield that wasn’t allowed to be visible.

As they left the room, the man spoke one last time.

“Allison,” he said, tone unchanged. “This is temporary.”

Allison turned her head just enough to acknowledge him.

Temporary was a word people used when they wanted you to stop asking how long.

She nodded again.

In the corridor, the hum felt louder. Allison realized she had been holding her breath.

Tomas did not speak until they were far enough away that no panel could activate without warning.

Then he said, very quietly, “Do not interpret ‘temporary’ as permission.”

Allison’s throat tightened.

“I won’t,” she said.

Tomas looked ahead, not at her. “Good,” he said. “Because they will.”


## Interlude 10.x — Misaligned Normalcy

The call connected on the second ring.

“Allison?” Her mother’s voice came through first, bright with relief and the faint clatter of a kitchen behind it. “Hi, sweetheart. We weren’t sure you’d be free.”

“I am,” Allison said. She set the comm unit on the desk and leaned back against the wall. The room’s light remained steady, indifferent.

Her father’s voice joined a moment later, warm and familiar. “You sound good,” he said immediately, as if saying it could make it true.

“I’m fine,” Allison said.

The phrase hung in the air, familiar and insufficient.

There was a brief shuffle of sound on the other end—someone moving a pan, a chair scraping softly. Ordinary life continuing.

“Where are you calling from?” her mother asked.

“My room,” Allison said. “It’s… quiet.”

“That’s good,” her mother said. “Quiet helps you rest.”

Allison pressed her shoulder more firmly into the wall. “I guess.”

They talked for a few minutes about nothing important. About a neighbor’s new dog that barked too much. About a recipe her mother was trying to perfect. About the weather shifting earlier than expected. Allison listened to the cadence of home—the way voices overlapped slightly, the way silence never lasted long.

“So,” her mother said eventually, carefully casual, “how’s everything going there?”

Allison inhaled.

“It’s… structured,” she said. True. “There’s a lot of observation.”

Her father hummed softly. “Observation like—tests?”

“No,” Allison said quickly. Too quickly. She adjusted. “Not tests. Just… watching.”

Watching was safe. Watching didn’t invite follow-up.

“And are you learning things?” her mother asked.

Allison opened her mouth, then closed it again.

Learning implied accumulation. Progress. Something she could name and bring back.

“I’m learning how things work,” she said instead.

There was a pause. Not awkward. Considerate. The kind of pause that waited for more without demanding it.

“And how do *you* feel about it?” her father asked.

Allison stared at the blank wall across from her. Her voice sounded steady in her own ears.

“I feel… busy,” she said after a moment. “Even when I’m not doing anything.”

Her mother laughed softly. “That sounds like school.”

Allison almost smiled.

“It’s different,” she said. Not defensive. Just precise. “There’s a lot of waiting.”

“Well,” her father said, “waiting can be good sometimes. Gives you time to think.”

The words landed wrong, though she knew they weren’t meant to.

“Are you eating okay?” her mother asked.

“Yes.”

“Sleeping?”

“Yes.”

“Do you need anything?” her mother asked, gently.

Allison hesitated.

“There’s nothing I need,” she said.

It was true.

And it wasn’t.

“That’s good,” her father said, relieved.

They talked a little longer. About a show they’d all watched once. About a book her mother thought Allison might like when she got back. About plans that assumed time would move the way it always had.

Allison responded when spoken to. She laughed once, softly, at something that wasn’t very funny. She kept her voice even.

When the call ended, it did so naturally.

“Be safe,” her mother said.

“I will,” Allison replied.

The connection closed.

The room fell quiet again, the Confluence’s low hum sliding back into place as if it had been waiting.

Allison stayed where she was, the comm unit cooling beneath her fingers.

Somewhere else, her family’s evening continued.

She folded her hands in her lap.

The room did not change.

The weight of what she hadn’t said settled in quietly.

Allison did not try to translate it.

She waited.


## Scene 10.2 — Aftermath Without Resolution

The schedule changed without announcement.

Allison noticed because the interval between sessions lengthened—not enough to feel like cancellation, just enough to register as adjustment. Her calendar populated itself with new placeholders that said nothing beyond *pending*. The spaces where observation had been were left intact, like outlines erased lightly enough to remain visible.

Tomas walked with her to the waiting area and stopped at the threshold. He did not follow her inside.

“That’s new,” Allison said, quietly.

“Yes,” Tomas replied. His voice carried the same measured tone it always did, but his pause came a fraction later than usual. “It will likely continue.”

The waiting area had not been designed for waiting.

It had chairs, certainly—comfortable, neutral—but no focal point. No screens. No panels. Nothing that suggested progress or countdown. The light remained steady, without dimming or brightening to mark time. Allison took a seat and placed her hands in her lap, mirroring a posture that had become familiar.

Tomas did not sit.

They waited in silence for a few moments before Allison noticed the shift—not sound, not movement, just a subtle change in how Tomas held himself. His attention had angled slightly away from her.

She followed his gaze.

A figure stood near one of the inactive wall panels, not part of the session flow. He wasn’t positioned like a liaison or a mediator. He wasn’t watching Allison directly at all. His focus was on a data surface projected low and close, dense enough that it was unreadable from where she sat.

He did not look up.

“That’s Toren Ashwill,” Tomas said quietly. “Outcome analysis.”

Allison absorbed the name.

Toren remained still, his attention fixed on the projection. The data shifted once, subtly, as if responding to something she couldn’t see. He made no note, no gesture of acknowledgment.

“Does he—” Allison began, then stopped.

“Not directly,” Tomas said. “He watches results.”

Allison looked back at the empty space in front of her. Being observed without being addressed felt different than the meetings. Less personal. More permanent.

“They will normalize the restriction,” Tomas continued, as if the interruption hadn’t happened. “That is how stability is maintained.”

Allison nodded once.

“What does that look like?” she asked.

Tomas considered the question. “Fewer sessions,” he said. “Shorter durations. More review language.”

“And less… scope,” Allison said.

“Yes,” Tomas replied. He did not correct her phrasing.

A liaison crossed the far end of the room, not approaching them, but visible enough to register presence. Toren did not move.

“This is not a response to something you did,” Tomas said. “It is a response to what you are capable of doing.”

Allison felt the distinction settle into place. Capability was not an action. It was a condition.

“I won’t push,” she said.

“I know,” Tomas replied. He did not sound reassured. He sounded resigned.

A chime sounded softly overhead—not a summons, just a marker. Allison’s next observation session had been delayed again. The system offered no explanation.

“This is where patience becomes instruction,” Tomas said. “They will observe how you respond to less.”

Allison’s fingers curled slightly, then relaxed.

“And if I respond… well?” she asked.

Tomas met her gaze fully for the first time since the meeting. His expression was careful, almost apologetic.

“Then nothing will happen,” he said.

The answer landed with quiet finality.

Nothing happening was the outcome. Nothing escalating. Nothing changing. Stability preserved through absence.

A second chime sounded, softer than the first. Allison’s schedule updated again. A session removed entirely, replaced with *review pending*.

She watched the text fade.

“I can feel it,” she said, after a moment. “The alignment. It doesn’t stop just because they say not to.”

Tomas’s jaw tightened. “I know.”

He lowered his voice, though no one was close enough to hear. “That is why restraint matters,” he said. “Not because the pathways disappear—but because acting on them would be observable.”

Allison understood. Recognition without action was invisible enough to tolerate. Pursuit would leave a trace.

“I won’t,” she said again.

Tomas inclined his head. “Good.”

The word carried no praise.

A door opened at the far end of the waiting area. Someone else was called forward. Toren moved then—not toward Allison, but away, the data surface collapsing as he did.

Allison remained seated.

Time passed. Or something like it.

When Tomas finally gestured for her to stand, it was not because anything had resolved. It was because the waiting period had reached an acceptable length.

“We’re done for today,” he said.

Allison rose. The room felt unchanged, as if she had never entered it.

As they walked back through the corridor, Allison became aware of the steady hum again—the Confluence resuming its normal cadence around her. The institution had absorbed the disruption and adjusted its edges. The restriction was now part of the environment.

At the doorway to her assigned quarters, Tomas stopped.

“This is where many people mistake silence for permission,” he said, carefully. “Do not.”

Allison met his eyes. “I won’t.”

Tomas hesitated, then added, “They will be watching for patience. Not progress.”

Allison nodded.

Inside her room, the light remained exactly as it had been before. Her schedule displayed its new, narrowed shape. Fewer entries. More empty space.

She sat on the edge of the bed and folded her hands in her lap.

The pathways were still there.

She did not reach for them.

The day ended without conclusion.
