## Chapter 13.5 — Not Ours to Stabilize

Naelis did not arrive through a gate.

That alone was a message.

The Fairy World did not respect Confluence boundaries the way other domains did—not because it could not, but because it refused to pretend those boundaries were natural. Naelis manifested instead at the margin of a neutral exchange space, where jurisdiction blurred just enough to make a point.

The air shifted first.

Not pressure. Not temperature. *Orientation.*

Ren felt it immediately. Iria a moment later.

Tomas was last—and only because he had trained himself not to react to anything until it finished happening.

Naelis stepped forward as if she had been there all along.

Her form was precise in the way the Fairy World preferred: not ornamental, not symbolic, but *intentional*. Every line of her presence suggested purpose without hierarchy. She wore no markers of rank. She did not need them.

“You’re late,” Iria said carefully.

Naelis smiled faintly. “You’re early.”

Ren inclined his head. “You are aware of the anomaly.”

Naelis’s gaze sharpened. “Stop calling her that.”

Silence tightened.

“I am aware of the *person*,” Naelis continued. “And of the way you are reshaping the world around her while insisting you haven’t touched her.”

Tomas said nothing.

Naelis turned to him briefly. “You’re the one measuring feedback,” she said. “You already know this.”

Tomas did not deny it.

“You’ve been very quiet,” Naelis said to Iria.

“We are being cautious,” Iria replied.

Naelis laughed softly. “You always say that when you’re deciding who gets hurt first.”

Ren spoke evenly. “Stability requires—”

“—distance,” Naelis finished for him. “Delay. Silence.” Her smile faded. “We’ve seen this before.”

Ren’s eyes narrowed. “In your domain.”

“In many,” Naelis said. “Expandables don’t belong to one world.”

She gestured lightly, and for just a moment the space felt *crowded*—not with beings, but with memory.

“We’ve watched this pattern repeat,” Naelis continued. “A system identifies someone who doesn’t fit. It stops speaking plainly. It starts shaping the environment instead. Restrictions. Soft walls. Careful quiet.”

Iria swallowed.

“The person feels the pressure,” Naelis said. “They don’t know why. So they blame themselves.”

Tomas shifted. “That’s not containment failure. That’s—”

“That’s design,” Naelis said calmly.

Ren’s voice sharpened. “Containment is meant to protect.”

“Yes,” Naelis agreed. “From *you*.”

The words landed without heat.

“You treat expandability like a fault,” she continued. “Like a crack that spreads if acknowledged. But expandability isn’t what destabilizes systems.”

Ren asked quietly, “Then what does?”

“Dishonesty,” Naelis said.

Iria stepped forward a fraction. “We are trying not to overwhelm her.”

Naelis met her gaze. “You are overwhelming her with absence.”

The air stilled.

“She already knows something is wrong,” Naelis said. “Your fear is louder than any explanation.”

Ren said, “Visibility creates claim.”

Naelis nodded. “Yes. And silence creates *distortion*.”

She turned slightly, as if addressing the room itself. “Every expandable we’ve lost trusted the system more than themselves at the beginning. By the time they realized the system was afraid of them, they were already bent out of shape.”

Iria’s voice was quiet. “Lost how?”

Naelis looked at her. “Some broke. Some vanished. Some became exactly what the system feared.”

Ren exhaled slowly. “You’re saying telling her accelerates expansion.”

“Yes,” Naelis said. “So does not telling her.”

Tomas finally spoke. “But without context, expansion is brittle.”

Naelis nodded once. “Exactly.”

Ren’s jaw tightened. “So what are you proposing?”

Naelis smiled again, but there was no warmth in it. “We’re not proposing. We’re refusing.”

“Refusing what?” Iria asked.

“Neutrality,” Naelis said. “Participation in containment that treats a person as infrastructure.”

Ren said, “That creates conflict.”

“Yes,” Naelis agreed. “But it creates *honest* conflict.”

She stepped closer—not threatening, just undeniable. “She will expand. With you. Around you. Or despite you.”

Iria whispered, “And if we choose wrong?”

Naelis’s voice softened, just slightly. “Then she learns to fear herself before she learns to understand herself.”

That settled heavily in the space.

Tomas looked away.

Naelis stepped back, her presence already beginning to thin. “The Fairy World will not stabilize a silence that is actively doing harm.”

Ren asked, “Is this a warning?”

Naelis shook her head. “It’s precedent.”

She paused at the edge of the space. “One more thing. You’re not running out of time because of power.”

She looked back at Iria.

“You’re running out of time because she’s already adapting.”

Then she was gone.

The room remained.

Iria closed her eyes. “We’ve lost neutrality.”

Ren nodded. “Yes.”

Tomas said quietly, “And gained truth pressure.”

No one disagreed.
