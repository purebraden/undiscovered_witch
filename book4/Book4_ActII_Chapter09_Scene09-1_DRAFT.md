## Scene 9.1 — Outcome Drift

The Fairy World did not announce the improvement.

If Allison had been looking for a moment to mark—some point where she could say *this is where it changed*—she would have missed it. The environment behaved as it always did: diffuse light without source, ground that resisted classification, air that felt organized rather than alive. There was no observer present this time, no concentrated authority to draw the eye or the mind.

And yet the process moved forward more cleanly.

Allison recognized that before Tomas spoke.

They had been placed differently than in the prior exposure—not far, not enough to count as relocation, but enough to register as a procedural adjustment. Tomas had framed it as a control variation, and Allison had accepted the explanation without asking for more. Control meant repetition with constraint. Control meant watching the same mechanism under slightly altered conditions.

“Remain,” Tomas said.

Allison remained.

The Fairy World engaged a minor process nearby. Allison could not see its beginning, only the way the environment prepared for it—the subtle gathering of attention, the redistribution of weight in the air. She had learned to notice that preparation the way someone notices a room inhale before a speech.

This was usually where friction appeared.

Allison felt the expectation of it in her body, like bracing for a door that always stuck. The sense of impending correction hovered just ahead of the moment, familiar enough that her mind filled in the shape without conscious effort.

The correction did not come.

Instead, the process resolved.

Not quickly. Not dramatically. Simply… without resistance.

Allison’s breath caught. She stilled it immediately, unwilling to let even that register as reaction.

Tomas’s wrist device chimed once.

He did not speak.

The Fairy World continued on its path, the minor process concluding with a quiet finality that left no residue of strain behind it. The environment did not ripple. It did not adjust further. It behaved as if the resolution had been expected all along.

That absence of aftermath was what unsettled Allison the most.

She had not touched anything. She had not reached. She had not even *thought* differently, not consciously. And yet she had known—before it happened—that the usual friction would not appear.

Recognition had preceded outcome.

The thought landed heavily. Allison forced herself not to follow it.

“Duration,” Tomas said, after a moment. “Thirty seconds more.”

Allison nodded once.

Another minor process engaged at the periphery of her awareness. Smaller than the first. Less consequential. The kind of thing that rarely drew attention on its own.

Allison recognized its structure too.

The recognition felt like pattern-matching, not insight. As if her awareness were lining up shapes she had seen before and noticing where they failed to snag. The absence of friction appeared again, faint but unmistakable.

The process resolved.

Tomas ended the session immediately.

“Withdraw,” he said.

Allison followed without hesitation. The movement felt almost abrupt after the quiet precision of the Fairy World’s behavior. The return to Confluence space brought the familiar hum rushing back, steadier and louder by contrast.

They stopped in a corridor Allison did not recognize. She noted the difference without comment.

Tomas looked at his wrist device for longer than before.

“That should not have resolved at baseline efficiency,” he said.

Allison stared at the floor. “I didn’t—”

“I know,” Tomas said. “Do not repeat that.”

The instruction carried more weight this time.

“This is outcome drift,” Tomas continued. “Positive deviation without causal input.”

Allison’s stomach tightened. “From me?”

“From your presence,” Tomas said. “Not from your action.”

Allison’s hands curled slightly at her sides, then relaxed. Presence was not something she could turn off. The realization made her feel exposed in a way action never had.

“This will be logged,” Tomas said. “Independently of the prior session.”

Allison nodded.

“The absence of resistance is the signal,” Tomas added. “Not the resolution itself.”

Allison swallowed. She had felt that too—the way the system had behaved as if it no longer needed to correct for something it usually did.

“You are not to anticipate outcomes,” Tomas said.

“I didn’t mean to,” Allison replied.

“I know,” Tomas said again, and the repetition no longer felt reassuring. “That does not change the effect.”

He adjusted his wrist device. The Confluence’s ambient hum shifted, tightening almost imperceptibly.

“Further exposure is suspended until review,” Tomas said.

“All right,” Allison said.

They stood there for a moment, the corridor holding them in careful neutrality.

Allison felt the urge to ask what the Fairy World had *done differently*, to understand why the process had resolved without friction. The urge passed when she recognized it as the same instinct Tomas kept warning her against.

Curiosity would be action.

She did not indulge it.

Tomas gestured down the corridor. “Logging,” he said.

Allison followed.

She carried with her the most unsettling realization yet: not that she could change outcomes, but that outcomes seemed to change in advance of her choosing anything at all.
