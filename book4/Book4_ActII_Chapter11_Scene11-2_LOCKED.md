## Scene 11.2 — Absence as Control

The absence arrived quietly.

Allison noticed it the next morning when her schedule loaded without comment. No explanation banner. No advisory note. Just fewer entries, arranged with the same neutral spacing as before, as if nothing had been removed at all.

She stared at it longer than she meant to.

Tomas arrived on time, which meant the day was proceeding as expected.

“You’re not late,” Allison said, as he joined her in the corridor.

“No,” Tomas replied. “Neither is the system.”

They walked in parallel for a few steps before Allison spoke again.

“There’s less,” she said.

“Yes,” Tomas said.

That was the confirmation. Not temporary. Not adjusted. Just yes.

They reached the waiting area and stopped. Tomas did not indicate that she should enter. Instead, he remained where he was, hands folded, posture neutral.

“This is intentional,” he said. “Reduction is a form of stabilization.”

Allison nodded. She could feel the truth of it in her body. Less exposure meant fewer chances to notice. Fewer chances to align. Fewer chances for anything to be observed.

“What am I supposed to do?” she asked.

Tomas considered the question carefully. “Nothing,” he said. “That is the point.”

Nothing as instruction.

He gestured, and Allison entered the waiting area alone. The room looked the same as it always had—chairs evenly spaced, light steady, no visible interfaces. The sameness felt more pronounced now that there was less scheduled to interrupt it.

She sat.

Time passed in measured increments that never announced themselves. Allison let her breathing settle, counting nothing, tracking nothing. She focused on the weight of her hands in her lap, the contact of her feet with the floor.

The pathways were still there.

They had not diminished. If anything, they felt clearer—unobstructed by active observation. The absence of sessions had not erased her recognition. It had simply removed the context that made it visible.

She understood, then, what the institution was testing.

Not whether she could act.

Whether she would remain still when nothing distracted her from the possibility.

A soft chime sounded—not a summons. A marker.

Her schedule updated again.

Another session removed.

Allison watched the text fade and felt a flicker of something she refused to name. Not frustration. Not fear. Awareness.

When Tomas returned, it was without ceremony.

“That’s it for today,” he said.

She stood and followed him back into the corridor.

“They’re watching how I respond to this,” she said.

“Yes,” Tomas replied. “Absence is informative.”

Allison absorbed that. “And if I don’t respond at all?”

Tomas paused. Just long enough to matter.

“Then the system will consider that a result,” he said.

They stopped at the junction where their paths diverged.

“This is where people tend to fill the space,” Tomas said, his voice lower now. “With questions. With attempts to be useful.”

Allison looked down at her hands, still folded. “You don’t want me to.”

“I want you,” Tomas said carefully, “to remain within scope.”

Scope again.

She nodded. “I can do that.”

“I know,” Tomas said. There was no relief in his voice. Only acknowledgment.

He turned away.

Allison continued on alone, the corridor stretching ahead of her with familiar precision. The Confluence hummed softly around her, balanced and untroubled.

In her room, the light adjusted automatically. Her schedule settled into its new, reduced form.

She sat on the edge of the bed and let the quiet take shape around her.

Nothing pressed her forward.

Nothing pulled her back.

The door remained exactly where it had always been.

She did not touch it.
