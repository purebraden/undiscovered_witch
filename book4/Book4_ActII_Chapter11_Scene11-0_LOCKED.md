## Scene 11.0 — Data Without Interpretation

The analysis space was not meant for people.

Allison understood that within seconds of entering it. The room was wide and shallow, its walls layered with projected surfaces that resolved and dissolved without ceremony. Data did not present itself so much as persist—arrays nested inside arrays, values updating quietly, never drawing attention to themselves.

Tomas stopped just inside the doorway.

“This is not a session,” he said. “You’re here for context only.”

Allison nodded and took the seat indicated by the floor’s subtle shift in texture. It wasn’t comfortable, but it wasn’t meant to be. Comfort would have implied duration.

The projections adjusted around her presence. Not toward her. Around her.

Numbers moved. Lines shifted. A column re-sorted itself and then stilled.

No one spoke.

Allison realized, distantly, that she was being shown the result of something already decided: that she would see this, but not act on it. The room was arranged so she could observe without participating.

She focused on one of the nearer panels. It displayed a sequence of timestamps paired with outcome markers she recognized only by pattern, not by label. The values were small. Precise. Repeating.

The repetition caught her attention.

“These are comparative runs,” Tomas said, quietly. “Control and presence.”

Presence.

The word landed with weight.

Allison did not ask which column was which. She could already tell.

The pattern wasn’t dramatic. There were no spikes. No anomalies that leapt out. Just a consistent, fractional difference that accumulated the longer the sequence ran.

She felt the familiar tightening in her chest—the recognition of structure without invitation.

“Is that—” she began, then stopped herself.

Tomas didn’t prompt her to continue.

“They’re not attributing cause,” he said instead. “Only recording variance.”

Variance. Another careful word.

Allison scanned the projections again, letting her eyes trace the way values aligned when one set of conditions was present and drifted when it wasn’t. She could feel the alignment beneath it—not as magic, not as something she could touch, but as a shape she recognized.

Her presence was a condition.

She sat back, deliberately, grounding herself in the physicality of the chair. No leaning forward. No focusing too deeply.

A new panel resolved briefly at the far end of the room, then dimmed. Allison caught only a fragment of it—an annotation field, populated and cleared too quickly to read.

Someone else was working.

She did not look for them.

“This is why the restrictions remain,” Tomas said. “Not because of risk. Because of uncertainty.”

Allison absorbed that. Risk could be managed. Uncertainty had to be contained.

“They will continue to collect,” Tomas added. “Longitudinally.”

“How long?” Allison asked, before she could stop herself.

Tomas didn’t answer immediately. When he did, his voice was precise.

“Until the variance resolves,” he said. “Or until it doesn’t.”

That was not a timeline. It was a condition.

Allison returned her attention to the data. The numbers continued to update, indifferent to her scrutiny. Nothing in the room acknowledged her understanding of them.

She felt the urge—not to act, but to *map*. To see how the alignment pathways might correspond to the subtle shifts on the panels. The urge frightened her with its familiarity.

She let it pass.

A figure moved at the periphery of her vision. Not approaching. Not receding. Just present enough to register.

Tomas didn’t look toward them.

“They won’t ask you to comment,” he said. “They won’t ask what you think.”

Allison nodded. That made sense. Interpretation created obligation.

The projections began to fade, one by one, resolving into neutral surfaces.

“That’s all for today,” Tomas said.

No conclusion. No summary.

Allison stood and followed him toward the exit. As she crossed the threshold, she glanced back once—not at the data, but at the empty space where it had been.

The room was already resetting, preparing itself to hold the next set of numbers.

Whatever those numbers would say, they would say it without her.
