## Chapter 16.3 — The One Who Could Not Leave

Allison saw them before she understood why she had known to come here.

They stood near the far edge of the crossing space, not centered, not elevated. If she hadn’t already been looking in that direction, she might have walked past without noticing. There was nothing theatrical about their presence. No shimmer. No announcement.

Just *certainty*.

They were older than most of the people Allison had encountered since arriving — not ancient, not distant, but settled in a way that suggested long familiarity with consequence. Their posture was relaxed without being casual, as if they were used to waiting in rooms where outcomes mattered.

Allison slowed, then stopped.

She didn’t need to be told who they were.

The recognition didn’t come from memory or lesson. It came from alignment — the same quiet internal click she had been noticing more often lately. Pathways shifted, not toward them exactly, but *around* them, like water finding the shape of a stone.

Royal.

The word surfaced without drama.

Not a title.  
A function.

The presence she had felt earlier resolved further, no longer abstract. It wasn’t drawing her closer now. It wasn’t doing anything at all.

It didn’t need to.

They noticed her at the same moment she stopped.

No surprise crossed their face. No assessment. Just acknowledgment — steady, deliberate. They inclined their head slightly, not in greeting, but recognition of shared awareness.

Allison felt her throat tighten.

She had expected — absurdly — that this would feel heavier. More intimidating. Instead, what pressed on her was the absence of distance. Whatever authority they carried wasn’t being aimed at her. It simply *was*.

She took one cautious step forward.

They didn’t move.

That mattered.

Allison searched their expression for something she could prepare against: judgment, expectation, reassurance. She found none of it. What she saw instead was restraint — the kind that comes from knowing words have weight and choosing not to spend them lightly.

“You knew I’d come,” Allison said, surprising herself with how steady her voice sounded.

The royal representative considered her for a moment, then nodded once. “Yes.”

Not *we arranged it*.  
Not *you were summoned*.

Just acknowledgment.

Allison swallowed. “I don’t know why.”

“No,” they agreed. “Not yet.”

That was the first warning.

Not threat.  
*Boundary.*

Allison wrapped her arms loosely around herself, grounding. “Am I in trouble?”

The question escaped before she could stop it. Habit, she realized. Learned reflex.

The royal representative’s expression shifted then — just slightly. Not toward comfort. Toward seriousness.

“No,” they said. “But you are at a point where misunderstanding becomes harmful.”

That sounded uncomfortably familiar.

Allison felt the urge to apologize rise instinctively — and stopped herself. She had promised herself she would notice when she did that.

“I didn’t do anything,” she said instead. Not defensively. Carefully.

“I know,” they replied.

The quiet stretched.

Allison became aware of the space between them — not empty, but held, like the pause before a door opens inward instead of out.

“Why you?” Allison asked at last. “Why someone from the Crown?”

The representative did not answer immediately.

“Because I cannot pretend this is optional,” they said finally. “And because once I speak, I cannot step back from what follows.”

Allison felt the truth of that land — not as intimidation, but as *constraint*. Whatever this was, it bound them too.

That unsettled her more than authority ever had.

She nodded slowly. “So this isn’t… advice.”

“No,” they said.

“And it’s not a warning,” Allison added, testing.

They met her gaze. “It is context.”

The word settled between them, heavier than it sounded.

Allison drew a slow breath. She could feel the pathways waiting — not pushing, not pulling. Simply present, ready to record what happened next.

She didn’t like that they were already adjusting.

“I don’t think I’m ready,” she said quietly.

The royal representative’s expression softened — not with reassurance, but recognition.

“That,” they said, “is why we could not wait.”

They shifted then — just enough to indicate that the next words would matter.

Allison straightened, bracing herself without knowing what she was bracing *for*.

The telling had not begun.

But the space for it had.
