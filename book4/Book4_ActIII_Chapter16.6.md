## Chapter 16.6 — The Question That Doesn’t Leave

Evan waited until no one else spoke.

That was becoming a habit.

The room still carried the weight of Tomas’s words. Not the phrasing — the finality. Evan could feel it pressing against the edges of the conversation, shaping what could be said without anyone needing to enforce it.

He cleared his throat quietly.

“I understand the boundary,” he said.

No one corrected him.

Caelen remained where he was, posture unchanged. Iria watched Evan carefully, as if already anticipating where he would land.

“But I don’t understand the framing,” Evan continued.

Ren wasn’t present. That mattered. This wasn’t governance. It was aftermath.

“You’ve all agreed that telling her now is necessary,” Evan said. “Not because it’s right. Because delay is worse.”

“Yes,” Caelen said.

“And you’ve acknowledged,” Evan went on, “that once she knows, the system adapts around her knowing.”

“Yes,” Caelen said again.

Evan nodded. “So you’re creating the condition that accelerates the thing you’re afraid of.”

Silence.

Iria spoke carefully. “We are choosing the least harmful acceleration.”

Evan looked at her. “By whose definition?”

She didn’t answer immediately.

“Because from where I’m standing,” Evan said, “you’re prioritizing system legibility over her consent.”

Allison’s presence sharpened in Evan’s awareness, though she hadn’t moved.

“That’s not accurate,” Iria said.

“It is,” Evan replied gently. “You’re not asking whether she wants this information. You’re deciding that she must have it.”

Caelen shifted his weight slightly. Not disagreement. Acknowledgment.

“Consent,” Caelen said, “is not meaningful when consequences propagate regardless of choice.”

Evan exhaled slowly. “That’s convenient.”

Caelen met his gaze. “It is descriptive.”

Evan turned slightly, addressing the room rather than any single person. “You’re treating her awareness as environmental.”

No one denied it.

“You’re saying that knowing changes the system,” Evan continued. “But not knowing also changes her.”

“Yes,” Iria said quietly.

“So why is her internal harm acceptable,” Evan asked, “but the system’s confusion isn’t?”

That landed harder than Evan had expected.

Iria folded her hands. “Because confusion at scale becomes collapse.”

“And harm to one person doesn’t?” Evan asked.

Caelen answered this time. “It does. But collapse does not stop with one.”

Evan swallowed. “Neither does precedent.”

The word hung there.

“I’m not saying don’t tell her,” Evan said. “I’m saying you’re pretending this is neutral when it isn’t.”

Caelen inclined his head. “Agreed.”

Evan frowned. “Then say that.”

The quiet stretched.

“You keep using words like stability and containment,” Evan went on. “But what you’re really doing is choosing who absorbs the uncertainty first.”

Iria closed her eyes briefly.

“That will always be the subject,” Evan said. “Unless you let it be yourselves.”

No one responded.

Evan looked at Allison then — not to seek agreement, but to anchor himself. She met his gaze, expression unreadable, present without signaling anything at all.

“That’s all,” Evan said finally. “I don’t have an alternative. I just need it said.”

Caelen nodded once. “It has been.”

Nothing changed.

No posture softened.  
No plan shifted.  
No reassurance was offered.

But the question remained — uncontained, unassigned, and unanswered.
