## Chapter 13.1 — Recorded Without Attribution

The chamber was not labeled.

That omission was intentional. The Confluence had learned long ago that naming rooms created expectations—purpose implied direction, and direction implied intent. This space was instead defined by function alone: stone walls calibrated for acoustic neutrality, seating arranged in a shallow arc that discouraged alignment, and a central dais left deliberately unused.

No one stood at the center.

Elder Ren arrived first.

He did not take a seat immediately. Instead, he paused just inside the threshold, allowing the room to register him before he registered it. His presence settled the ambient systems into a slightly tighter configuration—nothing dramatic, nothing visible—but enough that the Archivists’ consoles flickered once in quiet acknowledgment.

He inclined his head to no one in particular and took the chair furthest from the door.

Iria Vell arrived next, her pace unhurried, her posture relaxed in a way that read as deliberate rather than casual. She chose a seat opposite Ren, close enough that conversation would not require projection, far enough that neither could claim the other’s space.

The Archivists followed in pairs, as they always did. They did not sit together. That, too, was precedent.

When the last of them had settled, the room sealed itself—not locked, merely committed. The faint hum of the Confluence adjusted, registering that this conversation would not be interrupted.

No introduction was made.

Ren spoke first, as he often did—not because he held authority over Iria, but because he treated silence as inefficiency.

“We have precedent,” he said.

The word landed with weight, not accusation, not warning. A statement of fact.
