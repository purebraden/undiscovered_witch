## Scene 9.2 — Data Surfaces

The room was not designated for briefings.

That was the first thing Allison noticed when Tomas guided her inside. It lacked the usual affordances: no central table calibrated for group attention, no wall surfaces tuned for projection, no ambient cues that suggested explanation would occur here. The space was narrow, quiet, and deliberately undecorated, as if it existed for containment rather than communication.

“Stand there,” Tomas said, indicating a position just inside the doorway.

Allison complied.

She felt the Confluence register her placement immediately. Not as a participant. As a variable.

Tomas did not invite her to sit. He did not frame what was about to happen. He adjusted his wrist device and waited, eyes on a point just beyond Allison’s shoulder. The pause stretched long enough to become uncomfortable, then settled into something procedural.

A panel along the far wall activated without light or sound. Text resolved into place, thin and precise, arranged in columns that did not invite reading so much as acknowledgment.

Allison did not lean forward.

She recognized the structure anyway.

Not the content—the structure. The way the information had been grouped, weighted, and ordered. It felt familiar in the same way Tomas’s repeated phrasing felt familiar: designed to constrain interpretation.

“Variance report,” Tomas said. “Restricted circulation.”

The words landed like a label, not an invitation.

Allison’s gaze stayed level. She did not read the lines. She did not need to. The Confluence had learned that showing her information was not the same as giving her permission to engage with it.

Tomas lifted his wrist slightly. A single section of the panel emphasized itself—no color change, just a subtle shift in contrast.

“All flagged deviations,” Tomas said, “correlate with your supervised presence.”

Allison’s stomach tightened.

She did not speak.

Another section adjusted.

“Baseline efficiency exceeded,” Tomas continued. “Repeatedly.”

The word *repeatedly* hung in the air. Not dramatic. Just precise.

Allison felt the quiet pressure of recognition again—the sense of a pattern aligning before it was named. She kept her attention anchored to Tomas’s voice.

“These deviations persist across control variations,” Tomas said. “Distance, duration, and placement adjusted. Outcome improvement remains.”

Allison swallowed. She was very aware of the fact that she was breathing.

“Statistical likelihood of random occurrence is low,” Tomas said.

He paused, then added, “Significantly.”

Allison’s hands curled once, then relaxed. Numbers had always made her uneasy when they were used like this—not as explanation, but as verdict.

A name appeared in the attribution field.

Allison did not look directly at it. She did not need to. Tomas’s next sentence framed it clearly enough.

“Senior variance analysis has confirmed the pattern,” he said. “Attribution recorded.”

The name remained on the panel. Fixed. Uninterpreted.

Allison understood the implication without understanding the role. Someone else had seen this too. Someone else had measured it and found it sufficient to sign their name.

Tomas continued, voice steady. “The conclusion is limited.”

Allison almost laughed at that. Almost.

“Presence correlates with reduced friction,” Tomas said. “No causal mechanism identified.”

He turned to face her then, fully.

“This is not a compliment,” Tomas said. “And it is not an accusation.”

Allison nodded. “I know.”

“You are not being evaluated for intent,” Tomas said. “You are being evaluated for impact.”

The distinction settled heavily.

The panel shifted again, collapsing the data into a summary that looked almost gentle in its restraint.

“Non-random improvement,” Tomas read. “Persistent correlation.”

He lowered his wrist.

“The review continues,” he said. “Scope elevated.”

Allison waited for the part where she was told what that meant for her.

It did not come.

Instead, Tomas said, “You will not discuss this with anyone.”

“I wasn’t planning to,” Allison said.

“I know,” Tomas replied.

The repetition had changed. It no longer sounded like reassurance. It sounded like acknowledgment of inevitability.

“You will not attempt to contextualize what you have seen,” Tomas continued. “You will not attempt to understand it.”

Allison’s mouth went dry. “Then why show me?”

Tomas considered her for a moment. “Because you are the variable,” he said. “And variables are informed when the system decides they must be constrained more tightly.”

The panel went dark.

The room returned to neutrality.

Allison felt oddly hollow, like something had been named in her absence and then removed again.

“What does that mean?” Allison asked.

Tomas shook his head once. “It means the review applies to you,” he said. “Nothing more.”

He gestured toward the doorway. “There is one more conversation,” he said. “After that, you will wait.”

Allison nodded.

Waiting, at least, was something she understood.

She followed Tomas out of the room without looking back at the blank wall where the data had been.

The numbers were gone.

The weight of them was not.
