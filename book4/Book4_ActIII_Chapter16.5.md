## Chapter 16.5 — No Path Back

Caelen did not explain where they were going.

He simply turned, and Allison followed.

The space they entered was smaller than the chambers used for deliberation—narrower, quieter, built for precision rather than agreement. Allison felt the difference immediately. This room did not exist to decide things. It existed to define limits.

Others were already there.

Iria stood near the far table, posture composed, hands folded as if she were holding something fragile inside herself. Evan leaned against the wall, arms crossed, tension evident even before he looked up and saw Allison. Tomas stood apart from both of them, closer to the back than the center, as if proximity itself had become a liability.

No Archivists hovered.  
No records surfaced.

That absence was deliberate.

Caelen paused just inside the doorway, giving Allison time to orient herself—not to the people, but to the *weight* of the room.

“There is one constraint that must be named before we continue,” Caelen said calmly. “It is not precedent. It is not policy.”

He turned his head slightly, acknowledging Tomas without inviting debate.

“It is technical.”

Tomas straightened.

“I am not here to explain,” he said immediately. “Only to state a boundary.”

Allison’s shoulders tightened, but she nodded.

“When expandability is acknowledged,” Tomas continued, “the system compensates.”

Allison frowned slightly. “Compensates how?”

Tomas did not answer at once. He was choosing language, not for comfort, but for accuracy.

“By redistributing load,” he said. “Across structures that were never meant to share it.”

Caelen remained silent. Iria did not interrupt.

“In early stages,” Tomas went on, “this appears as restraint. Hesitation. Self-correction.”

Allison felt the recognition sharply.

“That is not containment,” Tomas said. “It is pre-alignment.”

Evan pushed off the wall. “You’re saying the system adapts before anyone acts.”

“Yes,” Tomas replied. “Including the subject.”

Allison swallowed. “So… me.”

“Yes.”

The word landed without emphasis.

“There is a point,” Tomas said, “at which expandability stops being a condition and becomes a property.”

Allison’s chest tightened. “What does that mean?”

“It means,” Tomas said, “that the system no longer asks *whether* expansion will occur. Only *how it will be expressed*.”

Iria closed her eyes.

Evan shook his head. “You’re talking about inevitability.”

“No,” Tomas said. “I’m talking about irreversibility.”

Caelen spoke quietly. “Say the limit.”

Tomas did not hesitate.

“If she expands,” he said, “we cannot walk her back.”

The words settled into the room, heavy and unmoving.

“There is no rollback,” Tomas continued. “No reversion to prior scope. No restoration of baseline behavior.”

Evan stared at him. “Even if she stops?”

Tomas turned to him. “Stopping is still alignment.”

Allison forced herself to breathe slowly.

“You’re saying,” she said carefully, “that knowing this changes things.”

“Yes,” Tomas said.

“And telling me,” she added, “changes things.”

“Yes.”

Silence followed—dense, absolute.

Iria spoke softly. “Is there any margin left?”

Tomas looked at her then, and something like regret flickered across his expression before being carefully set aside.

“Timing,” he said. “That’s all that remains.”

Caelen inclined his head once. “That aligns with precedent.”

Allison wrapped her arms around herself—not to protect, but to stay present. “What if I do nothing?”

Tomas answered without pause.

“The system will still respond,” he said. “It already is.”

No one contradicted him.

“That is all I can offer,” Tomas said. “A limit. Not guidance.”

Allison lifted her gaze to him. “Do you have any authority here?”

Tomas met her eyes steadily.

“None.”

That made the warning heavier.

Caelen stepped forward slightly, reclaiming the space.

“Thank you,” he said to Tomas.

Tomas inclined his head once and said nothing more.

The boundary had been named.

Nothing had been decided.

And there was still no path back.
