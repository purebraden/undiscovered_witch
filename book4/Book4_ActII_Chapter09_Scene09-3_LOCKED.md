## Scene 9.3 — Warning Without Accusation

The corridor outside the data room was narrower than most, its light set lower, its hum tuned to a quieter register. Allison noticed the difference immediately. The Confluence used space the way it used language: to discourage unnecessary movement.

Tomas did not walk right away. He waited until the door behind them sealed with a soft, final sound that carried more weight than a lock.

“This conversation is informal,” he said.

Allison’s shoulders tightened. “That doesn’t sound better.”

“It is not meant to,” Tomas replied. He turned to face her, positioning himself so the corridor extended evenly on both sides. No obvious exit. No implied direction.
“This is part of my role as your tutor,” he added. “Not the Confluence’s.”

Allison swallowed. “Am I allowed to respond?”

Tomas considered her for a moment. “Briefly,” he said.

That alone told her how serious this was.

“You have complied with every restriction placed on you,” Tomas began. “You have not tested boundaries. You have not sought explanation. You have not attempted to reproduce effects.”

“I didn’t know how,” Allison said quietly.

“That is not the point,” Tomas replied. “The point is that you did not try.”

He let the distinction settle.

“The data now indicates that your presence produces non-random improvement,” Tomas continued. “Not because you intervene. Because you recognize.”

Allison felt the familiar tightening in her chest. “I can’t stop noticing.”

“I know,” Tomas said. “And I am not asking you to.”

That surprised her enough that she looked up.

“What I am asking,” Tomas continued, “is that you do not explore.”

The word landed with more force than any prohibition he had issued before.

“All exploration contaminates data,” Tomas said. “It introduces intent, hypothesis, desire. The moment you choose to test what you can do, you change what is being measured.”

Allison nodded slowly. “So if I stay still…?”

“You remain a constant,” Tomas said. “As much as that is possible.”

The phrase *as much as that is possible* hung between them, acknowledging the lie without dismantling it.

“Allison,” Tomas said, and this time he used her name deliberately, “the safest thing you can do right now is remain uncurious.”

The word *safest* echoed in her head.

“If you attempt to understand this,” Tomas continued, “others will attempt to understand you. That shifts scrutiny from outcomes to agency.”

“And that’s bad,” Allison said.

“That removes your protections,” Tomas replied.
“And it removes my ability to slow things down for you.”

Allison felt cold. “You’re protecting me?”

Tomas did not answer immediately. When he did, his voice was quieter. “I am managing risk.”

The honesty in that was somehow worse than reassurance.

“The Confluence does not yet know what to do with you,” Tomas said. “Some see utility. Some see instability. Some see precedent.”

“And you?” Allison asked.

Tomas met her eyes. “I see timing.”

She frowned. “What does that mean?”

“It means that if this escalates too quickly,” Tomas said, “you lose the ability to refuse.”

Allison’s stomach dropped. “Refuse what?”

“Use,” Tomas said simply.

The word felt too large for the corridor.

“You have one advantage right now,” Tomas continued. “You are improving outcomes without claiming ownership of them. That gives you plausible neutrality.”

“All because I don’t do anything,” Allison said.

“Yes,” Tomas replied. “And because you do not ask.”

Silence settled between them. The Confluence did not intrude. The corridor held its quiet as if it understood this was not a moment to interrupt.

“Allison,” Tomas said again, “you are allowed to feel uncomfortable. You are not allowed to act on that discomfort.”

She nodded. Her hands had clenched without her noticing. She forced them open.

“What happens next?” she asked.

Tomas gestured down the corridor. “You wait,” he said. “The review will continue. Restrictions will remain.”

“And if they decide?” Allison asked.

“Then we will revisit this conversation,” Tomas said. “With witnesses.”

The implication was clear.

“This was a courtesy,” Tomas added. “Do not mistake it for leniency.”

Allison managed a small, tight smile. “I wouldn’t.”

Tomas stepped aside, opening one side of the corridor. This time, the direction was clear.

“Return to your room,” he said. “Do not review. Do not plan.”

“I won’t,” Allison said.

“I know,” Tomas replied, one last time.

She walked away with the hum steady beneath her feet, the corridor stretching out ahead.

In her room, Allison sat on the edge of the bed, hands folded, eyes unfocused.

She thought about curiosity the way she thought about fire: useful, dangerous, and currently forbidden.

She did not reach for it.

She waited.
