## Scene 12.0 — Stabilized Routine

The routine settled without announcement.

Allison noticed it over several days rather than all at once. Her schedule no longer fluctuated. It arrived each morning already resolved, sparse in a way that felt intentional rather than provisional. No sessions were added. None were removed. The empty spaces held their shape.

Tomas met her at the same interval every day.

“You’ll find it stays like this,” he said, not as warning or reassurance, but as fact. “Stability requires consistency.”

Allison accepted that without comment. Consistency had become a boundary.

They walked the corridor together, their pace unhurried. The Confluence’s hum was steady now, the same note repeating without modulation. Allison had learned to recognize the sound as a sign that nothing was being adjusted.

They stopped at the familiar junction. Tomas did not gesture toward the observation wing.

“No recalibration today,” he said. “Or tomorrow.”

“All right,” Allison replied.

She meant it.

The waiting area had become a place she passed through rather than entered. Its doors remained closed more often than not. When she did step inside, it was briefly—long enough to sit, long enough to be counted as present, then dismissed without explanation.

Today, she did not enter at all.

Instead, Tomas turned with her toward a quieter section of the Confluence, one without designated function. Transitional space. Designed for movement rather than use.

“This is the routine now,” he said. “Limited exposure. No expansion. Ongoing review.”

“For how long?” Allison asked.

Tomas did not hesitate this time. “Indefinitely.”

The word settled differently than deferral had. Indefinite meant complete. Not a pause awaiting change, but a state meant to persist.

Allison considered her response and found that she did not have one.

They reached her assigned quarters. Tomas stopped at the threshold as he always did, never crossing it.

“You’re not being sidelined,” he said, carefully. “You’re being preserved.”

Preserved. Another word she added to the growing list.

“I understand,” Allison said.

Tomas studied her for a moment, as if checking for something—resistance, curiosity, strain. Whatever he was looking for, he seemed satisfied.

“Good,” he said. “Understanding makes this easier.”

He left without further instruction.

Inside, Allison’s room looked unchanged. The light adjusted automatically. The desk surface cleared itself, blank and ready for nothing in particular. Her schedule hovered briefly, then minimized, content with its reduced form.

She sat on the edge of the bed and let the quiet arrive fully.

This was not waiting in anticipation.

This was waiting as maintenance.

She could feel the structure of it now—the way the Confluence had arranged itself to hold her exactly where she was. No pressure to move forward. No pull backward. Just stillness, reinforced daily.

Allison breathed slowly, deliberately.

The pathways were still present. She did not need to check to know that. They had become part of her awareness, like knowing where a door was in a dark room.

She did not approach it.

Time passed.

When nothing changed, Allison understood that this, too, was being measured—not as variance, but as compliance. Or restraint. Or something else she had not yet learned the word for.

She remained where she was.

The routine held.
