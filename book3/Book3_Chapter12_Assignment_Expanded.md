# Chapter 12 — Assignment
*(Evan POV)*

The meeting room was smaller than the main chamber.

Not diminished—just focused. A space designed for conversations that needed precision more than presence, where decisions were shaped rather than announced.

Evan arrived early.

He didn’t pace. He didn’t rehearse. He sat where indicated, set his hands flat on the table, and let himself become still. The Confluence didn’t reward urgency, and it didn’t respond well to performance. What mattered here was whether he understood the weight of what he was asking—and whether he was prepared for the answer to be no.

He had requested the meeting formally. In writing. With a stated purpose and no assumptions about outcome.

That mattered to him. Not as a courtesy, but as containment. If he was going to step into this at all, it had to begin inside process, not proximity.

The elders entered together.

Jonas first, then Maeve and Elira. Ruthan followed last, quiet and already attentive. No ceremony marked their arrival. No elevation. They took their seats across from him, the table between them deliberately bare.

“Evan,” Jonas said. “You requested time.”

“Yes,” Evan said. “Thank you for granting it.”

Maeve inclined her head slightly. “You stated the purpose as a liaison assignment request.”

“Yes.”

No one prompted him further.

That was intentional. This was his request to carry forward.

“I’m here to formally request consideration for liaison assignment,” Evan said. “Only liaison.”

He stopped there.

Not tutor.
Not combined roles.
Not implication.

Just liaison.

“Allison has already consented to the role under explicit conditions,” Evan continued. “My request is framed to operate entirely within those terms.”

Elira folded her hands loosely. “Name the conditions you’re anchoring to.”

“Autonomy. Transparency. Reversibility. Non-hierarchy,” Evan said. “My role would be procedural only—interface, translation, process alignment. No advisory authority. No interpretive authority. No influence over outcomes.”

“And no overlap with the tutor role,” Maeve said.

“No,” Evan said. “I don’t have the expertise for that, and it wouldn’t be appropriate.”

Ruthan’s attention sharpened slightly. Evan had learned to recognize that shift—not approval, but verification.

Jonas nodded once. “On what basis are you requesting assignment?”

“Accountability,” Evan said.

He let the word sit for a moment before expanding it.

“I already interact with Allison regularly,” he continued. “She’s familiar with how I communicate, and I’m familiar with how she defines consent and boundaries. That familiarity can reduce friction—but it can also introduce pressure if it isn’t bounded.”

Maeve’s gaze held his. “And you believe you can manage that risk.”

“I believe it can be managed only with oversight,” Evan said. “Which is why I’m requesting provisional status as part of the assignment.”

Ruthan spoke for the first time. “You’re asking to be constrained.”

“Yes,” Evan said. “Willingly.”

That distinction mattered. Constraint chosen was different from constraint imposed.

Before Jonas could respond, Evan continued.

“I also have questions.”

Elira nodded slightly. “Go on.”

“If I misjudge a boundary,” Evan said, “what is the corrective path? Not punishment—correction.”

Maeve answered first. “Immediate review. Adjustment of scope. If necessary, suspension of the assignment.”

“And priority?” Evan asked.

“Allison’s autonomy,” Elira said. “Always.”

Evan nodded. He had expected the answer. He needed to hear it anyway.

“Second,” he continued, “what information will I be restricted from specifically because I’m familiar to her?”

Jonas didn’t hesitate. “Strategic forecasts. Internal deliberations. Context that could bias her choices.”

“So I’ll be operating with less information than a non-familiar liaison,” Evan said.

“Yes,” Maeve replied. “By design.”

“That’s appropriate,” Evan said.

Ruthan watched him closely now, gaze intent rather than assessing.

“One more,” Evan said.

“If she terminates the assignment,” he continued, “how do we ensure that doesn’t cost her access, standing, or credibility?”

The room went still—not tense, but focused.

“It won’t,” Elira said. “Termination of liaison does not alter status.”

“And carries no inference,” Maeve added.

Jonas nodded. “The record would reflect alignment change, not failure.”

Evan absorbed that slowly. It told him where the Confluence placed consequence, and where it did not.

Jonas folded his hands. “Your request will be recorded.”

“Understood.”

“No assignment is made at this time,” Elira said. “Allison will be informed that a liaison request exists. She will have the opportunity to decline without consequence.”

“Yes,” Evan said.

Jonas gestured lightly toward the door. “Thank you for your clarity.”

Evan stood. “Thank you for the process.”

He left the room without looking back.

The broader corridors of the Confluence opened around him, movement resuming its steady, indifferent flow. People passed without recognition or pause. Decisions here were not accompanied by spectacle.

That was intentional.

Evan moved with the current until it carried him beyond the meeting spaces and into the quieter edge of the structure. Only then did he let his shoulders ease slightly—not in relief, but in release.

The request no longer lived inside him.

It lived inside structure now, where it could be accepted or refused without distorting anything else.

Whatever happened next would happen inside the terms Allison had already defined.

And that was exactly where it belonged.
