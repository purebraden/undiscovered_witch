# UNDISCOVERED WITCH — ALLISON’S NOTES APP SERIES BIBLE

## Overview

**Device:** Allison Carter’s smartphone  
**Application:** Default Notes app  
**Purpose:** Private pattern-tracking, emotional containment, self-surveillance

The Notes app functions as a semi-conscious coping mechanism and an unreliable narrator.  
It is where Allison allows herself to notice things she refuses to name aloud.

The Notes are never framed as magical in Book 1.
They are framed as *reasonable documentation*.

---

## Core Functions of the Notes App

The Notes app serves four primary narrative functions:

1. **Pattern Recognition**  
   Allison tracks coincidences, changes, and anomalies.

2. **Emotional Suppression**  
   Writing replaces feeling; naming replaces processing.

3. **Control Illusion**  
   Lists and rules give the impression of agency.

4. **Truth Leakage**  
   The Notes reveal Allison’s beliefs more honestly than dialogue.

---

## Rules for Writing Notes (Authorial Constraints)

To maintain consistency, all Notes must follow these rules:

- Written in plain language
- No metaphors
- No fantasy terms
- No emotional adjectives unless negated (“not upset,” “fine”)
- Often end with dismissal (“probably,” “maybe,” “coincidence”)
- Rarely include “I want” statements

The Notes should feel *boring on the surface* and unsettling in accumulation.

---

## Book 1 — Notes App Usage

### Narrative Role
- Introduces pattern tracking
- Establishes Allison’s need to dismiss her own observations
- Creates dramatic irony for the reader

### Structural Placement
- Appears at emotional pivot points
- Often after scenes where calm settles unnaturally
- Usually brief (3–6 lines)

### Sample Book 1 Entries

> **Probably Nothing**  
> Locker opened first try.  
> Quiz didn’t happen.  
> Kara smiled.  
>  
> Could be coincidence.

---

> **Follow-Up**  
> Kara texted me first.  
> Walked with me today.  
>  
> People change. That happens.

---

> **Weather**  
> Still hasn’t rained.  
> Forecast keeps changing.  
>  
> Not unusual for fall.

---

> **Emily**  
> She was really upset.  
> I didn’t do anything.  
>  
> It still stopped.

---

> **Pattern**  
> Things are easier when I don’t want much.  
>  
> That’s probably normal.

---

### End-of-Book Shift
By the end of Book 1:
- Notes shift from observation to questioning
- Dismissive language weakens
- First direct question appears

> **Question**  
> If no one needs me to fix things,  
> what am I for?

This is the first time a Note is not attempting to contain.

---

## Book 2 — Notes App Evolution

### Narrative Shift
- Notes become longer and less controlled
- Allison begins recording *responses* instead of events
- Deletes fewer lines

### Language Changes
- Fewer dismissals (“probably”)
- More conditional statements
- Increased use of questions

### Sample Book 2 Entry (Early)

> **Noticing**  
> Evan said something felt wrong.  
> I didn’t disagree.  
>  
> That feels important.

---

### Sample Book 2 Entry (Mid)

> **Rule (Unclear)**  
> Wanting things makes them louder.  
> Avoiding them makes them smaller.  
>  
> I don’t know which is better.

---

## Book 3 — Fracture Phase

### Narrative Role
- Notes lose reliability
- Entries contradict each other
- Allison begins addressing herself directly

### Structural Changes
- Entries increase in frequency
- Some notes are incomplete
- Some are never saved

### Sample Book 3 Entry

> **Do Not Delete**  
> This isn’t calm.  
> This is pressure.

---

## Book 4+ — Reclamation Phase

### Narrative Role
- Notes transform from surveillance to agency
- Allison uses Notes to assert choice
- Language becomes declarative

### Structural Changes
- Notes are intentionally revisited
- Earlier entries are quoted and challenged

### Sample Later-Series Entry

> **Choice**  
> I can end things.  
>  
> I don’t have to.

---

## What the Notes Never Do

- Never explain magic
- Never provide lore
- Never predict outcomes accurately
- Never replace action

They are reflection, not instruction.

---

## Thematic Function Across the Series

The Notes app represents:
- How girls are taught to self-monitor
- How systems externalize control
- The difference between naming and choosing

The evolution of the Notes mirrors Allison’s arc:
From containment → awareness → fracture → agency.

---

## Author Safeguards

When drafting:
- If a Note feels comforting, rewrite it
- If a Note explains too much, cut it
- If a Note sounds poetic, flatten it

The power of the Notes is their restraint.
