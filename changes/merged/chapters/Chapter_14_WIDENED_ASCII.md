# Chapter 14

The maintenance run smelled of damp metal and old smoke. Pipes sweated along the ceiling. The floor slanted just enough to keep her leg screaming.

Rachel followed the faintest line of light, the pressure bracketing her, tightening when she slowed, easing when she moved.

Ahead, a door stood ajar. Light spilled through it. Voices, close.

She took one step toward it.

Pain flashed behind her eyes. Her knees buckled. The pressure slammed down, a hard refusal that made her gag.

Rachel leaned into the wall and waited until she could see again.

The voices drifted closer, then passed, boots soft on stone.

As they went by, she caught a word.

"Another concurrent case."

The phrase landed and stuck.

She pushed away from the wall and kept moving, because the pressure did not give her a choice.

The corridor narrowed into a crawl. She ducked under a pipe and felt it vibrate, a low tremor running through the metal as if the whole system were waking up around her.

A drip hit the back of her neck. She flinched. The pressure twitched, annoyed.

Ahead, the passage opened into a taller shaft with a ladder bolted to the wall. Above it, faint light and voices. Below it, darkness and the smell of wet stone.

Rachel grabbed the ladder.

The pressure flared. Nausea hit. Her hands slipped.

She let go and stumbled back, breathing hard.

The ladder rattled anyway. A pipe above her burst with a sharp hiss, spraying hot water into the shaft. Someone yelped from above. The sound cut off, replaced by hurried footsteps.

Rachel backed away, heart racing. She had not touched the valve. The pressure had, in its blunt way, closed the exposure before it happened.

She turned and limped deeper into the dark, the only direction it would let her go.

The maintenance run ended in a junction that had been lit for no one.

Lines in the floor glowed faintly. Doors to either side sat half open, as if the corridor could not decide which route was safe.

Rachel stepped into the junction and felt the pressure tighten, alert and ready.

Someone was waiting.

Three people in dark coats stood at the far end, spaced out, hands visible, a strip of pale material stretched between two of them like a leash.

"Stop," one said, calm and flat.

Rachel froze.

The strip on the floor brightened. A shallow groove lit beneath her boots, hemming her in.

She looked left. The pressure shoved hard and she gagged, vision tunneling.

She looked right. The same. The pressure was not letting her near the doors.

The nearest person took one step forward. The strip between them hummed.

Rachel's chest burned. The pressure coiled, hot and tight, like a fist held back.

"Do not touch her," someone said from farther back. "If she spikes, drop it."

The strip hummed again.

Rachel took one step backward. The groove behind her lit.

She realized then that the only unlit path was forward, toward the strip.

She breathed in, then moved.

The pressure flared, not in refusal but in defense, and the stone under the strip warped with a low groan. The person holding it staggered as the floor dipped.

Rachel did not wait. She lunged into the gap, shoulder down, and the strip snapped as she passed, its pale material tearing with a sharp, dry sound.

"Now," someone shouted.

The corridor ahead opened into a cart lane, rails set into the floor, a slow-moving line of empty carts sliding by.

Rachel threw herself onto the nearest one. Her leg screamed. She bit down and held on as the cart pulled her forward.

Behind her, the floor lines flared. A shout broke. Something slammed into the stone where she had been.

The cart carried her into the next corridor, and the pressure settled heavy and focused, not toward safety but toward motion.

The cart line climbed.

Rachel clung to the rail as the corridor angled upward, the air warming, the sound of distant voices bleeding in from somewhere above. The pressure in her chest tightened with every rise, a steady refusal that made her teeth ache.

She could hear them now. People. Too many. A market hum, or a shift change, or something that meant open space.

The pressure slammed hard.

Not a voice. A single word pressed into her skull: Away.

Rachel cried out and tumbled off the cart as a side gate flashed open beside her. She hit the floor, rolled, and the gate snapped shut behind her with a heavy clang.

The cart line kept climbing. The voices faded.

Rachel lay on cold stone, gasping, and realized the pressure had not saved her from pain. It had saved her from being seen.

She pushed herself up and found a narrow passage, low and wet, the floor slick. Water pooled in the center, black and still.

On the wall, someone had scratched a line with something sharp. The marks were not letters she knew, but the meaning slid into her head anyway.

DO NOT LET THEM SEE YOU.

Below it, a second line.

THEY DO NOT STOP.

Rachel stared until her eyes burned.

The pressure tightened, urging her forward. She tore her gaze away and limped on, the words following her like a hand at her back.
