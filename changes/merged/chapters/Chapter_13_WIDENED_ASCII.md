# Chapter 13

The room started emptying before Rachel could get her breath back.

No announcements. No sirens. People backed out in quick, controlled steps, equipment lifted and rolled away, voices dropped to the edges of hearing. The lights thinned with every person who left.

The pressure inside her tracked it all, tightening, easing, tightening again, like it was keeping count.

"Move," a man said from the doorway, not looking at her. "You cannot stay in this chamber."

Rachel laughed once, raw and tired. "You just said clear the sector."

"This is not the sector anymore," he said, and that did not make sense until the floor lines shifted.

A seam opened near the far wall. Cold air breathed out.

The pressure leaned toward it.

Rachel pushed herself up, slow and shaking, and limped across the room before the floor decided for her. Her leg screamed. She bit down on it and kept moving.

The doorway behind her sealed as she crossed the threshold.

The corridor beyond was narrow, older, rough stone under her boots. The light lines here were thinner and less certain, as if they had not been used in a long time.

Voices carried from a junction ahead. Two, maybe three. The pressure snapped tight and shoved her sideways into a darker passage.

She did not argue. She did not have breath to waste.

The side passage dipped and tightened. Pipes ran along the ceiling, damp and rusted. A grate on her right opened into a lower run where a pair of workers hurried past, glancing up without seeing her.

Rachel took one step toward the grate. She opened her mouth to warn them, and no sound came out the way she meant it. A cold refusal pinched her throat closed.

Nausea knifed through her. The pressure locked her knees and she sagged against the wall until it eased.

The workers kept moving. One of them laughed, tired, and the sound hurt.

Rachel turned away and followed the narrow run downward as the corridor behind her sealed with a soft, final grind.

The maintenance run smelled of damp metal and old smoke. Pipes sweated along the ceiling. The floor slanted just enough to keep her leg screaming.

Rachel followed the faintest line of light, the pressure bracketing her, tightening when she slowed, easing when she moved.

Ahead, a door stood ajar. Light spilled through it. Voices, close.

She took one step toward it.

Pain flashed behind her eyes. Her knees buckled. The pressure slammed down, a hard refusal that made her gag.

Rachel leaned into the wall and waited until she could see again.

The voices drifted closer, then passed, boots soft on stone.

As they went by, she caught a word.

"Another concurrent case."

The phrase landed and stuck.

She pushed away from the wall and kept moving, because the pressure did not give her a choice.

The corridor narrowed into a crawl. She ducked under a pipe and felt it vibrate, a low tremor running through the metal as if the whole system were waking up around her.

A drip hit the back of her neck. She flinched. The pressure twitched, annoyed.

Ahead, the passage opened into a taller shaft with a ladder bolted to the wall. Above it, faint light and voices. Below it, darkness and the smell of wet stone.

Rachel grabbed the ladder.

The pressure flared. Nausea hit. Her hands slipped.

She let go and stumbled back, breathing hard.

The ladder rattled anyway. A pipe above her burst with a sharp hiss, spraying hot water into the shaft. Someone yelped from above. The sound cut off, replaced by hurried footsteps.

Rachel backed away, heart racing. She had not touched the valve. The pressure had, in its blunt way, closed the exposure before it happened.

She turned and limped deeper into the dark, the only direction it would let her go.
