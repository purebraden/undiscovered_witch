# Chapter 10

Rachel did not move toward the tools.

The pressure leaned that way anyway, subtle at first and then more insistent, the same sideways insistence she had learned to recognize, the promise of motion if she followed and the threat of collapse if she did not, and she stood there with her hand braced against the wall, her leg shaking hard enough now that she could feel it up through her hip.

"Don't," Lena said quietly.

Rachel turned her head before she decided to listen.

Lena stood farther back than before, one shoulder nearly touching the stone, her hands empty and visible at her sides, not looking at the rack or the half-sealed channel gap but at Rachel's face, as if it mattered which way her eyes went next.

"They want you to choose fast," Lena said. "That's how they keep it clean."

The pressure tightened in response, sharp enough that Rachel sucked in a breath and tasted metal.

"They're coming," Rachel said, the words scraping on the way out.

"I know," Lena said. "That's why I'm still here."

The chamber felt smaller now, the walls pressing in by degrees the way the corridors had earlier, and under that was the other pressure, the one that did not care about walls or exits or tools, only outcomes. Rachel looked back at the rack and felt how easy it would be to reach for something, anything, to hurt first and make herself impossible to handle.

The thought slid into place too smoothly.

She hated that.

Rachel turned back to Lena. "If I stay," she said, and her voice shook despite her effort to keep it steady, "does it make it worse for you."

Lena did not answer right away.

The pause stretched just long enough to tell the truth.

"Yes," Lena said finally. "Eventually."

The pressure pressed harder, almost pleased, and Rachel felt it like fingers tightening under her ribs.

She took a step toward Lena.

The pressure flared sharp and angry, no longer nudging but resisting, a sudden tightness that stole half her breath and sent sparks across her vision. Rachel gasped and nearly went down, her hand slapping the wall as her balance gave.

Lena moved without thinking, crossing the space and catching Rachel under the arm before she could slide, the contact brief but solid, human weight and heat anchoring her just long enough to matter.

The pressure recoiled.

Not gone.

Offended.

Rachel leaned into Lena's shoulder, breathing hard, aware of how wrong her body felt, how close she was to folding completely if she let go.

"They don't like that," Lena said.

Rachel let out a broken laugh. "Good."

Behind them, stone ground against stone, louder now, closer, and the gap widened enough that cold air spilled into the chamber, carrying the smell of clean rock and metal.

Lena pulled back but kept a hand on Rachel's arm. "If you trust me," she said, "you don't get to stop halfway."

Rachel met her eyes. "I don't have a halfway."

"That's not what I meant."

Another grinding sound followed, slower and heavier, and a shadow passed across the gap without rushing.

The pressure surged, no longer subtle, a hard shove toward the rack that made Rachel's teeth clack together. She hissed and planted her feet, pain tearing through her leg as she resisted, breath coming in sharp, ugly pulls.

Lena swore. "It's pushing."

"I know," Rachel said. "I can feel it."

Lena's grip tightened. "Then listen to me. Not it."

Rachel nodded once.

Lena turned and tugged her along, not toward the tools and not toward the channel gap but toward the far wall where the stone dipped and the light thinned into deeper shadow, a place Rachel had not really noticed before because she had been watching the obvious exits.

The pressure spiked.

Rachel stumbled and Lena nearly went down with her, both of them catching against the wall as the pressure slammed again, furious now, a sideways crush that made Rachel cry out as something in her chest pulled too hard and too fast.

"Sorry," Lena said through clenched teeth, hauling her upright. "I know it hurts."

"That's new," Rachel gasped.

"No," Lena said. "That's it realizing you're not useful this way."

The words landed wrong and right at the same time.

Behind them, the stone slid open another inch. A shape moved in the gap, tall and narrow, unhurried.

The pressure coiled tight, like a fist held back.

Lena shoved her shoulder into the wall and a section of stone scraped aside, not smoothly or cleanly, just enough to open a narrow recess that bit into Rachel's shoulder as Lena pushed her through first and twisted sideways to follow.

"You didn't say-" Rachel started.

"I said trust me," Lena said. "Not explain."

The pressure lunged.

This time it tore sideways through Rachel's ribs, sharp and disorienting, like being grabbed from the inside and yanked. Her knees buckled.

Lena dragged her anyway.

The recess was barely wide enough for two people, the air damp and rusted, the floor uneven. The pressure slammed again and this time struck stone instead of flesh, the force bleeding outward with a hollow boom that shook dust loose from above.

Something outside shouted.

"Here," Lena said, breathless. "Stay close."

Rachel pressed her back to the wall, lungs burning, and felt the pressure gather again, uncertain now, no longer aligned with an obvious outcome.

For the first time since the basement, it hesitated.

Footsteps sounded outside the recess, careful and measured. Light cut across the narrow opening as something passed in front of it.

"They'll find us," Rachel whispered.

"Yes," Lena said. "But not yet."

Rachel swallowed, her leg throbbing, her chest aching, the pressure hovering and resentful, its lean no longer clear.

She looked at Lena in the dim, at the bruises on her arm, the set of her jaw.

"Why help me," Rachel asked again.

Lena's mouth twitched. "Because you didn't pick the tools."

The footsteps paused.

Rachel held her breath.

The pressure pressed lightly outward, testing, as if it were deciding whether this trust was something it could still use.

The pressure did not push anymore.

Rachel noticed it because her body kept bracing for it anyway, muscles tight and waiting for the shove that never came, the absence sitting wrong in her chest like the moment after a sound cuts out and your ears keep reaching for it.

Footsteps passed the opening again, close enough that light slid across the narrow gap and touched the stone at Rachel's feet before pulling away. She held her breath without deciding to and felt how little air she actually needed when fear did the work.

Lena stayed still beside her, shoulder pressed into the wall, eyes on the opening. She did not look at Rachel. She did not need to.

Something shifted.

Not outside.

Inside.

Rachel's stomach clenched hard enough to make her gag silently, her hand flying to her mouth as nausea surged up fast and sharp, not the slow roll of pain or panic but a sudden rearranging, like her body had been grabbed and twisted a fraction out of place. She swallowed it back and tasted copper, thick and immediate.

Lena glanced at her then, her expression tightening. "That shouldn't be happening."

Rachel nodded, eyes watering. "It's-" She stopped as the sensation deepened, spreading under her ribs and down into her gut, not pain exactly but pressure without direction, like something settling where it did not belong. She sagged against the wall and made a low sound she did not recognize as her own.

Outside, someone spoke.

Not loud.

"...feel that?"

Another voice answered, quieter. "Yes."

Rachel's heart kicked.

Whatever was inside her went still.

The footsteps resumed, slower now, closer to the opening, and Rachel felt the pressure tilt, not toward the sound but sideways, brushing against Lena's presence in a way that made Rachel's chest tighten.

Lena stiffened. Her jaw set. "No," she said under her breath. "Don't."

The pressure squeezed.

Rachel cried out, sharp and involuntary, as heat flared under her skin and raced along her arms and across her back, too fast to follow. Her hands curled into fists without asking her, nails biting into her palms as the air around her thinned with a faint snapping sound, brittle and wrong.

Outside, the voices sharpened.

"There," someone said. "Again."

Rachel's vision swam. The recess felt smaller, the stone closer, and her breath came in short panicked pulls as her body reacted faster than her thoughts could keep up.

Lena grabbed her wrist. "It's doing something to you."

"I know," Rachel said, gasping. "I didn't-"

"I know," Lena said again, harder this time.

The pressure pulsed, warmer now, heavier, and Rachel felt a strange loosening under her sternum, like a knot easing without permission. The sensation spread, unpleasant and almost relieving at the same time, and she hated it for that.

Outside, light held steady.

A shape blocked the opening.

Rachel squeezed her eyes shut and the pressure eased just enough to let her think, the sudden shift making her dizzy.

"They're going to open it," Lena said.

Rachel swallowed, throat dry. The pressure gathered again, not sharp now but dense, humming faintly in her bones. Her skin prickled. Her pulse thundered.

Lena's grip tightened. "Look at me."

Rachel forced her eyes open.

"If you let it keep going," Lena said, voice tight, "they won't need the tools."

Rachel shook her head weakly. "What happens."

Lena hesitated.

"That depends," she said slowly, "on how much of you it wants to show them."

Stone scraped outside.

The wall shuddered as something heavy pressed against it, testing the fit.

Rachel slid down the wall until she was sitting on the uneven floor, breath tearing in and out of her chest, her head bowed. The pressure swelled inside her, not pushing her anywhere, just building, as if it were waiting for her to stop resisting long enough to finish whatever it had started.

Her thoughts scattered.

The tools flashed in her mind. The channel. The way the pressure had turned toward Lena like it had noticed her for the first time.

Rachel pressed her back harder into the stone until the ache grounded her, sharp and familiar, something she could name.

The pressure recoiled a fraction.

Outside, someone cursed softly.

The stone shifted again, slower now, uncertain.

Rachel stayed where she was, shaking, every muscle tight, the pressure coiled inside her no longer trying to move her hands or her feet.

Just waiting.
