# Chapter 31

The lights outside the room came on.

Not the ones over her head. Those stayed thin and weak, barely there. It was the light under the door, leaking in first, then slipping through the narrow seams in the walls, pale and wrong against the stone where there hadn't been any light at all before.

Rachel's stomach dropped before she figured out why.

The pressure tightened a beat later, sharp and sudden, like it had reacted before she did.

She pushed herself upright too fast and had to stop halfway, back scraping the wall as her legs shook and pins and needles flared hot up her calves. She sucked in a breath and held it, eyes locked on the door, on the light spreading across the floor like it had somewhere to go.

She waited for it to stop.

It didn't.

The hum in the walls deepened, uneven, like the building was pulling power from places it hadn't meant to wake. Something clicked overhead. Then another click farther away. The sound didn't travel right. It bent around corners it shouldn't have reached.

The etched lines on the floor caught the light, faint but there, brighter than they'd been, like chalk rubbed thin and suddenly remembered.

Rachel inhaled again and gagged. The air felt thick now, heavy in her lungs, sharp with that burned-metal smell she'd started to recognize whether she wanted to or not.

The pressure shifted, spreading low and wide in her chest, not flaring, not pulling back, just rearranging itself in a way that made her skin prickle.

The door stayed shut.

A thin line of light traced the seam, pulsed once, then steadied.

Rachel stared at it too long. She told herself it was a power fluctuation. Old buildings did that. Systems rerouted. Things flickered before they corrected themselves.

The pressure didn't buy it.

It stayed tight and watchful, close enough that she felt it when she swallowed.

The lights outside flickered.

One went out.

Another came on farther down, the glow sliding across the floor in a slow sweep that made her chest tighten. Rachel pressed her hands flat against the wall, grounding herself in the cold stone, fighting the urge to move just because standing still suddenly felt wrong.

A vibration rolled through the floor, softer than before but wider, unfocused, like the building shifting its weight and not settling back where it belonged.

The etched lines buzzed under her skin.

She shook her head hard once. "No."

The light under the door dimmed.

For a second she thought it was finally going out.

Then it surged brighter than before.

The pressure snapped tight in response, heat flaring under her skin as her body reacted without asking her first. She gasped and dropped to one knee, palms scraping stone, heart slamming as she fought to stay upright.

"Stop," she said, breathless, the word tearing out of her. "I didn't-"

The hum cut out.

Not faded.

Gone.

The silence hit like a blow. Rachel cried out without meaning to, her voice too loud in the sudden absence of sound. Her ears rang. The pressure lurched, confused, compressing painfully like it didn't know what it was supposed to be pushing against anymore.

The lights over her head flickered once.

Then nothing.

Darkness swallowed the room.

Rachel froze, lungs burning, heart racing so fast she thought she might pass out. She waited for the lights to come back, counted breaths she couldn't keep steady, told herself it was temporary, told herself a lot of things.

They didn't come back.

In the dark, the pressure shifted closer, heavy and unmistakable, the only thing she could locate with any certainty.

Somewhere beyond the door, something moved.

Not fast.

Not loud.

Just enough to make the silence feel crowded.

Rachel pressed her back harder into the wall, hands shaking, skin slick with sweat she hadn't noticed until then.

"Okay," she whispered, voice breaking in the dark. "Okay. I'm still here."

The pressure settled close enough to feel like breath against her ribs.

The light did not return.

And whatever was moving on the other side of the door did not leave.
