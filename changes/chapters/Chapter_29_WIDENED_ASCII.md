# Chapter 29

The lights dimmed again.

Not all at once. One bank went soft, then another, shadows stretching and collapsing in uneven patches across the room. The hum in the walls deepened, dropped, then came back wrong, like something had lost track of how much power it was supposed to use.

Rachel stiffened.

"No," she said, because it felt like the only word that fit.

The pressure inside her tightened immediately, sharp and hot, crowding her breath as her body reacted before she could think through it. She pressed her hands against the wall, palms slick with sweat, trying to ground herself in something solid.

The hum stuttered.

The lights flickered.

Rachel's vision swam as the room darkened another notch, the edges of everything blurring as if distance itself had softened. The pressure surged in response, heat flaring under her skin in a way that made her gasp.

"Stop," she whispered.

The word barely left her mouth before the lights cut again.

This time, something snapped.

The pressure burst outward.

Rachel cried out as the heat tore free of her chest in a sudden, uncontrolled wave, not aimed, not shaped, just released. The air rippled visibly, a shudder running through the room as if it had been struck.

The etched lines flared bright.

Stone cracked.

Rachel collapsed forward onto her hands as the force rolled past her, knocking loose dust from the ceiling and rattling whatever systems were still alive in the walls. Something shattered nearby, glass bursting in a sharp, echoing pop that sent fragments skittering across the floor.

The lights surged back on, too bright, too fast.

Rachel screamed, more in shock than pain, as the pressure recoiled inward again, slamming back into her chest hard enough to knock the breath from her lungs. She curled instinctively, shaking, hands clutching at the stone as her heart hammered out of control.

The hum dropped into silence.

Not off.

Muted.

Rachel lay there, gasping, ears ringing, the room eerily still except for the sound of her own breath. The etched lines dimmed slowly, fading back to their faint, half-seen presence.

Something dripped from the ceiling.

Rachel rolled onto her side, chest heaving, and stared at the new cracks spidering across the wall opposite her, dust still drifting lazily down through the light.

She hadn't meant to do that.

She wasn't even sure she'd done anything at all.

The pressure inside her shifted, no longer frantic, no longer flaring at random. It felt spread out now, thinner somehow, like it had reached farther than it was supposed to and hadn't fully come back yet.

The lights flickered once more.

Rachel flinched hard, heart jumping into her throat, but nothing else happened. The lights steadied, dim but stable.

She pushed herself up slowly, hands trembling, and leaned back against the wall again.

The room smelled different now, sharp and burned, like overheated metal.

Rachel swallowed hard.

"Okay," she said aloud, her voice shaking. "Okay. Don't do that again."

The pressure didn't respond.

Not immediately.

Somewhere deep in the walls, something clicked, slow and deliberate, like a system trying to decide whether what had just happened counted as a failure or a trigger.

Rachel closed her eyes and listened, pulse roaring in her ears.

Whatever had been keeping the room quiet had noticed.

And whatever she had just done hadn't stayed inside her.
