# Chapter 25

The room started emptying before Rachel could get her breath back.

No announcements. No sirens. People backed out in quick, controlled steps, equipment lifted and rolled away, voices dropped to the edges of hearing. The lights thinned with every person who left.

The pressure inside her tracked it all, tightening, easing, tightening again, like it was keeping count.

"Move," a man said from the doorway, not looking at her. "You cannot stay in this chamber."

Rachel laughed once, raw and tired. "You just said clear the sector."

"This is not the sector anymore," he said, and that did not make sense until the floor lines shifted.

A seam opened near the far wall. Cold air breathed out.

The pressure leaned toward it.

Rachel pushed herself up, slow and shaking, and limped across the room before the floor decided for her. Her leg screamed. She bit down on it and kept moving.

The doorway behind her sealed as she crossed the threshold.

The corridor beyond was narrow, older, rough stone under her boots. The light lines here were thinner and less certain, as if they had not been used in a long time.

Voices carried from a junction ahead. Two, maybe three. The pressure snapped tight and shoved her sideways into a darker passage.

She did not argue. She did not have breath to waste.

The side passage dipped and tightened. Pipes ran along the ceiling, damp and rusted. A grate on her right opened into a lower run where a pair of workers hurried past, glancing up without seeing her.

Rachel took one step toward the grate. She opened her mouth to warn them.

Nausea knifed through her. The pressure locked her knees and she sagged against the wall until it eased.

The workers kept moving. One of them laughed, tired, and the sound hurt.

Rachel turned away and followed the narrow run downward as the corridor behind her sealed with a soft, final grind.
