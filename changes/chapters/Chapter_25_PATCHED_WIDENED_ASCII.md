# Chapter 25

No one touched her again.

They hovered instead, the handlers who had rushed in slowing as soon as they crossed the threshold, boots stopping just short of the etched lines in the floor. Someone swore under their breath. Someone else said something she couldn't hear.

She stayed where she was, back against the wall, hands flat on the stone like she could feel it breathing. Her shoulder throbbed. Her leg burned. The pressure inside her shifted and rolled, restless now, as if it had been startled awake and didn't like the noise.

"Clear the room," a voice said. Not shouted. Tight.

"No," another voice answered. "We need eyes."

"We need space."

A pause. Then footsteps backing away, reluctant, uneven. The room felt larger with every step they took, emptier, the light too bright now that there was no one standing under it.

She swallowed. Her throat hurt.

The slate-holder knelt by the fallen handler, careful not to cross the lines. They didn't touch them either. Just leaned close enough to look.

"They're breathing," they said. "Shallow."

"Move them," someone said.

The slate-holder shook their head. "Not yet."

The pressure twitched at that, quick and sharp, and the etched lines flared faintly in response.

She squeezed her eyes shut. The room tilted anyway.

"Okay," she whispered, though nothing had asked.

Someone spoke again, closer this time. "Can you hear me?"

She opened her eyes.

A woman stood just outside the lines, hands open, empty, her stance careful like she was approaching a skittish animal. Her face was flushed, hair pulled back too tight, eyes flicking between her and the floor like she was trying to read both at once.

"Yes," she said. Her voice sounded wrong to her ears. Too loud. Too thin.

The woman nodded, relief flashing and disappearing. "Good. Stay where you are."

"I wasn't going anywhere," she said, and almost laughed.

The pressure stirred.

The woman's eyes flicked to her chest, just for a second.

"We're adjusting," the woman said. "Something went wrong with intake."

"Yeah," she said. "I noticed."

A murmur rippled through the room. Someone hissed at someone else to stop talking.

The woman took a careful half-step closer and then stopped herself. "I need you to tell me what you're feeling."

She frowned. "You want a list?"

"Anything," the woman said quickly. "Heat. Pressure. Dizziness."

She hesitated.

The pressure leaned in, heavy and curious.

"Like something's standing too close," she said finally. "Inside."

The room went very quiet.

"That's not," someone started.

"Log it," another voice snapped.

The woman nodded once, sharp. "Okay."

It didn't feel okay.

The etched lines brightened again, pulsing softly now, slow and steady. Her skin prickled. She shifted her weight without meaning to and the pressure surged in response, making her gasp.

"Don't move," three people said at once.

She froze, heart hammering.

"Right," she said. "Sorry."

The woman swallowed. "We're going to change the environment," she said, speaking carefully, like each word had weight. "We need to reduce stimulus."

The lights dimmed.

The pressure reacted immediately, flaring hot, and she cried out as the sudden shift sent pain ripping through her chest, sharp and breath-stealing.

"Undo it," someone shouted.

The lights snapped back on.

She sagged, gasping, tears leaking out of her eyes without permission. The pressure settled again, tight and angry.

"Not that," the woman said, voice shaking now. "Okay. Not that."

The slate-holder looked up from the floor. "We don't have the right field generators."

"We don't have time," someone else said.

The woman looked at her again, really looked this time, eyes wide and bright with something close to fear. "Can you help us?"

She laughed, raw and hoarse. "You're kidding."

"I mean," the woman faltered. "Can you tell us what makes it worse."

The pressure shifted, slow and deliberate.

She wiped her face with the back of her hand. "When you argue," she said. "When you change things too fast."

The room stilled.

"That's not actionable," someone muttered.

The etched lines pulsed brighter.

Her breath hitched. "When you touch me," she added. "That's the worst."

The woman nodded hard. "Okay. No contact."

Someone scoffed. "That's not containment."

"It is for now," the woman said. "Back up. All of you."

They did, grudgingly, boots scraping stone as they retreated to the edges of the chamber.

The space between her and the others widened.

The pressure eased by a fraction.

She noticed immediately and hated herself for it.

"There," she said softly.

The woman exhaled, shaky. "Okay."

A new sound cut through the room, low and mechanical, a vibration under the floor that made her teeth ache.

"What's that?" she asked.

No one answered.

The slate-holder's face went pale. "We've triggered secondary protocols."

The pressure stirred, interested.

She pressed her hands harder into the stone, heart pounding. "That sounds bad."

The woman met her eyes. "It usually is."

The vibration deepened.

Somewhere nearby, something heavy began to move.
