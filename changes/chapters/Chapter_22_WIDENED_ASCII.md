# Chapter 22

Rachel tried to stand and nearly blacked out.

The stone lurched sideways when she pushed herself up, her leg screaming in protest as her weight shifted wrong, too fast, and she had to slap a hand against the wall to keep from folding back down. The pressure inside her flared at the sudden movement, not stopping her, not helping, just reacting, like it didn't appreciate surprises.

She stayed bent for a moment, breathing hard, forehead pressed to the stone where Lena had shoved her, the imprint of that shove still sharp in her shoulder. When she straightened again she did it slower, piece by piece, waiting for the world to object.

It didn't.

That was worse.

The recess was darker than she remembered, the air stale now that the opening had sealed. No lantern light. No voices. Just stone and the faint echo of her own breathing bouncing back at her wrong, too loud.

"Lena," she said, and the word scraped her throat raw.

Nothing answered.

The pressure tightened, low and steady, a reminder rather than a shove. Rachel swallowed and turned in place, feeling her way along the wall until she found the bend Lena had pushed her behind, the narrow angle that had kept her hidden. From here she could see the seam where the recess had closed, the stone fitted tight and clean like it had never opened at all.

Rachel stared at it too long.

Her chest ached. The pressure pulsed once, impatient.

"Fine," she whispered, though she wasn't sure who she was saying it to.

She tried the seam.

Her fingers traced the stone, searching for the irregularity Lena had known to look for, the give that meant the wall wasn't meant to be permanent. There was nothing. Smooth rock, cool and unyielding, dust packed into the cracks.

Rachel leaned her weight into it and immediately regretted it as her leg flared hot and bright, pain tearing up her side and stealing her breath. She hissed and staggered back, catching herself before she fell.

The pressure surged at the sound.

Not outward.

Upward.

Rachel froze, teeth clenched, waiting for it to finish whatever it was about to do, but instead it tightened and held, dense and heavy, like it was listening again.

She lowered herself carefully back to the floor, every movement slow and deliberate, and waited.

Nothing happened.

The quiet stretched.

Rachel pushed herself back up, slower this time, and the pressure eased by a fraction, enough that she could breathe without feeling like she was being watched from the inside.

"That's how it is now," she muttered. "Great."

She turned away from the sealed recess and followed the narrow space deeper, the passage bending and narrowing until it opened into a rough corridor she hadn't seen before. The stone here was older, less finished, the floor uneven under her boots, forcing her to watch where she stepped.

The pressure shifted as she moved, not guiding so much as bracketing her, tightening when she slowed too much, easing when she kept going, a crude agreement she didn't remember making.

The corridor sloped downward.

Rachel swore softly and adjusted her pace, using the wall for balance as her leg complained with every step. The air grew cooler, damper, and somewhere ahead she heard a faint mechanical hum, distant and irregular, like something running badly.

The corridor ended in another opening.

This one wasn't hidden.

This one was wrong.

The space beyond was larger, a chamber cut wider and cleaner than the passage, with straight edges and embedded lines in the floor that glowed faintly, pale and steady. No tools. No frames. Just open space and the quiet hum that made her teeth itch.

Rachel stepped in and felt the pressure spike hard enough to make her gasp.

The glow brightened.

Somewhere overhead, stone shifted.

Rachel's heart kicked. She backed up a step and the pressure surged again, sharper now, more demanding, and she understood with a sick clarity that this room was meant to notice movement, not hide it.

"Of course it is," she breathed.

The hum deepened.

A voice cut in from nowhere and everywhere at once, flat and distant, not loud enough to echo.

"Subject relocated."

Rachel spun, searching for the source, her pulse roaring in her ears. The pressure inside her tightened, hot and eager, like it recognized the words.

"Stand by," the voice said.

Rachel laughed, short and breathless. "I don't think that's an option."

She backed toward the corridor and felt the pressure shove hard this time, a solid wall against her chest that stole the air from her lungs and sent her stumbling forward instead. She caught herself, barely, boots skidding on the stone.

The glow flared brighter.

"Isolation breach confirmed," the voice said. "Reassessment required."

Rachel sagged against the wall, chest heaving, every muscle tight. The pressure coiled heavy and watchful inside her, no longer pretending to be neutral.

She was alone.

She was visible.

And whatever rules had applied when Lena was here were gone.
