# Chapter 26

The vibration didn't stop.

It settled instead, heavy and low, the kind that worked its way up through the floor and into Rachel's bones until it was hard to tell whether it was sound or pressure or something else entirely. The etched lines around her brightened, then dimmed, then brightened again, out of rhythm with the lights overhead.

Someone swore.

"That shouldn't be on," a voice said, too loud, too fast.

"Kill it," someone else said. "Kill it now."

The vibration answered them, deeper this time.

Rachel pressed her palms into the stone and felt heat there, not burning yet, but close enough that it made her pull her hands back and then put them down again anyway because there was nowhere else to put them. Her shoulder throbbed. Her leg screamed. The pressure inside her shifted, restless, crowding her breath.

"What's happening?" she asked.

No one answered.

The lights flickered and dropped to a dull amber that stretched shadows too long across the room. The air thickened, sharp and metallic, and Rachel gagged as it burned the back of her throat.

"Clear the chamber," someone shouted.

"That's not possible," another voice snapped. "She's still-"

The floor shuddered.

Stone ground open along one wall, a seam splitting wider than it should have, and something spilled out low and fast. Not smoke. Not liquid. It hugged the floor, dense and heavy, swallowing the light where it passed.

Rachel scrambled backward on instinct.

The etched lines flared bright and the pressure slammed down, crushing and sudden, pinning her in place hard enough that her vision went white at the edges.

"No," she gasped.

The dark reached her boots.

Cold punched through the soles, sharp and biting, crawling upward with a weight that dragged instead of flowed. Rachel cried out as it wrapped her ankles, her calves, burning and freezing all at once in a way her body couldn't sort.

"Temperature-" someone started.

"That's not temperature," another voice yelled.

The word vanished as the dark surged higher, swallowing sound and bending it wrong.

Rachel clawed at the stone, skin tearing as she tried to pull herself free. The pressure inside her flared wild and jagged, heat bursting through her chest in uneven pulses that made everything worse.

The dark crept past her knees.

She screamed.

The sound came out broken, pressed flat by the air, wrong in her ears.

Red light flashed.

A shrill tone cut through the room, sharp enough to make her clamp her hands over her ears. The pressure vibrated with it, inside her, teeth-rattling and violent.

"Rollback," someone shouted.

"We can't," another voice said, high and thin. "It's already-"

The cold bit deeper.

Rachel's legs went numb, the sensation dropping away so suddenly it scared her more than the pain had. Her thoughts scattered, slipping sideways, unable to hold onto anything long enough to make sense of it.

The pressure compressed, tighter and tighter, until her ribs ached and her breath came in shallow, panicked pulls.

Then it shifted.

Not gone.

Just different.

Rachel sucked in a breath she hadn't meant to take, sharp and shaky, and the heat under her skin steadied, no longer bursting at random. The etched lines flickered, their glow stuttering like they'd lost the beat.

The dark slowed.

Just a little.

"Did-" someone whispered.

"Hold," another voice snapped. "Don't touch anything."

Rachel sagged forward, gasping, her hands sliding on the stone as the cold pulled back an inch, then another. Her legs burned as feeling crept back in, painful and uneven.

The vibration stuttered.

Lights steadied.

The shrill tone cut off mid-note, leaving a ringing silence behind.

The dark drained back toward the seam, retreating in uneven ripples until stone ground shut over it again, sealing the wall like it had never opened.

Rachel collapsed onto her side, shaking so hard her teeth chattered. The pressure settled heavy and close inside her, not quiet, not loud, just there.

Around her, boots scraped stone. Voices dropped low and fast.

"That wasn't-" someone said.

"No," someone else answered. "That wasn't."

Rachel lay there, chest heaving, staring at the floor lines as they dimmed back to their faint, etched nothing.

Her legs hurt. Her shoulder hurt. Everything hurt.

Whatever they'd turned on hadn't stopped cleanly.

And whatever had changed hadn't come from them.
