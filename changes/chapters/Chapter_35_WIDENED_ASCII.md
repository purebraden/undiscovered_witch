# Chapter 35

The hum sank lower.

Not louder. Not faster.

Lower, like the building had dropped into a register her bones could hear.

Rachel stayed on the floor because standing felt like asking for it. One palm braced on stone, the other pressed to her ribs, breath shallow as she waited for the pressure to spike again.

It didn't.

It hovered wrong and late inside her chest, like it was listening from too far away.

The light under the door slid sideways.

Rachel stared at it, throat tight.

"No," she said, and the word came out thin.

The etched lines brightened all at once.

Heat followed a half second later, too late to stop her from flinching, too late to brace. She cried out and curled in on herself as the pressure snapped tight around pain that had already arrived. Her vision smeared at the edges and the floor shuddered under her like the room was trying to shake her loose.

The lock went.

Not a clean click.

A staggered series of metallic snaps, ugly and wrong, like joints popping out of place.

Rachel tried to back away. Her hands skidded on stone. Her palms burned.

The door didn't swing.

It slid.

Sideways, as if the wall had decided the doorway belonged somewhere else.

Cold air punched into the room, sharp enough to steal her breath. Rachel gagged and blinked hard, eyes watering as the gap widened into something that shouldn't have been there at all.

Beyond it wasn't the corridor she'd been staring at through seams and light.

The color was different. Dimmer. Bluer.

The surfaces looked older too, layered with cable runs and panel seams that hadn't been opened in years. It felt like looking into the inside of a machine.

Rachel stared, heart hammering.

The pressure tightened, late again, reacting after her fear had already taken hold.

"No," she said again, louder, like volume could change physics.

The floor shifted.

Not a tremor.

A tilt.

Rachel screamed as the stone beneath her rolled slowly, then faster, the etched lines flaring bright as gravity betrayed her. She slid toward the opening, fingers scrabbling for purchase on useless smooth stone.

Heat ripped through her ribs. The pressure surged, wild and unfocused, and it didn't help. It didn't catch her. It just burned.

She hit the threshold shoulder-first.

Pain exploded down her arm and she saw white.

Then she was through.

The world flipped. Metal under her now, cold and hard, her body skidding, breath tearing out of her lungs in a sound that didn't feel human.

The room behind her sealed itself with a grinding shriek.

Stone and metal snapped back into place. The gap vanished as if it had never opened. No seam. No door. Just wall.

Rachel lay sprawled on the metal floor, gasping, one arm tucked tight to her chest, trying not to move because moving meant learning what else hurt.

The pressure finally tightened.

Late.

Useless.

It wrapped around pain that was already cresting and falling, like a hand showing up after the fight and asking if she needed help.

"Jesus," she whispered, voice breaking, and tried to laugh but it came out wrong.

She rolled onto her side and dragged in air that tasted damp and old. The corridor around her was narrow, dim, lit by intermittent strips that flickered with an uneven pulse. Pipes ran along the walls and ceiling. Something dripped steadily somewhere ahead, each drop landing with a sound that carried too far.

Rachel pushed herself up with her good arm and got halfway to standing before dizziness slammed her back down. She sat there, panting, forehead nearly touching her knees, waiting for the room to stop sliding.

Behind her, the wall was solid stone.

No seam.

No frame.

Nothing.

Ahead, the corridor sloped downward into shadow.

The hum followed her.

Not from one direction. From the walls, the floor, the air itself, as if the whole place had decided she belonged to it now.

Rachel swallowed hard and took a shaky step forward.

The pressure shifted inside her chest, not guiding, not warning. Just present. Delayed. Wrong.

Whatever had moved her hadn't freed her.

It had simply put her somewhere else.

And this felt like the place things were sent when no one wanted to touch them.
