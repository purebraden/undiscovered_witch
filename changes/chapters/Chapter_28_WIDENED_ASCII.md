# Chapter 28

They didn't announce it.

The room just started emptying.

One person backed toward the door and another followed, then another, boots scraping stone in uneven rhythms as equipment was lifted, powered down, rolled away. Someone unplugged something and the lights flickered, not off, just weaker, thinner, like the room had decided it didn't need to try so hard anymore.

Rachel lay where she was and watched it happen.

No one came close. No one looked at her for longer than a glance. Orders moved sideways, murmured between shoulders, hands gesturing instead of pointing. With every person who left, the pressure inside her shifted, tightening, easing, tightening again, like it was keeping track.

"Hey," Rachel said. Her voice sounded wrong in the thinning air. Too loud. "Hey- what's happening?"

No one answered.

"Where am I?" she said. "What is this place?"

A cart bumped a threshold and stopped. Someone cursed under their breath and abandoned it half in, half out of the room.

"Why are you doing this?" Rachel said. "Why are you- why are you leaving?"

Her legs still wouldn't cooperate. She tried to bend one knee and pain flared sharp and bright, stealing her breath. The pressure stirred immediately, alert, and she froze, breathing shallow until it settled again.

The man from before crouched near the boundary, careful, like the floor might give way if he trusted it. He didn't look at her at first. He stared at the etched lines, jaw tight, like he was memorizing them.

"Where am I supposed to go?" Rachel said. The words piled up now, tripping over each other. "What did you just do to me? What was that thing-"

He exhaled, slow.

"We're clearing the sector," he said.

"That doesn't tell me anything," Rachel said, heat creeping into her voice. "You can't just say that and walk away. How long? How long are you leaving me here?"

His eyes flicked toward the door, toward the people still moving out.

"Long enough," he said.

"That's not an answer," she said. Her chest felt tight, breath coming too fast. "What happens after? Where do I go after?"

He shook his head once. "We don't know."

The pressure tightened, slow and heavy, like something settling into place.

"You don't know?" she said. "You brought me here and you don't know?"

He didn't respond.

He stood and stepped back, careful not to cross the etched line. Someone grabbed his arm and tugged him toward the door. He resisted for half a second, then let himself be pulled away.

The woman who'd argued earlier lingered near the edge of the room, hands raised slightly, like she didn't trust herself to move wrong. Her eyes flicked between Rachel and the exits, calculating.

"This is temporary," she said, too fast.

Rachel laughed, sharp and brittle. "Everything is temporary."

The woman swallowed. She didn't answer.

She backed away, never turning her back until she reached the doorway.

The door hissed closed.

The sound echoed longer than it should have.

Rachel was alone.

Not silence, not yet. The room hummed softly, systems still alive somewhere deep in the walls, but the human noise was gone. No breathing. No footsteps. No voices arguing over what to do next.

The pressure inside her shifted, spreading into the space they'd left behind.

Rachel lay there, heart pounding, nerves buzzing, waiting for something else to happen.

Nothing did.

Minutes passed. Maybe more. Time loosened without anyone marking it.

The lights dimmed again.

Rachel dragged herself upright at last, slow and shaking, back sliding up the wall until she was sitting. The etched lines were faint now, barely visible unless she leaned forward, but she could feel them anyway, like a memory pressed into the floor.

Her breathing sounded too loud.

She pressed her lips together, fighting the urge to speak just to prove she still could.

The pressure leaned closer.

Not touching.

Just present.

Rachel closed her eyes.

"Okay," she whispered, though she didn't feel okay at all. "Okay. I get it."

The pressure didn't answer.

It didn't need to.
