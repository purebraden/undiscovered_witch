# Chapter 33

Rachel woke with her cheek against the floor and a dull, sour ache behind her eyes.

For a second she lay there, breathing shallow, not sure where she was. The stone was cold under her face. Her mouth tasted like blood and dust.

Then the pressure shifted.

Her stomach clenched and memory slammed back into place.

She groaned and rolled onto her side, the movement sending a spike of pain through her shoulder and down her arm. She stopped halfway, waited for the room to steady, then pushed herself upright inch by inch until her back hit the wall.

The door was still wrong.

Light leaked through the seam, steady now, not flickering, not dimming. Just there. Like it had decided on that shape and wasn't interested in changing it again.

Rachel swallowed and wiped her mouth with the back of her hand. Her head throbbed. Every breath scraped on the way in.

The etched lines were dim, barely visible, but she could feel them anyway. A low buzz under her skin, faint and constant, like standing too close to something running.

She closed her eyes.

That was when she heard the voices.

At first she thought it was the building. The hum shifting, echoes folding in on themselves. She almost ignored it.

Then a word broke through.

Not clear. Not whole.

"-can't-"

Rachel froze.

She leaned her head back against the wall and listened harder, heart picking up speed as the sound came again, louder this time, layered with another voice.

"...no, that's not-"

The words bent and stretched as they traveled, smeared by distance and stone. She couldn't tell where they were coming from, only that they weren't inside the room.

They were arguing.

Rachel pressed her hand to her mouth, afraid even her breathing might carry.

"-every time-"

Another voice cut over it, sharper, strained. "-we said-"

Something banged. Not close. Not far. The sound rang oddly, like it had hit too many corners on the way to her.

Rachel's chest tightened.

The pressure inside her shifted, subtle but unmistakable, not flaring, not pulling back. Just paying attention.

She slid her knees up slowly and wrapped her arms around them, curling inward on instinct. The floor was cold through her clothes, grounding in a way nothing else had managed to be.

A fragment came through clearer than the rest.

"-send people back-"

Her breath hitched.

Another voice, lower, clipped. "-not yet-"

The sound overlapped again, words stacking on top of each other until they blurred into noise. Rachel shook her head once, sharp, like she could knock understanding loose.

"-route's unstable-"

"-if it spikes again-"

"-we don't-"

Rachel squeezed her eyes shut.

They weren't talking to her.

That much she understood.

The voices rose, then fell, the argument losing its edge as distance crept in. Someone said something she didn't catch at all. Another voice answered, quieter this time, the words swallowed before they reached her.

Rachel waited.

For footsteps.

For the lock to finish what it had started.

For someone to say her name.

Nothing happened.

The voices thinned and drifted away, fading into the walls until she couldn't tell where they'd gone or if they were still there at all.

Rachel stayed curled against the wall, breathing shallow, listening to the pressure shift and settle close inside her chest.

The light under the door didn't change.

The door didn't move.

Whatever they were deciding, it wasn't happening here.
