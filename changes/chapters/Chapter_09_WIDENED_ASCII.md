# Chapter 9

The line led her downward. The air cooled. The light thinned to seams in the stone that glowed faintly like embers under ash.

She heard work above her. Not the open noise of a city, but voices measured and low, boots stopping and starting, the scrape of wood dragged across stone.

A side passage opened to her left, barred with iron. Through it she saw men with lanterns placing a painted cloth across an alley mouth. A woman with a broom stopped, listened, then turned away when a guard touched her shoulder.

Rachel kept moving. The pressure tightened when she paused.

Another junction. Three marks on the stone, painted in dull white. One crossed through. One smeared. One intact. Her stomach rolled when she looked at the crossed mark. The pressure loosened at the intact one.

She took that path.

The corridor narrowed, then widened into a long service run. Overhead, a grate rattled as a cart rolled across it. Voices carried down.

"Outsider status confirmed."
"Binding is active."
"Keep the lower run clear. No public exposure."

A muffled cry came from somewhere farther down the run, cut short. The pressure shoved her sideways before she could turn toward it.

She stumbled, caught herself on the wall, and kept moving.

The line in the floor reappeared and led her to a hatch. The pressure eased when she touched it.

She pushed.
