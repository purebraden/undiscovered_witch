# Chapter 23

Rachel hit the chute hard and slid, the stone tilted just enough that she could not slow herself. Air tore past her ears. The pressure inside her spiked and then flattened, as if it was bracing for impact it could not prevent.

Voices echoed above, distorted by distance and motion.

"No stabilizers."
"She is already in transit."
"Log it."

She slammed into a bend, shoulder first. Pain flashed white behind her eyes. The chute leveled and spit her into a smaller room, and she tumbled onto stone that vibrated under her weight.

Light came on. Clean. Steady.

She pushed herself upright, breath tearing, and pressed her back to the wall. The pressure stayed coiled and watchful under her ribs.

The door across the room hissed open.

Two people entered. Light gear, clean clothing, no obvious weapons. One carried a slate. The other rolled a low cart with thin instruments that looked too delicate to trust.

"Delivered," the slate holder said, glancing down. "Late."

"Within tolerance," the other replied, already pulling gloves on.

Rachel shook her head. "Don't touch me."

The slate holder looked up, not alarmed. "Speak when prompted."

The pressure flickered, sharp and brief, like a held breath.

"Classification?" the other asked.

"Low," the slate holder said without looking at Rachel. "Routine."

The word landed wrong, heavy as a dropped plate.

"No," Rachel said. "That's wrong."

They moved in anyway.

The instant fingers closed around her wrist, the room lurched. Heat ripped through Rachel's arm and chest, a tearing sensation that felt like being pulled apart and shoved back together wrong. She screamed as force burst outward, not precise, not shaped, just there.

The handler flew backward and hit the floor hard. The cart tipped, instruments clattering across stone.

The floor lines flared bright.

No alarm sounded.

The slate holder stared at the fallen body, then at Rachel, then at the slate. "That is not-"

Rachel sagged against the wall, gasping, vision dimming around the edges. The pressure coiled tight inside her, heavy and present, humming faintly.

Footsteps arrived at a run. More than two. The door hissed again.

"We have a misfile," the slate holder said, voice tight now.

Rachel closed her eyes.

Whatever rules had failed to follow her into this room had finally arrived.
