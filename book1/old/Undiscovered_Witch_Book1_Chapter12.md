# Chapter 12 — What She Doesn’t End

Kara’s seat stayed empty.

That was the first thing Allison noticed when she walked into homeroom — not the whispering, not the way a few people glanced up and then looked away too fast, but the physical shape of the absence.

The desk was still there.

Chair pushed in.

Textbook stacked neatly underneath, like Kara might still come back for it.

Allison stood in the doorway for half a second longer than normal, backpack heavy on her shoulder, waiting for the moment where the room would correct itself.

It didn’t.

She moved to her own seat and sat carefully, like sudden motion might draw attention she didn’t know how to handle.

Emily slid in beside her a few seconds later without speaking.

They didn’t look at each other.

The teacher started attendance.

“Kara M—?”

A pause.

The teacher cleared her throat. “Absent.”

The word landed too hard.

Absent meant temporary. It meant excused. It meant not here right now.

It didn’t mean gone.

Allison stared at the corner of her desk and tried to breathe evenly.

Nothing snapped.

Nothing surged.

Nothing bent.

That was new.

And it was harder than she expected.

By second period, the looks had changed.

Not curious anymore.

Careful.

People didn’t stare outright. They glanced, checked Allison’s face like it might tell them what they were allowed to feel, then moved on.

A girl Allison barely knew dropped her pencil and muttered, “Sorry,” like it mattered.

Another whispered something behind her hand and then laughed too quickly, too loud, like laughter could erase guilt.

Allison kept her head down.

She took notes she didn’t process.

She focused on the physical world — pen against paper, the hum of the projector, the predictable rhythm of the clock.

Every time her mind drifted toward why, she pulled it back.

Why led to imagining outcomes.

Outcomes led to pressure.

Pressure led to wanting to fix things.

And she couldn’t afford that.

In the hallway between classes, someone said Kara’s name like it was a test.

“Allison?”

She stopped.

Rachel stood a few feet away, arms crossed, expression unreadable in a way that felt intentional.

“So,” Rachel said, tilting her head. “Your friend just… disappear?”

The hallway noise dimmed, not magically — socially. People pretended not to listen, which was worse.

Allison felt the heat rise in her chest.

She felt the familiar tightening, the world’s subtle lean toward her attention.

She could feel how easy it would be to make this stop.

One push. One correction. One quieting.

Instead, she looked at Rachel and said, flat, “Move.”

Rachel blinked, surprised.

“What?”

“I said move,” Allison repeated, her voice steady in a way that surprised even her.

For a moment, Allison thought Rachel would push back.

Then Rachel scoffed, rolled her eyes, and stepped aside. “Whatever,” she said. “Not my drama.”

Allison walked past without looking back.

The lights didn’t flicker.

The hallway noise returned.

Her heart pounded like she’d run a mile.

She hadn’t fixed anything.

But she hadn’t made it worse.

That felt like something.

At lunch, Kara’s table stayed empty.

Not abandoned — avoided.

People steered around it without comment, trays balanced carefully like the space might contaminate them.

Emily hovered beside Allison, indecisive.

“Do we…?” Emily gestured vaguely.

Allison shook her head. “No.”

Emily hesitated. “Okay.”

They sat somewhere else.

The cafeteria felt wrong. Too loud. Too performative.

Allison ate half her food and stopped, nausea creeping in without warning.

She pulled her phone out once, then put it face down on the table.

She didn’t want to know what people were saying.

She didn’t want proof.

Proof made things permanent.

In English, the teacher assigned a group activity.

Allison ended up with two people she didn’t usually talk to.

They were polite in the strained way people were polite when they thought someone might break.

No one mentioned Kara.

That somehow made it worse.

When the bell rang, Allison packed up fast and left before anyone could say anything else.

She didn’t go to the creek.

She didn’t trust quiet yet.

Instead, she walked the long way home, letting the cold air bite at her face until it drowned out her thoughts.

She passed the field behind the school and didn’t look toward the bleachers.

She wasn’t ready for Evan.

She wasn’t ready for understanding.

At home, her mom asked, “How was school?”

Allison shrugged. “Fine.”

The lie tasted thin.

Her mom watched her for a second too long. “You sure?”

Allison nodded. “Yeah.”

Her mom didn’t push.

That felt like mercy and abandonment all at once.

That night, Allison lay in bed staring at the ceiling, listening to the house settle.

She thought about all the ways this could end.

A meeting.

An apology.

A return.

A transfer.

A silence that stretched until Kara became a story instead of a person.

Allison felt the pressure gather — not sharp, not urgent, but heavy and patient.

The world waiting.

She closed her eyes.

She didn’t reach for it.

Not tonight.

Not yet.

Some things, she was learning, didn’t end because you wanted them to.

And some things only ended when you tried too hard to stop them.
