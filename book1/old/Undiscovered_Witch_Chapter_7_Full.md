# CHAPTER 7 — What It Costs to Stay

Lunch always smelled the same.

That was the first thing Allison noticed as she slid her tray onto the table—warm plastic, fries that had cooled too fast, something faintly sweet and artificial that clung to the air no matter what day it was. The cafeteria was loud in the familiar way, voices layered over one another until they blurred into noise rather than meaning.

Normal.

Kara was already sitting, phone face down beside her milk carton, braid falling loose over one shoulder. She was talking—something about a math quiz, something about how the substitute teacher had called her by the wrong name—and Allison half listened, nodding in the right places, responding when expected.

Across the room, Evan sat alone near the windows, notebook open but untouched. He wasn’t looking at them. Not directly. But Allison had learned by now that didn’t mean much.

Kara laughed, short and sharp. “I swear, if he does that thing where he says ‘you people’ one more time—”

The laughter started at another table.

It wasn’t loud at first. Just a ripple. A sudden burst that cut through the steady din of the room.

Kara faltered mid-sentence.

Allison felt it then—a subtle shift, like a pressure change. Not sharp. Not urgent. Just… present.

More laughter. A few gasps. Phones tilted upward, screens angled toward friends.

Kara’s smile faded. “What?” she asked.

Allison followed her gaze. Two tables over, a girl Kara barely knew was holding her phone out, laughing so hard she had to wipe at her eyes. Someone leaned in closer to look.

Kara frowned and reached for her phone.

Allison’s stomach tightened.

“Kara,” she said quietly.

Kara had already unlocked the screen.

The color drained from her face.

She stared, unblinking, as the video loaded. Her breath caught once—sharp and shallow—then again, faster this time.

Allison leaned closer, just enough to see.

The video was short. Too short. Fifteen seconds, maybe.

It showed the girls’ bathroom door swinging open. Kara stepping out, backpack slung over one shoulder, laughing at something someone off-camera had said.

Then the angle shifted.

White paper trailing behind her. Toilet paper. Long enough to brush the floor as she walked.

The camera zoomed in.

Someone snorted.

The video looped.

“Oh my god,” Kara whispered.

Her phone slipped from her fingers and clattered onto the table.

The world did not stop.

Laughter continued, louder now. Not everyone. Not even most people. But enough. Enough that it felt like the sound was everywhere at once.

Kara’s face flushed deep red, spreading down her neck. She shook her head slowly, like she was trying to dislodge the image from her mind.

“I didn’t—” she said. “I checked. I always—”

Her voice broke.

Tears spilled over, sudden and uncontained.

Allison’s chest tightened painfully.

Kara covered her face with both hands, shoulders shaking. “I didn’t know,” she said, voice muffled. “I didn’t know.”

Allison felt the pull immediately.

It was stronger here. Sharper. The pressure coiled tight behind her ribs, familiar and urgent.

Make it stop.

Around them, the cafeteria hesitated. A few people looked away, suddenly uncomfortable. Others kept watching, drawn by the spectacle.

Someone murmured, “That sucks.”

Another voice said, “It’s not even that bad.”

A boy at the next table snickered. “It’s kind of funny, though.”

Kara sobbed harder.

Allison stood halfway out of her seat before she realized she was moving.

Her hands shook.

This was exactly the kind of moment she was supposed to let happen.

She knew that now.

Kara needed to feel this. To be angry. To be embarrassed. To rage and cry and maybe scream if she wanted to.

Allison remembered Emily’s voice. I want to be mad.

She remembered Kara’s words from yesterday. You don’t have to manage it.

She felt Evan’s presence across the room like a weight.

And still—

Kara gasped for breath, fingers digging into her sleeves like she was trying to hold herself together by force.

“I can’t,” Kara choked. “I can’t be here.”

A teacher at the edge of the cafeteria noticed the commotion and started toward them, concern already giving way to the practiced relief of intervention.

Allison’s fear spiked.

If I don’t do something, she’ll break.

That was the lie. Or maybe it was the truth she’d always lived with.

Allison reached out and placed her hand on Kara’s arm.

Kara flinched but didn’t pull away.

“It’s okay,” Allison said softly.

The words came easily. Too easily.

“It was an accident,” she continued. “Everyone has something like this happen at least once. This will blow over.”

Kara’s sobs hitched.

Allison kept her voice steady, gentle. “You didn’t do anything wrong. It doesn’t mean anything about you.”

The pressure released.

Not dramatically. Not all at once. Just enough.

The noise around them softened. The laughter tapered off. People lost interest as quickly as they’d found it.

Kara’s breathing slowed.

Her shoulders sagged.

The teacher stopped a few steps away, visibly relieved. “Everything okay here?”

“Yes,” Allison said automatically.

Kara nodded, wiping her face with shaking hands. “Yeah. I’m fine.”

The teacher smiled, already turning away. “Good. Let me know if you need anything.”

Someone nearby murmured, “You handled that really well.”

Allison felt the words like a bruise.

Kara sniffed and picked up her fork.

“I don’t even remember why I was crying,” she said, confused. She frowned down at her tray. “That’s weird.”

Allison’s stomach dropped.

The cafeteria returned to its usual rhythm. Noise layered over noise. Lunch resumed.

Across the room, Evan was watching now.

Not openly. But clearly.

He didn’t smile. He didn’t nod. He didn’t look relieved.

He just watched.

Kara ate in silence.

She didn’t joke. Didn’t complain about the fries. Didn’t bring up the quiz again.

She stayed.

And that somehow felt worse than if she’d run.

---

The rest of the day passed like a dream Allison couldn’t quite wake from.

In history class, someone whispered about the video, but the tone had shifted—less mockery, more boredom. Old news already.

By last period, it was gone.

That night, Allison sat on her bed with her phone in her hands, the house quiet but not settled.

She opened the Notes app.

Her fingers hovered.

She typed.

> I knew what would happen.  
> I did it anyway.  
>  
> It worked.  
>  
> Kara didn’t finish what she was saying.

She stared at the screen for a long time.

She didn’t delete it.

She set the phone down and lay back, staring at the ceiling crack.

It looked different tonight.

Like something that wouldn’t close again.
