# CHAPTER 8 — What Remains

By Wednesday, the video was gone.

Not deleted—Allison knew better than that—but buried. Pushed down beneath new posts and new jokes and new minor catastrophes that filled the school’s collective attention span. By the time first period ended, no one was actively talking about it anymore.

Which meant, as far as Alder Creek was concerned, it had been resolved.

Allison noticed the difference immediately. Not because people were kinder—if anything, they were brisker—but because the edges had smoothed again. The awkwardness that should have lingered didn’t. Teachers moved on. Students refocused. The cafeteria noise returned to its usual steady roar.

The town had absorbed the disruption and flattened it.

Allison sat in English, staring at the margins of her notebook instead of the board. Her pen hovered uselessly above the page.

She had expected something worse.

That realization unsettled her more than the quiet.

When the bell rang, Kara packed up quickly without looking at her.

“All right,” Kara said, slinging her bag over her shoulder. “I’ll see you later.”

Allison blinked. “Later?”

“Yeah,” Kara said, already halfway into the aisle. “Probably.”

The word landed strangely. Probably.

Allison watched her go, a faint ache settling in her chest.

---

They still sat together at lunch.

That was the first thing Allison checked for when she walked into the cafeteria: Kara’s braid, the familiar tilt of her shoulders, the space she always left open on the bench.

Kara waved when she saw her. Smiled, even.

Relief washed through Allison so quickly it made her dizzy.

But something was different.

Kara talked about homework. About a show she’d started watching. About a rumor involving two juniors Allison barely knew. Her voice was steady, casual. Light.

She did not mention the video.

She did not mention yesterday.

She did not mention how she’d cried so hard she could barely breathe.

Allison nodded and laughed in the right places, trying to match Kara’s tone.

Across the room, Evan sat in his usual spot near the windows. He glanced over once, briefly, then looked away.

He didn’t intervene.

That mattered.

When lunch ended, Kara stood and said, “I’m going to walk with Jess today.”

“Oh,” Allison said. “Okay.”

Kara hesitated, just a fraction of a second. “I’ll text you later.”

She didn’t.

---

The day dragged.

In chemistry, Allison found herself waiting for moments of tension that never quite materialized. Conversations stayed surface-level. Disagreements softened before they could form.

It felt like living in the aftermath of a storm that no one remembered seeing.

Allison’s chest ached with a pressure she didn’t know how to release.

She opened her Notes app twice and closed it without typing anything.

After school, she walked home alone.

The creek was lower today, the water moving more slowly, as if exhausted by yesterday’s rush. Allison leaned against the railing and watched it slide past, dull and gray.

She thought of Kara’s face when she’d said, I don’t even remember why I was crying.

The memory made her throat tighten.

She pulled out her phone and opened a new note.

Her fingers hovered.

She typed:

> **Aftermath**  
>  
> Kara is fine.  
> Everyone says she’s fine.  
>  
> I don’t know if that means anything.

She stared at the words, then added:

> She didn’t say goodbye today.

She didn’t add a dismissal.

She saved it.

---

At home, Emily was sprawled on the living room floor, textbooks spread around her like she’d been dropped in the middle of studying. She looked up when Allison came in.

“Hey,” Emily said.

“Hey.”

Emily squinted at her. “You look tired.”

“I’m fine,” Allison said automatically.

Emily raised an eyebrow. “You say that a lot.”

Allison smiled faintly. “I know.”

Emily hesitated, then asked, “Is Kara okay?”

The question caught Allison off guard. “Why?”

Emily shrugged. “People were talking. Not, like, mean talking. Just… talking.”

Allison nodded. “She’s okay.”

Emily studied her for a moment longer than necessary. “Are you?”

Allison opened her mouth.

Closed it again.

“I think so,” she said.

Emily rolled onto her side. “That’s also new.”

Allison huffed a quiet laugh. “You’re very observant.”

“I know,” Emily said smugly. Then she sobered. “Just… don’t forget that not everything has to stop immediately.”

Allison’s breath caught.

“I won’t,” she said softly.

Emily nodded, apparently satisfied, and went back to her homework.

The house stayed imperfect.

---

Thursday morning brought rain.

Not the gentle, cooperative drizzle Allison was used to, but a steady, soaking downpour that left puddles everywhere and turned the sidewalks slick. Umbrellas bloomed across the school entrance like dark flowers.

Allison stood under the overhang and watched Kara approach with Jess, laughing at something Allison couldn’t hear.

Kara looked… normal.

Happy, even.

When Kara saw her, she smiled and waved. The smile didn’t quite reach her eyes.

Allison waved back.

They didn’t walk in together.

In history class, the teacher paired students up for a discussion. Kara ended up with Jess again.

Allison was paired with a boy she barely knew.

The conversation was polite. Efficient. Forgettable.

At the end of class, Kara caught her at the door.

“Hey,” Kara said. “Sorry I’ve been kind of… busy.”

“It’s okay,” Allison said quickly. Too quickly.

Kara smiled. “You’re always so understanding.”

The words twisted something inside Allison.

“Yeah,” she said.

---

Evan found her by the lockers after last period.

Not casually. Not accidentally.

“Allison,” he said, stopping a few feet away.

She looked up, startled. “Hey.”

He studied her face. “How are you holding up?”

The question felt heavier than the others had.

“I’m okay,” she said.

He waited.

She sighed. “I think I did something wrong.”

Evan nodded slowly. “I know.”

The simple agreement stole her breath.

“You saw it,” she said.

“Yes.”

“You didn’t stop me.”

“No.”

“Why?” The word came out sharper than she intended.

“Because you weren’t out of control,” Evan said. “You chose.”

Allison swallowed. “That doesn’t make it better.”

“No,” Evan agreed. “But it makes it yours.”

They stood in silence, the hallway emptying around them.

“She’s not mad at me,” Allison said quietly.

Evan tilted his head. “No.”

“She’s just… less.”

“Yes.”

Allison closed her eyes.

“This is what happens,” Evan continued, carefully, “when you preserve something instead of letting it change.”

She opened her eyes. “I thought I was helping.”

“You were,” he said. “Just not her.”

Allison let out a shaky breath. “Then who?”

Evan met her gaze. “Yourself.”

The truth of it hurt.

---

That night, Allison lay in bed listening to the rain hammer against the roof.

Her phone buzzed.

A text from Kara.

Sorry I’ve been distant. I just needed some space.

Allison stared at the screen.

She typed, deleted, typed again.

I understand, she sent.

Three dots appeared. Disappeared.

Thanks, Kara replied.

Nothing else followed.

Allison opened her Notes app.

She added to the earlier entry:

> I got what I wanted.  
>  
> She stayed.

Her fingers trembled.

She finished the thought.

> I don’t think that’s the same thing.

She saved it.

The rain kept falling.

And for the first time, Allison didn’t wish it would stop.
