# UNDISCOVERED WITCH — NOTES APP EVOLUTION BIBLE (POST-MIDPOINT)

## Purpose of This Document

This bible locks the **voice, function, and constraints** of Allison’s Notes app *after the Book 1 midpoint*.

Up through Chapter 6, the Notes functioned as:
- Pattern tracking
- Emotional containment
- Plausible deniability

At the midpoint (Chapter 7), that function **breaks**.

From this point forward, the Notes are no longer a coping mechanism.
They become a **record of awareness and cost**.

---

## Phase Overview

### Phase 1 — Pre-Midpoint (Ch. 1–6)
**Status:** Complete

**Characteristics:**
- Short
- Dismissive
- Defensive
- Minimizing language

**Common phrases:**
- probably
- not unusual
- that happens

**Function:**  
Self-surveillance without self-knowledge

---

### Phase 2 — Midpoint Rupture (Ch. 7)
**Status:** Locked / Irreversible

**Midpoint Entry (Canonical):**
```
I knew what would happen.
I did it anyway.

It worked.

Kara didn’t finish what she was saying.
```

**Why this entry matters:**
- No denial
- No dismissal
- No metaphor
- No justification

This is the first Note that cannot be reframed later.

---

### Phase 3 — Immediate Aftermath (Ch. 8–10)
**Status:** Locked

From this point forward, Notes must follow the rules below.

---

## Post-Midpoint Rules (Non-Negotiable)

### Rule 1 — No Emotional Resolution
Notes may name discomfort, but never resolve it.

❌ Not allowed:
- I’ll be okay
- It’ll pass
- I just need time

✅ Allowed:
- It stayed
- I don’t know what to do with this
- This didn’t fix anything

---

### Rule 2 — Observation Without Control
Allison may observe consequences, but cannot fix them on the page.

❌ Not allowed:
- Advice to herself
- Preventative rules
- “Next time I’ll…”

✅ Allowed:
- Listing what changed
- Naming absences
- Noticing withdrawal

---

### Rule 3 — No Moral Self-Absolution
The Notes must never defend Allison.

❌ Not allowed:
- I meant well
- I was trying to help
- Anyone would have done the same

✅ Allowed:
- I chose
- It worked
- That doesn’t mean it was right

---

### Rule 4 — Declarative Language Shift
Hedging language fades after the midpoint.

| Before Midpoint | After Midpoint |
|-----------------|----------------|
| probably        | is             |
| maybe           | happened       |
| might           | didn’t         |
| coincidence     | result         |

---

## Approved Post-Midpoint Note Types

All Notes from Chapters 8–10 must fit **one** of the following categories.

### 1. Absence Notes
What is missing now.

Example:
```
Kara still sits with me.
She doesn’t talk about things that matter.
```

---

### 2. Cost Notes
What changed because of Allison’s action.

Example:
```
Things ended faster.
People were relieved.
Kara was quieter.
```

---

### 3. Mislabeling Notes
Allison still misnames growth as failure.

Example:
```
Everyone says this is better.
I don’t feel better.
```

---

### 4. Containment Failure Notes
Moments where Allison notices her ability failing.

Example:
```
I wanted it to stop.
It didn’t.
```

---

## What Notes Must NOT Do Yet

Until late Book 1:

- No speculation about power
- No naming magic
- No identity language (witch, bloodline, etc.)
- No future planning
- No explanatory logic

The Notes are about **now**, not understanding.

---

## Structural Placement Rules

Post-midpoint Notes should:
- Appear less frequently
- Land after consequences, not during action
- Often contradict the apparent outcome of scenes

If a scene ends cleanly, the Note should feel wrong.
If a scene is messy, the Note should sit quietly beside it.

---

## Author Checklist

Before keeping a Note, ask:

- Does this make Allison feel better? → ❌ CUT
- Does this explain too much? → ❌ CUT
- Does this justify her choice? → ❌ CUT
- Does this name loss or cost? → ✅ KEEP
- Does this feel unresolved? → ✅ KEEP

If a Note feels comforting, it’s lying.
