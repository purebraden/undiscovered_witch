# EPILOGUE — Evan

Evan waited until the house was quiet.

Not asleep—just settled. The television murmuring softly in the living room. The low hum of the dishwasher finishing its cycle. The familiar rhythm of the house when it wasn’t asking anything of him.

He sat at the kitchen table, notebook closed in front of him, hands folded like he was bracing for a test he hadn’t studied for.

His mother noticed first.

She paused in the doorway, her expression sharpening in a way most people never saw.  
“You’re thinking too loudly,” she said.

Evan looked up. “I need to tell you something.”

That was enough.

She crossed the room and sat across from him without asking why. A moment later, his father joined them, leaning against the counter, arms crossed—not defensive, just attentive.

Evan took a breath.

“There’s a girl at school,” he said.

Neither of them reacted. Not surprise. Not curiosity. Just listening.

“She was… helpful,” Evan continued, searching for words that didn’t feel too large. “Things settled around her. Conflicts didn’t escalate the way they usually do. Outcomes improved.”

His father nodded once. “And the adults noticed.”

“Yes,” Evan said. “The school did. They tried to build something around it.”

His mother’s fingers stilled on the table. “How?”

“Programs. Roles. Language,” Evan said. “They tried to make it official.”

Silence followed. Not heavy. Focused.

“She said no,” Evan added.

That changed the air.

His mother leaned forward slightly. His father straightened.

“She refused?” his father asked.

“Yes.”

“Without incident?” his mother asked.

Evan nodded. “No complaints. No drama. She didn’t sabotage anything. She just… declined.”

His parents exchanged a glance Evan couldn’t read.

“What happened after?” his mother asked.

“They adapted,” Evan said. “They replaced her. Formalized the process anyway.”

“And the results?” his father asked.

Evan hesitated. “Worse,” he said finally. “Quieter. More controlled. Less… human.”

Silence stretched again.

Then his mother asked, gently, “What’s her name?”

Evan looked down at his hands.

“I don’t want to say,” he said.  
After a moment, he added, “Not yet.”

His father frowned slightly. “Why?”

Evan searched for the reason. He knew it mattered, but not *how*.

“It doesn’t feel like it’s mine to give,” he said. “And I think… I think she wouldn’t want it used like that.”

His mother studied him for a long moment. Then something in her expression softened—not approval, not concern. Recognition.

“What do you think she’s doing?” she asked.

Evan didn’t answer right away.

He thought of Allison standing by the creek. Of the way she refused without flinching. Of how the world kept moving—and she stayed herself anyway.

“She’s choosing not to be controlled,” he said quietly.  
“Not to be folded into it.”

His mother exhaled slowly.

“That’s harder than people realize,” she said.

His father nodded. “And rarer.”

His mother stood and walked to the window, looking out into the dark where Alder Creek’s lights blurred into the trees.

“Keep watching,” she said. “But don’t interfere.”

Evan looked up. “You don’t want me to approach her?”

“Not yet,” she said. “Not until she decides what that choice means to her.”

“And if she never does?” Evan asked.

His father met his gaze. “Then the system will keep trying.”

Evan closed his eyes briefly.

“She’s still here,” he said.

His mother nodded once. “That’s how it starts.”

The house settled around them again.

Nothing had changed.

And everything had.
