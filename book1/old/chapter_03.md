# CHAPTER 3 — The New Kid

The announcement came halfway through second period, cutting off Mr. Levin mid-sentence.

“And if you look at the way the author structures—”  
The intercom crackled.  
“Sorry, Mr. Levin.”

Mr. Levin sighed the long-suffering sigh of someone who had taught long enough to know better than to expect uninterrupted thoughts. “Go ahead.”

“Good morning, Alder Creek High,” the principal said, his voice too cheerful for the hour. “We have a new student joining us today. Please help make him feel welcome.”

There was a brief pause, like the announcement itself wasn’t sure what came next.

“Evan Hale will be joining the senior class.”

The intercom clicked off.

A low murmur rippled through the room.

“Midyear?” someone whispered.

“Senior?” someone else said. “Why?”

Mr. Levin cleared his throat. “All right. As I was saying—”

Allison felt it before she understood it.

Not excitement. Not curiosity.

A shift.

It was the same sensation she got when the air pressure changed before a storm, or when a room went quiet too fast after someone said something uncomfortable. She straightened slightly in her chair, pen hovering over her notebook.

Nothing happened.

That, somehow, felt like the problem.

---

Evan Hale arrived in third period.

He stood in the doorway while the teacher finished explaining an assignment, hands tucked loosely into the pockets of a gray hoodie, backpack slung over one shoulder. He wasn’t nervous in the way people expected new students to be—no rigid posture, no forced smile—but there was a carefulness to him, like he was watching where he stepped.

“This is Evan,” the teacher said. “He’ll be joining us for the rest of the semester.”

Evan nodded once. “Hey.”

It wasn’t loud. It wasn’t performative.

Allison watched the room react.

Girls glanced up, then away. Boys assessed him with the quick, instinctive calculus of hierarchy and threat. The teacher gestured toward an empty desk near the back.

“Take a seat wherever.”

Evan’s gaze swept the room—not lingering, not scanning, just *noticing*. It passed over Allison without stopping.

She felt an unexpected flicker of relief.

He sat two rows behind her.

The rest of the class passed uneventfully. No whispered notes. No dramatic interruptions. Evan took out a notebook and wrote when everyone else wrote. He asked one clarifying question that made the teacher pause, then nod appreciatively.

When the bell rang, the room erupted into movement.

Allison packed up methodically, sliding her notebook into her bag. She stood, waited for the aisle to clear, and stepped into the flow of bodies.

She was almost to the door when Evan spoke.

“Hey.”

She turned.

He was looking at her—not intensely, not flirtatiously. Just directly.

“You dropped this,” he said, holding out her pen.

Allison blinked. “Oh. Thanks.”

She took it, fingers brushing his briefly. The contact was unremarkable, but she felt it anyway, a tiny spark of awareness that lingered longer than it should have.

“No problem,” he said. He smiled, quick and genuine. “I’m Evan.”

“Allison.”

He nodded, like he’d expected that. “Nice to meet you.”

“Yeah,” she said, then mentally winced. “You too.”

They stood there for half a second longer than necessary.

“Well,” Evan said lightly. “See you around, I guess.”

“Yeah,” Allison said again.

He moved past her, merging into the hallway traffic.

Allison stood still until someone behind her said, “Excuse me,” and she startled, stepping aside.

As she walked to her next class, she tried to shake the feeling that something had almost happened.

She didn’t know what that something was.

---

By lunch, everyone knew Evan Hale’s name.

He sat at a different table than Allison, surrounded by a loose cluster of people asking the same questions he’d already answered a dozen times.

“Where’d you move from?”

“Why’d you transfer?”

“Is it better here or there?”

Evan answered easily, but without giving much away.

“North of here.”

“Family stuff.”

“Different.”

Allison watched from across the cafeteria, not meaning to.

She noticed things she couldn’t explain why she was noticing.

The way conversations around Evan didn’t quite resolve. The way laughter lingered a second too long, or cut off abruptly. The way people leaned forward when he spoke, like they were waiting for something.

She looked down at her tray.

“Earth to Allison.”

She glanced up. Kara was grinning at her. “You’ve been staring.”

“I have not.”

Kara followed her gaze and smiled. “The new guy?”

Allison felt heat rise to her face. “I was just—he’s new.”

“Uh-huh.”

“He dropped my pen.”

Kara laughed. “A classic meet-cute.”

“It wasn’t like that.”

Kara raised an eyebrow. “You’re cute. He’s cute. That’s usually how it starts.”

Allison shook her head, but she couldn’t stop smiling.

Across the room, Evan laughed at something someone said. The sound carried—warm, unguarded. For a moment, the cafeteria felt louder than usual.

Allison realized she was holding her breath.

She exhaled slowly.

---

The first thing Evan noticed about Alder Creek wasn’t the quiet.

It was the way the quiet *worked*.

He’d been in quiet towns before. Places where silence was enforced by distance or boredom or mutual disinterest. This was different. This was a quiet that moved with people, adjusted itself around them.

Arguments deflated before they peaked. Awkwardness evaporated before it could settle. Teachers lost their tempers less often. Students stopped pushing just short of escalation.

It was subtle enough that most people wouldn’t clock it.

Evan did.

By the end of the day, he was certain of two things:

One: Something was off about this place.  
Two: Whatever it was, it wasn’t happening around one specific girl.

Allison Carter.

He’d noticed her absence before he noticed her presence.

She didn’t interrupt conversations. She didn’t fill silences. She didn’t perform.

She stepped in only when things were about to tip—and then somehow, they didn’t.

Evan watched her move through the hallway between classes, slipping through gaps like she knew where they would open before they did.

When someone bumped into her, she apologized.

When someone apologized to her, she waved it off.

She smiled at the right moments. Laughed softly. Listened more than she spoke.

It wasn’t shyness.

It was calibration.

---

After school, Evan wandered.

He didn’t know where he was going, exactly. He just followed the flow of students until it thinned and split—some heading toward buses, others peeling off into neighborhoods.

Allison took the path toward the creek.

Evan followed at a distance, telling himself it was coincidence.

The footbridge came into view, narrow and weathered. Allison paused in the middle, resting her hands on the railing, staring down at the water.

Evan slowed.

She looked peaceful there, framed by trees and water and the soft hum of insects. Like she belonged in a way that didn’t require effort.

He watched the air around her change.

Not visually. Not dramatically.

Just… settle.

Evan swallowed.

He waited until she turned, startled slightly when she saw him.

“Oh,” she said. “Hi.”

“Hey,” he said. “Sorry. I didn’t mean to sneak up on you.”

“It’s fine,” she said. “I just didn’t hear you.”

“Yeah,” he said. “Me neither.”

They stood there, the creek murmuring below them.

“Do you walk this way every day?” Evan asked.

“Most days.”

“Me too, I think,” he said. “At least now.”

She smiled faintly. “It’s quieter.”

“Yeah,” he said. “That’s one word for it.”

She tilted her head, curious. “You don’t like quiet?”

“I do,” he said carefully. “Just not when it feels… rehearsed.”

Allison frowned. “Rehearsed?”

He shrugged. “Never mind. That sounded weird.”

She hesitated, then said, “People say that about this town sometimes.”

“That it’s rehearsed?”

“That it’s calm,” she said. “Predictable.”

“Is it?”

She thought about it. “Mostly.”

Evan nodded, like that confirmed something for him.

“Well,” he said, stepping back. “I’ll see you tomorrow.”

“Yeah,” Allison said. “See you.”

He walked away without looking back.

Allison stayed on the bridge longer than she meant to.

---

That evening, Emily was in a mood.

She slammed cabinets. She argued with their mother over nothing. She flopped dramatically onto the couch and declared she was bored with her entire life.

Allison watched from the doorway.

“Do you want help with your homework?” she asked.

Emily groaned. “No.”

“Do you want to—”

“No.”

Allison nodded and retreated.

Later, she lay on her bed, phone in hand.

She opened the Notes app.

She stared at the screen.

Then she typed.

> **New Variable**  
>  
> New student.  
> Things don’t smooth around him.  
>  
> That’s probably unrelated.

She paused.

> He notices things.

She deleted that line.

Saved the note.

Set the phone face-down on the bed.

---

The next few days confirmed what Evan already suspected.

Things didn’t end around him.

A disagreement in history class stretched on, unresolved. A group project turned tense and stayed that way. A teacher snapped at him once, then didn’t apologize.

Evan didn’t mind.

What he minded was the way Allison reacted.

She watched him like she was waiting for something to break.

Once, in chemistry, she leaned over and whispered, “Are you okay?”

He blinked. “Yeah. Why?”

“You just seemed—” She stopped. “Never mind.”

“No,” he said gently. “Go ahead.”

She shook her head. “It’s nothing.”

He believed her.

Which didn’t mean he let it go.

---

Friday afternoon, they ended up paired for a lab.

Allison tensed visibly when the teacher called their names.

“You don’t have to—” Evan began.

“It’s fine,” she said quickly. “I just—sometimes things get weird.”

“Weird how?”

She opened her mouth, then closed it. “They don’t. It’s fine.”

He watched her hands as she worked—steady, precise, efficient. She anticipated problems before they arose. Adjusted measurements. Prevented spills.

“You’re really good at this,” he said.

She smiled faintly. “I just pay attention.”

“Yeah,” he said. “I can tell.”

They finished early.

Nothing exploded. Nothing spilled.

The teacher nodded approvingly. “See? That’s how it’s done.”

Allison stiffened.

Evan noticed.

---

After school, Emily didn’t calm down.

She yelled. She cried. She accused Allison of not listening.

Allison stood there, hands clenched, trying not to think.

The house didn’t settle.

The silence didn’t come.

Emily slammed her door and stayed upset.

Allison felt something sharp and unfamiliar twist in her chest.

She went to her room and sat on her bed, shaking.

Her phone buzzed.

A text from Evan.

*Are you okay?*

She stared at it.

*Yeah,* she typed. *Just family stuff.*

*Same,* he replied after a moment. *If you ever want to walk instead of going straight home, I’m usually around.*

Her heart skipped.

*Okay,* she typed.

She didn’t write a note about it.

Not yet.

---

That night, the rain came hard and sudden, drumming against the roof like it had been waiting.

Allison lay awake listening, the sound unfamiliar and loud.

Down the hall, Emily cried herself to sleep.

The house did not quiet.

And for the first time in as long as Allison could remember, that felt… honest.
