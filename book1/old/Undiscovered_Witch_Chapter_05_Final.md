# Chapter 5 — Perimeter

Allison tried not to look for him.

She failed before she even got through the front doors.

It wasn’t deliberate—not in the way she would have accused herself of if she’d had the energy. It was the kind of noticing that happened before thought, like your eyes tracking movement without asking permission. Like your body deciding something mattered and refusing to explain why.

She walked the same path through the hallway she always did, slipping between clusters of people, keeping her face neutral. She kept her gaze mostly forward, occasionally dropping it to her locker combination as if numbers could anchor her.

Rules, she reminded herself.

She’d made them yesterday like they meant something.

Don’t wish.
Don’t want.
Don’t notice.

In the cold light of morning, they felt less like rules and more like a superstition.

Allison slowed as she approached her locker, aware of every inch of space around her in a way that felt almost predatory.

She could feel people passing behind her, close enough that the fabric of their sleeves brushed the air near her arms. Voices overlapped in bursts—inside jokes, complaints, half-finished sentences that didn’t need to include her.

She wondered if people ever noticed how much effort it took just to stand normally.

She rolled her shoulders back, then immediately worried she’d done it too deliberately. She shifted her weight, then worried that looked uncertain. There was no neutral way to exist once she started thinking about it.

Stop, she told herself. You’re fine.

She focused on the dial.

Thirty-six.
Twenty-one.
Back to nine.

The numbers steadied her hands, even as her chest stayed tight.

She told herself she was being ridiculous. That this was just a hallway. Just a morning. Just another day she’d walked this exact path a hundred times before.

The feeling didn’t go away.

She reached her locker and spun the dial with careful precision. The metal was cold under her fingertips. Her hands didn’t shake, which felt like a victory even though it shouldn’t have mattered.

Voices swelled around her. Laughter. The slam of locker doors. A teacher telling someone to take their hood down. Normal, normal, normal.

Allison’s brain tried to relax into it.

Then she felt it—the shift.

Not sound, not movement. Something subtler. Like a new weight in the air, like the space behind her had changed density.

She didn’t turn.

She kept her eyes on her books, sliding one out, swapping it for another, moving with exaggerated calm.

“Hey.”

The word landed close enough that she felt it in her ribs.

Allison’s fingers froze on the edge of her binder.

She knew that voice already. Calm. Measured. Not loud enough to draw attention, but placed like it was meant to be heard.

She forced herself to breathe in and out before she turned.

Evan stood a few feet away, not crowding her space, not leaning. Just… present. His backpack was slung over one shoulder. His expression was neutral in a way that didn’t feel blank. It felt attentive.

Allison’s pulse ticked up.

“Hi,” she managed.

Evan glanced at her locker, then back to her face. “Is it always this loud in here?”

Allison blinked. “It’s… school.”

A flicker of something crossed his expression. Almost a smile. Not quite.

“Right,” he said. “I’m still adjusting.”

Allison nodded like that was a normal thing to say. Like this was a normal conversation she was built for.

She wasn’t.

“Are you—” Evan paused, eyes shifting briefly past her shoulder as a group of girls walked by, laughing. When he looked back, his gaze landed on her with the same quiet precision as before. “Are you okay?”

For half a second, Allison almost told the truth.

Not the whole truth. Just something small and manageable. Something like I’m tired or I didn’t sleep well or school’s just been weird lately.

The words stacked up behind her teeth, pressing forward.

Then she imagined Evan responding.

Imagined concern, or worse—understanding.

Her chest tightened sharply.

“I’m fine,” she said instead.

The relief she felt was immediate and sour.

Evan didn’t look disappointed.

That somehow made her chest ache.

She watched his face for signs—confusion, judgment, boredom—and found none of them. Just attention, steady and unhurried.

Like he wasn’t waiting for her to be different.

Like he was waiting for her to be honest.

The thought scared her enough that she forced her gaze down, pretending to check the books in her bag.

Evan didn’t push. He didn’t fill the silence.

He nodded once, like he was filing her answer away somewhere.

Allison’s hands tightened around her binder. She forced her grip to loosen.

“Sorry,” she added. “I’m just… tired.”

Evan’s gaze dropped to her hands for half a second.

Then he looked up again, and his voice stayed calm. “Yeah. You seem tired.”

Allison felt heat rise in her face.

Seen.

Her mind started throwing rules at her faster than she could catch them.

Don’t react.
Don’t say something weird.
Don’t think about how he said that.
Don’t wish he’d stop looking at you.

The last one slipped through like a splinter.

Allison swallowed hard.

Evan shifted his weight slightly, glancing down the hallway as if checking time. “Anyway,” he said, like he was giving her an exit. “I just… wanted to say hi. I don’t know anyone yet.”

Allison’s chest tightened.

She knew what it felt like not to know anyone. To exist on the edges of rooms, pretending you weren’t alone.

But Evan didn’t feel like the kind of person who got stuck on the edges.

That thought made her suspicious. Of him. Of herself.

“Yeah,” she said, then realized she hadn’t actually answered him. “Hi.”

Evan’s mouth twitched. “Okay.”

Then, before she could decide whether to speak again, he nodded once and moved on, slipping into the flow of students like he belonged there.

Allison watched him go.

She hated herself for watching.

She hated that part of her was already replaying it.

When she finally looked away, Kara was standing a few lockers down with her eyebrows raised.

“Oh my god,” Kara said, grinning. “He talked to you.”

Allison’s stomach dropped. “No.”

Kara laughed. “Yes. I literally watched it happen.”

Allison turned back to her locker, pretending to be busy. “He just said hi.”

Kara leaned closer. “He said hi to you.”

“I wanted to run away,” Allison muttered.

Kara snorted. “Sure you did. You’re blushing.”

“I’m not.”

“You are.”

Allison shut her locker harder than she meant to. The sound echoed. She flinched.

Kara’s grin softened. “Okay, okay. I’m teasing. But seriously—what was that about?”

Allison forced a shrug. “Nothing.”

Kara studied her for a beat. “You’ve been weird today.”

Allison’s stomach tightened.

“I’m just tired,” she said.

Kara chewed on her straw. “You keep saying that.”

Allison smiled. “It’s been a week.”

Kara accepted the answer, but something about her expression shifted—not suspicion. Distance.

The guilt hit harder than Allison expected.

She wasn’t lying.

She just wasn’t telling the truth either.

Allison spent the rest of the morning struggling to focus.

In English, she stared at the same paragraph without absorbing a single word. Her foot bounced under the desk until she forced it still.

She kept expecting something to happen.

That was the worst part—the waiting.

When the teacher began calling on students to read aloud, dread rose automatically.

She clamped down on the familiar wish.

No wishing.

The names moved closer.

Then skipped her.

Just like that.

Relief hit first.

Then fear.

She was sure she hadn’t wished.

By lunch, Allison felt hollowed out by vigilance.

Kara talked like nothing had changed—teachers, quizzes, someone’s boyfriend drama. Allison smiled when expected, laughed once when appropriate.

Across the cafeteria, Evan sat alone again.

Not lonely.

Unclaimed.

It didn’t make her relax.

After school, Allison didn’t wait around.

She left with her head down and her pace just fast enough that no one could accuse her of lingering.

She crossed the street and turned onto the narrower side road that led away from the school.

She didn’t look back.

She knew better than to look back.

The late afternoon light flattened everything into muted blues and grays. Houses blurred past. Her feet fell into the rhythm she usually relied on.

It didn’t work today.

She turned down the side street that led toward the creek path, pavement cracking into dirt, weeds pressing in.

The sound of traffic faded.

She felt it again—that subtle shift.

“Allison?”

Her name stopped her cold.

She turned slowly, heart slamming.

Evan stood several yards back, hands lifted slightly. “Sorry,” he said quickly. “I didn’t mean to scare you.”

“It’s fine,” she said automatically.

“You go this way a lot?” he asked.

“Sometimes.”

“I’m still figuring out how to get home,” he said. “This town’s smaller than I expected.”

“Yeah,” she said. “That happens.”

“You’re from here, right?”

“Yeah. Born somewhere else. I don’t remember it.”

“Do you like it?”

She hesitated. “It’s just… the kind of place where people already think they know you.”

Evan nodded. “Even if they don’t?”

She looked at him, surprised.

“Where’d you move from?” she asked.

“Michigan,” he said. “My dad got transferred. We’ve moved before, but this one feels different.”

“How?”

“Quieter,” he said. “Like things are happening under the surface and no one talks about them.”

Her fingers curled into her sleeves.

They walked together as the path narrowed.

“You seem like you notice stuff,” Evan said.

“I don’t,” she said too quickly.

He smiled faintly. “You do. You just don’t draw attention to it.”

She stared at the ground. “I try not to.”

“Why?”

“I don’t know,” she said. “It’s easier.”

“I guess I notice people,” Evan said after a moment. “Maybe more than I should.”

She swallowed.

They reached the clearing.

“This is where I usually sit,” she said.

“I get why,” Evan said quietly.

“It’s just a place.”

“Yeah,” he said. “But some places are different.”

She hugged her arms around herself. “I should head home.”

“Yeah. Of course.”

They walked back together.

“Thanks,” Evan said. “For letting me walk with you.”

She nodded. “Yeah.”

“I’m glad I ran into you,” he added.

Her stomach flipped.

“Me too,” she said.

He smiled once. “See you tomorrow?”

“Yeah.”

He turned away.

Allison stayed until he disappeared, then sat down hard at the edge of the path, the creek suddenly too close.

Not hers anymore.
