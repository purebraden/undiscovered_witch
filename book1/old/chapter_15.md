# Chapter 15 — The Shape of Choosing

Nothing happened.

That was what unsettled Allison most.

The days kept arriving in their usual order. Morning followed night. School followed morning. Homework followed dinner. The world didn’t pause or stutter or acknowledge that anything had gone wrong. It just… continued.

Kara’s absence had stopped being sharp.

It had smoothed into routine.

Allison noticed it in the way teachers no longer hesitated during attendance, the way classmates stopped lowering their voices when she walked past. Even Emily complained about homework again, like she used to, instead of circling silence with careful words.

Normal was returning.

And with it came a different kind of weight.

It wasn’t the tight, urgent feeling she’d learned to fear. It was heavier than that. Quieter. Like something settling into place and daring her to pretend she didn’t feel it.

One morning, Allison sat on the edge of her bed with her backpack at her feet and realized something that made her stomach twist.

The world had stopped waiting for her to act.

It had started assuming she wouldn’t.

At school, she drifted between classes without really engaging. The hallways felt wider again, less brittle. People laughed. Someone dropped a stack of papers and swore. A substitute teacher asked for directions.

Life, insisting on itself.

Allison watched it like she was slightly out of sync, half a step behind everything else.

In biology, the group at the next table started arguing. Not yelling. Not cruel. Just the low, grinding friction of people talking past each other.

Normally, she would have felt it immediately—the tightening behind her eyes, the sense that the moment was leaning toward her, asking for something.

She felt that now.

Faint. Waiting.

Allison froze.

The argument sharpened. One voice went flat. Another snapped back. The teacher glanced over.

Allison’s breath caught.

She could make this stop.

Not fix it. Not solve it. Just… dull the edge. Let it pass without becoming something heavier.

Her attention brushed the moment, and the familiar pressure stirred.

Allison pulled back hard.

She forced herself to look down at her notebook, to trace the lines of a diagram she didn’t care about, to stay where she was instead of leaning into the feeling.

The voices lowered on their own.

The argument fizzled out, awkward and incomplete.

Allison’s hands were shaking.

She pressed them flat against the desk until they stopped.

That was new.

Not the feeling.

The choice.

Emily caught up with her after school, breathless and irritated.

“You’ve been weird,” she said, not unkindly.

Allison blinked. “I’ve always been weird.”

Emily snorted. “Different weird. You’re like… here, but not.”

Allison frowned. “I’m trying not to mess things up.”

Emily stopped walking.

“That’s not the same as doing nothing,” she said carefully.

Allison looked away. “It feels like it is.”

Emily studied her face for a long moment. “When you stop reacting,” she said quietly, “it kind of feels like you’re disappearing.”

The words landed harder than Emily probably meant them to.

“I’m still here,” Allison said, too fast.

“I know,” Emily said. “I just… want you to know that too.”

They walked the rest of the way without speaking.

Allison didn’t go to the creek.

She went to the field.

The bleachers were empty, metal cold under her palms as she sat. The grass bent in the wind without caring which direction it leaned.

Evan arrived a few minutes later, like he’d expected her.

He sat a few rows down, close enough to talk without crowding.

“You’ve been holding yourself very still,” he said after a while.

Allison huffed a breath. “Is it that obvious?”

“To someone who notices patterns,” Evan said. “Yeah.”

She stared out at the field. “I’m scared that if I do anything, I’ll make it worse.”

Evan nodded. “That’s possible.”

Allison winced.

“But doing nothing isn’t neutral,” he added.

“I know,” she said.

Evan tilted his head. “What do you think you’re actually responsible for?”

Allison swallowed. “I don’t know.”

“Then maybe that’s the wrong question,” he said.

She turned to look at him. “What’s the right one?”

“What are you willing to be responsible for?”

The question sat between them.

Allison felt the familiar pressure stir—not demanding, not sharp. Just present.

She thought about Kara. About the way fixing had started to feel like love and ended up feeling like control.

“I won’t push on people,” Allison said slowly. “I won’t make things stop just because I want them to.”

Evan nodded, like that mattered.

“I won’t decide for them,” she continued. “Even if I think I know better.”

He watched her carefully.

“But I won’t disappear either,” Allison added. “I won’t pretend I don’t see things.”

“That sounds survivable,” Evan said.

Allison let out a shaky breath. “That’s a low bar.”

“Low bars keep you from falling,” he said.

That night, Allison opened her notebook.

Not the one for school.

The other one.

She didn’t write about Kara.

She didn’t write about what she was afraid of.

She wrote a list.

Things I won’t do.  
Things I might.  
Things I need to think about more.

The list was messy. Incomplete. Full of crossed-out lines and half-thoughts.

It felt real.

When she closed the notebook, the pressure was still there.

But it wasn’t steering her.

She was.

For the first time since everything had broken without collapsing, Allison felt something take shape inside her.

Not certainty.

Direction.

And that was enough to keep moving.
