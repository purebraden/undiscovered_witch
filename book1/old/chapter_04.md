# CHAPTER 4 — Normal Enough

Allison’s guidance counselor’s office smelled like lemon cleaner and old paper.

The room had been redecorated recently—new posters about college readiness taped over faded corkboards, a small plant on the windowsill that looked like it had survived several near-death experiences. The chair Allison sat in was just slightly too low, her feet not quite flat on the floor.

She folded her hands in her lap and waited.

Ms. Raymond skimmed a folder on her desk, nodding to herself. “Allison Carter,” she said. “Senior year. Can you believe it?”

Allison smiled politely. “Not really.”

Ms. Raymond smiled back. “I say that to everyone. It’s supposed to be comforting.”

Allison nodded, though she wasn’t sure what exactly she was supposed to feel comforted about.

“Your grades are excellent,” Ms. Raymond continued. “Strong test scores. Teachers speak very highly of you.”

“That’s good,” Allison said.

“It is,” Ms. Raymond agreed. “You’re doing very well.”

There was a pause.

Allison waited for the rest of it—the concern, the warning, the suggestion that she push herself harder or aim higher. It didn’t come.

Ms. Raymond set the folder down. “So. Have you thought about what you want to do next year?”

Allison hesitated. “I have some ideas.”

“Such as?”

“I applied to a few schools,” Allison said. “Nothing too far away.”

Ms. Raymond nodded approvingly. “Staying close to home can be a great choice. Support systems matter.”

“They do,” Allison agreed.

Another pause.

Ms. Raymond leaned back slightly in her chair. “You don’t seem stressed.”

Allison blinked. “I’m not.”

“That’s good,” Ms. Raymond said again, like she was reassuring herself. “A lot of seniors come in here overwhelmed.”

“I’m okay,” Allison said. She meant it, mostly.

Ms. Raymond studied her for a moment longer than necessary. “You always are,” she said, not unkindly. “You’ve always been very… steady.”

Allison felt a familiar tightening in her chest.

“Thank you,” she said.

Ms. Raymond smiled, satisfied. “Well. Keep doing what you’re doing.”

The meeting ended like that. No action items. No follow-up. No pressure.

As Allison left the office, she felt oddly hollow.

---

She saw Evan in the hallway outside, leaning against a locker with his phone in his hand. He looked up when he saw her.

“Hey,” he said. “How’d it go?”

“All right,” Allison said.

“That sounded convincing.”

She shrugged. “It was fine.”

He pushed off the locker and fell into step beside her as they walked. “What did they say?”

“That I’m doing well.”

“That’s it?”

“That’s it.”

He frowned slightly. “Did they ask what you want?”

She hesitated. “Kind of.”

“And?”

“And I said I had ideas.”

“And that satisfied them?”

“Yes.”

Evan shook his head. “That’s… weird.”

Allison glanced at him. “Why?”

“Because most adults I know don’t stop at ‘ideas.’”

She smiled faintly. “Maybe they trust me.”

“Or maybe they’re assuming,” he said gently.

She didn’t respond to that.

They walked in silence for a few steps.

“Do you want more?” Evan asked suddenly.

Allison startled. “More what?”

“Anything,” he said. “Than ‘doing well.’”

She opened her mouth, then closed it again. “I don’t know.”

“That wasn’t a trick question,” he said.

“I know.”

They reached the stairwell. Evan paused. “You don’t have to answer.”

“I know,” she said again.

But the question stayed with her.

---

At lunch, Kara leaned across the table. “So? Guidance counselor?”

“She said I’m fine,” Allison said.

“Of course she did,” Kara said. “You’re the least concerning person in this school.”

Allison laughed, though something about the phrasing made her uncomfortable.

“Is that bad?” Kara asked, noticing.

“No,” Allison said quickly. “It’s good.”

“Exactly,” Kara said. “Who wants to be concerning?”

Allison nodded. She didn’t trust herself to speak.

Across the cafeteria, Evan sat alone today, flipping through his phone. Allison felt a pull toward him she didn’t quite understand—not romantic, not exactly. More like curiosity mixed with recognition.

She caught his eye. He smiled and raised his eyebrows, a silent question.

She smiled back.

---

After school, she walked home with Emily.

Emily kicked at the edges of the sidewalk, scowling. “I hate school.”

“What happened?” Allison asked.

“Nothing,” Emily said. Then, louder: “Everything.”

Allison waited.

“They changed my group for science,” Emily said. “And Mrs. Palmer says I have to ‘work on my attitude.’”

Allison smiled gently. “You do have an attitude.”

Emily shot her a look. “So do you. Yours is just quiet.”

The words landed harder than Emily probably meant them to.

Allison didn’t respond.

At home, Emily slammed her backpack down and went straight to her room.

Their mother sighed. “Middle school,” she said. “She’ll grow out of it.”

Allison nodded.

---

Later, she stood in the doorway of Emily’s room. Emily lay sprawled on her bed, headphones on, staring at the ceiling.

“You okay?” Allison asked.

Emily shrugged.

Allison stepped inside. “Do you want to talk?”

Emily pulled one headphone off. “You always ask that.”

“I know.”

“And then you fix it,” Emily said. “Or it just… stops.”

Allison froze. “What do you mean?”

Emily sat up. “I don’t know. I’ll be mad and then suddenly I’m not. And everyone’s like, ‘See? You’re fine.’”

Allison’s throat tightened. “That’s a good thing.”

“Is it?” Emily asked.

Allison didn’t answer.

Emily sighed and lay back down. “I kind of miss being mad.”

Allison left the room quietly.

---

That night, Allison lay in bed staring at the ceiling crack.

She opened her Notes app.

> **Guidance**  
>  
> They say I’m doing well.  
> No one asked what I want to do if I don’t keep doing well.

She stared at the words.

> Maybe this is enough.

She deleted that line.

Saved the note.

---

The next day, Evan caught up to her at her locker.

“Hey,” he said. “Walk with me?”

“Where?”

“Anywhere.”

She hesitated, then nodded.

They walked without destination, drifting toward the creek without quite deciding to.

“I talked to my counselor yesterday,” Evan said.

“Oh,” Allison said. “How was it?”

“They asked what I wanted to do. Then they asked why. Then they asked if I was sure.”

Allison winced. “That sounds exhausting.”

“It was,” he said. “But also… clarifying.”

She glanced at him. “How?”

“I realized I didn’t have a good answer,” he said. “Which felt important.”

She smiled faintly. “Important how?”

“Like maybe that’s where you start,” he said. “With the not knowing.”

They reached the footbridge. Allison stopped.

“I don’t think I’m good at that,” she said.

“At what?”

“Not knowing,” she said. “I’m good at… staying balanced.”

Evan leaned against the railing. “Balanced how?”

She searched for words. “Like… not tipping things too far. Not making trouble.”

“Trouble for who?”

She shrugged. “Anyone.”

He studied her. “That sounds exhausting.”

She laughed softly. “It’s not.”

He didn’t argue. “You sure?”

She didn’t answer.

The creek flowed beneath them, louder today, the water churning slightly from the rain.

“Can I ask you something?” Evan said.

“Okay.”

“Do you ever feel like things end around you?” he asked.

Allison’s breath caught. “What?”

“Not in a bad way,” he said quickly. “Just… conversations. Arguments. Moments. Like they stop short.”

She stared at him. “Why would you think that?”

He shrugged. “I notice stuff.”

“So do I,” she said defensively.

“I know,” he said gently. “That’s kind of my point.”

She felt exposed in a way she didn’t like.

“I should go,” she said.

“Okay,” he said immediately. “I didn’t mean to push.”

“I know,” she said. She hesitated. “You didn’t.”

She walked away before she could say something she couldn’t take back.

---

That evening, the house felt tight.

Emily sulked. Their parents argued quietly about bills. Allison moved between rooms like a ghost, smoothing where she could, staying out of the way where she couldn’t.

She caught herself halfway through opening her mouth to intervene and stopped.

The argument continued.

It was uncomfortable. Sharp. Real.

Eventually, it resolved—not neatly, but honestly.

Allison sat on the stairs, heart racing.

No one thanked her.

No one looked at her.

She wasn’t sure how she felt about that.

---

Later, in her room, she opened the Notes app again.

> **Question**  
>  
> If no one needs me to fix things,  
> what am I for?

She stared at the words until her vision blurred.

She didn’t delete them.

She turned the phone face-down and lay back, listening to the house—still noisy, still imperfect, still standing.

And for the first time, she didn’t wish it quiet.
