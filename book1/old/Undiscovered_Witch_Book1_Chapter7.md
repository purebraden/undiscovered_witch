## Chapter 7 — The Cost of Staying Close

The bell rang like nothing had happened.

That was the part Allison hated most—the way sound itself refused to acknowledge consequence. The same sharp tone cut through the hallway, and everyone obeyed it automatically, bodies shifting, backpacks hoisted, doors opening in practiced rhythm.

Order without understanding.

Allison slipped into her seat just before the teacher started talking. She didn’t hear the first few sentences. Her attention snagged on movement instead—Rachel sliding into the desk two rows over, shoulders hunched, eyes down. A hood was pulled up despite the school’s no-hoods rule, the fabric shadowing her face.

No one stopped her.

That, too, felt new.

Yesterday, a hood like that would have drawn whispers. A reminder. A joke. Someone enforcing a rule just to see her flinch.

Now there was a pocket of quiet around her desk, like the air itself had decided to give her space.

Allison didn’t know if that was better.

She felt Kara before she saw her—an awareness like pressure against her side. Kara dropped into the chair next to her with a practiced thump, knee bumping Allison’s under the desk.

“You alive?” Kara murmured, eyes forward.

“All evidence suggests yes,” Allison whispered back.

Kara snorted, then caught herself and coughed into her hand when the teacher glanced their way.

For a few minutes, class proceeded normally. Notes on the board. A worksheet passed down the rows. Someone’s phone vibrating faintly before being silenced.

Allison tried to focus. She really did.

But her attention kept drifting—not outward, but between things. Between Rachel’s rigid posture and the careful neutrality of the students around her. Between Kara’s easy confidence and the tight way her fingers tapped against her desk, a tell she didn’t usually bother to hide.

Staying close, Allison realized, meant noticing too much.

The teacher turned back to the board.

Kara leaned in. “So. Lunch.”

Allison stiffened. “What about it?”

Kara glanced toward Rachel without turning her head. “Ms. Telford asked if I’d… you know. Sit with her.”

Allison’s pulse jumped. “You don’t have to.”

Kara’s mouth twitched. “I know. But I’m not going to just pretend she doesn’t exist.”

“I didn’t think you would,” Allison said quickly.

Kara studied her for a second. “You okay with it?”

The question landed heavier than it should have.

Allison hesitated, then nodded. “Yeah. Of course.”

Kara’s eyes lingered, searching. “You sure?”

Allison forced herself to meet her gaze. “I’m sure.”

Kara leaned back, satisfied—for now. “Good. Because if anyone gives her trouble, I’m not in the mood to be subtle.”

Allison’s chest tightened at the casual promise of protection.

---

Lunch was louder than it had any right to be.

Trays clattered. Chairs scraped. Conversations overlapped until they became noise instead of words. The cafeteria smelled like fries and disinfectant, comfort food and obligation.

Allison scanned the room automatically, eyes mapping familiar faces, exit routes, places she could disappear if she needed to.

Rachel hovered near the drink machines, clutching her tray like it might slip away.

Kara spotted her at the same time.

“Hey,” Kara called. “Over here.”

Rachel froze.

Then she took a breath and walked over.

“Hi,” Rachel said softly.

“Hi,” Kara replied. She pulled out a chair with her foot. “Sit.”

Rachel did.

Allison slid into the seat across from them, aware of how deliberate the arrangement felt.

---

They walked together after lunch.

Staying close felt like the right thing to do.

But right things, she was learning, weren’t free.

And the closer she stayed, the harder it would be to tell where her influence ended—and where other people’s choices began.
