## Chapter 6 — Fracture Without Fallout

The worst part was that everything still looked normal.

The fluorescent lights still buzzed. The lockers still slammed. Teachers still asked for homework like homework mattered more than the way a room could tilt without anyone noticing.

Allison had started to understand that “normal” didn’t mean safe.

It just meant everyone agreed to keep moving.

She stood at her bathroom sink a little longer than necessary, watching her own reflection like it might flinch first. The mirror offered her the same face it always had—sleep-softened, pale under the overhead light, hair pulled back with the same elastic she always used.

Nothing about her looked like someone who had been *wrong* yesterday.

Nothing about her looked like someone who could bend a day sideways with a thought she didn’t even admit to having.

She rinsed, wiped her hands, and stared at the notes app open on her phone.

A blank line waited under yesterday’s entry like the page expected her to keep confessing.

Allison didn’t type.

She locked her phone instead.

It was easier to pretend she was making rules. It was harder to admit she was keeping records.

Downstairs, the house sounded like it always did—Emily arguing with the toaster, the dog’s nails clicking across the kitchen tile, her dad’s muffled cough behind a newspaper. Her mom’s voice floated up from the coffee maker like a steady hum.

Allison moved through it all like she was trying not to disturb the air.

“Morning,” her mom said when Allison entered.

“Morning,” Allison echoed, and forced herself to take a bowl from the cabinet like this was a day that belonged to her.

Emily looked up from where she was leaning against the counter in her hoodie, phone in one hand, toast in the other.

“You look… normal,” Emily said, squinting like she was trying to decide if Allison was sick.

Allison’s stomach tightened. “Thanks?”

Emily shrugged. “Just saying. You were weird yesterday.”

Allison’s spoon paused halfway to the cereal box.

Emily waved the toast. “Not like *bad* weird. Just—quiet weird. Like you were listening to something I couldn’t hear.”

Allison forced a laugh that sounded like it came from someone else. “I was tired.”

Emily accepted that explanation with the casual cruelty of a younger sibling. “Sure.”

Allison ate because everyone else ate. She answered when spoken to. She nodded at the right moments. She let the minutes pass like stepping stones across a river she didn’t want to look down into.

When the bus arrived, she stood at the curb with her backpack straps gripped too tight, watching the street like she expected it to change direction.

It didn’t.

She climbed on. Found her usual seat. Stared out the window.

The world stayed obedient.

It was the kind of obedience that made her uneasy.

---

School had edges now.

Not visible ones. Not anything anyone else would name.

But Allison felt them—tiny borders around her choices, lines around her attention. Like the air itself had started drawing boundaries.

Don’t wish.  
Don’t want.  
Don’t notice.

She said the rules in her head as she walked down the hallway, as if repetition could turn them into protection instead of superstition.

She spotted Kara before Kara spotted her.

Kara was near the trophy case, half turned toward the wall like she’d gotten stuck mid-step. Her shoulders were lifted too high. One hand pressed flat against her sternum like she was trying to hold herself together with her palm.

Allison slowed.

Kara didn’t look like herself.

Normally Kara moved through space like she owned it—chin up, eyes sharp, mouth ready with a comment that made people laugh even when they didn’t want to. Kara’s confidence was a kind of armor. It didn’t make her invincible, but it made her loud.

This Kara was quiet in a way that felt wrong.

Allison approached carefully, like a skittish animal might bolt if she moved too fast.

“Kara?”

Kara’s head snapped up. Her eyes were glassy, wide, unfocused for half a second before they locked onto Allison like a rescue line.

“Oh—thank God.” Kara laughed, but the sound cracked. “Okay. Okay. I’m fine.”

Allison knew that tone.

It was the same tone she used at home when Emily asked if she was okay.

Allison stepped closer. “What’s going on?”

Kara shook her head quickly. “Nothing. Just—nothing. It’s stupid.”

“Kara,” Allison said again, softer.

Kara swallowed. Her throat bobbed like she had to force the motion.

“They pulled me out of first period,” Kara said, words tumbling now that they’d started. “Like—some lady from the office came and was like, ‘We need to talk to you.’ Like I’m in trouble, Allison. Like I did something.”

Allison’s stomach tightened. “Did you?”

Kara stared at her like Allison had insulted her. “No. *No.* That’s the point. They said it was ‘concerns.’ Concerns about what? Existing? Having a personality?”

Kara’s voice dropped.

“Someone reported me.”

Allison blinked. “Reported you for what?”

Kara’s mouth twisted. “For being ‘mean.’”

Allison’s eyebrows lifted before she could stop them.

Kara saw it and flinched like she’d been slapped. “Don’t. Don’t do that face.”

Kara’s hands were shaking now. She clasped them together hard.

“They said someone told them I’ve been ‘targeting’ Rachel,” Kara said. “Like I’m stalking her or something. Like I’m the one who made everyone film her.”

Allison felt cold move through her chest.

Rachel.
