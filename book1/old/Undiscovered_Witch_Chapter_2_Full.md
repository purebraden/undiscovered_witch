# CHAPTER 2 — Patterns

Allison didn’t think of herself as superstitious.

She didn’t knock on wood or avoid cracks in sidewalks. She didn’t have lucky numbers or rituals she followed before tests. She believed in preparation, in showing up early, in not tempting things by wanting too much. That felt different. That felt reasonable.

Still, when she woke up the next morning and saw that it was 6:01 a.m., she lay very still and waited for something to happen.

Nothing did.

The house stayed quiet. The ceiling crack stayed exactly where it had been the night before. Her phone buzzed once with a notification—an automated reminder about a library book due next week—and then went silent again.

Allison exhaled, embarrassed at herself, and got out of bed.

---

Breakfast unfolded the same way it always did.

Her mother stood at the counter with coffee. Her father read the news on his phone. Emily complained loudly about having to wear jeans instead of leggings because it was “too cold for this nonsense.”

Allison listened, responded when spoken to, passed things across the table.

“Don’t forget you have that meeting with the guidance counselor today,” her mother said to Allison as she rinsed a mug.

“I know.”

“You don’t seem nervous,” her father observed mildly.

“I’m not,” Allison said. Which was mostly true.

Emily snorted. “That’s because she’s already perfect.”

“I am not,” Allison said, reflexively.

Emily grinned. “You kind of are.”

Their parents laughed, and Allison felt the familiar, complicated mix of warmth and distance settle into place.

---

At school, the hallway noise swelled and receded in predictable waves. Allison moved through it easily, slipping into gaps without thinking. People rarely bumped into her. Teachers rarely corrected her. She existed just enough to be acknowledged and not enough to be managed.

In English, the teacher handed back essays. Allison’s had a neat A written in red ink at the top, with a comment beneath it she’d seen variations of her whole life.

*Thoughtful. Clear. Well-balanced.*

She stared at the word *balanced* for a moment longer than necessary.

---

During third period, the announcements crackled over the intercom. A pep rally scheduled for Friday was postponed. The reason wasn’t given. No one seemed particularly disappointed.

“Probably for the best,” someone behind her muttered. “Those things are always chaotic.”

Allison nodded along with the sentiment without being asked.

---

At lunch, Kara slid into the seat across from her without hesitation.

“Thanks for the homework,” she said, unwrapping her sandwich. “You saved me.”

“No problem,” Allison said.

Kara smiled again—that same lingering smile from the day before—and then launched into a story about her weekend. Allison listened, chiming in at appropriate intervals, careful not to steer the conversation too much in any direction.

When the bell rang, Kara hesitated. “Hey,” she said. “Do you want to walk to class together?”

“Sure,” Allison said, surprised at how easy the word felt.

They walked side by side down the hallway, their pace matching without effort.

---

By the end of the day, Allison felt tired in a way that didn’t quite match what she’d done. Not exhausted—just… compressed. Like she’d been holding herself in all day, careful not to spill.

She skipped the creek on the walk home and went straight to the library instead.

The building was nearly empty, as usual. The librarian—Mrs. Halloway—looked up from her desk when Allison entered and smiled.

“Afternoon, Allison.”

“Hi.”

“New books came in this morning,” Mrs. Halloway said. “You might like some of them.”

“All right,” Allison said. “Thanks.”

She wandered the stacks without urgency, fingers trailing along spines. She picked a book at random, then another, then put them both back. Eventually, she settled at one of the long wooden tables near the windows and pulled out her phone.

She opened the Notes app.

She stared at yesterday’s entry.

> **Probably Nothing**  
>  
> Locker opened first try.  
> Quiz didn’t happen.  
> Kara smiled.  
>  
> Could be coincidence.

She read it twice, then added a new note.

> **Follow-Up**  
>  
> Kara texted me first.  
> Walked with me today.  
>  
> People change. That happens.

She saved it quickly, like she was worried someone might see.

Mrs. Halloway walked past a few minutes later, pushing a cart of books. “You doing all right, dear?” she asked, not stopping.

“Yes,” Allison said. “Just studying.”

Mrs. Halloway nodded, satisfied, and moved on.

Allison didn’t study.

---

The rest of the week unfolded with the same subtle skew.

The rain forecast kept being pushed back. A test in math was delayed because the teacher misplaced her answer key. Emily’s practice was canceled twice for reasons that were never fully explained.

At dinner, her parents commented on how calm things had been lately.

“Feels like one of those weeks where everything just works,” her father said.

Allison felt a flicker of something at that—something like pride, quickly swallowed.

That night, she added another note.

> **Weather**  
>  
> Still hasn’t rained.  
> Forecast keeps changing.  
>  
> Not unusual for fall.

She paused, then added a line she immediately regretted.

> I kind of like it.

She deleted that line before saving.

---

On Thursday, something went wrong.

It wasn’t dramatic. That was the worst part.

Emily came home from school in tears, backpack thrown carelessly onto the floor. She slammed her bedroom door hard enough to rattle the picture frames in the hallway.

Their mother looked up from the sink, startled. “Emily?”

“I hate her,” Emily shouted from behind the door. “She’s ruining everything.”

Allison froze halfway down the hall.

“Who?” their mother asked.

“Everyone!” Emily screamed. “No one listens to me.”

Their father appeared behind Allison, concern etched into his face. “What happened?”

“I don’t know,” Allison said, though she could guess. Middle school friendships were a revolving door of loyalty and betrayal.

Emily’s crying escalated, sharp and panicked.

Allison felt the familiar urge rise up inside her—the need to *fix*. To step in and smooth the edges before they cut too deeply.

She hesitated.

She thought of the Notes app. Of patterns. Of coincidence.

She stood very still and tried not to think of anything at all.

The crying continued.

Their mother knocked on Emily’s door, voice softening. “Honey, open the door.”

“No!”

Allison’s chest tightened. She took a step forward before she could stop herself.

“It’s okay,” she said, quietly.

The house seemed to lean toward her.

Emily’s sobs hitched, then slowed.

Allison didn’t touch the door. She didn’t say anything else.

After a moment, the door creaked open.

Emily looked at her, eyes red, breath shaky. “She said I was embarrassing,” she whispered.

Allison swallowed. “That’s not true.”

Emily sniffed. “You don’t know that.”

“I do,” Allison said. She didn’t know how she knew. She just did.

Emily’s shoulders sagged. The anger drained out of her like air from a balloon.

Their mother exhaled in relief. “There,” she said softly. “See? Everything’s okay.”

Emily nodded, already calmer. “I’m going to my room.”

The door closed gently this time.

Allison stood in the hallway, heart racing.

“That was good,” her father said. “You always know what to say.”

Allison nodded, the words echoing uncomfortably in her ears.

---

That night, she didn’t want to open her Notes app.

She did anyway.

> **Emily**  
>  
> She was really upset.  
> I didn’t do anything.  
>  
> It still stopped.

Her fingers hovered over the screen.

> That happens sometimes.

She deleted the last line before saving.

---

Friday arrived with a sky so clear it felt deliberate.

The pep rally was rescheduled after all. The gym buzzed with energy that felt slightly out of place, like a party thrown on the wrong day. Allison sat in the bleachers with Kara and the others, clapping when appropriate, laughing when cued.

At one point, the principal tapped the microphone.

“Let’s all remember to keep things respectful and safe,” he said. “We want this to be fun for everyone.”

The crowd quieted almost instantly.

Allison felt a strange, weightless sensation in her chest.

*This is nice,* she thought, and immediately wished she hadn’t.

The rally passed without incident. No fights. No disruptions. No mess.

People commented on it all afternoon.

“That was actually enjoyable,” someone said.

“Best one we’ve had in years,” another agreed.

Allison walked home feeling hollow.

---

She stopped at the creek.

The water was higher than usual, despite the lack of rain, flowing smooth and steady. Allison crouched at the edge and dipped her fingers in. The water was cold, grounding.

She pulled out her phone.

> **New Rule**  
>  
> Don’t think about things being nice.  
>  
> Don’t think about wanting calm.  
>  
> Just let things happen.

She stared at the words.

She didn’t believe them.

---

That evening, Kara texted again.

*Are you okay? You were quiet today.*

Allison typed and erased three different responses before settling on something neutral.

*Just tired. Long week.*

*Same,* Kara replied. *We should hang out this weekend.*

Allison’s chest tightened—not with fear this time, but with something dangerously close to hope.

She didn’t write a note about it.

Not yet.

---

The rain finally came overnight.

Allison woke to the sound of it tapping softly against her window, steady and unthreatening. She lay in bed listening, feeling oddly disappointed.

Downstairs, her mother commented on it over breakfast.

“About time,” she said. “The ground needed it.”

Allison nodded.

At school, nothing unusual happened. That, somehow, felt worse.

At lunch, she checked her Notes app without meaning to.

She added one last entry before closing it.

> **Pattern**  
>  
> Things are easier when I don’t want much.  
>  
> That’s probably normal.

She locked her phone and slid it back into her pocket.

Outside, the rain slowed to a drizzle, then stopped entirely by the time the final bell rang.
