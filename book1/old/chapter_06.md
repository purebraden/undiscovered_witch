# CHAPTER 6 — What Lingers

By Tuesday morning, the town had decided nothing happened.

Allison noticed it immediately—not because anyone said it out loud, but because of the way people didn’t. The way the day slipped back into its usual grooves with practiced ease. The way the air felt lighter, like a held breath released.

Emily went to school.

Their parents drank coffee and talked about schedules.

The house was calm.

That, somehow, felt worse.

Allison stood in front of the bathroom mirror longer than usual, toothbrush idle in her hand. She looked the same as she always did—hair pulled back, hoodie zipped halfway, eyes a little tired but not alarmingly so. No visible evidence of the night before.

No evidence of anything lingering.

She rinsed her toothbrush and left the bathroom.

At the breakfast table, Emily picked at her cereal without speaking. Her posture was guarded, shoulders slightly hunched, gaze fixed on the bowl.

“Do you want a ride today?” their mother asked, too casually.

Emily shrugged. “I’ll walk.”

“All right,” their mother said. “Just text when you get there.”

Emily nodded without looking up.

Allison waited for something to smooth. For a familiar sense of rightness to settle over the table.

Nothing did.

Her father cleared his throat. “We should probably talk later,” he said.

Emily stiffened.

“We will,” their mother said quickly. “Tonight.”

Emily nodded again and stood, grabbing her backpack.

“I’ll see you after school,” Allison said.

Emily paused at the door. For a moment, Allison thought she might say something—anything.

Instead, Emily said, “Thanks. For not.”

Allison blinked. “Not…?”

Emily shrugged. “You know.” She opened the door and left.

The house stayed quiet.

But it wasn’t the quiet Allison was used to.

---

At school, the shift was subtler—but unmistakable.

People were calmer.

Too calm.

Allison moved through the hallways with the same practiced ease she always had, but something had changed. It wasn’t resistance exactly—no one blocked her path or glared—but the absence of friction felt… wrong.

Like she was walking through water that didn’t ripple.

She stopped at her locker and frowned.

It opened on the first try.

She exhaled sharply and shut it, then reopened it.

Still smooth.

That shouldn’t have bothered her. It always used to feel like a relief.

Today, it made her uneasy.

In English, the teacher announced a pop discussion instead of the scheduled presentation. Groans rippled through the room—but they faded quickly, replaced by resigned acceptance.

“This is fine,” someone muttered.

Allison wrote it down without meaning to.

The discussion itself was oddly efficient. No tangents. No disagreements. People took turns. The bell rang early.

As the class emptied, Allison lingered, reorganizing her bag for no reason.

Evan passed her desk on the way out. He paused—not close, not obvious—and said quietly, “You okay today?”

She nodded. “Yeah.”

He studied her for a second longer than necessary. “That wasn’t a question you answered.”

She almost smiled. Almost.

“I’m here,” she said instead.

Evan nodded once and left.

He didn’t push.

That mattered.

---

Kara caught up with her at lunch.

“Hey,” Kara said, sliding into the seat across from her. “You disappeared yesterday.”

Allison blinked. “I did?”

“Yeah. You left without saying anything.” Kara frowned. “Are you okay?”

There it was again.

“Yes,” Allison said automatically. Then she hesitated. “I think so.”

Kara leaned back slightly, studying her. “That’s new.”

“What is?”

“That pause,” Kara said. “You usually don’t have to think about it.”

Allison looked down at her tray. “I had a family thing.”

“Oh.” Kara softened immediately. “Sorry. You don’t have to talk about it.”

Allison nodded, relieved.

But the relief didn’t last.

Kara picked at her food, then said, “You feel… different.”

Allison’s stomach tightened. “Different how?”

“I don’t know,” Kara said slowly. “Quieter? But not in the normal way.”

Allison’s fingers curled around her fork. “What’s the normal way?”

Kara smiled faintly. “You know. You’re always kind of… steady.”

There was that word again.

“And now?” Allison asked.

Kara hesitated. “Now it feels like you’re holding something.”

Allison laughed, too quickly. “I’m always holding something.”

“That’s true,” Kara said. “But usually it feels like it’s working.”

The words landed harder than Kara intended.

“I’m sorry,” Kara added quickly. “That came out wrong.”

Allison shook her head. “No, it’s fine.”

She meant it.

She just didn’t like what it implied.

---

Across the cafeteria, Evan watched them.

Not obviously. Not in a way that would draw attention. He sat alone today, notebook open but unused, gaze flicking up in brief intervals.

He saw Kara lean forward.

He saw Allison’s posture shift—subtle, defensive.

He didn’t move.

This wasn’t his moment.

That restraint felt like standing still in a current that wanted to carry him forward.

---

The afternoon dragged.

In chemistry, a group assignment went sideways when two students disagreed on a method. The argument didn’t escalate—but it didn’t resolve either. The tension lingered, thick and uncomfortable.

Allison felt the pull immediately.

Say something.  
Fix it.  
End it.

She pressed her pen harder into the page instead.

The argument fizzled out into silence—not peace, not agreement. Just avoidance.

The teacher noticed. “All right,” she said briskly. “Let’s move on.”

The unresolved tension stayed in Allison’s chest like a swallowed stone.

---

After school, Kara walked with her without asking.

They made it halfway down Main Street before Kara said, “Can I ask you something kind of personal?”

Allison nodded. “Okay.”

“Do you ever feel like you’re… responsible for how things go?” Kara asked.

Allison stopped walking.

Kara flushed. “Sorry. That was weird.”

“No,” Allison said quietly. “Why would you ask that?”

Kara shrugged. “Because when things are awkward, you’re always the one who smooths it over. And when you don’t…” She trailed off.

“What happens?” Allison asked.

Kara hesitated. “It’s uncomfortable.”

Allison nodded slowly. “Yeah.”

They stood there, the late afternoon light stretching shadows across the sidewalk.

“I don’t think that’s your job,” Kara said finally.

Allison swallowed. “I never said it was.”

“I know,” Kara said. “But it feels like you think it is.”

Allison didn’t have an answer for that.

Kara sighed. “I just—if something’s wrong, you can tell me. You don’t have to manage it.”

Allison smiled faintly. “I know.”

But even as she said it, she wasn’t sure she did.

They parted at the corner, Kara heading toward her neighborhood, Allison continuing on alone.

---

She didn’t go straight home.

The creek was restless again, water moving faster than it should have for such a calm day. Allison stood on the bridge and watched it, hands gripping the railing.

She pulled out her phone.

The Notes app opened automatically.

She stared at the last entry.

> **Failure**  
> It didn’t end.

She scrolled back through earlier notes. The language felt distant now—overly cautious, overly dismissive.

She opened a new note.

Her fingers hovered.

She typed:

> **After**  
>  
> People are calmer.  
> I’m not.

She stared at it.

She didn’t add a dismissal.

She saved it.

---

When she got home, Emily was already there, sprawled on the couch with her homework spread out around her. She looked up as Allison entered.

“Hey,” Emily said.

“Hey.”

They shared a look—tentative, cautious.

“How was school?” Allison asked.

Emily shrugged. “Fine. Weird.”

“Weird how?”

Emily frowned. “Everyone kept asking if I was okay.”

Allison winced. “Sorry.”

Emily smirked. “Not your fault.” She hesitated. “Actually… kind of is.”

Allison blinked. “What?”

Emily shrugged again. “You didn’t fix it. So now everyone thinks something’s wrong.”

Allison sat down slowly. “Is something wrong?”

Emily considered that. “Yeah. But I think that’s okay.”

Allison smiled despite herself.

Their parents came in later, tired but lighter than the night before. Dinner was quieter—not tense, not smooth. Just… human.

No one praised Allison.

No one thanked her.

The house stayed imperfect.

---

That night, Allison lay in bed listening to the sounds of the house—not settling, not escalating. Just existing.

Her phone buzzed.

A text from Evan.

*How’d today feel?*

She stared at the screen for a long moment.

*Different,* she typed. *Unfinished.*

*That’s not a bad thing,* he replied after a pause.

She considered that.

*I don’t know what to do with it.*

*You don’t have to,* Evan wrote. *Yet.*

She set the phone down and stared at the ceiling crack.

It looked less like a question tonight.

More like a fracture.
