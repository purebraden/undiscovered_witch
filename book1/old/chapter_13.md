# Chapter 13 — Interlude: Aftermath

The incident was addressed.

That was the language used.

Not resolved.  
Not understood.  
Not harmful.

Addressed.

A meeting was held behind closed doors. Notes were taken. A decision was recorded. The timeline was shortened until it fit neatly into a single line on a report.

An event occurred.  
An intervention followed.  
The matter was concluded.

The school sent out an email to parents that afternoon.

It emphasized safety.  
It emphasized community values.  
It emphasized that appropriate steps had been taken.

No names were mentioned.

In the days that followed, the building adjusted.

Attendance lists updated automatically. Seating charts were revised. Teachers were instructed to be flexible, though no guidance was given on what flexibility should look like in practice.

Some were lenient.

Some were not.

Counselors kept their doors open longer. A box of tissues appeared on a side table. A poster about resilience was taped to the wall where the edges curled slightly, like it hadn’t been pressed down long enough to stay.

Students were reminded about digital responsibility.

Assemblies were scheduled.

The focus was forward-looking.

There were no formal acknowledgments of loss.

There was no collective pause.

The hallways absorbed the change the way they always did — quietly, unevenly, with certain corners carrying more weight than others.

Some students spoke in whispers for a few days, voices dipping when teachers passed. Others joked too loudly, relief curdling into noise. A few asked careful questions and received vague answers in return.

Eventually, most stopped asking.

The absence remained.

It lived in the gaps between lockers. In the chair that was no longer there. In the way conversations shifted without anyone consciously deciding to change them.

The system preferred this version of events.

Contained.  
Documented.  
Closed.

By the end of the week, the incident was no longer considered active.

It existed only in records, filed away under language that suggested order had been restored.

From the outside, it appeared that nothing had broken.

From the inside, something essential had been quietly removed.

And the structure, satisfied with its response, moved on.
