# Chapter 2 — Things That Go Smoothly

The worst part was that nothing felt dramatic.

If Allison had woken up with a headache, or a sense of dread, or even that hollow, buzzing feeling she sometimes got before exams, she would have trusted it more. But she woke up feeling… fine.

Not rested. Not good.

Just fine.

The ceiling crack was still there. The house sounded the same. Emily was already talking to someone through her bedroom door, her voice bright and fast like she was narrating her own life.

Allison pulled on clean jeans and a sweater and told herself yesterday had been a fluke.

People had arguments all the time. Teachers skipped students accidentally. Bells rang late. It wasn’t like she’d asked for anything specific.

She packed her bag, double-checked she had her homework, and left the house on time.

Fine.

School looked the same too.

The parking lot buzzed with engines and laughter. Someone shouted across three rows of cars. A group of girls leaned against the brick wall near the entrance, phones held up at identical angles, faces tilted just enough to catch the light.

Allison walked past them, invisible in the way she preferred.

First period was English.

She slid into her seat just as the bell finished ringing and opened her notebook. The room smelled faintly like dry erase markers and whatever perfume the girl two rows up insisted on bathing in.

Mrs. Harlan stood at the front, flipping through papers.

“Okay,” she said, clapping her hands once. “We’re doing cold reads today.”

Allison followed along half-listening, eyes on her notebook, tracing the margin of the page with her pen.

Please don’t call on me, she thought absently — the same way she always did during cold reads.

Voices read aloud. Pages turned.

It wasn’t until the reading ended that Allison looked up.

Her name was still untouched.

She frowned.

Mrs. Harlan was already moving on, collecting papers, the moment clearly over.

Allison flipped back through her notes, trying to remember if she’d misheard the order. If the seating chart had changed. If she’d missed something obvious.

She hadn’t.

The realization settled slowly, like cold water.

That was my turn.

Second period was biology.

Group lab day.

Allison slid onto a stool at the lab table, hands folded in her lap. She scanned the room automatically, noting who was already paired off and who wasn’t.

Her stomach sank when she saw who was still standing alone.

Anyone but him, Allison thought.

She immediately felt bad for it.

She wasn’t cruel. She wasn’t even unkind. She’d work with him if she had to.

Mrs. Grant clapped her hands. “Okay, looks like we’ve got one extra today. Let’s shuffle just a bit.”

A girl at the next table groaned. “Seriously? We already set up.”

“Just one swap,” Mrs. Grant said cheerfully. “You’ll live.”

She pointed. “You—go join them. And you—switch places.”

The awkward boy blinked, startled, then shuffled toward the other table.

Someone else took the empty seat beside Allison instead.

Allison stared at the lab equipment in front of her, ears ringing softly.

Third period was history.

Allison checked the clock on her phone as she rounded the corner near the science wing.

Two minutes.

By the time she reached the stairwell, she knew she was going to be late.

Not disastrously late. Just enough to draw attention.

Just this once, she thought, distracted and breathless.

She pushed through the classroom door.

No bell.

Mr. Alvarez was still shuffling papers at his desk. A few students were talking. Someone laughed.

Allison slipped into her seat, pulse loud in her ears.

The bell rang thirty seconds later.

She sat very still.

Relief came first.

Then unease.

The hallway between third and fourth period was slower than usual.

Not quieter — just clogged, bodies moving in uneven clumps, stopping and starting without warning.

Allison nearly ran into someone at the corner and apologized automatically.

She reached her locker, leaned back against the cool metal, and tried to breathe.

Nothing is happening, she told herself.

Fourth period was math.

She noticed herself almost thinking please don’t call on me and stopped the thought before it finished forming.

She stared at her notebook.

Mr. Jacobs called on students.

Not her.

She didn’t feel relieved.

She felt watched by her own attention.

Lunch was louder than she expected.

She spotted Kara at their usual table and slid into the seat across from her.

They talked. They laughed. Kara complained about a quiz. Allison made the right faces at the right times.

Normal.

Except Allison felt like she was holding something unsaid the entire time.

She imagined telling Kara.

She imagined Kara laughing or worrying.

She said nothing.

After school, Allison didn’t go straight home.

She turned toward the narrow path behind the houses and followed it to the creek.

The water moved steadily over smooth stones, indifferent and constant.

She sat and watched it.

She didn’t do anything.

She didn’t wish.

She stayed until the air grew colder and the light shifted.

Then she went home.

She went to her room.

She opened her phone.
