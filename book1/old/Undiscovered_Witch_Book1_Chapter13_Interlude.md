# Chapter 13 — Interlude: Aftermath

Nothing dramatic happened.

That was the problem.

The building stayed standing. Classes continued. Bells rang on schedule. Teachers kept teaching, students kept moving, the day kept insisting on normalcy with a stubborn, grinding persistence.

But something had shifted.

It showed up in small systems first.

A chair scraped too loudly and then didn’t scrape again. A laugh cut off halfway through. A hallway conversation that changed subjects when someone new stepped into earshot.

The school didn’t fracture.

It re-routed.

Administrative emails went out in careful language. Meetings were scheduled. Words like *incident*, *concern*, and *appropriate next steps* circulated without ever touching the thing itself.

No one said Kara’s name in those messages.

On a whiteboard in the counselor’s office, three colors appeared in neat blocks:

Red — escalation  
Yellow — mediation  
Green — resolution  

They were supposed to be neutral. They weren’t.

Red meant eyes.  
Yellow meant watching.  
Green meant *we believe this is over.*

Most situations, Allison realized later, never made it to green.

They just faded into yellow and stayed there, unresolved but quiet enough to be tolerated.

That was the real goal.

Quiet.

By the end of the week, the video stopped circulating — not because it disappeared, but because attention moved on. Something newer. Something easier.

The damage didn’t move on.

It settled.

It lived in glances that lingered too long and conversations that never quite started. It lived in the empty desk that stayed empty until someone finally moved it, and even then the space felt wrong.

The system logged the event as handled.

The people inside it carried the rest.
