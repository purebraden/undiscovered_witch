# CHAPTER 10 — Resistance

Allison decided to stop answering.

Not dramatically. Not all at once. She didn’t announce it or write it down or make a rule she could fail at later. She simply… paused. Let messages sit unread. Let conversations move past her without stepping in.

It felt wrong immediately.

Her phone buzzed during first period—someone from math asking if she could talk to the teacher about retaking a quiz. Allison turned the screen face-down on her desk and stared at the whiteboard instead.

The buzzing stopped.

Her chest tightened anyway.

In the hallway between classes, two girls argued near the lockers. Voices sharp, words clipped. Allison felt the familiar pressure rise, coiling tight behind her ribs.

She kept walking.

The argument didn’t stop. It escalated—one girl’s voice cracking, the other snapping back, footsteps drawing attention. A teacher appeared, irritation already written across her face.

Allison didn’t look back.

By lunch, she felt brittle.

---

The cafeteria was louder than usual, or maybe Allison was just more aware of it now. Conversations overlapped, laughter spiked and fell, chairs scraped loudly against the floor.

Kara sat with Jess and two other girls Allison didn’t know well. There was still space beside her.

Allison hesitated, tray in her hands.

She sat anyway.

Kara smiled. It was warmer today. Not the careful politeness of the last week, but not the old ease either.

“Hey,” Kara said.

“Hey.”

Jess was mid-rant about a group project. “—and then he just didn’t show up. Like, at all. And now we’re supposed to cover for him?”

One of the other girls scoffed. “That’s such a Mark thing.”

Kara rolled her eyes. “I swear, half the school would fall apart without reminders.”

The conversation edged toward frustration. Voices sharpened.

Allison felt it—the pull, immediate and insistent.

She wrapped her fingers tighter around her fork.

Jess glanced at her. “What do you think?”

Allison swallowed. “I think… you should tell him he needs to show up.”

Jess blinked. “That’s it?”

“That’s it,” Allison said.

The moment stretched.

Then Jess shrugged. “Yeah. I guess.”

The tension didn’t dissolve. It lingered, awkward and unresolved.

Kara watched Allison closely.

When lunch ended, Kara walked with her toward the doors.

“You okay?” Kara asked.

“I think so,” Allison said.

Kara nodded slowly. “You seem different.”

Allison waited.

“Not bad,” Kara added quickly. “Just… different.”

They parted at the hallway junction without hugging.

Allison felt the absence like a bruise.

---

In chemistry, Mark slid into the seat beside her.

“Hey,” he said, lowering his voice. “I texted you earlier.”

“I know,” Allison said.

He hesitated. “You didn’t answer.”

“I didn’t,” she agreed.

Mark frowned. “Did I do something?”

“No,” Allison said. “I just… can’t do that anymore.”

“Do what?”

“Fix things,” she said.

Mark laughed awkwardly. “You make it sound like you have superpowers.”

Allison didn’t laugh.

Mark shifted uncomfortably. “It’s not a big deal. I just thought—everyone says you’re good at helping people calm down.”

The words landed heavily.

“I’m not responsible for that,” Allison said, more sharply than she intended.

Mark recoiled slightly. “Okay. Sorry.”

He turned away, shoulders tense.

Allison stared down at her lab manual, heart racing.

She had wanted this—to stop being asked.

She hadn’t expected how much it would hurt.

---

The rest of the day followed suit.

A teacher paused expectantly when a class discussion grew heated. Allison kept her gaze fixed on her notes.

A girl in the bathroom cried quietly in one of the stalls. Allison washed her hands and left.

A rumor flared and burned through three conversations without Allison touching it. The aftermath was messier than usual—people upset longer, misunderstandings lingering.

The town didn’t break.

But it didn’t smooth itself either.

By the time the final bell rang, Allison felt raw.

She didn’t go home right away.

She walked toward the creek instead, the path muddy from last night’s rain. The water moved faster today, swollen and restless.

Allison leaned against the railing and closed her eyes.

The pressure inside her hadn’t gone away.

If anything, it felt worse—less focused, more diffuse. Like she’d unplugged something essential and the system didn’t know how to reroute yet.

Her phone buzzed.

She almost ignored it.

Almost.

It was Evan.

*Are you okay?*

She stared at the screen.

*I don’t know,* she typed. *I stopped helping.*

A pause.

*How does that feel?* he replied.

She considered the question carefully.

*Like I’m doing the wrong thing for the right reasons,* she sent.

Another pause.

*That’s still progress,* Evan replied.

She wasn’t sure she believed him.

---

At home, tension waited for her.

Emily was pacing the living room, phone clutched tightly in her hand.

“Allison,” she said, the moment Allison stepped inside. “Can you talk to Mom?”

Allison froze. “About what?”

Emily gestured wildly. “She’s being impossible. She won’t listen to me.”

Allison took a breath. “What’s going on?”

“She won’t let me go to Jenna’s tonight,” Emily snapped. “For no reason.”

“That’s not true,” their mother called from the kitchen. “It’s a school night.”

Emily turned on Allison. “You see? She never does this to you.”

Allison felt the familiar pull flare.

She forced herself to stay still.

“Emily,” Allison said carefully, “I’m not getting involved.”

Emily stared at her like she’d slapped her.

“What?”

“I’m not getting involved,” Allison repeated. “This is between you and Mom.”

Emily’s face crumpled. “You always help.”

“I know,” Allison said softly. “I can’t right now.”

Emily’s voice rose. “Why not?”

Allison swallowed. “Because it doesn’t feel right.”

Their mother appeared in the doorway, concern etched into her face. “What’s going on?”

Emily laughed harshly. “Your favorite mediator just quit.”

Allison flinched.

“That’s not fair,” her mother said.

Emily rounded on her. “Then you talk to her!”

Their mother sighed. “Emily, we’ll discuss it later.”

Emily stormed upstairs, slamming her door.

The house rang with the sound.

Allison’s hands shook.

Her mother turned to her. “You okay?”

Allison nodded, even as her chest ached. “I think so.”

Her mother studied her for a long moment. “You don’t have to fix everything.”

Allison laughed weakly. “Everyone keeps saying that.”

“Then maybe it’s time to listen,” her mother said gently.

Allison nodded again.

She didn’t feel relieved.

---

That night, Allison lay awake staring at the ceiling.

The house was quiet, but not calm.

She replayed the day in fragments—Mark’s confusion, Jess’s lingering frustration, Emily’s hurt.

She opened her Notes app.

Her fingers hovered.

She typed:

> **Refusal**  
>  
> I didn’t step in.  
> Things kept going.  
>  
> People were upset longer.

She paused.

Then added:

> This didn’t make me invisible.

She stared at the words.

They felt dangerous.

She saved the note anyway.

---

Friday arrived with a brittle edge.

By third period, Allison could feel eyes on her—glances that lingered a beat too long, conversations that stalled when she approached.

She wasn’t being avoided.

She was being recalibrated around.

In the hallway, a boy bumped into her and muttered an apology. Normally, she would have smiled, softened the moment.

She nodded and kept walking.

The boy frowned after her, unsettled.

At lunch, Kara didn’t sit beside her right away.

She hesitated, tray in hand, then chose the seat across from Allison instead.

They ate in silence for a few minutes.

Finally, Kara said, “You didn’t fix things yesterday.”

Allison’s heart skipped. “No.”

Kara nodded. “I noticed.”

“I’m trying something,” Allison said.

Kara considered that. “Does it hurt?”

“Yes,” Allison said honestly.

Kara smiled faintly. “Good.”

Allison blinked.

“I mean,” Kara continued, “not good-good. Just… real.”

They shared a quiet moment, fragile but honest.

Across the room, Evan watched and said nothing.

---

The real test came last period.

A group presentation spiraled when two students disagreed loudly over who had done more work. Voices rose. Accusations flew.

The teacher looked overwhelmed.

Allison felt the pressure spike—sharp and urgent, demanding release.

She gripped the edge of her desk.

This was the kind of moment she had always ended.

She didn’t.

The argument burned itself out slowly, clumsily. Apologies were half-hearted. The presentation continued, awkward but intact.

The class survived.

The bell rang.

Allison exhaled shakily.

As students filed out, the teacher caught her eye.

“You okay?” she asked.

Allison nodded. “Yes.”

The teacher hesitated. “You usually help keep things… smooth.”

Allison met her gaze. “I’m not doing that anymore.”

The teacher studied her, then nodded. “Fair enough.”

The acknowledgment felt heavier than praise.

---

After school, Evan walked with her toward the exit.

“You did it,” he said.

“I don’t feel victorious,” Allison replied.

“Good,” Evan said. “Victory would mean you’d replaced one role with another.”

Allison frowned. “Then what is this?”

“Adjustment,” he said. “Systems don’t like it.”

She glanced back at the school. “Neither do people.”

“People adapt,” Evan said. “Eventually.”

Allison stopped walking. “What if they don’t?”

Evan met her gaze. “Then you’ll learn who they are without you.”

The thought terrified her.

---

That night, rain fell again.

Allison sat by her window, watching it streak the glass.

She opened her Notes app one last time.

She added a line beneath the earlier entry.

> I thought resistance would feel powerful.  
>  
> It feels lonely.

She closed the app.

The rain didn’t stop.

But it didn’t drown her either.
